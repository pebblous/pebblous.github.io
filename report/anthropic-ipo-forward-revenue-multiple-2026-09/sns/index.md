## LinkedIn (EN)

The roughly $2 trillion attached to Anthropic's planned listing is not divided by this year's revenue. It is divided by a forecast for 2028.

The Wall Street Journal reported on September 19 that the offering has slipped to November so investors can see third-quarter numbers first. The arithmetic behind the price tag sits elsewhere.

A month earlier, Reuters reported that bankers and investors are applying enterprise value-to-revenue multiples to forecasts, and that the forecast in question is Anthropic's own internal projection of $190 to $200 billion in 2028 revenue.

Against that forecast, the price tag is roughly ten times. Against the annualized run rate recorded in July, it is roughly thirty-one. Neither figure is wrong. They divide by different years.

Reuters attached a caveat in the same story. Revenue multiples are common for high-growth software companies without a mature profit profile, but looking two years ahead is less typical. No public registration statement has appeared yet, and once the denominator moves two years out, the party carrying a shortfall is whoever buys the stock at that price.

The money that would fill the 2028 denominator has to come out of next year's budgets at the organizations now running agent pilots. In Anthropic's own survey of more than 500 US technology executives, the barriers named most often were integration with existing systems, cost, and data access and quality. Model capability was not among them. For a company reading this news, the document worth opening is not the vendor's prospectus but its own contract.

## Facebook (EN)

"Two trillion dollars."

That is the figure attached to the Anthropic listing. For a few days now it has been travelling on its own, detached from everything printed beside it.

What held me was the smaller print. The number is not divided by this year's revenue. It is divided by a forecast for 2028.

2028 has not arrived. Nobody has earned that money yet.

So where is it supposed to come from?

Out of next year's budgets at the organizations now running agent pilots. People sitting in meeting rooms, redrawing a line item for tools. Those approvals, added up, are the 2028 denominator.

I have started calling it "our line in someone else's forecast."

What blocks that line has already been measured, by the vendor itself. Anthropic surveyed more than 500 US technology executives, and the report closes by saying that the largest obstacle to getting value from AI is not model capability but organizational readiness: data accessibility, and the work of assembling context.

The word revenue is in a similar state. An annualized run rate, a quarter's actual receipts, and a forecast two years out all travel under the same name, and that name has no agreed grammar yet. A figure that loses the year behind it only looks more precise as it grows.

"Whose growth curve is our data sitting inside, and where is the consideration written down?"

Conversations with our customers tend to start here. Whether the contract names which internal systems the agent may reach. Whether our data is used for training, and where it remains, and for how long. Whether what the agent did is logged on our side, and whether it can be deleted. Six columns, and not one of them asks about model performance.

Verifying someone else's price tag may not be our job. But if our line sits in the denominator of that price, only we can check where our own terms are written into it.
