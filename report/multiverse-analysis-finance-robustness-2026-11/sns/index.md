같은 자연실험을 나눠 쓴 금융 논문 324편을 AI 에이전트가 원자료부터 다시 모아 통째로 다시 돌렸다. 논문 자신의 설정에서 주요 추정치가 다시 나온 것은 발표된 검정이 있는 방향성 주장 305건 중 128건이었다.

미국 UT 댈러스의 Ruijiang Gao와 Steven Chong Xiao가 11월 18일 arXiv에 올린 프리프린트다. 저자가 고른 설정 하나만 보는 대신 그 논문의 맥락에서 정당화할 수 있는 설정을 전부 만들어 한꺼번에 돌리는 방법을 다중우주 분석이라 부른다. 지금까지 연구 한 편을 두고 쓰던 방법을 한 분야의 문헌 전체에 걸었다는 것이 저자들이 내세우는 차별점이다.

결론은 한 번에 무너지지 않고 한 칸씩 깎였다. 저자 설정에서 재현된 114건을 보면 저자 자신의 자리에서 주장을 지키는 비율이 81%인데, 정당한 다른 선택지로 하나만 갈아 끼우면 64%로 내려간다.

다만 흔들린 쪽은 방향이 아니라 확신이다. 방향성 주장 304건의 평균으로 발표된 부호를 그대로 지키는 설정은 68%이고, 부호와 유의성을 함께 지키는 설정은 25%다. 논문은 결론부에 선을 하나 그어 두었다. 주장을 지키는 비율이 낮다는 것은 그 주장이 분석 선택에 의존한다는 뜻이지 거짓이라는 뜻이 아니다.

결론을 가장 자주 쥐고 있던 선택은 추정 기법이 아니라 결과 변수를 무엇으로 정의하느냐였고, 바로 그런 선택들에 대해 논문은 대개 아무 말도 적어 두지 않았다. 재현되지 않은 추정치 가운데 관측치 수를 견줄 수 있었던 163건 중 141건은 설정을 비교하기도 전에 표의 행 수부터 발표값과 1% 넘게 벌어져 있었다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

AI agents rebuilt the underlying data for 324 finance papers that share the same natural experiments and reran each one across every setup the paper's own context could justify. At the specification each paper actually reported, 128 of 305 directional claims with a published test came back.

The preprint was posted to arXiv on 18 November by Ruijiang Gao and Steven Chong Xiao of UT Dallas. Multiverse analysis, which builds every defensible combination of analytical choices instead of reporting one, has until now been applied to a single study at a time. Running it across a whole literature is what the authors put forward as new.

Conclusions did not collapse at once. They were shaved. Among the 114 claims that reproduced at the authors' own setup, that setup supports the claim 81% of the time, and swapping a single choice for another defensible option drops it to 64%.

What erodes is confidence rather than direction. Averaged across 304 directional claims, specifications keep the published sign 68% of the time and keep sign and significance together 25% of the time. The paper draws its own line in the conclusion: a low share does not establish that the claim is false, only that it depends on analytical choices.

The choice that most often held the conclusion was not the estimator but how the outcome variable was defined, and those were mostly choices the papers never discussed. Earlier still, among failed reproductions where the observation count could be compared at all, 141 of 163 differed from the published count by more than 1% before any specification was varied.

## Facebook (EN)

Have you ever rebuilt a table you first pulled six months ago?

The query is the same, and the row count is not.

A study posted to arXiv last week counted how often that happens in published research.

Two researchers at UT Dallas gathered 324 finance papers built on the same natural experiments and had AI agents collect the raw data again, rebuild the tables, and rerun the analysis.

What stayed with me was not the reproduction rate. It was the step before it.

Among the estimates that failed to reproduce, and where the observation count could be compared at all, 141 of 163 had a row count more than 1% away from the published one.

Before anyone argues about estimators, the same table is not coming back.

Filter conditions. Join order. How missing values were handled. Where the sample window was cut.

I have started thinking of these as the unwritten choices.

Not wrong choices. Choices that are nowhere on the page.

In the same study, the thing that most often held the conclusion was not the estimator either. It was how the outcome variable had been defined.

And those were the choices the papers mostly said nothing about.

The direction of the findings largely survived. What went first was the verdict that a result was statistically significant.

The authors draw the line themselves in the conclusion: a low share does not establish that a claim is false, only that it depends on analytical choices.

Pebblous measures whether data is fit to use, with DataClinic. Data can pass every check for gaps, duplicates and labels and still fail to return the same number, if the choices made on the way to that number were never recorded.

"Would the number in our own report still stand if one setting were changed?"

A better model lowers the cost of running everything again. It does not help if there is no record of what was chosen the first time.
