## LinkedIn (KO)

같은 점수가 네 군데에 적혀 있는데 분모를 함께 적은 곳은 한 곳뿐이다. 그 한 곳이 적은 분모가 논문의 분모와 다르다.

11월 9일 앤트로픽이 내놓은 Claude Fable 5.2가 Remote Labor Index에서 41.25%를 받아 맨 윗자리에 올랐다. 업워크 등에서 실제로 돈이 오간 프로젝트를 사들여 만든 시험이고, 평가자 세 명이 발주자가 받아 줄 만한지를 다수결로 가린다. 분자와 분모를 나란히 적어 둔 곳은 앤트로픽 시스템 카드뿐이고, 99 of 240이라고 쓰여 있다.

논문은 다르게 적는다. 개인정보를 지키고 문제가 학습 데이터로 새는 것을 막으려고 비공개 230건을 정량 평가에 쓴다고 적혀 있다. 11월 13일 공개 리더보드 스물세 줄을 전부 내려받아 두 수로 되곱아 봤다. 240을 곱하면 스물세 줄이 모두 정수로 떨어지고, 230을 곱하면 한 줄도 떨어지지 않는다. 해명은 논문과 부록, 각주, 공식 사이트 어디에도 없었다. 230이 틀렸다고 단정하지는 않는다. 두 분모가 맞지 않는다는 데까지가 확인된 것이다.

분모가 둘이면 무슨 일이 생기는지는 지난여름에 드러났다. 2026년 7월 1일 미국 AI 안전센터는 같은 모델의 자동화율을 기관 블로그에 15.8%로, 같은 날 엑스에 16.1%로 올렸다. 엑스 쪽에는 미국 정부 자료에 접근하지 못해 스물두 건을 평가하지 못했다는 단서가 붙었다.

본지도 그때 16.1%를 제목에 걸었다. 오늘 리더보드에 올라 있는 같은 모델의 점수는 15.83%다.

역산해 보면 갈린 것이 분모만은 아니다. 15.8%는 240분의 38이고 16.1%는 218분의 35다. 분모가 스물두 건 줄어드는 사이 분자도 세 건이 줄었는데, 그 세 건이 어디로 갔는지는 두 채널 어디에도 없다. 분모 하나로 설명이 끝난 것처럼 보였을 뿐이다.

페블러스의 DataClinic이 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단한다. 같은 데이터셋의 성적서 두 장이 어긋날 때도 사정이 비슷하다. 대상 범위가 달랐다는 설명을 한 번 보고 나면 나머지 칸은 잘 세지 않게 된다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

One score, printed in four places, and only one of them prints a denominator. The denominator it prints is not the one in the paper.

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9 and it went to the top of the Remote Labor Index at 41.25%. The test runs on real paid projects bought mostly off Upwork; three graders decide by majority whether a client would accept the result. Only Anthropic's system card sets the numerator next to the denominator: 99 of 240.

The paper puts it differently. To keep personal data protected and the questions out of training sets, it writes, quantitative evaluation runs on a private set of 230 projects. On November 13 we pulled all twenty-three leaderboard rows and multiplied every score back out by both. Against 240 every row resolves to a whole number. Against 230 not one does. The paper, its appendices, the leaderboard footnotes and the official site carry no explanation. That is not grounds for calling the 230 wrong, only for saying the two do not agree.

What two denominators do to a number showed itself once already, last summer. On July 1, 2026 the Center for AI Safety posted the same model's automation rate as 15.8% on its blog and 16.1% on X. The post on X carried a caveat: twenty-two projects could not be evaluated, the US government materials they needed being unavailable.

This publication put that 16.1% in its own headline then. The leaderboard today has the model at 15.83%.

Work the two figures back into counts and the denominator turns out to be only part of what moved. 15.8% is 38 over 240.

16.1% is 35 over 218. The denominator lost twenty-two projects and the numerator lost three at the same time, and where those three went is recorded in neither channel. One explanation was offered, and it covered half of it.

DataClinic is the Pebblous tool that puts numbers on AI training data. Two reports on one dataset disagree the same way. A line explaining that the scope differed is usually enough to close it, and the rest of the cells go uncounted. The useful count is not the cells that reconciled but the ones nobody checked.

## Facebook (EN)

Before a piece of work goes outside, somebody sits down and writes one page.

What is to be made. What state counts as finished. Where the input files are.

Nobody bills for that page.

The Remote Labor Index changed hands on November 9, which is why I opened it again. Scale AI and the Center for AI Safety went onto Upwork and elsewhere, bought 240 jobs that clients had genuinely paid for, gave an AI the same brief, and laid its output next to what the human had delivered. Three graders decide by majority whether a client would accept it.

Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 came back at 41.25%.

The appendix is where the 240 get a history.

Those 240 are the survivors of 550 candidates. Three hundred and ten were dropped, and the conditions that dropped them are written out: one freelancer has to be able to finish it alone, the job cannot require going back and forth with the client, and the result has to be judgeable on the spot.

Each survivor arrives with a standardized brief, a complete set of input files, and an agreed bar for what finished means.

Which is to say the page had been written before the test began.

"The work before the brief."

"Who in your own organization is writing that page right now?"

Pebblous sifts AI training data with DataClinic and sets a figure on what it finds. That figure answers for the material as it arrived. It carries no field for who sorted the material into that state, or how long that took them.

The automation rate gets a fresh reading every time a model ships. Where the person doing the work before the brief sits on an org chart is something we have not worked out on our own side either.
