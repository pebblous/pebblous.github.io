피지컬 AI 순위표를 떠받치는 벤치마크 열두 개 가운데, 서로 같은 것을 재고 있던 쌍이 둘 있었다.

Metric AI Lab이 모델 51개와 벤치마크 12개로 짠 성긴 행렬을 메우고 그 겹침을 통계로 쟀다. 나올 수 있는 쌍이 하나도 빠짐없이 양의 상관이었고, 그중 두 쌍은 사실상 대체재였다.

두 쌍을 각각 한 열로 합쳐 순위를 다시 계산하자 모델 22개가 세 계단 넘게 자리를 옮겼다. 전체의 40%가 넘는다. 총점이 벤치마크 점수의 평균이면 겹치는 능력은 두 번 세어지고, 무게는 능력이 아니라 그 능력을 몇 번 쟀는가를 따라가기 때문이다.

구글 딥마인드의 Gemini Robotics 1.5 리포트는 임바디드 추론 총점의 절반을 포인팅 계열 벤치마크 넷에 준다. 그 넷 안에 논문이 대체재로 지목한 쌍이 들어앉아 있다.

저자들은 새 순위를 올바른 리더보드라고 주장하지 않는다. 쌍을 합치는 것 자체가 하나의 선택이라서다. 열두 개 가운데 로봇 팔이 실제로 물건을 집어 올렸는지를 재는 시험지가 하나도 없다는 것도 저자들이 적어 둔 한계다.

평가 스위트도 결국 데이터셋이다. 중복이 있고 가중치가 기울고 출처가 엇갈린다면, 학습 데이터에 하는 일을 모델을 채점하는 데이터에도 해야 한다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Twelve benchmarks hold up the physical AI leaderboard. Two pairs of them were measuring the same thing.

Metric AI Lab filled in the sparse matrix of 51 models against 12 benchmarks and measured the overlap statistically. Every possible pair correlated positively, and two of them behaved as substitutes for each other.

Collapsing each pair into a single column and recomputing the ranking moved 22 models by more than three places, over 40% of the field. The arithmetic is plain: when a headline number is the average of a set of benchmarks, an ability measured twice gets counted twice, and the weight it carries follows how often it was tested rather than how good the model is.

Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 1.5 report gives half of its embodied reasoning score to four pointing benchmarks. One of the substitute pairs sits inside those four.

The authors are careful about what this shows. They do not propose the de-duplicated ranking as the correct leaderboard, since collapsing pairs is itself a choice. They also note that none of the twelve benchmarks measures whether a robot arm actually picked anything up.

An evaluation suite is a dataset. If it carries duplicates, skewed weights and inconsistent provenance, whatever we already do to training data belongs on the data we grade models with.

## Facebook (EN)

Picture the moment you have to choose between two models to put on a robot.

You open each model card. The exam one company lays out is not the exam the other lays out. A few columns overlap, and the rest are simply blank.

Where they do overlap, the trouble is worse.

I went and opened Google's Gemini Robotics 1.5 report. Four of its benchmarks test one ability, pointing at the coordinate where an object should be picked up, and half of the embodied reasoning score comes from those four.

An exam that counts the same thing twice. On a table like that, the total follows not how capable the model is but how often that capability was tested.

Metric AI Lab, an applied research lab, measured the overlap statistically. They filled in the hole-ridden table for 51 models and 12 benchmarks and found the pairs that stand in for one another. Counting each pair once instead of twice moved 22 models by three or more places.

The authors did not call the new ordering the correct leaderboard. Collapsing a pair, they wrote, is itself a choice.

A question stays with me all the same.

"Is this model ahead because of what it can do, or because of how the exam was assembled?"

That is why we spent so long with this paper. Stripping duplicates out of training data and checking whether the representation has tilted is our daily work. The paper runs that same procedure on the data we grade models with. An evaluation suite turns out to be a dataset like any other.

The data a report card diagnoses gets measured.
