정부가 산 것은 착공이고, 내려놓은 것은 하드웨어 자립을 밀어붙이던 강제력입니다. 국산 칩은 이제 명령이 아니라 시장 검증을 통과할 때만 데이터센터에 들어갑니다.

문제는 그 검증입니다. 이기종 칩을 공정하게 비교할 표준 벤치마크와 고품질 검증 데이터는 아직 얇은데, 자본은 칩에 쏠리고 그 칩이 제 값을 하는지 재는 데이터 인프라는 뒤처져 있습니다. 착공은 시작됐지만, 국산 엔진이 쓸 만한지 판별할 데이터 인프라는 아직 첫 삽을 뜨지 않았습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Korea's national AI compute center, which drew zero bidders across two tenders, broke ground in Q3 2026 after the government struck a clause requiring domestic NPUs.

The project in Solasido, Haenam had stalled under three overlapping conditions: 51% public ownership, a buyback obligation, and a mandate to fill up to half the center's silicon with domestic NPUs by 2030. When the third tender relaxed all three, Samsung SDS's consortium came in as the sole bidder, and the state stepped back to a 29% minority stake.

What the government bought was groundbreaking; what it set down was the coercive push for hardware self-reliance. Domestic chips now enter the data center only when they pass market validation, not by decree.

That validation is the hard part. Standard benchmarks and trustworthy validation data for comparing heterogeneous chips remain thin, even as capital pours into the silicon itself. The ground has been broken; the data infrastructure to judge whether the domestic engine is any good has not.

고속도로는 국가가 짓되, 그 위를 달릴 엔진을 국산으로 채울지는 의무가 아니라 선택으로 돌린 셈입니다.

소버린 AI에서 진짜 주권은 컴퓨트라는 그릇일까요, 아니면 그 위에 흐르며 그릇을 검증하는 데이터일까요. 착공은 시작됐지만, 그 답을 가려 줄 데이터 인프라는 아직 첫 삽을 뜨지 않았습니다.

## Facebook (EN)

This summer, an excavator started breaking ground on a plot in Haenam, on Korea's southwest coast.

It was the national AI compute center, the one that had drawn zero bidders through two straight tenders, finally getting underway.

So what changed? The government loosened the clauses that had cornered private investors, and the most symbolic of them was this: it dropped, entirely, the requirement to fill up to half the center's chips with domestic NPUs by 2030.

The state would still build the highway. Whether the engine running on it is Korean-made became a choice, not a command.

Where the mandate used to be, a quieter question now sits.

"Is this domestic chip actually good enough?"

Under the old rule, that question could wait; half the slots had to be filled regardless. Now it is the one thing that decides adoption. And to answer it fairly, you need agreed-upon benchmarks and trustworthy validation data. Money and chips are surging, yet the data that measures whether those chips are worth it keeps ending up as a blank line in the budget.

In sovereign AI, where does real sovereignty live: in compute, the vessel, or in the data that flows through it and tells us whether the vessel holds? The ground has been broken. The data infrastructure to answer that has not.
