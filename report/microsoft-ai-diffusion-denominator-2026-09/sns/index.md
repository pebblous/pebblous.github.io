## LinkedIn (KO)

마이크로소프트가 9월에 공개한 나라별 AI 사용률 순위에서, 147개 경제 가운데 36곳은 자기 나라를 관측한 값을 갖고 있지 않다.

보고서가 읽으라고 지목한 기술 논문의 부록 표에는 행마다 칼표가 붙어 있고, 표 아래 범례가 그 뜻을 한 줄로 밝힌다. 텔레메트리 커버리지가 불충분해 지역 평균으로 대입한 추정치라는 것이다. 그렇게 표시된 행이 36개다. 나이지리아의 10.4%는 나이지리아를 잰 값이 아니라 서아프리카 열두 나라가 나눠 쓰는 하나의 숫자이고, 대입된 경제의 근로연령 인구를 모두 더하면 약 6억 명이다.

은폐가 아니다. 논문은 방법을 본문에 적었고 부록에 행별로 표시했다. 걸리는 것은 그 표시가 따라오는 거리다. 인용자가 실제로 보는 공개 데이터셋과 보고서 리더보드에는 어느 행이 대입값인지 알려 주는 열이 없다.

헤드라인으로 나간 집계 세 개는 공개 자료로 계산되지 않았다. 세계 18.8%, 글로벌 노스 28.8%, 사우스 16.2%다. 어떤 나라가 노스이고 어떤 나라가 사우스인지 적은 명단이 데이터셋에도 보고서 본문에도 없다.

명단 없이 그 크기만 역산하면 노스는 세계 근로연령 인구의 약 5분의 1이어야 한다. 세계은행 고소득국도, 경제협력개발기구 회원국도, 국제연합 선진지역도 그 크기에 닿지 않는다. 재현이 막힌 지점은 가중치 계산이 아니라 가중치를 매길 명단이다.

그런데도 이 보고서는 드문 사례다. 데이터셋을 MIT 라이선스로 열었고, 다음 판에서 세는 도구를 넓히면 거의 모든 나라의 수치가 오를 것이며 그 상승은 채택의 변화가 아니라 측정의 개선을 주로 반영할 것이라고 발행자가 먼저 적어 두었다. 현재 목록에 들어 있지 않은 도구 가운데 하나가 월 사용자 3억 8,200만 명의 중국 최대 AI 앱이다.

페블러스가 데이터 품질 진단에서 매주 만나는 질문도 같은 모양이다. 이 칼럼의 어느 행이 관측이고 어느 행이 채워 넣은 값인가. 계보를 남기는 일과 그 계보가 최종 소비 지점까지 따라오게 만드는 일은 다른 작업이고, 대개 뒤쪽이 빠진다.

외부 지표를 기획서에 끌어올 때 물어야 할 것은 순위가 아니라 분모다. 그 숫자는 무엇을 세었고, 무엇을 세지 않았는가.

## LinkedIn (EN)

In the country rankings of AI use that Microsoft published in September, 36 of the 147 economies have no observed number of their own.

The technical paper the report tells readers to consult carries a dagger on those rows, and the legend under the table spells out what it means: an estimate imputed from a regional average because telemetry coverage was insufficient. Thirty-six rows are marked that way. Nigeria's 10.4% is not a measurement of Nigeria but a single figure shared among twelve West African countries, and the working-age population of all the imputed economies adds up to roughly 600 million.

None of this is hidden. The paper states the method in its text and marks every affected row in the appendix. What is worth noticing is how far that marking travels. The public dataset and the leaderboard that citers actually see carry no column telling anyone which rows were imputed.

Three headline aggregates cannot be computed from the public material at all: 18.8% worldwide, 28.8% for the Global North, 16.2% for the South. No list of which economies fall on which side appears in the dataset or in the report. Back out the implied size and the North has to hold about a fifth of the world's working-age population, which is more than the World Bank high-income group, the OECD membership, or the UN's developed regions each account for.

And yet this publisher is a rare one. The dataset is open under an MIT license, and the report states in advance that widening the set of tools counted will raise the figure in nearly every economy, and that those increases will mainly reflect improved measurement of existing usage rather than a sudden change in adoption. One tool absent from the current list is China's largest AI app, with 382 million monthly users.

The question comes up every week in data quality work at Pebblous. Which rows in this column are observed and which were filled in. Recording provenance and making that provenance travel all the way to the point of consumption are two different jobs, and it is usually the second that goes missing.

When an external indicator enters a strategy deck, the thing to ask about is not the rank but the denominator. What did that number count, and what did it not count.

기획서에서 이런 문장을 여러 번 봤습니다. 뒤에 붙는 결론은 대개 예산이나 전략입니다.

그렇게 표시된 행이 서른여섯 개였습니다.

순위표는 그 자체로 아무것도 숨기지 않습니다. 다만 어느 칸이 관측이고 어느 칸이 이웃에게 빌려 온 값인지는, 부록을 열어 본 사람만 압니다.

## Facebook (EN)

"Our country ranks nth in the world for AI use, therefore."

I have read that sentence in a lot of strategy decks. What follows it is usually a budget or a plan.

This piece is a record of following one of those numbers all the way down. The country-level share of AI users in the Global AI Diffusion Report that Microsoft published in September.

So I opened the paper the report names as its own methodology document. In the full appendix table, each row carried a small dagger, and the legend beneath the table explained it in one line. An estimate imputed from a regional average, where coverage was insufficient.

Thirty-six rows were marked that way.

I did not get past the first one for a while.

Nigeria's figure was not a measurement of Nigeria. It was a single number shared with eleven other West African countries. Ethiopia and Tanzania carried a value split eight ways. Add up the working-age population of every economy wearing one of these "shared numbers" and it comes to around 600 million people.

Nothing was concealed. The paper states the method in its text and marks each affected row in the appendix. The dataset is open under an MIT license. The publisher even wrote, ahead of time, that when the next edition counts more tools the figures will rise, and that this rise will reflect better measurement rather than a changed world. Indicators that set out their own limits this plainly are rare.

What was short was the distance that marking travelled. Not the public ranking table, not the regional press release, carried a column saying which rows were imputed.

"The number we are benchmarking against: what did it count, and what did it leave out?"

Data quality work at Pebblous keeps arriving at the same place. When observed values and filled-in values sit in the same column, can the person in front of the dashboard tell which row is which? Provenance usually does get written down somewhere. Carrying it as far as the final screen is the next problem, and that is where it tends to stop.

The ranking table hides nothing on its own. It is just that which of its cells were observed, and which were borrowed from a neighbor, is known only to whoever opened the appendix.
