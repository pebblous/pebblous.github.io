챗봇이 학술 인용을 지어내던 버릇에 만료 판정이 내려졌다. 같은 해에 나온 다른 감사는 생의학 논문에서 가짜 참고문헌 4,046건을 가려냈고, 그것을 담은 논문의 98.4%가 아직 아무 조치도 받지 않았다.

스탠퍼드의 Owen Queen과 James Zou는 널리 인용된 LLM 연구 결과 쉰 개를 모델 스무 개에 다시 돌려 스물두 개를 만료로 분류했다. 인용 지어내기는 그중 두 번째로 오래 버텼다. 31.7개월로, 만료된 항목 중앙값의 두 배가 넘는다. 추정 만료 시점은 올해 4월 말이다. 네이처는 11월 19일 이 분석을 보도하면서 그것이 연구실 블로그 글이고 동료심사를 거치지 않았다는 사실을 함께 적었다.

그 서른한 달 동안 기록 쪽에는 가짜 참고문헌이 쌓였다. Lancet에 실린 감사가 PMC 공개접근 논문 250만 편을 훑어, 네 데이터베이스 어디에도 없는 참고문헌 4,046건을 확정했다. 문제의 참고문헌을 담은 bioRxiv 프리프린트를 출판본까지 따라간 다른 분석에서는 환각 인용의 85.3%가 심사를 통과해 그대로 남았다.

이 판정에는 단서가 둘 붙는다. 만료를 선언한 문서 자체가 블로그 글이다. 그리고 같은 해에 모델 스물여섯 개에게 빠진 참고문헌을 직접 채우게 한 프리프린트에서는 2026년에 공개된 모델도 35.3%가 날조였다. 과제를 바꾸면 답이 바뀐다. Lancet 감사도 정밀도는 쟀고 재현율은 재지 않았다고 스스로 적어 두었다.

눈금이 갈리는 자리가 여기다. 모델 쪽 오류는 비율로 보고되고, 기록 쪽 오류는 건수로 쌓인다. 쓰는 사람이 늘면 비율이 내려가는 동안에도 건수는 늘고, 건수는 누가 지우기 전까지 그 자리에 남는다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The habit that defined early chatbots, inventing academic citations, has been ruled expired. An audit published the same year confirmed 4,046 fabricated references across biomedical papers, and 98.4% of the 2,810 papers carrying them have had no correction or retraction.

Owen Queen and James Zou of Stanford re-ran fifty widely cited LLM findings against twenty models and classified twenty-two of them as expired. Citation fabrication was the second-longest survivor on that list at 31.7 months, more than double the median of the expired set, with an estimated expiry date in late April of this year. Nature reported the analysis on 19 November and noted in the same piece that it appeared as a blogpost and has not been peer reviewed.

The record kept filling through those thirty-one months. An audit in The Lancet screened 2.5 million open-access biomedical papers and isolated 4,046 references that appear in none of four databases. A separate team followed bioRxiv preprints carrying such references through to their published versions and found that 85.3% of the hallucinated citations passed review intact.

Two things sit alongside the verdict. The document declaring the habit expired is itself a blogpost. And a separate preprint from the same year, which asked twenty-six models to supply a missing reference outright, found that even models released in 2026 fabricated 35.3% of the time. Change the task and the answer changes. The Lancet team also wrote that their design estimates precision but not recall.

The two sides are read off different scales. Model error is reported as a rate. Record error accumulates as a count. With more people writing, the count can rise while the rate falls, and the count stays until somebody removes it.

## Facebook (EN)

What would it be like to read a paper you had never written, with your name on it?

Guillaume Cabanac, a computer scientist at the University of Toulouse, had that experience.

His name sat in the reference list of a dental paper as an author. He had written no such thing, and the DOI printed beside it led nowhere.

A paper that does not exist had borrowed the name of a person who does, and taken a line in somebody else's footnotes.

Joe Shockman, co-founder of Grounded AI, calls entries like these "Frankenstein citations". A real author, a real journal, and a paper that was never written, stitched together in one line.

This year three teams set out to count how many such entries are sitting in the scientific record. The three came back with three different numbers.

All three are right. One counted references confirmed absent from four databases, one estimated papers, and one estimated the excess above a pre-LLM baseline.

News from the other direction arrived in the same year. Fifty widely cited LLM findings were re-run against current models, and the habit of inventing citations was classified as expired after thirty-one months.

The line I went back to was the one where neither piece of news cancels the other.

The model side speaks in rates. A fabrication rate fell from one percentage to another.

The record side speaks in counts. 4,046 references. 2,810 papers.

With more people writing, a count can rise while the rate falls. And a count stays until somebody removes it. Of those 2,810 papers, 98.4% have had no correction and no retraction.
