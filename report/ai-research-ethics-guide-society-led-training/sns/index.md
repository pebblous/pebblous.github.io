## LinkedIn (KO)

AI 연구윤리는 규정이 미비하다는 말이 자주 들립니다. 석 달 사이에 나온 문서 세 편을 나란히 놓으면 모자란 것이 규정의 양은 아니라는 쪽으로 보입니다.

교육부·한국연구재단이 6월 30일 대학 연구자 가이드를 냈고, 미국 연구진실성관리국이 8월에 생성형 AI 가이던스를, 과기정통부·KISTEP이 9월에 국가연구개발 가이드와 해설서를 냈습니다.

한국 문서가 훨씬 두껍다는 점은 눈에 바로 들어옵니다. 다만 그 차이는 성실성이 아니라 부정행위 목록의 길이에서 옵니다. 미국 연방 기준의 연구부정행위는 위조와 변조와 표절뿐이고, 자기표절과 저자성 분쟁은 조문이 이름을 붙여 명시적으로 배제합니다. 그래서 미국 문서에는 "AI는 저자가 될 수 없다"를 쓸 자리가 애초에 없습니다.

정작 두 제도가 겹치는 대목은 다른 곳입니다. 옳고 그름의 마지막 근거를 양쪽 다 그 분야에서 통하는 관행에 미뤄 두고, 검증 책임은 양쪽 다 연구자의 소속 기관에 지웁니다. 기준을 만드는 자리와 판정을 내리는 자리가 갈라져 있는데, 앞쪽에는 아무도 위임받지 않았습니다.

그 자리를 먼저 채운 쪽은 학회입니다. 대학 연구자 가이드가 예시로 옮겨 실은 국내 학회 규정만 다섯 건이고, 가장 이른 한국유통학회 규정은 국가연구개발 가이드보다 약 열네 달 앞섭니다. 한국영화학회는 영상 분석과 자막 생성을 조문에 적었고, 한국유통학회는 보조 교정까지는 따로 공개하지 않아도 된다며 면제선을 스스로 그었습니다. 모든 분야에 동시에 맞는 면제선이 없으니 국가 문서는 이 선을 긋지 못합니다.

여기에 단서를 하나 답니다. 분야가 같은 사람끼리 가르치면 더 효과적이라는 명제는 연구 서른 편을 모은 메타분석에서 기각된 가설입니다. 그 연구가 지지한 것은 실제 사례로 토론하고 실습하게 하는 설계였고, 학회의 강점은 분야가 같다는 사실이 아니라 그 재료를 갖고 있다는 데서 옵니다.

그런데 두 국가 문서는 학회를 교육 주체로 한 번도 부르지 않습니다. 「학술진흥법」은 학술단체를 정의해 두었고, 교육부 훈령은 학술단체를 대학·연구기관과 묶어 정기적인 연구윤리 교육 의무를 지웠으며, 연구자가 이수할 교육기관을 장관이 지정하도록 했습니다. 비어 있는 것은 조문이 아니라 지정과 예산으로 보입니다.

페블러스가 데이터 품질을 진단할 때도 같은 갈림을 만납니다. 스키마 위반과 결측은 규칙으로 잡히지만, 이 라벨이 이 공정에서 뜻이 통하는가는 그 도메인의 관행을 아는 사람만 압니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Korea is often told that its rules for AI in research are underdeveloped. Three documents published within three months suggest the shortage lies somewhere else.

The Ministry of Education and the National Research Foundation issued a generative AI research ethics guide for university researchers on June 30. The US Office of Research Integrity issued its own guidance in August. The Ministry of Science and ICT and KISTEP followed in September with a national R&D guide and a longer commentary volume.

The Korean documents are far thicker. The gap is not one of diligence but of how many acts each country counts as misconduct. Under the US federal standard, research misconduct is fabrication, falsification and plagiarism, and the regulation names self-plagiarism and authorship disputes in order to exclude them. That is why no American document has anywhere to say that an AI cannot be an author.

Where the two systems converge is easier to miss. Both defer the final test of right and wrong to the accepted practice of the relevant research community, and both place the duty to investigate on the researcher's own institution. The seat that sets the standard and the seat that applies it have been separated, and nobody has been given the first one.

Scholarly societies took it anyway. The university guide reprints AI clauses from five Korean societies, and the earliest of them, the Korea Distribution Association, predates the national R&D guide by roughly fourteen months. The Korean Society of Cinema Studies names video analysis and subtitle generation in its clause. The Distribution Association drew a line in the other direction, holding that AI-assisted copy editing need not be disclosed separately. No national document draws either line, because no single exemption fits every field at once.

The case should not be overstated. A meta-analysis of thirty studies rejected the hypothesis that single-discipline cohorts train better than mixed ones. What it supported was a design built on discussion and hands-on application, and the material for that design sits inside the field.

Neither Korean document names societies as providers of training. Korean statute already does. The Academic Promotion Act defines scholarly bodies, the Ministry of Education directive folds them in with universities and research institutes and obliges that group to run regular research ethics training, and the same directive lets the minister designate where researchers complete it. What is missing is a designation and a budget line, not an eighth recommendation.

Pebblous meets the same split in data quality work. Schema violations and missing values are caught by rules. Whether a label still means anything on a given production line is a judgement only someone who knows that domain's practice can make.

## Facebook (EN)

"The rules are underdeveloped."

It is the sentence I hear most often whenever AI and research ethics come up in the same room.

I had given review comments on both of this year's Korean documents, so the sentence stayed with me longer than it should have. Is it really the quantity of rules that is short?

I read the three documents of the past three months side by side. The university researchers' guide from June 30, the US Office of Research Integrity guidance from August, the national R&D guide and its commentary volume from September.

The American document is much thinner. The thinness turned out not to be a lack of preparation.

What the US federal regulation counts as research misconduct is fabrication, falsification and plagiarism. Self-plagiarism and authorship disputes are named in the text so that they can be excluded. Fewer acts to cover, fewer words to write.

What the two countries share sits somewhere else entirely.

Both leave the final test of right and wrong to the accepted practice of the field. And neither says who is supposed to write that practice down.

I have started calling the blank 'the seat where practice gets written'.

"Where exactly is the practice of this field written down?"

If there is nothing to open when an investigation committee sits, the word practice only pretends to point at something that already exists.

It was not a government that filled the seat first.

The Korean Society of Cinema Studies wrote video analysis and subtitle generation into its clause. The Korea Distribution Association went the other way, ruling that AI-assisted copy editing need not be disclosed separately, and then supplying the wording of the declaration itself. The earliest of these clauses predates the national guide by more than a year.

No exemption line fits every field at once. That is why a national document cannot draw one.

And yet neither national document ever names societies as the party that should teach.

Korean statute already seats them. The Academic Promotion Act defines scholarly bodies. The Ministry of Education directive folds them in with universities and research institutes, obliges that group to run regular research ethics training, and lets the minister designate where researchers complete it. What is empty is not the statute but the designation and the budget.

We meet the same split at Pebblous when we diagnose data quality. Schema violations and missing values fall to rules. Whether a label still means anything on a given production line is known only to someone who knows that domain's practice. Going from twenty checklist items to forty does not cross that layer.

Should an expert not be able to tell what may and may not be done without a guide in hand? Asking where that judgement gets cultivated may get us there faster than waiting for an eighth recommendation.
