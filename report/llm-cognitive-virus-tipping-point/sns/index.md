## LinkedIn (KO)

AI 의존이 번지기 시작하는 문턱과, 그 인구가 원래 자리로 돌아오는 문턱이 같은 값이 아니었다.

물리학과 복잡계를 연구하는 아홉 명이 LLM 확산을 감염병 전파 모델로 옮겨 세운 결과다. 사람을 세 칸으로 나눈다. 안 쓰거나 약하게 쓰는 사람, 쓰면서도 읽기와 검증을 자기 손에 남긴 사람, LLM이 인지 작업을 대신하게 된 사람이다. 칸 사이를 오가는 비율에 항 하나를 더하자 문턱이 둘로 갈렸다.

더한 항은 하나뿐이다. 자기 힘으로 생각하는 사람이 주변에 많을수록 그렇게 일하기가 쉬워진다는 되먹임을, 그 비율의 제곱에 비례하도록 적었다. 생태학의 앨리 효과에서 빌려 온 모양이다. 제곱이라 자율적인 사람이 줄기 시작하면 되돌리는 힘은 줄어드는 속도보다 빠르게 무너진다.

두 문턱 사이 구간에서는 같은 압력을 받는 두 사회가 서로 다른 자리에 머물 수 있다. 어느 쪽에 있느냐를 지금의 조건이 아니라 여기까지 온 길이 정한다. 논문의 문장으로 예방이 복구보다 상당히 쉬울 수 있다.

논문이 기대는 실증은 남의 실험이다. 고교생 약 천 명을 대상으로 한 현장 실험에서, 일반 챗봇처럼 생긴 도구를 쓴 학생은 도구가 있는 동안 성적이 48% 올랐다. 그런데 도구를 거두자 애초에 써 본 적 없는 학생보다 낮았다. 학습을 보호하도록 프롬프트를 설계한 튜터 쪽에서는 그 하락이 거의 나타나지 않았다.

읽는 폭은 좁게 잡는 편이 맞다. 이 논문은 사용자 데이터를 한 건도 수집하지 않았고, 문턱의 숫자도 그림을 그리려고 고른 예시 파라미터에서 나온 단위 없는 값이다. 저널에 실린 판본도 아직 없다.

그래도 조사에서 가장 뚜렷하게 남은 것은 계측 쪽이었다. 파라미터 다섯 가운데 셋은 어느 통계기관도 발표하지 않고, 그 셋이 전부 복구 쪽 손잡이다. 조직이 남기는 AI 로그는 얼마나 썼는지는 재도 그 사람이 지금 어느 칸에 있는지는 재지 않는다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The threshold at which AI dependence takes hold in a population is not the threshold at which that population comes back.

Nine researchers in physics and complex systems rebuilt the spread of LLMs as an epidemic model. People fall into three compartments: those who barely use these systems, those who use them while still doing the reading and the verifying themselves, and those for whom the model has taken over the cognitive work. Adding a single term to the rates that move people between compartments split one threshold into two.

Only that one term is new. It states a feedback: working without the model gets easier when people who work that way are common, written as proportional to the square of their share. The shape is borrowed from the Allee effect in ecology. Because it is squared, the force pulling a population back collapses faster than the numbers behind it fall.

Between the two thresholds, two societies under identical pressure can sit in different states. Which one they sit in is set by the path they took, not by present conditions. In the paper's words, prevention can be considerably easier than reversal.

The empirical weight comes from someone else's experiment. In a field trial with roughly a thousand high school students, those given a tool that mimics a standard chatbot scored 48% higher while they had it. Once it was taken away they scored lower than classmates who had never had access. Students given a tutor whose prompts were designed to safeguard learning barely showed that drop.

The reading should stay narrow. This paper collected no user data of its own, its threshold values are dimensionless numbers from illustrative parameters chosen to draw a figure, and no journal version exists yet.

The research turned up its sharpest observation on the measurement side. Three of the five parameters are published by no statistical agency, and all three sit on the reversal side. Corporate AI logs record how much people use the tools, never which compartment a person is in.

## Facebook (EN)

Students who scored 48% higher with the tool scored lower than classmates who had never had it, once it was taken away.

That is from a field trial with roughly a thousand high school students.

It happened to the group given a tool that mimics an ordinary chatbot. The group given a tutor whose prompts were designed to safeguard learning barely showed the drop at all.

Same tool, different outcomes, and I kept coming back to the thought that there is a compartment sitting between "used it" and "didn't."

The paper I read this week builds that compartment at the scale of a population. Nine researchers in physics and complex systems rebuilt the spread of LLMs as an epidemic model and sorted people into three groups: those who barely use these systems, those who use them while still doing the reading and the verifying themselves, and those for whom the model has taken the work over.

The line between the last two has nothing to do with volume, and that is the part that held me.

One person uses the model ten times a day and checks the grounds for every answer. Another uses it once and lets that once settle the judgment. The model does not separate them by count.

And from that a result follows. The value that lets dependence take hold and the value that lets a population come back are different numbers.

Between the two, societies under identical pressure can sit in different states, and which one is set by the path taken rather than by present conditions.

The phrase that stayed with me through the research was "the door back."

Plenty of organisations already block access during exams, mandate human review of output, bring back oral examinations. Everyone is locking a door, or fitting an instrument that checks after the fact. Almost nobody is building the door you walk back in through.

"How open is the way back, and what are we measuring it with?"

Three of the model's five parameters are published by no statistical agency, and all three sit on the coming-back side. The AI logs organisations keep record how much people use the tools, never which compartment a person is in.

What we do at Pebblous is open up data and count what is missing from it. That is why the place this paper gets stuck did not read as someone else's problem. There is a difference between saying there is no data and saying the data that exists does not measure what you want to know.

The paper collected no user data of its own, and its threshold values are illustrative numbers chosen to draw a figure. They do not transfer onto a real-world scale.

Still, what to record next to the adoption rate seems like a question worth sitting with, for anyone who owns the logs.
