## LinkedIn (KO)

로봇을 움직이는 AI 열다섯 종을 줄 세운 목록이 10월 31일 링크드인에 올라와 널리 퍼졌다. 그 순위 맨 위 일곱은 공개 리더보드에 한 줄도 없다.

잴 잣대가 없어서 생긴 공백이 아니다. 비영리 재단이 운영하는 공개 보드 RoboDojo는 목록이 올라오기 하루 전까지 갱신되고 있었고, 시뮬레이션 66행과 실제 로봇 13행이 같은 절차 위에 올라 있었다. 열다섯 가운데 실기 보드에 이름이 걸린 것은 엔비디아 GR00T N1.7 하나, 성공률 1.70%다. 그 행조차 엔비디아가 제출한 값이 아니라 보드 운영 팀이 직접 돌린 기준선이다. 목록 쪽에는 숫자가 한 개도 없다.

그런데 보드를 끝까지 열면 그 결론이 반밖에 맞지 않는다. 각 행에는 누가 제출했는지 적는 칸이 있는데, 그 칸이 빈 행은 32위에서 시작한다. 빈 행은 개설 때 운영 팀이 직접 돌린 서른 종이고, 위쪽 서른한 줄에는 하나도 없다. 제출하면 점수가 잘 나온다고 읽으면 틀린다. 고르는 효과와 세대 차와 미세조정 허용 범위가 뒤엉켜 있고, 공개 자료로는 가를 수 없다.

보드에 없다는 사실이 못한다는 증거도 아니다. 보드의 과제 목록 안에는 가정집 거실도, 한 시간짜리 연속 작업도 없다. Figure Helix 2.5는 보드 밖에서 가정집 서른 채를 빌려 420회 중 237회 성공이라고 적었다. 보드의 예순여섯 행 가운데 시도 횟수가 적힌 행은 하나뿐이다.

한 가지는 따로 적어 둘 만하다. 10월 29일 1위를 발표한 회사의 발표문은 모델을 "GO-3 Preview"라 적는데, 자기가 제출한 보드 행의 이름은 "GO-3 Nano Preview"다. 버전 한 낱말을 지운 쪽이 바깥의 제3자가 아니라 만든 회사 본인이다. 페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 수치로 매겨 진단한다. 거기서 가장 자주 걸리는 결함도 같은 모양이다. 같은 이름이 서로 다른 개체를 가리키는 줄.

순위표는 등수가 틀려서 못 쓰는 것이 아니다. 그 줄을 누가 올렸는지 적혀 있지 않아서 못 쓴다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A list ranking fifteen robot action models went up on LinkedIn on October 31 and travelled fast. The top seven have no row on any public leaderboard.

This is not for want of a ruler. RoboDojo, a public board maintained by a non-profit, had been refreshed the day before the list appeared, with 66 rows in simulation and 13 on real robots scored through one pipeline. Of the fifteen models, exactly one reaches the real-robot board: NVIDIA's GR00T N1.7, at a 1.70% success rate. Even that row is a baseline the RoboDojo team ran itself rather than a number NVIDIA submitted. The list carries no scores at all, only an order.

Read the board to the end, though, and the tidy conclusion holds up only halfway. Every row has a column naming who submitted it, and the rows where that column is blank begin at rank 32. Those blanks are the thirty policies the organisers ran themselves at launch, and not one sits in the top thirty-one. The causation cannot be settled from public data: entrants submit when they expect a good result, the blank rows are an older generation, and nobody has published whether fine-tuning limits match on both sides.

Absence is also not evidence of weakness. The simulation task list holds no living rooms and no hour-long jobs. Figure's Helix 2.5 never entered the board yet reported 237 successes in 420 attempts across thirty rented homes. Of RoboDojo's sixty-six rows, exactly one records how many attempts were run.

One detail is worth keeping. The company that announced a first-place finish on October 29 calls the model "GO-3 Preview" in its press release and "GO-3 Nano Preview" on the board row it submitted itself. The word that went missing was dropped by the maker, not by an outsider. Pebblous puts a reading on the quality of AI training data with DataClinic, and the defect that surfaces most often has exactly that shape: one name standing for two different things.

A ranking is not unusable because the order is wrong. It is unusable because nothing on the row says who put it there.

## Facebook (EN)

Within one month, two companies each announced that they were number one on the same robot leaderboard.

Assuming one of them had to be overstating it, I walked the board's 28 changelog entries back day by day.

Both were right.

On October 9 the top row belonged to ROBOTERA's VPP2. On October 29 it belonged to AGIBOT's GO-3. The second of those runs ended after three days: on October 30 another model arrived and took the row, and the LinkedIn list ranking fifteen robot action models appeared the day after that.

Neither company said anything false. Neither printed a date.

'A first place three days long.'

What the board left out was not performance. It was when.

Something in the earlier announcement caught too. ROBOTERA hung an oddly fussy qualifier on its press release headline: without additional data or agent RSI. For a promotional line it was strangely careful. Rolling the board back to that date is what explains it. The row directly below was not a single policy but an assembly of parts, and the board's table had folded that difference into one column. The vendor's release recorded a distinction the scoreboard did not.

So the board had nowhere to write the date and nowhere to write the conditions. The parties who did write them down were the ones being scored.

Pebblous puts a reading on the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. In that work the argument turns on the timestamp as often as on the score. The same dataset reads differently in October than it did in September, and when the report omits when the reading was taken, the two values look like they are contradicting each other. Usually they are both correct.

"That number one we keep quoting — how many days long is it?"
