## LinkedIn (EN)

The $825 billion figure that circled the world on October 22 is not a number Meta wrote.

Meta's third-quarter Form 10-Q, filed that day, sets out three figures in separate paragraphs of Note 10: $355.73 billion of leases that have not yet commenced, $454.52 billion of non-cancelable contractual commitments, and $14.72 billion of contingent cloud purchase obligations.

Add them and you get the headline. No sentence in the filing performs that addition. The press did. Bloomberg, unusually, disclosed the arithmetic inside its own story: "Bloomberg reported the total by adding together three items in the filing." Several outlets that ran the same wire copy dropped exactly that line.

All three figures match the filing and the sum is correct. What the filing never says is whether the three overlap. Data centers, energy infrastructure and network equipment appear in both the lease description and the commitments description, and a full-text search of the notes across Q1, Q2 and Q3 turns up no sentence declaring the two buckets mutually exclusive. The gap runs the other way as well. The $68.4 billion maximum exposure Meta discloses for two unconsolidated joint ventures it holds 20% of sits outside the total entirely.

So the number may be too high, and it may be too low. It is not a wrong number. It is a number that does not reproduce.

## Facebook (EN)

We tend to believe a large number the moment we read it in a story.

$825 billion. The money Meta has committed to spend.

I opened the 10-Q to see where that number was written.

It was not there.

What was there were three figures sitting apart in the notes. Leases that have not yet commenced. Non-cancelable contractual commitments. Contingent obligations to purchase cloud capacity.

Add the three and you get the headline. The side that performed the addition was not the company. It was a reporter.

All three figures matched the filing. The sum was correct. This piece does not dispute the total.

Checking the figures took no time at all. What did not finish was the question that came after.

"How far does this total reach, and what was taken out of it?"

The report does not answer.

Data centers, energy infrastructure and network equipment appear in both the lease description and the commitments description, and across Q1, Q2 and Q3 there is no sentence stating that the two buckets do not overlap.

The other direction was empty too. The maximum exposure for two unconsolidated joint ventures in which Meta holds 20% is a figure Meta disclosed itself, and it sits outside the total.

So the number may be too high. It may also be too low.

I have started calling numbers like this "numbers that do not reproduce."

Not wrong. Just numbers that will not come out the same when someone else does the same work from the same source.

The same day, the price of Meta's five-year credit default swap came out at 119.3bp from one data provider and 114.79bp from another.

Same day. Same maturity. Same company.

Three months earlier there was a total of the same kind, built from pieces defined differently. Subtract one total from the other to state an increase and you count one piece twice.
