The Deployment Layer That Edits Answers Before the Token Is Picked

A language model does not choose words. It assigns a score to every word in its vocabulary, and drawing one word out of that table is a separate step that happens afterward. Between the scoring and the drawing sits a layer that almost nobody audits, and Google has been running production traffic through it for two years.

The instrument is SynthID-Text, Google's text watermark. It leaves training alone and leaves the weights alone. It changes only the sampling procedure, pairing candidate words against each other in a tournament so that a statistical signature settles into the finished sentence. The Nature paper describing it states plainly that it is deployed in Gemini.

The same paper priced the intervention. A Gemma 7B model served on four TPUs produced a token in 15.527 milliseconds; switching on 30-layer tournament sampling took that to 15.615, an increase of 0.57 percent. Quality was measured by splitting roughly 20 million live responses between watermarked and unwatermarked models. The gap in thumbs-up rate was 0.01 percent and in thumbs-down 0.02 percent, neither statistically significant. And because the computational complexity of the watermark does not grow with the model, the relative cost falls by orders of magnitude as models get larger.

What a paper posted last week does with that fact

On 25 August 2026, a consultant in Brazil posted a conceptual paper to arXiv formalizing three ideas around this layer. It contains no experiments and names no company. Its argument is that the model is not the deployed system, and that between the two sits something it calls an inference policy: an externally supplied score table added on top of the model's own scores before a token is drawn.

Watermarking appears in that paper for architectural reasons rather than accusatory ones, and the distinction is worth preserving. What watermarking proves is one narrow thing: deliberately perturbing token probabilities for an external objective, while holding output quality and coherence, is feasible at commercial scale. Its objective happens to be provenance, that objective is public, and the method and cost were published in a journal.

Why this layer, out of nine

The paper's most quotable contribution is a table comparing where behavioral steering can live in a deployment stack. Read the last two columns and the outlier is obvious:

  • Alignment training (RLHF, DPO) changes the weights, costs no context tokens, and is extremely hard to detect from outside.
  • A hidden system prompt leaves the weights alone but consumes context tokens and can be extracted, which makes it moderately detectable.
  • Retrieval injection also consumes context and leaves a trace in the citation list.
  • A logit policy changes no weights, consumes zero context tokens, carries no prompt-leak risk, and can only be caught by auditing distributions.

Each of the first three deposits something somewhere. Alignment lands in the weights, a system prompt lands in the context window, retrieval lands in the citation list. The logit policy lands nowhere.

That asymmetry explains an incident better than any audit narrative does. When Grok's system prompt was modified without authorization in May 2025 and unrelated queries started collecting political commentary, it was public within two days. Not because oversight worked. Because prompts consume context, leak, and repeat the same sentence until users notice. Every deployment-layer incident we know about happened in a layer coarse enough to be caught.

Keeping the measured apart from the hypothetical

The paper's commercial primitive, probability placement, is labeled hypothetical three times over. No company has been shown doing it, and the discipline matters, because the same material could be assembled into a conspiracy with one careless sentence.

What has been measured is all adjacent. Watermarking. Prompting. Autocomplete. Style-attribute control. Three large experiments published in Science in December 2025 found that prompt-level adjustments alone, with weights untouched, raised the persuasiveness of conversational AI by up to 27 percent across 76,977 participants and 707 political issues, and that the methods raising persuasion systematically lowered factual accuracy across 466,769 fact-checked claims. What sits at the model matters less than what wraps it. Nobody has measured semantic framing steered at the logit layer, and this piece does not pretend otherwise.

Whether anyone could check

Auditing this requires the difference between the served distribution and the model's own, which requires log probabilities. OpenAI returns them for the top 20 candidates, filling everything below with −9999, against a vocabulary in six figures. Google returns top-N. Anthropic returns none. The window is also API-only, absent from the consumer chat interfaces where the Gemini app alone passed a billion monthly users this August.

Meanwhile OpenAI's logit_bias parameter is documented as being added to the model's logits immediately prior to sampling, which means the paper's formalism already exists as a shipped product feature used by developers. And system_fingerprint, the one standard field that let outsiders notice backend configuration changes at all, is marked deprecated in the chat completions schema.

Where this lands for anyone building on someone else's model

Pebblous works on the state data is in before it reaches a model, so the honest conclusion here is a limit on our own thesis. Establish the lineage from training data to weights completely, and the stretch from model to user still has no lineage instrument in it at all. Data provenance is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one.

Two items are actionable today, and neither needs a regulator. Inventory the deployment-layer interventions your own service applies over a commercial API, with purpose and start date, because a 2021 result established that steering requires access to the output logits and nothing else, which makes every reseller and gateway operator one of these operators already. Then put log-probability access into procurement terms, since that permission does not appear later if it is not written at contract time.
