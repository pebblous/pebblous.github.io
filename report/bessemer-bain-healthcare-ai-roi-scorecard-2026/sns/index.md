베세머 벤처 파트너스와 베인이 미국 병원·보험사·제약사 임원 226명에게 업무 65개의 AI 도입 결과를 물었다. 사람 승인 없이 끝까지 돌아가는 일은 진료실 밖에 몰려 있었다.

진료비 청구 업무는 67%가 반자율 또는 완전자율 에이전트로 돌아가고, 병원 임상 업무는 4%다.

이 대비를 "청구 업무만 자동화됐다"로 읽으면 틀린다. 보험사 가입자 응대가 62%, 병원 접수·안내가 49%로 같은 수준이다. 보고서가 그은 선은 청구와 나머지 사이가 아니라 행정과 임상 사이에 있다.

수익률로는 그 간격이 설명되지 않는다. 아홉 영역의 회수 배수는 모두 2.3배에서 4.0배 사이에 들어가 가장 높은 값과 가장 낮은 값의 비가 두 배도 안 되는데, 같은 두 업무의 자율성 비는 열여섯 배가 넘는다. 보고서가 임상 정체의 이유로 든 것은 넷이다. 믿지 못하고, 사고가 나면 담당 임상의가 책임을 지고, 규제 경로가 불확실하고, 보험이 값을 쳐 주지 않는다. 모델 성능은 그 넷에 없고, 데이터도 없다.

다만 이 배수는 임원이 설문에 스스로 적어 낸 값이다. 보고서 자신이 세로축을 자기보고 회수 배수로 정의했고, 표본 설계와 응답률은 두 해 모두 공개된 적이 없다. 발표 주체가 보고서에 거명된 회사들의 투자자라는 사실도 본문에 적혀 있다.

그런데 임상의에게 무엇이 있으면 믿겠느냐고 물었을 때 가장 많이 돌아온 넷은 더 정확한 모델이 아니었다. 검증 근거, 추천의 이유, 환자별 성능, 기존 기록과의 연결. 전부 그 일이 끝난 뒤에 남아 있어야 하는 기록이다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Bessemer Venture Partners and Bain asked 226 US provider, payer and pharma executives what came back after they put AI into 65 activities. The work that now runs without a human signing off sits outside the exam room.

In provider revenue cycle work, 67% runs on semi- or fully autonomous agents. In provider clinical work, the figure is 4%.

Reading that contrast as "only billing got automated" gets it wrong. Payer member engagement runs at 62% and provider front office work at 49%. The line the report draws is not between billing and everything else but between administrative and clinical work.

Returns do not explain the gap. The realized multiples for all nine domains fall between 2.3x and 4.0x, so the highest is less than twice the lowest, while the autonomy ratio between those same two activities is more than sixteen to one. What the report blames for the clinical standstill is four things: clinicians do not trust the output, the attending physician carries the liability, the regulatory path is uncertain, and insurers will not pay for it. Model performance is not on that list. Neither is data.

Those multiples are also numbers executives wrote down about themselves. The report defines its own vertical axis as self-reported return, and neither edition has ever disclosed sample design or response rates. That the publisher invests in companies the report names is stated in the body as well.

And when clinicians were asked what would make them trust clinical AI, the four answers that came back most often were not about a better model. Validation evidence, the reasoning behind a recommendation, patient-specific performance, a join to the existing record. All four are records that have to survive the work.

## Facebook (EN)

A denied claim comes back with a reason code attached.

Right or wrong, a verdict arrives from outside for every single item, and it is written into the record as a code.

That is the passage I went back and reread in the survey Bessemer and Bain ran with 226 US healthcare executives.

In revenue cycle work, seven in ten activities run to the end without anyone signing off. In the judgments made with one patient in front of you, it is one in twenty-five.

My first thought was that billing codes are standardized, so of course it went first. Checking it, that is half true at best. Billing has plenty of disputes. A study of external appeals in New York State found close to half of denials overturned once they were disputed again.

So it did not go to the machines first because it has right answers.

"Does this work tell you afterwards who was right?"

I have started keeping that question as the difference between work where a verdict comes back and work where none does. Billing has that outside party. A clinical judgment does not, and where something like it exists, it arrives years later.

When the report asked clinicians what would make them trust clinical AI, the answers that came back most often were not about a better model either. Validation evidence, the reasoning behind a recommendation, patient-specific performance, a join to the existing record. Every one of them has to survive after the work is done.

What Pebblous looks at in DataClinic is mostly the data before it enters a model. Missingness, duplicates, distribution and the like. What this survey lights up is the other side, and to be honest, that inspection is not in our own tooling yet either.

Whether data is ready to go into a model, and whether what the model did is ready to be adjudicated afterwards, seem to me to be two different inspections.
