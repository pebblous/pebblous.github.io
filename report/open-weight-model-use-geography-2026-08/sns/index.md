2026년 상반기, 모델을 한 계열만 쓴 논문의 44.0%가 오픈웨이트를 골랐다. 그중 절반은 Qwen이었다.

독일 슈투트가르트대학의 Zackary Okun Dunivin이 8월 arXiv에 올린 프리프린트의 계측 결과다. 논문 2,100만 편을 훑어 LLM을 실제로 계측기로 쓴 15만 7천여 편을 골라냈고, 원고를 다듬는 데 챗봇을 쓴 사례는 따로 걷어냈다. 남은 논문에서 중국 기관 소속 연구자가 오픈웨이트를 고를 승산은 2.23배였다.

개방성을 더 중시했기 때문이라는 설명은 데이터가 직접 반박한다. 같은 논문들이 비중국 오픈웨이트를 고를 조정 확률은 15.0%로, 중국 연결이 없는 논문의 18.5%보다 오히려 낮았다. 열려 있다는 사실이 이유였다면 나올 수 없는 결과다. 쏠린 것은 개방성이 아니라 특정 모델 생태계였다.

한계도 분명하다. 동료심사를 거치지 않은 v1이고, 승산비는 연관이지 인과가 아니다. 세부분야를 통제하는 것만으로 2.87이 2.13으로 내려앉는데, 저자는 그 하락을 감추지 않고 세 모델을 모두 실었다.

가중치를 내려받을 수 있다는 사실과 3년 뒤 같은 결과를 얻을 수 있다는 사실은 다르다. 체크포인트 리비전과 양자화, 디코딩 파라미터와 시드가 남지 않으면 같은 가중치로도 결과는 갈린다. 연구와 검증 파이프라인에 모델을 넣는 조직이 남겨야 하는 것은 "오픈웨이트를 씁니다"라는 한 줄이 아니라 모델을 특정하는 좌표다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

In the first half of 2026, 44.0% of papers that used a single model family reached for open weights. Half of those papers used Qwen.

The count comes from a preprint posted to arXiv this month by Zackary Okun Dunivin of the University of Stuttgart. It screened 21 million papers, kept the 157,000 or so that used a language model as an actual instrument, and stripped out the cases where a chatbot merely polished the manuscript. In what remained, researchers at Chinese institutions had 2.23 times the odds of choosing an open-weight model.

The obvious reading, that these researchers value openness more, is refuted by the study's own data. Their adjusted probability of picking a non-Chinese open-weight model was 15.0%, lower than the 18.5% for papers with no Chinese affiliation. Had openness itself been the reason, that number would have moved the other way. What concentrated was not openness but one model ecosystem.

The limits are stated plainly. This is a v1 preprint that has not been peer reviewed, and the odds ratio is an association rather than a cause. Controlling for subfield alone pulls it from 2.87 to 2.13, and the author reports all three models instead of the flattering one.

Downloadable weights and reproducible results are not the same claim. Without the checkpoint revision, the quantization, the decoding parameters and the seed, the same weights still give different answers. What a team running models inside a research or validation pipeline owes its future readers is not the sentence "we used an open-weight model" but the coordinates that identify the model.

## Facebook (EN)

"We used an open-weight model."

When that sentence turns up in a methods section, I relax a little. The weights can be downloaded, so someone, someday, could check the result again.

A study posted to arXiv this month counted what that sentence actually meant. It screened 21 million papers, kept the 157,000 or so that used a language model as a genuine instrument, and set aside the ones where a chatbot had only tidied up the prose.

By the first half of 2026, open-weight use had climbed to 44%.

And half of the papers that chose open weights chose a single family: Qwen.

What stayed with me was not that researchers at Chinese institutions had more than twice the odds of choosing open weights. It was the smaller number sitting quietly beside it. Those same researchers were slightly less likely to pick open-weight models made outside China. If openness itself had been the reason, that could not happen.

What diverged first was not a belief in openness but "the list of what there was to choose from."

Pebblous works on data in the state it is in before it reaches a model, which is why the study's question did not feel like someone else's. We keep careful lineage for datasets, and then the lineage of the model that read those datasets ends at a name.

"What did we read this data with?"

Open weights seem to offer the possibility of answering that question rather than the answer itself. Without the checkpoint revision, the quantization, the decoding parameters and the seed, the same weights still part ways.

Korea does not appear in this study. Its axis is the United States and China, so Korean researchers are simply folded into the not-China side. No one has yet measured, by the same yardstick, what Korean researchers actually use as their instruments.

For now, that empty space may be the most accurate report we have.
