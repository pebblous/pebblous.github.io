세미애널리시스가 중국 AI 기업 아홉 곳의 모델 출시 857건을 한 줄씩 세었다. 안전 시험 결과가 붙은 것은 서른한 건이었다.

출시되던 날 결과가 함께 나온 것은 아홉 건이다. 널리 옮겨진 3.6%는 앞의 수에서 나왔다. 그런데 이 조사가 센 것은 안전이 아니라 기록이다. 보고서가 못 박은 계수 규칙은 결과가 그 모델 이름에 묶여 있어야 한다는 것이고, "안전하게 학습했다"는 진술은 세지 않는다.

그래서 3.6%의 반대말이 96.4%가 아니다. 아무 공개도 없는 출시는 94.9%다.

두 수 사이에 열세 건이 있다. 평가했다는 말만 있거나 언론·투자자 기록으로만 알려진 출시다. 규칙을 느슨하게 하면 비율은 올라가는데, 인용되는 쪽은 규칙이 아니라 수다.

회사를 지목하는 데에도 이 수는 쓸 수 없다. 딥시크는 출시 93건 중 6건으로 전체 평균의 거의 두 배다. 알리바바의 238건은 모든 Qwen 크기와 스냅숏을 하나씩 센 값이라고 보고서가 직접 밝힌 분모다. 그래서 보고서는 기업별 비율을 순위가 아니라 참고치라고 적었다.

공개 비율은 이 보고서의 결론이 아니라 증거다. 결론은 제도 쪽에 있다. 보고서가 구속력 있는 규칙으로 표에 올린 아홉 건 가운데, 모델의 능력이나 연산량으로 켜지는 의무는 하나도 없다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

SemiAnalysis went line by line through 857 model releases from nine Chinese AI companies. Thirty-one of them carry a safety test result.

Nine carried one on the day the model shipped. The 3.6% that travelled comes from the first number. What the study counted, though, is documentation rather than safety. Its rule is explicit: a result has to be tied to the named model, and a statement that a model was "safety-trained" does not count.

That is why the complement of 3.6% is not 96.4%. Releases with no disclosure at all come to 94.9%.

Thirteen releases sit between those two figures: ten that claim an evaluation without putting a number on it, three known only from press or investor records. Loosen the rule and the rate goes up. What gets quoted is the number, not the rule.

Nor can the figure name a company. DeepSeek has results on 6 of its 93 releases, close to twice the overall rate. Alibaba's 238 releases are, as the report itself states, every Qwen size and snapshot counted one by one. That is why the report calls per-company rates indicative rather than a ranking.

The disclosure rate is this report's evidence, not its conclusion. The conclusion sits on the regulatory side. Of the nine binding rules the report tabulates, not one is triggered by a model's capability or by the compute used to train it.

## Facebook (EN)

arXiv does not delete the old version of a paper.

I opened the DeepSeek-R1 technical report and clicked back and forth between v1 and v2. The first version, from January 2025, has no safety appendix. The second, posted in January 2026, contains one titled "D.3 DeepSeek-R1 Safety Report."

Counting from the day the model was released, that is 349 days.

In the SemiAnalysis report that went through 857 model releases from nine Chinese AI companies one line at a time, those 349 days are the longest delay on record.

My first assumption was that the company had held the results for a year and then let them out. Opening it, that was not what happened. There was no separate announcement. The paper was revised, and the safety appendix went in then for the first time. You cannot call that concealment. What you can say is that on the day the model shipped, the appendix was not there.

The figure everyone carried away was 3.6%. Thirty-one out of 857.

But what this study counted is documentation, not safety. The counting rule the report sets down is short. A result has to be tied to the named model. A sentence saying a model was "safety-trained" does not count.

Which leaves thirteen releases between the 94.9% with no disclosure at all and the 3.6% with a result. Ten of them claim an evaluation without a number; three are known only from press or investor records. Another 82 drop out of the quarterly tally because their dates could not be confirmed, and that fact appears nowhere in the prose, only in a note beneath a chart.

I have started calling those cells "safety that does not get counted."

"If two companies run the same test and only one of them is counted, what separated them, the test or the record?"

One more thing worth setting down. The figure cannot name a company. DeepSeek has results on 6 of its 93 releases, close to twice the overall rate, and the report said before anyone else that per-company rates are indicative rather than a ranking.

What Pebblous does in DataClinic, taking in somebody else's dataset and checking it for missingness, duplicates and identifier consistency, is the same kind of work. What counts as one release is a primary key design problem, and until that is settled, every ratio built on a count of releases wobbles. To be honest about it, Pebblous has no formal procedure either for tying its own evaluation results to a version identifier and leaving them in a form anyone can open.

Because arXiv does not delete old versions, I could count those 349 days. Whether what we have done is left behind the same way, I have not yet gone and counted.
