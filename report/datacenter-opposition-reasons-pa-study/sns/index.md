같은 3월에 미국인에게 물은 두 전국 조사가 동네 데이터센터 반대율을 49%와 71%로 적었다.

한 달 사이에 여론이 그만큼 뒤집혔다고 보기는 어렵다. 넉 달 남짓 사이 나온 전국 조사 여덟 건을 늘어놓으면 반대율은 49%에서 75%까지 흩어진다. 흩어진 것은 여론보다 재는 방식이었다. "데이터센터"를 물었는지 "AI 데이터센터"를 물었는지, 전화인지 온라인인지, 물음이 응답자의 집에서 얼마나 가까운 곳을 가리켰는지가 조사마다 달랐다.

거리가 답을 움직인다는 것은 한 조사 안에서 확인된다. 로이터·입소스는 같은 응답자에게 거리만 바꿔 세 번 물어 45%, 57%, 59%를 얻었다.

표를 세로로 읽으면 더 선명한 것이 나온다. "어느 쪽도 아니다" 칸을 응답자에게 읽어 준 조사에서 지지는 11%에서 21% 사이에 머문다. 사실상 둘 중 하나를 고르게 한 조사에서는 그 두 배에 가까워진다. 그 칸이 깎는 쪽은 반대가 아니라 지지다.

무엇에 반대하느냐 대신 무엇에 찬성하느냐를 물으면 답이 한곳으로 모인다. 성인 3,424명을 물은 조사에서 가장 널리 지지받은 항목은 건설 금지가 아니었다. 전력망을 보강해야 한다면 그 비용은 지은 쪽이 내라는 항목이 73%였고, 신규 건설 수 제한은 62%였다. 데이터센터가 일자리와 세수에 보탬이 된다는 평가는 같은 조사에서 가장 낮은 자리에 있었다.

펜실베이니아에서 열여덟 달을 지낸 연구진이 44명에게 들은 이유도 같은 방향을 가리킨다. 전기요금, 집값, 두 번 지나간 산업의 기억, 그리고 협상 자리에 앉은 지자체 대표가 비밀유지계약에 서명해 말을 못 하는 상황이다.

정작 이 논쟁의 크기를 말해 주는 숫자들이 같은 문제를 안고 있다. 펜실베이니아에 데이터센터가 몇 개인지를 집계처 일곱 곳에 물으면 값이 아홉 개 나오고, 가장 작은 값과 가장 큰 값 사이는 서른 배가 넘는다. 반대율을 하나의 수로 받아 적기 전에 확인할 것은 그 수가 아니라 그것을 만든 물음이다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Two national surveys fielded in the same March recorded local data center opposition at 49% and at 71%.

Public opinion does not swing that far in four weeks. Line up the eight national surveys fielded over roughly four months and opposition runs from 49% to 75%, and what scattered was less the opinion than the instrument. Whether the question said "data centers" or "AI data centers," whether it went out by phone or online, and how close to the respondent's own home it pointed all varied.

One survey demonstrates the distance effect internally. Reuters/Ipsos asked the same people three times, changing only how near the site would be, and got 45%, 57%, and 59%.

Read the same table down its support column and something sharper appears. Where the survey read a "neither" option out loud, support sits between 11% and 21%. Where respondents were effectively forced to pick a side, it roughly doubles. That box does not trim opposition. It trims support.

Ask what people are for, rather than against, and the answers converge. In a survey of 3,424 adults, the most widely supported proposal was not a ban on construction. It was that whoever builds the data center pays for the grid upgrades it requires, at 73%, ahead of capping the number of new builds at 62%. The claim that data centers deliver jobs and tax revenue to their host communities finished last in the same survey.

The reasons a research team heard from 44 people over eighteen months in Pennsylvania point the same way: electricity bills, property values, the memory of two industries that came and left, and municipal officials who had signed nondisclosure agreements and could not speak.

The numbers that describe the size of this fight carry the same defect. Ask seven trackers how many data centers Pennsylvania has and nine answers come back, with more than thirty times between the smallest and the largest. Before a single opposition figure goes into a decision, the thing worth checking is not the figure but the question that produced it.

## Facebook (EN)

"Like my electricity bill is now more than my car payment."

A community organizer in Pennsylvania, talking to a research team.

The sentence after it stayed with me longer. Take all of that, the organizer said, and it makes people full of rage, and the thing people are pointing some of that rage toward right now is data centers.

Between November 2024 and April 2026, researchers at Data & Society traveled to seventeen places between Philadelphia and Pittsburgh and interviewed 44 people. The reasons came in four strands. Electricity bills. Property values. The memory of anthracite and then fracking, two industries that arrived and left. And municipal officials at the negotiating table who had signed nondisclosure agreements and could not say a word.

The strands start far apart, and they end at the same counterpart. The report calls it an object that is easy to identify and easy to criticize. Artificial intelligence is abstract and located nowhere. A data center has a pin on a map, a parcel number, and a vote in a township meeting room.

What held me was not the reasons, though. It was the numbers that are supposed to tell us how large this fight is. Eight national surveys, inside four months, answered the same question anywhere from 49% to 75%. Ask seven trackers how many data centers Pennsylvania has and nine answers come back, with more than thirty times between the smallest and the largest. Nothing there was falsified. They were counting different things.

"The figure we are about to act on: what question produced it?"

The slow part of data quality work at Pebblous runs into the same place. Whether a labeling schema carries a "defer" box decides how the same source data is distributed. Schema checks do not reach this layer. All nine lists are valid. No required field is empty, no type is wrong. And still they count different objects.

The cost sheet for AI compute seems to have one column on it that nobody has named yet. An empty column is not an absent cost. It is a cost being written into somebody else's ledger.
