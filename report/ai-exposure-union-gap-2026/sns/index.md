## LinkedIn (KO)

미국에서 AI 자동화에 가장 크게 노출된 직군일수록 노조의 보호는 가장 얇다.

컴퓨터·수학 직군에서 단체협약의 보호를 받는 사람은 노동자 100명 중 4명 남짓(대표율 4.4%)이다. 반면 자동화 압력이 훨씬 낮은 교육 직군에서는 그 비율이 35.9%에 이른다. 방패가 정작 그것이 가장 덜 필요한 자리에 쌓여 있다.

이유는 구조적이다. 미국 노조는 오랫동안 공공·교육·제조에 집중됐고, 테크와 사무직 화이트칼라가 자동화의 한복판으로 밀려 들어올 무렵 그들은 이미 조직 바깥에 있었다.

다만 노출과 보호의 음의 상관은 전체 노동시장으로 보면 약하고(r≈−0.16), 전문직 안으로 좁혀야 뚜렷해진다(r≈−0.70). 결론은 정확한 노출 점수가 아니라 노출의 방향과 보호의 방향이 어긋난다는 데 있다.

캘리포니아가 행정명령(EO N-6-26)으로 이제야 조사에 착수했지만 실제 입법은 2027년 이후에나 가능하다. 노출도와 보호율이라는 두 장의 데이터를 포개어 보면, 정책이 지금 어디를 비워 두었는지가 지도처럼 드러난다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

In the U.S., the occupations most exposed to AI automation are the ones with the thinnest union protection.

In computer and math work, only about four in a hundred workers are covered by a collective-bargaining agreement (a 4.4% representation rate). In education, where automation pressure is far lower, the figure reaches 35.9%. The shield has piled up exactly where it is needed least.

The cause is structural. American unions long concentrated in the public sector, education, and manufacturing; by the time tech and clerical white-collar work moved into the path of automation, they were already outside organized labor.

The negative correlation between exposure and protection is weak across the whole market (r≈−0.16) and sharp only within professional occupations (r≈−0.70). The point is not a precise exposure score but that the direction of exposure and the direction of protection pull apart.

California's Executive Order N-6-26 has only just opened a study, and actual legislation is unlikely before 2027. Lay the two data layers over each other — exposure and protection — and the gap policy has left open reads like a map.

The people whose jobs AI is most likely to touch first.

And yet there is no table where they can sit across from an employer and set the terms.

Overlay the numbers and a strange picture appears. In computer and math work, only about four in a hundred people are covered by a collective agreement. In education, where the pressure of automation is far lower, the share climbs past 35%. The shield has gathered precisely where it is needed least.

How did it come to this? American unions long stood with the public sector, with schools, with the factory floor. By the time tech, finance, and clerical white-collar work were pulled into the center of automation, they were already outside the fold.

There are exceptions. The actors' union SAG-AFTRA wrote into its contract how far AI may go, and how consent and compensation should work. When a table exists, even high exposure can be negotiated.

"So what are the people without a table supposed to speak with?"

California has only just begun to look at this, and real rules are still years away.
