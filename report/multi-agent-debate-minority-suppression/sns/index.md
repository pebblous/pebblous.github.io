## LinkedIn (KO)

버지니아공대 연구진이 오답으로 끝난 AI 토론 3,211건을 라운드 단위로 뜯어 라벨을 달았다. 고장 난 자리가 업계의 짐작과 달랐다.

추론 오류는 성공한 토론과 실패한 토론에서 같은 비율로 일어났다. 정답으로 끝난 토론 500건을 결과를 가린 채 다시 읽혔더니 그중 91.2%에 오류가 최소 하나 있었다. 갈리는 것은 틀렸느냐가 아니라 되돌아왔느냐다.

그리고 되돌아오는 길을 막는 힘은 머릿수를 늘릴수록 세진다. 첫 라운드에 다수파가 틀렸던 대화에서, 맞는 소수 의견이 결과를 바꾼 비율은 7.87%에서 2.50%로 줄었다. 묻힌 비율은 반대로 올랐다.

에이전트를 세 기에서 일곱 기로 늘린 구간이다. 같은 구간에서 다수파가 스스로 답을 고친 비율은 거의 움직이지 않았다. 머릿수는 생각할 머리를 보태지만 불확실성과 이견을 다루는 방식은 바꾸지 않는다는 것이 논문의 요약이다. 질문을 바꾸지 않고 머릿수만 바꾼 통제 비교에서도 방향이 같았다.

이 결함은 최종 정답률로 잡히지 않고, 에이전트가 스스로 적는 확신으로도 잡히지 않는다. 토론을 거치며 확신은 양쪽 다 오르는데 틀린 쪽이 두 배 가까이 더 오르고, 끝났을 때 두 집단의 차이는 3.76점만 남는다.

남는 신호는 답이 어떻게 움직였는가 하나다. 논문의 조기 경보 모형은 메시지 본문을 한 글자도 쓰지 않고, 라운드마다 어떤 답이 나왔고 누가 답을 바꿨는지만으로 한 라운드 앞서 경보를 냈다. 생성형 AI 관측의 표준 속성 어휘를 열어 보면 토큰이 얼마나 들었는지에는 이름이 붙어 있고, 답이 어떻게 움직였는지에는 아직 이름이 없다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Virginia Tech researchers took 3,211 multi-agent LLM debates that had ended in a wrong answer and labelled them round by round. The broken part is not where the field assumes it is.

Reasoning errors appeared at the same rate in debates that succeeded and debates that failed. When 500 debates that had ended correctly were relabelled with the outcome hidden, 91.2% of them carried at least one error. What separates the two groups is not whether an error appeared but whether it was reversed.

The force that blocks reversal grows with headcount. In debates where the majority was wrong in the first round, the share in which a correct minority changed the outcome fell from 7.87% to 2.50%. The share in which that correct minority was buried moved the other way.

That is the range from three agents to seven. Majority self-correction barely shifted across it. Adding agents adds heads to think with, the authors conclude, but it does not change how a group handles uncertainty and dissent. A controlled comparison that held the questions fixed and varied only the headcount pointed the same way.

None of this surfaces in the final accuracy number, and none of it surfaces in the confidence the agents report. Confidence climbs through a debate on both sides, and it climbs almost twice as fast on the side that is wrong. By the last round the two groups sit 3.76 points apart.

What is left is the shape of the answers themselves. The paper's early-warning model reads no message text at all, only which answers were on the table each round and who changed theirs, and it raises a flag one round ahead. The standard attribute vocabulary for generative AI telemetry has a field for how many tokens were spent. It has no field yet for how the answers moved.

## Facebook (EN)

"Stop when confidence clears 90."

If you have ever run a panel of agents against a hard question, you have probably written a line like that somewhere. I had it filed under sensible safeguards.

Virginia Tech researchers opened 3,211 AI debates that ended in a wrong answer and read them a round at a time.

The debates that ended wrong closed at an average self-reported confidence of 89.34. The ones that ended right closed at 93.10.

3.76 points. Draw the line wherever you like and both groups end up on the same side of it.

What held my attention longer was the climb. Confidence rises through a debate on both sides, and it rises almost twice as fast on the side that is wrong. The thickness of the agreement was being translated into confidence. The thickness of the evidence was not.

The obvious next move is to add more eyes. The same paper has a number for that too. In debates where the majority was wrong in the first round, the share in which a correct minority changed the outcome fell from 7.87% to 2.50% as the panel grew from three agents to seven. The share in which that minority was buried went the other way. Majority self-correction stayed roughly where it was.

What headcount grew was not the power to correct. It was the power to absorb.

"For the agent pipeline in front of you, is the middle of the work being kept, or is quality being judged off the final answer alone?"

What Pebblous has mostly done in data quality is measure whether a value is right. The place this paper points at is next door to that: measuring when and how something went wrong.

And measuring it turns out not to require the transcript. The paper's early-warning model reads no message text whatsoever. It looks only at which answers were on the table each round and who changed theirs.

Yet open the standard attribute vocabulary for generative AI telemetry and there is a field for how many tokens were spent, and still no field for how the answers moved from round to round.

The cost of the thinking has a name. The shape of the thinking does not have one yet.
