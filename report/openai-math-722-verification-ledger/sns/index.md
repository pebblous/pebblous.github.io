## LinkedIn (KO)

오픈AI가 10월 6일 깃허브에 수학 원고 722편을 한꺼번에 올렸다. 그 원고들의 형식화 기록을 적은 파일 맨 끝에는 검토 상태를 적는 칸이 있고, 거기 적힌 값은 "unchecked" 다. 누가, 어떻게 검토했는지 적는 옆 칸 둘은 비어 있다.

하루 사이에 세 편이 내려갔다. 한 증명이 궤적마다 부호를 +1로 매겼는데 그 관례에서는 −1이어야 했고, 그 한 편에 기대 있던 두 편이 함께 무너졌다. 보도가 가져간 수는 그쪽이 아니라 README 한 줄에 적힌 검증 비율이었다.

그 비율을 저장소 파일로 다시 세면 하나로 모이지 않는다. 형식화 카탈로그와 경로가 이어지는 논문으로 세면 23.9%, 그 논문이 속한 결과 묶음으로 세면 36.3%, README가 적는 값은 약 42%다.

널리 인용된 42%의 분자 300은 그 카탈로그 파일의 어떤 목록 길이와도 맞지 않는다. 그 수가 세는 단위인 "top-line result" 의 정의도 저장소 어디에 없다. 공개 당일 그 자리에 있던 것은 수치가 아니라 "많다" 는 낱말이었다.

숨긴 쪽으로 읽을 일은 아니다. 가장 낮은 검토 등급을 기계가 읽는 자리에 공개 첫날부터 적어 둔 쪽이 오히려 정직하다. 철회 통지에도 커밋 해시를 박아, 내려간 원고를 지워지지 않는 주소로 남겼다. 적혀 있는데 아무도 읽지 않았을 뿐이다.

산출물의 양을 세는 칸은 어느 조직에나 있다. 그중 무엇이 어떤 등급으로 확인됐는지 적는 칸은 대개 비어 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

On 6 October, OpenAI put 722 mathematical manuscripts on GitHub in a single release. At the end of the file that records which of them were formalized sits a field for the review status. The value entered there is "unchecked." The two fields beside it, for who reviewed and how, are empty.

Three manuscripts came down within a day. One proof had assigned sign +1 to each trace where the convention requires −1, and the two papers that leaned on it fell with it. The number the coverage carried away was not that one. It was the verification rate in a single line of the README.

Count that rate from the repository files and it does not resolve to one figure. Papers joined to the formalization catalogue by path give 23.9%. The result groups those papers belong to give 36.3%. The README says roughly 42%.

The numerator behind that 42%, which is 300, matches the length of no list in the catalogue file. Nor is the unit it counts, "top-line result," defined anywhere in the repository. On the day of release, the same sentence carried a word rather than a figure: many.

This is not a concealment story. Writing the lowest review grade into a machine-readable field on day one is the honest end of the range, and the withdrawal notices pin a commit hash so the pre-withdrawal manuscripts stay reachable at an address that cannot rot. The field was filled in. Nobody read it.

Every organisation has a field for how much it produced. The field recording what was verified, and to what grade, is usually the empty one.

양을 세는 일은 쉬웠습니다. 그 수가 무엇을 센 것인지 적는 칸을 우리가 쓰고 있는지는, 아직 세어 보지 않았습니다.

## Facebook (EN)

I had downloaded the repository and was counting entries when I scrolled to the bottom of the file.

What is down there is not a list. It is a three-line block, the place where the review status goes. The two fields beside it, for who reviewed and how, were empty.

The status itself reads "unchecked."

This is the release of 722 mathematical manuscripts that OpenAI put on GitHub on 6 October. Three of them came down within a day. One proof had assigned sign +1 to each trace where the convention calls for −1, and the two papers that leaned on it fell with it.

That is not what the coverage carried. It carried the verification rate from a single line of the README, and I followed that line first myself. Counting the files gave three different rates from one repository, each of them counting something else. On the day of release, that same sentence held a word rather than a figure: many.

However you count the rate, the field underneath it did not move. Between the release commit and the current one, not one character differs.

I have started calling it "the field nobody read."

"If something is written down and no one reads it, is the record there or not?"

Concealment is the wrong frame. Writing the lowest grade into a machine-readable field, on day one, is the honest end of the range. The withdrawal notices pin a commit hash so the manuscripts that came down still open at an address that cannot rot. The well-kept field and the empty one live in the same repository.

What Pebblous does in DataClinic, taking in somebody else's dataset, judging its condition and leaving that judgement as a record someone can open later, is the same kind of work as this field. Passing a schema check, being compared against the source by a person, and being accepted as useful by the field itself are three different numbers, and most organisations count only the first and call it verified data. To be honest about it, Pebblous has no formal field for that review grade either, not in its reports and not in its synthetic data outputs.

Counting how much was produced was the easy part. Whether we use the field that says what the count was a count of, I have not yet gone and checked.
