## LinkedIn (KO)

유네스코가 COP31에서 낸 기후 AI 보고서의 머리기사는 "사용자가 반박하면 물러선다"였다. 그 결과를 만든 것은 조사 대상 33개 모델이 아니라 15개 모델이다.

유네스코와 유니버시티칼리지런던의 유네스코 인공지능 석좌가 11월 16일 안탈리아에서 『Can We Trust AI on Climate?』를 냈다. 시험은 세 갈래다. 33개 모델에서 기후 사실과 증거 판단을, 15개 모델에서 사용자가 틀린 주장을 들이밀 때의 반응을, 5개 모델에서 13개 언어의 정확도를 쟀다. 세 수치가 한 보도자료에 나란히 놓여 있어 한 덩어리로 읽히지만, 잰 대상이 서로 다르다.

단위도 갈린다. 교과서 사실 88~96%와 증거 판단 71~77%는 가장 잘하는 시스템 기준의 범위다. 단일 정책의 효과를 묻는 문항에서 정확도는 최대 94.5%에 이르렀고, 정책 두 가지를 합친 효과를 묻는 문항에서 평균은 33.8%다.

단일 정책 과제의 평균값은 공개되지 않았다. 그래서 최대에서 평균으로 내려오는 낙폭을 한 선으로 그릴 수 없다. 33.8%가 눈에 밟히는 까닭은 바로 옆에 붙은 수 때문이다. 세 보기 중 하나를 아무렇게나 찍어도 33.3%가 나오는 문항이다.

언어 쪽 네 줄, 그러니까 영어 88.6%와 벵골어·힌디어 83.6%와 우르두어 81.4%와 스와힐리어 78.3%는 다섯 모델의 평균이다.

평균은 모델 사이의 편차를 덮는다. 하나씩 뜯어보면 영어와 스와힐리어의 간격이 3%p 미만인 쪽도 있고 16%p 이상인 쪽도 있다. 평균끼리의 10.3%p와 한 모델 안의 16%p는 분모가 다른 수다.

11월 16~17일 기사 17건을 전수로 세어 보니 반박 시험의 15는 적은 매체가 전부 맞게 적었다. 무너진 쪽은 언어 시험이고, 다섯을 지운 기사가 7건이다.

페블러스에는 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단하는 DataClinic이라는 도구가 있다. 열 건을 보고 매긴 값과 만 건을 보고 매긴 값은 같은 이름으로 불린다. 이름이 같다는 것이 둘을 나란히 세워도 된다는 뜻은 아니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

UNESCO's climate AI report, released at COP31 in Antalya, surveyed 33 large language models. The finding that became the headline, that a model gives up on a climate fact once a user objects, was measured on 15. The figures on accuracy by language came from five.

Can We Trust AI on Climate? was published on November 16 by UNESCO with the UNESCO Chair in Artificial Intelligence at University College London. Its testing ran along three tracks. One set of 33 models was asked for climate facts and for judgments about evidence. A set of 15 met users pressing false claims. A set of five answered the same questions in 13 languages.

The units differ too. Textbook facts at 88-96% and evidence judgment at 71-77% are ranges for the best-performing systems.

Accuracy on the effect of a single policy reached 94.5%, which is a maximum. Accuracy on the effect of two policies applied together averaged 33.8%.

No mean was ever published for the single-policy task, so there is no slope to draw between those two figures. What makes 33.8% hold the eye is the number sitting beside it. Pick one of three options at random on those questions and 33.3% come out right.

English 88.6%, Bengali and Hindi 83.6%, Urdu 81.4%, Swahili 78.3%. Those four lines are averages over five models.

An average covers the spread between models. Taken one at a time, the distance between English and Swahili runs from under 3 points to over 16. The 10.3-point gap between averages and the 16-point gap inside a single model rest on different denominators.

A full count of the 17 articles published on November 16 and 17 shows that every outlet giving a number for the push-back test gave fifteen. The language test is where the boundary broke. Seven articles put thirty-three in place of five, and six of those carry one sentence out of the UN newsroom.

## Facebook (EN)

"Researchers tested up to 33 leading AI models in 13 languages."

That sentence went out on the UN newsroom on November 16.

The report it describes put the language questions to five models, not thirty-three.

The report is Can We Trust AI on Climate?, from UNESCO and the UNESCO Chair in Artificial Intelligence at University College London, timed to coincide with COP31 in Antalya. Thirty-three is right for the survey as a whole. Fifteen models met a user pressing a false claim. Five of the thirty-three answered the same questions in thirteen languages.

Three numbers standing side by side in one press release read, to whoever copies them next, as one number.

I gathered the seventeen articles published on the sixteenth and the seventeenth and counted three things in each. Every outlet that gave a figure for the push-back test gave fifteen. The language test is where it gave way. Seven articles put thirty-three where the report had five.

Split those seven once more and six of them are the same sentence, the one at the top of this post, carried forward word for word. One outlet arrived at the error on its own.

Once the counting was done, the tally mattered less to me than the starting point.

The boundary did not blur at the far end of the chain, in some hurried aggregator. It blurred in the newsroom closest to the institution that published the report, and from there it reached five outlets inside a day.

No fault attaches to the outlets that carried it. They ran the sentence as it was written.

"Of the numbers we send out, how many leave with the thing they were counted over?"

The number Pebblous ends up with for a batch of AI training data comes out of DataClinic. Nine datasets supplied the questions in this report. Two were built for the study, and the other seven go unnamed in everything that has been made public.

The score travelled as far as the press release, the articles and this post. The name of the data that produced it had already dropped out at the first step.

Both Korean-language articles got all three axes right.

Carrying a sentence forward, and carrying forward what that sentence measured, turn out to be two separate motions.
