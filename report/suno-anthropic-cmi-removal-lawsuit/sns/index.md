음악 퍼블리셔 리저부어가 10월 21일 캘리포니아 북부연방지법에 수노와 앤트로픽을 상대로 소장 두 건을 냈다. 청구 총액은 3억 3,778만 달러다. 눈여겨볼 것은 총액이 아니라 청구서가 세 층으로 쪼개져 있다는 점이다.

아래에서 두 번째 층이 저작권 관리 정보 제거다. 허락 없이 썼는지와 무관하게, 작품에 붙어 있던 제목과 작사가와 퍼블리셔를 떼어 낸 행위에만 매겨진 값이다. 수노 2,625만 달러, 앤트로픽 2,133만 달러, 합쳐서 4,758만 달러.

제소 직후 요약 여럿이 "곡당 2만 5천 달러"에 "수노 몫 약 2,890만 달러"를 나란히 적었는데 두 수는 짝이 맞지 않는다. 2,890만 달러는 접근통제 우회까지 합친 DMCA 전체 금액이다. 이름표를 뗀 값만 떼어 내 단가로 나누면 소장이 특정한 악곡 수가 그대로 나온다.

들어갈 문은 다섯 주 전에 열렸다. 9월 16일 제9순회항소법원은 코파일럿 사건에서 산출물을 겨눈 같은 조항의 청구를 물리치면서, 학습 단계에서 떼어 냈다는 주장은 판단하지 않고 남겨 뒀다. 리저부어는 35일 뒤 그 판결이 구속하는 바로 그 법원에 제소했다.

다만 작품당 2만 5천 달러는 확정된 값이 아니라 천장이다. 조문이 정한 범위는 2,500달러에서 시작하고 소장은 위쪽 끝을 적었다. 법원이 하한을 택하면 열 배가 줄고, 위반자가 몰랐음을 입증하면 더 깎이거나 면제될 수도 있다.

데이터 쪽에 남는 질문은 승패가 아니다. 음악은 작품마다 영구 식별번호를 붙이는 체계를 수십 년 운영해 온 산업이고 번호를 하나 더 붙이는 비용은 0에 수렴한다. 그런데도 학습 파이프라인을 지나는 사이에 표시는 사라졌다. 붙여 두는 비용과 지운 값 중 어느 쪽이 큰지, 이제 한쪽에만 숫자가 적혔다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The music publisher Reservoir filed two complaints against Suno and Anthropic in the Northern District of California on 21 October, claiming $337.78 million. The number worth reading is not the total. It is the fact that the bill arrives in three separate layers.

The second layer from the bottom is removal of copyright management information. It is priced on one act alone, regardless of whether the work was used without permission: taking the title, the songwriter and the publisher off the work. That layer is $26.25 million from Suno and $21.33 million from Anthropic, about $47.58 million together.

Several summaries published after the filing set "$25,000 a song" beside "roughly $28.9 million from Suno." Those two do not reconcile. The $28.9 million figure bundles in the circumvention count. Divide the stripping layer alone by the unit price and you land exactly on the number of compositions the complaint identifies.

The door was opened five weeks earlier. On 16 September the Ninth Circuit rejected a claim under the same section aimed at what Copilot generated, and declined to decide whether removal during training counts. Reservoir filed 35 days later, in the very court that ruling binds.

The per-work figure is a ceiling rather than a price. The statutory band starts at $2,500, and the complaints wrote in the top of it. If the court picks the floor the figure drops tenfold, and a violator who shows it did not know can have it cut further or waived.

For anyone working with data, the open question is not who wins. Music has run permanent identifiers on every work for decades, and one more code costs effectively nothing. The credit still came off somewhere in the training pipeline. Of the two sides, the cost of carrying provenance and the price of having erased it, only one now has a number written against it.

## Facebook (EN)

At the back of the complaint there is a schedule of songs.

Johnny Cash's 'Ring of Fire'. Joni Mitchell's 'Both Sides Now'. Madonna's 'Papa Don't Preach'.

What held me was not that list but the table a few pages earlier. The second row from the bottom.

The claim written there is not "they used it without permission." It is "they took the title, the songwriter and the publisher off the work." Counted separately from the infringement, with its own figure beside it.

This is the pair of complaints the music publisher Reservoir filed against Suno and Anthropic in the Northern District of California on 21 October. The money attached to the stripping alone comes to $47.58 million across the two companies.

I have started calling it the price of the erasure.

The assumption underneath it, that erasing means nobody can tell, is also giving way. A study this year decided whether a given track had been trained on by querying the model alone, without touching its parameters or its logs, and reported accuracy as high as 98.6%. Provenance does not disappear when you delete it. The cost of reconstructing it simply moves from the party who erased it to the party who lost it.

"For one record in the dataset in front of you, can you say right now where it came from?"

When Pebblous describes AI-Ready Data, "provenance can be traced" has always been on the list. It was a recommendation. An item with no number against it is the first thing to fall off an agenda, and these complaints have written a per-work band into that line for the first time. Raising a quality score and carrying provenance to the end of the pipeline are work on different axes, and to be honest about it, we have not formally stood up the instrument that measures the second one either.

Music was data where the cost of attaching a number was close to nothing. A registered code runs under a cent, and on the composition side it is free.

The credit came off anyway, somewhere along the pipeline.

So consider the material with no numbering system at all. Text and images pulled off the web carry no work identifier, no register of rights holders, no field for terms of use.

Nobody has sent a bill for that side yet.
