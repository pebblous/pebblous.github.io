## LinkedIn (KO)

2026년 9월 1일, TTA 표준화위원회 과제 데이터베이스에서 AI 레디 데이터라는 이름의 표준은 검색되지 않았다. 검색 기능이 고장 난 것은 아니다. 같은 창구에 온톨로지를 넣으면 37건이 정상으로 돌아온다.

우산은 없지만 부품은 실재한다. 데이터 카탈로그 어휘, 데이터옵스, 데이터 패브릭, AI 모델 학습데이터세트 메타데이터가 2025년 12월 5일에 나란히 표준공고됐다. 카탈로그 어휘 표준 자체는 2017년에 제정된 것이라, 늦게 출발한 지형이 아니라 이름이 없는 지형이다.

압력은 이미 점수로 돌아왔다. 2025년 공공데이터 제공·운영실태평가에 AI 친화·고가치 데이터 개방이 신규 지표로 들어가면서 첫 점수가 37.8점으로 나왔다. 제도의 성숙도를 재는 관리체계 영역은 같은 평가에서 89.5점이다.

정책 문서가 의미 층에 배정한 지면은 거의 없다. 국가 카탈로그 표준 가이드 전문에 온톨로지는 0회이고, 행정안전부 가이드라인에는 부록 체크리스트의 권장 문항 하나로 1회 나온다. 그렇다고 빠뜨렸다고 읽으면 틀린다. 같은 개념에 같은 용어를 쓰라는 요구는 그 체크리스트의 필수 문항이고, 온톨로지 언어의 주소도 네임스페이스 표에 이미 선언돼 있다. 요구는 흩어진 채로 있고, 그 요구를 어떤 순서로 충족하라는 문서가 없다.

순서에 관해서는 올해의 증거가 한 방향을 가리킨다. 4월에 공개된 짝지어 비교 실험은 창고 스키마에 4KB짜리 손으로 쓴 문서 한 장을 얹은 것만으로 프론티어 모델 세 개 모두에서 자연어 질의 정확도가 20%포인트 안팎 올랐다고 보고했다. 그 논문의 저자 소속이 시맨틱 레이어를 상용으로 파는 회사라는 점은 함께 봐야 하지만, 반대편의 벤치마크 논문도 결론이 어긋나지 않는다. 단순 사실 검색에서는 지식그래프가 평범한 검색을 넘지 못하고, 그래프가 앞서는 구간은 여러 문서에 흩어진 정보를 이어 붙여야 하는 질문이었다.

가장 싼 층에서 가장 확실한 이득이 나오고, 가장 비싼 층은 조건부다. 국가 카탈로그 표준 가이드가 부록에 스스로 적어 둔 순서도 같은 방향이다. 없는 것은 재료가 아니라 조립 설명서다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Korea's standards register has no entry for AI-Ready Data. A search of the Telecommunications Technology Association project database on 1 September 2026 returned nothing for that name, or for four variants of it. The search itself works fine: enter ontology and 37 projects come back.

What exists instead is components. A data catalog vocabulary, DataOps, a data fabric and training-dataset metadata were all published as standards on the same day, 5 December 2025. The catalog vocabulary dates back to 2017, so this is not a late start. It is an unnamed one.

The pressure is already scored. Korea's statutory assessment of public-data provision added AI-friendly, high-value data release as a new indicator in 2025, and it came in at 37.8, against 89.5 for the governance dimension that measures institutional maturity.

On the semantic layer the policy documents go quiet. Ontology appears zero times in the full text of the national catalog standard guide, and once in the Ministry of the Interior and Safety guidelines, as a recommended item on an appendix checklist. Reading that as an oversight would be wrong. The requirement to use one term for one concept sits in the same checklist as mandatory, and the namespace table already declares the address of OWL. The demands are there, scattered. The document that puts them in order is not.

This year's evidence leans one way on that order. A paired-comparison experiment released in April found that attaching a single 4 KB hand-written document to a warehouse schema lifted natural-language query accuracy by roughly 20 points, and did so across all three frontier models tested. Its authors sell semantic layers commercially, which is worth holding in view. Yet the benchmark paper pulling the other way agrees on sequence: on simple factual lookup, graph-based retrieval does not beat ordinary retrieval, and the graph only pulls ahead when an answer has to be stitched together from several documents.

The cheapest layer returns the surest gain and the most expensive one is conditional. What Korea is missing is not the parts. It is the assembly instructions.

## Facebook (EN)

Picture one person who looks after data at a public institution.

The AI-friendly management guidelines landed on their desk this spring, and they are working down the 38 checklist items in the appendix, one row at a time.

In the section on meaning and standards, their hand stops.

"Where a domain standard terminology system or ontology exists, has a procedure for checking consistency against that standard (including mapping and verification steps) been established and applied?"

Where one exists.

In front of that conditional clause there is not much a person can do, because nowhere in the document does it say how to build one if none exists.

Across the full text of the guidelines, that single checklist item is the only place the word ontology appears. In the 60-page national catalog standard guide published five months earlier, it does not appear at all.

And yet reading this as an oversight is probably wrong.

The same checklist carries a mandatory item asking whether one concept is labelled with one consistent term, and the namespace table both documents print already declares the address of the ontology language itself. The requirements are all there, scattered.

What is missing is the 'assembly instructions'. The parts are stacked up; the page telling anyone what to assemble, and in what order, has not been written.

On that order, one experiment from this year gives an unexpected answer. Adding a single 4 KB hand-written explanatory document to a condition that had only the warehouse schema raised query accuracy by around 20 points, and it did so for all three frontier models tested. Pulling the other way, full knowledge graphs are repeatedly reported to fall short of ordinary retrieval on simple lookup tasks.

The cheapest layer returns the surest gain, and the most expensive one is conditional.

That is close to why we drew this map at all. Pebblous measures the present state of data and hands back a report on it, and what we found this time is that the same work is already written into a policy document as a required item. If a diagnostic tool can stand where someone is filling in 38 rows by hand, that is not a need we invented. It was there first.

Which leaves one question.

"Before commissioning an ontology design, has anyone measured what state our data is actually in?"
