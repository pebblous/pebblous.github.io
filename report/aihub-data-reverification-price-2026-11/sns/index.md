## LinkedIn (KO)

비식별을 마쳤다며 공개한 데이터를, 공개한 쪽이 다시 열고 있다. AI허브가 기개방 데이터 150종을 대상으로 발주한 개인정보 재검증 및 비식별 용역이 12월에 끝난다. 예산은 3억원이고, 대상은 이미 1차 비식별화가 끝나 공개된 데이터다.

왜 다시 여는지는 올여름에 한 번 드러났다. 8월 27일 과기정통부와 NIA가 독자 AI 파운데이션 모델 프로젝트의 학습데이터를 AI허브에 올렸다. 그날 보도자료는 품질검증과 개인정보 검증을 마쳤다고 적었다. 닷새 뒤 그중 넷이 비식별화 미흡을 사유로 내려갔다. 9월 17일 뉴스포스트는 그 넷 가운데 하나에서 실존하는 사람의 이름과 전화번호와 주소를 확인했다고 보도했다.

입구 쪽 설계를 보면 이유가 하나 더 보인다. 올해 민간을 향해 열린 창구에서 국가가 대는 것은 데이터값이 아니라 품질컨설팅과 비식별화 비용이고, 한도는 1종당 6,000만 원이다. 그런데 그 창구가 반드시 받는 서류는 데이터 명세서와 샘플데이터 둘뿐이다. 품질관리 결과서는 "제출가능한 자료에 한해" 받는 묶음 안에 들어 있다. 보증 문서가 선택이면 그 보증이 언제 찍혔는지도 기록되지 않는다.

이 글이 겨누는 것은 정부의 실수가 아니다. 한 번 내보낸 데이터를 스스로 다시 열겠다고 공고로 적은 공공 플랫폼은 드물다. 미국 data.gov도 EU data.europa.eu도 등록 없이 내려받는 구조라 누가 사본을 쥐고 있는지 모른다. AI허브는 본인인증과 데이터셋별 신청·승인을 거치므로 아는 쪽이다.

조항에 적히지 않은 것이 셋이다. 얼마 만에 다시 보는가, 그 보증을 누가 지는가, 이미 받아 간 사람에게 어떻게 알리는가. 이용정책은 이용자에게 개인정보를 발견하면 신고하고 삭제하라고 적지만, 반대 방향으로 알린다는 조항은 확인되지 않는다. 온타리오 정보·프라이버시 커미셔너는 지난해 10월 지침에서 재식별 위험 재평가의 통상 간격을 2~3년으로 적었다. 숫자가 꼭 2년이어야 한다는 말이 아니라, 주기를 숫자로 적어 둔 문서가 이미 있다는 말이다.

2027년 4월 21일부터는 정부 사업으로 만든 학습용데이터를 이 창고에 연계·제공하는 것이 의무가 된다. 들어오는 양이 늘면 다시 여는 일의 값도 같이 오른다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The body that published the data is reopening it. AI Hub, South Korea's state repository for AI training data, has commissioned a KRW 300 million ($207K) review of 150 datasets it had already released as de-identified. The work closes in December.

Why became visible in late August. The science ministry and NIA opened training data from the country's sovereign foundation-model programme, stating that quality checks and personal-information checks had been completed. Five days later four of those datasets were pulled for inadequate de-identification, and in September a Korean outlet reported finding real names, phone numbers and addresses inside one of them.

The intake side explains the rest. What the state pays for is not the data but the work of cleaning it: quality consulting and de-identification, capped at KRW 60 million ($41,000) per dataset. Yet the only documents a submitting company must file are a data specification and a sample. The quality management report sits in a bundle filed "only where it can be submitted." When the assurance document is optional, the date the assurance was stamped is never recorded either.

This is not a story about a government getting it wrong. Few public platforms publish a tender to reopen data they have already declared clean. data.gov and data.europa.eu hand files to anyone without registration, so neither knows who holds a copy. AI Hub does: downloads require identity verification and per-dataset approval.

Three things are missing from the statute. How often the data is looked at again, who carries the assurance, and how anyone who already downloaded it gets told. The usage policy obliges users to report and delete if they find personal information in what they received; nothing runs in the other direction. Ontario's Information and Privacy Commissioner, in guidelines issued last October, puts the usual interval for re-assessing re-identification risk at two to three years. The point is not that two years is the right number. It is that a document somewhere already writes one down.

From 21 April 2027, routing training data produced under government projects into this repository becomes mandatory. The more that arrives, the more the unanswered question costs.

De-identification is not a state. It is a timestamp.

## Facebook (EN)

Somewhere there is a hard drive with a dataset on it that was downloaded from AI Hub two years ago and never touched since.

On the day it was downloaded, that file was marked de-identified.

Whether it still is, neither the person holding it nor the body that released it has had occasion to check.

AI Hub, the state repository Korea has been filling with AI training data since 2017, has commissioned a review of 150 datasets it already published, looking for personal information still inside them. The work closes in December. Every one of those datasets had been through de-identification once already.

What prompted it showed itself in late August. Training data from the sovereign foundation-model programme went up with a statement that quality and personal-information checks were complete. Four of those datasets came down five days later. In September a reporter opened one of them and found real names and phone numbers.

An assurance with a shelf life.

That phrase followed me through the whole piece. "De-identification complete" is a conclusion reached with the auxiliary information available then and the detection methods available then, and both of those move. Ontario's Information and Privacy Commissioner, in guidelines issued last October, asks for re-identification risk to be reassessed every two or three years.

No interval of that kind turns up in the Korean statute.

What stayed with me longer than the interval was the notification. AI Hub's usage policy tells users that if they find personal information in data they received, they must report it and delete their copy. Nothing is written for the other direction.

"If this review of 150 datasets turns something up, who tells the people already holding copies?"

AI Hub is one of the few platforms that could. Downloading requires identity verification, a request for each dataset, and an approval, which means it knows who took what. The American and European national data portals hand files over without registration, so no route to tell anyone exists at all. This reads less like an inability than like something not yet written down.
