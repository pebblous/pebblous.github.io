## LinkedIn (KO)

같은 논문의 방법과 실험 설정, 보고된 수치를 그대로 둔 채 문장만 고쳐 쓴 원고 4,080편을, 다섯 개 LLM 심사위원이 42,396번 채점했습니다. 메릴랜드대와 버지니아공대, MBZUAI, 워털루대 연구진이 이달 arXiv에 올린 통제 실험입니다.

점수는 갈렸습니다. 다만 결론은 "AI 심판이 화려한 문장에 넘어간다"가 아니었습니다. 여섯 개 수사 차원 가운데 근거를 어떻게 프레이밍했는가가 가장 크게 움직여 약한-채택 확률을 13.0%포인트 갈랐고, 어휘와 구문을 어렵게 만드는 조작은 대비가 0.011에 그칩니다.

크게 반응한 축은 근거와 참신성처럼 읽는 쪽의 판단이 필요한 속성이었고, 반응하지 않은 축은 문체처럼 표면에서 바로 확인되는 속성이었습니다. 더 불편한 것은 그 변화가 기록된 자리입니다. 바꾼 것은 문장뿐인데, 모델은 그것을 표현이 좋아졌다고 적지 않고 기여와 타당성이 달라졌다고 적었습니다.

프롬프트를 조이는 대응도 실험 조건에 들어 있었습니다. 표현을 보상하지 말라고 명시한 엄격 채점은 평균 총평을 1.36점 끌어내렸지만 문장에 대한 민감도는 함께 줄지 않았고, 어떤 조합에서는 오히려 커졌습니다. 심판을 Claude Sonnet 5로 바꾸면 같은 조작에 점수가 내려가 부호까지 뒤집혔습니다. 모두가 같은 방향으로 속는 문제가 아니라는 뜻입니다.

평가를 모델에 맡긴 조직에 남는 질문은 어느 심판 모델이 더 좋은가가 아닙니다. 같은 내용을 다르게 서술한 입력에 우리 파이프라인의 판정이 유지되는지, 검사한 기록이 있는가입니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Researchers froze the methods, the experimental setup and the reported numbers of 120 ICLR 2026 submissions, rewrote only the prose into 4,080 manuscripts, and had five LLM reviewers score them 42,396 times. The controlled study, from teams at the University of Maryland, Virginia Tech, MBZUAI and the University of Waterloo, went up on arXiv this month.

The scores moved. But not in the way the headline would suggest. Of six rhetorical dimensions, how the evidence was framed moved scores the most, swinging the probability of a weak accept by 13.0 percentage points, while making the vocabulary and syntax harder produced a contrast of 0.011, which is to say almost nothing.

The dimensions that moved were the ones a reader has to judge, such as evidence and novelty. The ones that did not move were the ones anyone can check on the surface, such as register and word choice. The more uncomfortable finding is where the movement was recorded. Only the sentences changed, yet the models did not write it down as better presentation. They wrote it down as changed contribution and soundness.

Tightening the prompt was tested too. A strict rubric that explicitly told judges not to reward phrasing pulled average overall scores down by 1.36 points, but sensitivity to prose did not fall with it, and in some cells it grew. Swap the judge to Claude Sonnet 5 and the same rewrite pushed scores down instead of up, flipping the sign. This is not a case of everyone being fooled in the same direction.

For an organization that has handed judgment to a model, the first question is not which judge is better. It is whether anyone has checked, and logged, that the verdict holds when the same content arrives written a different way.

더 좋은 심판 모델을 고르는 일보다, 그 심판이 흔들리는지 먼저 재는 일이 앞에 있는 것 같습니다.

## Facebook (EN)

"Keep the results. Just rewrite how you say it."

Anyone who has had a paper rejected has probably heard some version of this. For a long time I thought it was fair advice. The experiments had not changed, after all.

While writing this report, I read a study that ran that advice at a scale of 4,080 manuscripts.

The researchers took 120 papers submitted to ICLR 2026, locked the methods, the experimental setup and every reported number, rewrote only the prose, and had five LLM reviewers score the result more than forty thousand times.

The scores moved. But they did not move for just any sentence.

Making the vocabulary and syntax harder produced almost no reaction. What moved the scores was how the evidence was put forward, and how strongly novelty was claimed. So it was not a case of judges being dazzled by fine writing. They wavered exactly where judgment was required.

What I kept returning to was not the size of the movement, but where it was recorded.

Only the sentences had changed. The models did not write that down as better presentation. They wrote it down as changed contribution and soundness.

Which is why detection does not close this hole. A hidden prompt or an artificial perturbation can be caught. But stating your evidence more clearly and leading with your contribution is what an advisor tells a student to do. I have been holding this whole class of edits under one name while reading: content-preserving variation.

"Does our evaluation pipeline return the same verdict when the same content arrives written a different way? Has anyone actually measured that?"

This is where Pebblous keeps returning to the paper. The moment the judge of a dataset's condition shifts from a person to a model, a quality metric stops being a function of what state the data is in and becomes a function of how the model read it.

Choosing a better judge model seems like the second question. Measuring whether the judge holds still comes first.
