## LinkedIn (KO)

남이 모아 둔 데이터를 원래와 다른 주제로 옮겨 쓴 논문일수록 자기 분야의 인용 흐름을 갈라놓았다. 정작 인용 수에서는 아무 차이도 확인되지 않았다.

머신러닝 논문 1만 2,286편을 놓고 돌린 회귀의 결과다.

재활용의 정도가 1 표준편차 오를 때 3년 파괴성 지수는 0.10 표준편차 높아졌다. 인용 쪽 계수는 같은 표에서 사실상 0이었다.

재활용을 세는 방식이 이 연구의 핵심이다. 같은 데이터셋을 쓴 앞선 논문들과 초점 논문의 제목 임베딩 거리를 데이터셋 겹침으로 가중해 연속값으로 만들고, 같은 해 같은 급의 논문끼리만 견주었다.

다만 그 재활용이 남에게 이어진 경우는 드물었다. 데이터셋의 나중 쓰임이 새 방향보다 옛 자리에 가까웠던 논문이 55%다. 뒤로 건너간 소수에서만 파괴성과 인용이 함께 올랐고, 인용 쪽 상승분에는 논문이 스스로 적어 둔 기계적 성분이 섞여 있다.

이 표본을 만든 Papers with Code는 2025년 7월 문을 닫았다. 최종 스냅샷은 보존 조직에 남았지만 더 갱신되지 않고, 과제별 리더보드는 이관되지 않았다. 다시 쓰려면 먼저 찾을 수 있어야 하는데, 찾게 해 주던 층은 한 회사의 운영 결정 하나로 사라졌다.

한 번의 재활용이 값을 낼 확률이 낮다면, 값은 여러 번 시도할 수 있는 조건에서 나온다. 데이터가 검색되고, 수집 목적과 한계가 읽히고, 라이선스가 2차 사용을 막지 않는 상태다. 페블러스가 데이터 품질 진단에서 재사용 가능성 평가로 한 걸음 옮기는 근거도 여기에 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Papers that carried someone else's dataset into a different subject disrupted their own field more. On citations they gained nothing at all.

That comes from a regression over 12,286 machine learning papers.

One standard deviation more repurposing came with a 0.10 standard deviation rise in the three-year disruption index. The coefficient on citations sat at effectively zero in the same table.

How repurposing was counted is the substance of the work. The researchers took the title-embedding distance between a focal paper and the earlier papers that had used the same dataset, weighted it by how far the datasets overlapped, and compared papers only against others published the same year at the same tier.

Reuse seldom travelled onward, though. In 55% of the papers, the dataset's later users stayed nearer its original application than the direction the focal paper had opened. Only in the minority that did travel did disruption and citations rise together, and the paper flags a mechanical component inside that citation figure itself.

Papers with Code, the platform that produced this sample, shut down in July 2025. The final snapshot survives in a preservation organisation on Hugging Face, no longer updated, and the per-task leaderboards were never carried over. Reuse begins with being able to find the data, and the layer that made it findable disappeared on one company's operating decision.

If a single act of reuse has a low chance of paying off, the value lies in being able to try often. That means data that can be found, a collection purpose and its limits left legible, and a licence that does not block a second use. It is also the axis along which Pebblous is moving, from diagnosing data quality to assessing whether data can be reused at all.

## Facebook (EN)

Have you ever rebuilt a dataset from scratch because you could not find the one that already existed?

An audit published in Korea last month has that story in it.

Of 9,000 household-waste images a district office collected, 5,200 closely resembled data a national programme had already built in 2020.

Nothing was wrong with the quality. The problem was that nobody knew the data was there.

Around the same time I read a preprint that measured, across 12,286 machine learning papers, what happened to research that took a dataset someone else had collected and put it to work on a different subject.

The further the data travelled from its earlier use, the more the paper split its field's citation flow. On citations received, there was no difference at all.

What stayed with me was the next figure. In more than half the papers, the dataset's later users went back to the old ground rather than the new direction the paper had opened.

Reuse, for the most part, was a thing that happened once.

Papers with Code, the platform that made this sample possible, shut down last summer. The final snapshot sits in a preservation organisation, no longer updated.

So I have come to read all of this as a question not about the quality of data, but about the layer where data becomes findable.

That layer disappears most easily when it lives outside your own organisation.

If any single act of reuse has a low chance of paying off, the value has to come from being able to try often: data that can be found, a collection purpose and its conditions left legible, a licence that does not close the second use.

That is close to why we keep wanting to write reusability next to accuracy when we diagnose a dataset.

"Of the data we hold right now, how much could another team find and open for a purpose we never had in mind?"

Very few organisations can answer that with a number, and that is where this piece began.
