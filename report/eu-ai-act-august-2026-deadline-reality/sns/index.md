학습 데이터의 대표성과 편향 관리를 요구하는 Article 10을 포함한 고위험 의무는 Digital Omnibus 입법으로 2027년 12월로 이미 미뤄졌다. 연기는 논의가 아니라 법이 됐다. 그날 실제로 켜지는 스위치는 따로 있다. 챗봇·딥페이크 라벨링을 요구하는 Article 50 투명성 의무, 그리고 범용 AI(GPAI) 제공자에게 매출 3% 벌금을 물릴 수 있는 집행위의 제재 권한이다. 규칙은 1년 전부터 있었고, 이제 이빨이 돋는다.

핵심은 유예의 성격이다. Article 10이 요구하는 건 데이터가 아니라 데이터에 관한 증거다. 대표성을 확인한 기록, 라벨 품질을 잰 문서, 출처가 어디서 왔는지에 대한 계보. 이 증거는 모델을 다 학습시킨 뒤에 소급해 만들 수 없다. 학습이 끝난 다음 재구성하려 하면 그 재구성 자체가 감사관에게 적신호가 된다.

ISO 17025 시험소 인정도, GDPR 집행도 같은 패턴이었다. 행정 처리는 빠르지만 그 앞의 축적이 진짜 병목이고, 조용하던 집행은 몇 년 뒤 급증한다. 조직의 78%가 아직 손도 대지 않은 지금, 18개월은 면제가 아니라 준비 시계다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The claim that the EU AI Act's high-risk provisions take full effect on August 2 is simply wrong.

Those obligations, including Article 10 on training-data representativeness and bias, were already pushed to December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus. The delay didn't stall in debate; it became law. What actually switches on that day is different: the Article 50 transparency duties, and the Commission's newly active power to fine general-purpose AI providers up to 3% of global revenue. The rules existed a year ago. Only now do they get teeth.

The real point is what Article 10 asks for. Not data, but evidence about data. Records that representativeness was checked, that label quality was measured, that provenance was traced. None of it can be reconstructed after a model is trained. Attempt the reconstruction late, and the reconstruction itself becomes the red flag an auditor is trained to spot.

ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation and GDPR enforcement both rehearsed this. The paperwork moves fast; the accumulation behind it is the real bottleneck, and quiet enforcement tends to surge years later. With 78% of organizations yet to start, the 18-month deferral is not an exemption. It is a preparation clock.

## Facebook (EN)

"The high-risk provisions take full effect on August 2." I read some version of that line three times this week.

The truth runs almost the other way.

Article 10, the clause on training-data representativeness, was already moved to December 2027. The delay became law. What actually switches on that day is something else: transparency duties, and the Commission's power to fine general-purpose AI providers.

What stayed with me was a smaller question. How do you read a deferral?

Because Article 10 doesn't ask for data. It asks for evidence about data. Records that representativeness was checked, that labels were measured, that provenance was traced. You can't assemble those after the model is trained. They have to accumulate while the pipeline runs.

So the question turns into this:

"Is 2027 a reason to rest, or is the clock already running?"

Lab accreditation and GDPR told the same story from different angles. The paperwork clears in weeks; the thing the paperwork certifies takes a year to build. Quiet enforcement has a way of surging later.

Pebblous keeps returning to this because proving, by diagnosis, that a dataset is representative and checked for bias is exactly the evidence regulators have started to ask for.

Eighteen months sounds generous. It may already be a narrowing window.
