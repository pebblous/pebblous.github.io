## LinkedIn (KO)

KAIST 연구진이 공개 저장소에 올라온 에이전트 설정 파일 5만 3천여 건으로 직업 지도를 다시 그렸다. 2017년 자동화 위험 목록과의 겹침은 0.05였다. 두 목록을 무작위로 섞었을 때 기대되는 값보다도 낮다.

재는 것이 다르기 때문이다. 기존 지표들은 AI가 그 일에 어디까지 닿는지를 쟀다. 이번 지표는 사람이 그 일을 실제로 넘겼는지를 본다. 증거를 대화 로그가 아니라 설정 파일에서 찾은 이유가 여기에 있다. 목표와 절차와 도구를 미리 적어야 만들어지고, 한 번 만들면 반복 실행되니까.

값을 움직이는 힘도 달랐다. 도구가 그 일에 닿는지 하나로 위임 변이의 57.8%가 설명됐다. 임금과 학력을 전부 넣은 모형은 34.9%에 그쳤다.

곡선은 학사 학위 수준에서 정점을 찍고 양쪽으로 내려간다. 아래쪽 하락은 도구가 아직 그 일에 못 닿아서라고 설명되고, 그 변수를 넣는 순간 격차가 사라진다. 석사 이상 직업의 미달분만 남는다. 도구는 닿는데 넘기지 않았다는 뜻이다.

저자들은 그 이유를 고르지 않는다. 그 일이 미리 적힐 수 없는 종류이거나, 적을 수 있는데도 전문직이 자기 일이 코드화되는 속도를 늦추거나. 데이터로는 둘을 가를 수 없다고 적는다. 어느 쪽이든 갈림길에 놓인 것은 능력이 아니라 미리 적을 수 있는가다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Researchers at KAIST redrew the map of which occupations AI is taking on, using 53,000 agent configuration files that people had published to open repositories. The new map barely touches the 2017 computerisation risk list. Their Jaccard overlap is 0.05, lower than two unrelated lists would produce by chance.

The difference is in what is being measured. Existing indices ask how far AI can reach into an occupation's work. This one asks whether a worker has handed that work over. That is why the evidence is a configuration file rather than a chat log. A configuration has to be written before it runs, it names goals and steps and tools, and it executes again every time it is invoked.

What moves the number is different too. Whether a tool already reaches the work explains 57.8% of the variation in delegation on its own. Wages and education together explain 34.9%.

The curve peaks at bachelor's-degree occupations and falls away on both sides. The fall at the lower end disappears once tool availability enters the model, because the tools simply do not reach that work yet. The shortfall at master's-and-above occupations survives the control. The tools reach, and the work still is not handed over.

The authors decline to say why. Either the work cannot be specified in advance, or it can be and professionals have both the autonomy and the incentive to slow the pace at which their work is codified. Their data cannot separate constraint from choice. Either way the dividing line is not capability. It is whether the work can be written down first.

## Facebook (EN)

There are a few files like that on my own laptop.

A short document with a name and a description at the top and a procedure written out underneath. An agent reads it and does the work.

Writing the first one took time, and almost none of it went into code. It went into sentences. Setting down the order I had been doing the work in, and finding the places where judgment enters.

Researchers at KAIST gathered 53,000 files of that kind. People had written them for their own work and published them to open repositories. Out of those files the researchers drew a map of occupations.

It is not a map of what AI is able to do.

It is a map of what people have handed over.

The place my eye kept returning to was the top of it.

The curve rises, peaks somewhere around bachelor's-degree work, and then comes back down. The fall at the lower end has an explanation. The tools do not reach that work yet. The fall at the upper end happens where the tools do reach.

"Is this work that cannot be written down in advance, or work that could be and is not?"

The authors do not answer. They write that their data cannot separate the two, and they stop there.

That pause has stayed with me. It means the thing standing at the top of the map is not a question of ability. Work that has not been handed over is not hard work. It is work that has not yet been written down.

The problem Pebblous works on sits in the same place. A model can only use data, so the material has to become data first. An agent can only take on a specification, so the work has to be written down first. Work that has not been specified is not outside automation. It is at the front of the queue to become data.

Read that way, the map is not a record of jobs AI has consumed. It is closer to a line drawn between the work that has already become data and the work that has not.

How much of your own work could be written down in advance?
