## LinkedIn (EN)

Open-weight models handled 71.5% of the tokens crossing Vercel's AI Gateway in September. They took 23% of the spend. Vercel published the figures on 15 October.

Most coverage stopped there. The same report takes the 23% apart. About two-thirds of the recent growth in open-weight spending came from models priced above four times the open-weight average, led by Kimi K3. The denominator in that comparison is not closed models. It is the average of the open models themselves.

At the other end sits DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, which handled close to half of September's gateway tokens and took 2.6% of the spend. Open weight means the weights can be downloaded and run. It is a fact about a license, not about a price.

What splits the two numbers is the work rather than the camp. Classification, extraction and bulk preprocessing go down to low-priced models; long reasoning and final judgments go up to expensive ones. Of the teams that raised open-weight spending in September, 68% raised closed-weight spending in the same month. They did not switch camps. They split the work.

Anthropic's share of gateway spend fell from 63.7% in August to 43.9% in September, below half for the first time. The axis matters here: 43.9% is a share of spend, and Anthropic's September token share has never been published in any edition. Total estimated gateway spend is now about thirteen times its December 2025 level, so a falling share can sit alongside a rising dollar amount.

The edges of the sample belong in the same reading. This is traffic through one gateway, and the spend is estimated from list prices rather than invoiced. Over the last 90 days, 91.3% of token volume came from accounts billed in the United States and 65.6% from startups. The 71.5% is not an industry-wide ratio.

Pebblous puts numbers on the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Splitting work by price tier comes down to one question in practice, which is whether a given job can go to something cheaper, and answering it takes data with known answers for that job plus an evaluation set that shows what breaks when the job moves down.

In September alone, 48.0% of teams changed the model they use most. In a market where the model changes month to month, the evaluation set outlives the model.
