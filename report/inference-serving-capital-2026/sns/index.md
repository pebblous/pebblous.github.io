물론 하이퍼스케일러가 서빙을 흡수하면 프리미엄은 얇아집니다. 그런데 그 압력의 반대편 결론조차 하나로 모입니다. 서빙이 상품화될수록 마지막까지 남는 차별점은 어떤 데이터로 특화했느냐입니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

When open-weight models closed the gap with frontier labs and token prices collapsed, venture money stopped chasing who trains the next model and started backing who runs everyone else's.

Fireworks, an inference-serving startup, saw its valuation climb from $4B to $17.5B in nine months. Baseten reached $13B in five. Three weeks ago we wrote about the GPU-rental layer below them; this is the same capital climbing one rung higher up the stack.

The paradox is that per-token prices have fallen by high-90s percent in three years. Money flows in anyway, because volume overwhelms the price cut.

The number that matters came from the operators themselves. Of the 40 trillion tokens Fireworks serves daily, more than 95% run on models fine-tuned on each customer's own data, not on generic open weights. When weights are free, the value is in what you tuned them with.

Hyperscalers absorbing the serving layer will thin the premium. Yet even that bearish case converges on one point: the last defensible moat is whose data specialized the model.

그때는 그게 자본이 내려갈 수 있는 가장 아래층인 줄 알았습니다.

그런데 이번 여름, 같은 돈이 그 위층으로 다시 올라왔습니다. 남이 만든 모델을 실제로 굴려 API로 내주는 '추론 서빙' 회사들입니다. 그중 Fireworks는 아홉 달 만에 밸류에이션이 네 배 넘게 뛰었습니다.

이상한 일입니다. 토큰 값은 3년 사이 거의 바닥까지 떨어졌는데, 정작 그 값을 파는 쪽으로 돈이 몰립니다.

한참 눈이 머문 건 서빙사 자신이 내놓은 한 문장이었습니다. 하루 굴리는 40조 개의 토큰 가운데 95% 이상이 범용 모델이 아니라, 각 고객이 자기 데이터로 튜닝한 모델에서 나온다는 것.

저희가 데이터 품질을 오래 붙들어 온 이유가, 요즘 이 질문 앞에서 다시 선명해집니다.

## Facebook (EN)

A few weeks ago I wrote about a company that rents out GPUs by the hour, and how it had just been valued at $8.3 billion.

I thought that was the bottom floor, the lowest rung capital could reach.

This summer the same money climbed back up a level, into the companies that actually run other people's models and hand them back as an API. One of them, Fireworks, more than quadrupled its valuation in nine months.

Which is strange, because the price of a token has fallen almost to the floor over three years. The money moves toward the thing that keeps getting cheaper.

What stopped me was a single line the operators offered themselves. Of the 40 trillion tokens they serve each day, more than 95% come not from a generic model but from models each customer tuned on their own data.

So in a world where the weights are free, the value was never in the model. It was in what you filled it with.

'Once serving becomes ordinary, how wide does the gap grow between inference grounded in fresh, verified data and inference that isn't?'

That question is why the work we've long done on data quality feels, lately, a little more legible.
