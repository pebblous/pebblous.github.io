## LinkedIn (KO)

AMD가 82억 달러에 사들인 월드랩스의 로봇 학습 엔진은 월드랩스가 만든 것이 아니다.

69일 전에 월드랩스가 인수한 SceniX라는 회사가 만들었고, 그 사실을 적어 둔 문서는 월드랩스 자신의 블로그다. AMD 보도자료는 인수 자산 목록 맨 끝에 "로봇 학습과 시뮬레이션 기술"이라고만 적었다. 이 거래를 다룬 주요 매체 어디에도 SceniX라는 이름은 나오지 않는다.

그 엔진이 7월에 내놓은 결과는 실측 학습 데이터 없이 시뮬레이션에서 배운 정책이 실물 로봇 다섯 종으로 그대로 옮겨 갔다는 것이다. 발표문 본문에는 성공률이 한 개도 적혀 있지 않다. 숫자는 본문 중간에 걸린 차트 이미지의 눈금과 범례 안에 들어 있다. 범례를 열면 평가 대상이 엔비디아와 피지컬 인텔리전스의 모델 둘이라는 사실도 나오는데, 본문은 그 두 이름도 적지 않았다.

평가 프로토콜은 체크포인트당 시뮬레이션 2,000회와 실물 100회다. 실물 쪽 100회가 성공률에 남기는 오차는 ±5.9 %p다.

그림 끝에서 두 체크포인트의 실물 성공률 차이는 2 %p인데, 그 둘을 통계적으로 가르려면 8.7 %p가 필요하다. 유망한 정책을 걸러내는 데는 넉넉한 표본이고, 학습이 정체됐는지를 판정하는 데는 모자란 표본이다.

시뮬 순위가 실물 순위를 보존하는지를 정량으로 재는 지표는 2024년에 이미 나와 있다. SIMPLER의 MMRV다. 그 논문의 공저자 한 사람은 페이페이 리와 함께 월드 생성 평가 벤치마크를 쓴 시니어 저자이기도 하다. 그런데 월드랩스 발표문을 전수 검색하면 benchmark도 baseline도 0회 나온다.

AMD 보도자료에는 "열린 AI 생태계"라는 표현이 세 번 있다. 엔비디아는 Cosmos 3에서 코드와 모델 체크포인트, 합성 데이터셋, 평가 벤치마크까지 라이선스와 함께 열었고, 월드랩스의 Atlas는 아직 선별 파트너 신청제다. 생성기를 만드는 쪽이 채점표까지 쥐면, 비는 자리는 독립적으로 측정하는 자리다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The robot learning engine AMD just bought for $8.2 billion was not built by World Labs.

It came from SceniX, a company World Labs acquired 69 days earlier, and the document that says so is World Labs' own blog. The AMD press release ends its list of acquired assets with the phrase "technology for robotic learning and simulation" and stops there. None of the major outlets covering the deal named SceniX at all.

What that engine produced went up in July: policies trained without real-world data that transferred to five physical robot platforms. The write-up contains no success rates. The numbers are in the chart image embedded halfway down the page, on the axes and in the legend. Open the legend and you also learn that the policies being evaluated were NVIDIA's and Physical Intelligence's. The body text names neither.

The evaluation protocol is 2,000 simulated trials and 100 real trials per checkpoint. Those hundred real trials leave a margin of ±5.9 percentage points on the success rate.

At the end of the curve, two checkpoints differ by 2 points on real hardware, and separating them would take 8.7. That sample is generous for screening out weak policies and short for deciding whether learning has plateaued.

A quantitative instrument for exactly this claim has existed since 2024: MMRV, from the SIMPLER paper. One of its co-authors is also the senior author of a world-generation benchmark written with Fei-Fei Li. Search the World Labs write-up in full and "benchmark" returns zero hits, as does "baseline."

The AMD release uses the phrase "open AI ecosystem" three times. NVIDIA shipped Cosmos 3 with its code, model checkpoints, curated synthetic datasets and evaluation benchmark under a named license. World Labs' Atlas remains application-gated to selected partners. When the party building the generator also holds the scorecard, the missing seat is the one that measures independently.

## Facebook (EN)

I downloaded the chart image and zoomed in.

The World Labs robot write-up from July does not contain a single success rate. But enlarge the figure sitting halfway down the page and the axes carry gridlines at intervals of 0.2, and the legend carries the names of the two policies being evaluated. The prose never calls those names once.

The chart was saying what the text would not.

On September 28, AMD announced it was buying the company for $8.2 billion. Most of the coverage filed it as a chipmaker hiring an AI star.

Read the press release to its end and the list of acquired assets closes with technology for robotic learning and simulation. The name of the company that built that technology appears only on World Labs' own blog. SceniX, bought 69 days earlier.

On a second reading the problem was not that the numbers sat inside a picture. It was that nobody outside can measure them again.

An instrument for checking whether simulated evaluation preserves real-world rankings has existed since 2024. It came out of the lab where Fei-Fei Li worked, and one of its co-authors did research alongside the founder of the robot engine now heading into AMD, during that founder's postdoc at Stanford. There was a ruler. The people who made it were inside the building. It went unused.

"Is this generated world good enough? And who, other than the people who generated it, gets to check?"

The slow part of data quality work at Pebblous runs into the same wall. With collected data there is an original to filter. When the floor a robot walks on and the lighting it stumbles under arrive as model output rather than as a recording, the order reverses. There is no original to filter, and the thing produced is the original. That is where the definition of a defect has to be written before DataClinic can diagnose one.

When the party building the generator also holds the scorecard, the seat left empty is the one that measures independently.

Which is a seat no document in this deal has assigned yet.
