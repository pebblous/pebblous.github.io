## LinkedIn (KO)

감사원이 8년간 1조 6328억 원이 들어간 공공 AI 학습데이터를 점검했습니다. 정부가 이미 만든 데이터 위에 지자체와 공공기관이 같은 데이터를 다시 만들고 있었습니다.

대전 서구가 새로 만든 생활폐기물 이미지 9000장 중 5200장은 이미 공개돼 있던 것과 유사했습니다. 개인정보보호위원회가 따로 만든 구강 이미지는 1000장 전부가 유사했습니다.

중복은 원인이 아니라 결과입니다. AI 허브에 공개된 데이터가 908종, 26개 기관이 자체 포털에 올린 데이터가 313종인데 두 목록은 한자리에서 검색되지 않습니다. 없으니까 만든다는 판단이 틀린 것이 아니라, 판단의 재료가 없었습니다.

품질도 구조가 같습니다. 구축 실적 상위 20개 기관 중 9곳은 제3자 검증 없이 데이터를 공개했고, 조사된 데이터의 84.2%는 대조할 규격서가 없어 품질검사 자체가 성립하지 않았습니다. 불합격률이 아니라 채점 불가능 비율입니다. ISO/IEC 5259는 감사가 진행되던 해에 이미 발행돼 있었으니, 없는 것은 규범이 아니라 주체였습니다.

한국만의 문제도 아닙니다. 미국 감사원도 연방기관 23곳의 AI 활용 목록을 점검해 5곳만 온전했다고 보고했습니다. 차이는 문제의 유무가 아니라, EU 포털처럼 등록 시점에 기계가 메타데이터 규격을 검사하는 장치가 있느냐입니다. 한국은 4년치를 사람이 표본으로 셌습니다.

공공 데이터를 무료라는 이유로 검증 없이 파이프라인에 넣어 온 쪽이라면, 내려받기 전에 확인할 항목이 이 감사 결과 안에 그대로 들어 있습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Korea's Board of Audit and Inspection examined ₩1.63 trillion of public AI training data built between 2017 and 2024. It found agencies rebuilding datasets the central government had already published.

One district in Daejeon commissioned 9,000 household waste images, and 5,200 of them resembled data already in the public repository. A set of 1,000 dental images built by the Personal Information Protection Commission matched an existing collection in full.

Duplication here is a symptom rather than a cause. Korea's AI Hub lists 908 datasets; another 313 sit on the portals of 26 individual public bodies, and the two lists cannot be searched together. An agency that looks for an existing dataset, finds nothing and commissions its own has not made a bad call. It has made a call without material.

The quality findings have the same shape. Of the twenty largest builders, nine released data with no third-party verification. In the sample examined, 84.2% of datasets had no syntax specification, so inspection could not run at all. ISO/IEC 5259 was published while the audit was under way. What was absent was not a rule but an owner.

Nor is this a Korean anomaly. The US GAO reviewed AI inventories at 23 federal agencies and found only five complete. The difference is not whether the problem occurs but whether anything catches it continuously. The EU's open data portal validates metadata against a schema at registration; Korea counted by hand, four years later.

Anyone pulling public datasets into a training pipeline because they are free will find the pre-download checklist already written into this audit.

## Facebook (EN)

Have you ever searched a government data portal for something you needed?

I have heard the same ending more than once. Nothing came up, so the team decided to build it themselves.

That was the scene I kept returning to while reading the audit Korea's Board of Audit and Inspection released this month.

One district in Daejeon built 9,000 household waste images. Of those, 5,200 resembled images that had already been published. The same held for the wildlife images built by Korea Expressway Corporation, and for the 1,000 dental images built by the Personal Information Protection Commission, which matched an existing set in full.

Before writing this off as carelessness, one detail is worth sitting with.

Korea's AI Hub lists 908 datasets. Another 313 sit on the portals of 26 individual public bodies.

The two lists cannot be searched in one place.

So if someone looked, found nothing, and decided to build, that judgment was not wrong. What was missing was not the judgment but the material for it. I have started thinking of this as the problem of the invisible inventory. Duplication is what that invisibility produces. It is a result, not a cause.

The quality findings repeat the shape. In the sample examined, 84.2% of datasets had not failed inspection. There was no specification to check them against, so no inspection could take place. The standards were not the missing part. ISO/IEC 5259 was already published in the year the audit was running.

"The rules exist. Who is supposed to apply them?"

When that question has no assigned answer, the verdict arrives four years later, in an audit. That is roughly the ground Pebblous has been standing on. Not the side that builds more data, but the side that judges whether the data already in hand is in a usable state.

What makes data unusable is rarely the data. More often it is the one line that was never attached to it.
