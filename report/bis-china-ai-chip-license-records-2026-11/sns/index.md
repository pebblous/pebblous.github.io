미 하원이 공개한 대중국 AI 칩 수출 허가 기록에서, 승인된 스물한 줄의 상태 칸은 '승인'에서 끝나지 않는다. 뒤에 '아직 출하되지 않음'이 붙어 있다.

하원 외교위원회가 11월 19일에 내놓은 네 쪽짜리 표다. 줄은 54개이고, 한 줄마다 언제 들어온 신청인지, 누가 내고 누가 받는지, 칩이 몇 개이고 지금 어떤 상태인지가 적혀 있다. 제목에 박힌 기준일은 공개일이 아니라 올해 5월 11일이다.

승인은 21건 87만 9868개인데, 그날까지 배를 탄 것은 한 개도 없다. 미국이 허가를 내줬고, 중국이 본토 반입을 건별 승인으로 묶었고, 그다음 엔비디아가 출하를 멈췄다. 세 단계가 차례로 겹쳐 있다.

"절반 넘게 거부됐다"는 요약은 숫자만 맞힌다. 거부는 표 전체에서 한 건이고, 칩을 가장 많이 걷어낸 칸의 이름은 무대응 반려다. 돌려보낸 신청은 보완해 다시 낼 수 있다.

따로 세어 볼 자리도 있다. 공개 다음 날부터 널리 복사된 기업별 배분표는 14줄인데 원본의 승인 줄은 18개다. 더해 보면 네 줄이 빠져 있고, 가장 작은 줄은 여덟 개짜리였다.

승인율도 하나로 나오지 않는다. 칩 수로 세면 25.5%, 신청 건수로 세면 38.9%다. 어느 쪽도 틀리지 않았고, 어느 단위로 센 값인지 적지 않으면 둘 다 떠돈다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Twenty-one export licenses for AI chips bound for China were approved. For every one of them the status column carries two more words after that: not shipped.

The House Foreign Affairs Committee published the table on 19 November, four pages covering 54 applications. Each row carries a submission date, an exporter, a receiving party, a chip count and a status. The date in the title is not the date of release. It is 11 May 2026, and six months sit in that gap.

Across the 21 approvals the chips come to 879,868, and by that cutoff not one had left. Washington issued the licenses. Beijing then held mainland imports behind case-by-case clearance. Nvidia has since stopped shipping the part altogether.

The summary to avoid is that most of the applications were refused. There is exactly one rejection in the table. What cleared out the most chips is a different column, returned without action, and an application returned that way can be corrected and filed again.

One more thing is worth adding up. The company-by-company tally that circulated after the release runs to 14 rows, while the original has 18 approval rows. Four rows are missing from it. The smallest of them was eight chips.

Ask for the approval rate and there are two answers. By chips it is 25.5%. By applications it is 38.9%. Neither is wrong, and whichever one travels without its unit will be read as the other.

Move a record that carries status into a number that does not, and status is the first thing to go.

## Facebook (EN)

There is a date in the title of the four-page table. 11 May 2026.

The table was made public on 19 November. Six months sit between those two days.

The House Foreign Affairs Committee had released the Commerce Department's record of China AI chip export licenses, application by application, and outside the document it was summarized as 879,000 approved.

One line travelled further than the rest. That 30.9% of the approved chips were destined for four companies the Pentagon lists as Chinese military companies.

That line is not read off one table. It is assembled from two.

One is the license status table, current to 11 May. The other is the designation list, dated 8 June. Three of the four companies entered on the later version. The two tables are four weeks out of step with each other.

And before the dates there is a plainer question. Do the two keys mean the same thing? A designation closes the Pentagon's procurement door. A license opens an export one. Names that rhyme are not names that match.

The ranking moves in the same place.

Take only those four companies and Tencent sits at the top of them. Read the whole table and ByteDance was approved for the same 150,000, with Didi behind it.

Carry a ranking built from the narrow table over to the wide one and those two rows are gone.

"When we put two tables side by side, do we check first that they are current to the same day?"
