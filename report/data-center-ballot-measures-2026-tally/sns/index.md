같은 표를 두 번 세면 머리기사가 둘 나온다.

2026년 11월 3일 미국 중간선거 투표용지에 데이터센터를 묻는 안건이 마흔한 건 올랐다. 가결과 부결만 세면 27 대 14다. 전국 매체가 쓴 숫자는 40 대 1이었다. 두 수는 다른 표에서 나온 것이 아니라 같은 마흔한 줄에서 나온다.

사이에 든 것은 칸 하나다. 추적 기관은 줄마다 그 안건의 찬성표가 데이터센터를 막자는 쪽인지 짓게 하자는 쪽인지를 손으로 적어 둔다. 그 칸을 얹으면 부결 열넷 가운데 열셋이 제한 쪽 승리로 넘어간다. 허용하자는 안건이 한 건도 가결되지 않았기 때문이다. 투표함이 적은 것은 가·부뿐이고, 방향은 사람이 얹은 값이다.

목록 쪽도 하나가 아니다. Ballotpedia는 그날을 41건으로, Redbud 트래커는 47건으로 적는다. 차이 여섯 건은 기준 다툼이 아니라 소도시 안건의 누락이다. 원인은 배관이었다. 주 차원 집계처가 없어 오하이오 결과를 카운티 선거관리위원회 열다섯 곳에서 하나씩 긁어 와야 한다.

두 곳 모두 자기 범위를 "13개 주"라고 적는데, 그 열셋이 같은 열셋이 아니다. 한쪽에는 아칸소가, 다른 쪽에는 조지아가 들어 있다.

더 조용한 쪽은 방법론 문서에 있었다. 선거 사흘 전인 10월 31일, 한쪽 트래커가 상대 목록에서 여섯 줄을 받아 자기 등록부를 채웠다고 적혀 있다. 그 여섯 줄에 노스다코타 하우드가 들어 있다. "40 대 1"의 그 1이다. 두 집계처가 같은 수를 말할 때, 두 번 센 것인지 한 번 세고 물려받은 것인지는 수만 봐서는 갈리지 않는다.

원자료를 받아 네 번 따로 셌고 네 번 같은 값이 나왔다. 셈이 꼼꼼해서가 아니라 등록부와 개표 파일과 방법론 문서가 다 공개돼 있어서 가능한 일이었다. 페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 행 단위로 진단한다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Count the same table twice and you get two headlines.

Forty-one data center questions reached U.S. ballots on November 3, 2026. Count the passes and the defeats and the result is 27 to 14. The number that travelled was 40 to 1. Both come out of the same forty-one rows.

What sits between them is one column. Trackers write down by hand, for each row, whether a yes vote on that measure would restrain data center development or enable it. Add the column and thirteen of the fourteen defeats turn into wins for the restricting side, because not one of the measures that would have eased development passed. The ballot box recorded pass or fail. The direction was supplied by a person.

The list is not single either. Ballotpedia logs the day as 41 measures, the Redbud tracker as 47. The six-row gap is not a dispute over definitions but missing small-town measures, and the cause is plumbing: with no statewide feed for local questions, Ohio results have to be pulled from fifteen county boards one at a time.

Both organisations describe their scope as thirteen states, and the thirteen are not the same thirteen. One list holds Arkansas, the other Georgia.

The quieter finding was in the methodology file. Three days before the election, on October 31, one tracker filled six rows of its registry from the other's list. Harwood, North Dakota is one of those six. It is the "1" in 40 to 1. When two tallies agree, the agreement by itself does not say whether they counted twice or whether one inherited from the other.

We pulled the raw files and counted four separate times, and the four counts agreed. That was not diligence. The registry, the results file and the methodology notes were all published. Pebblous goes over the quality of AI training data with DataClinic.

## Facebook (EN)

Four hundred and thirty people voted on the measure in Harwood, North Dakota.

One hundred and five for, three hundred and twenty-five against.

That is 0.17 percent of the quarter of a million votes cast on data center questions across the United States that day.

By the following morning, in a line that travelled everywhere, this one ballot box had become "the only place where the data center side won."

Open the Harwood ballot and the words data center never appear. What voters rejected was not a ban. It was a procedural amendment that would have required independent review and a public hearing before any large industrial project could be approved.

For that single word, rejected, to become a win for the data center side, whoever is keeping the tally has to add one more column to the row. It is a column that was never printed on any ballot.

A column outside the ballot box.

All forty-one rows carry it, and that is where 27 to 14 becomes 40 to 1.

One person filled that column in. Whether a second reader would have written the same label into the same row was never checked against anything. On a volunteer-scale operation running on election night, that is the ordinary case rather than the lapse.

Two organisations tallied that day. One of them records in its methodology notes that three days before the election it filled six empty rows of its registry from the other's list. Harwood is among those six.

The two tallies agreed. The agreement was not the product of counting twice.

"When two sources write down the same value, where do you look to find out whether they counted separately?"
