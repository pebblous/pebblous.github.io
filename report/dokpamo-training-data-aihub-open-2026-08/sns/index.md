## LinkedIn (KO)

8월 27일 과학기술정보통신부와 NIA가 「독자 AI 파운데이션 모델」 정예팀들이 국비로 만든 학습데이터 29종을 AI허브에 열었다. 창구에 가 보면 목록에 놓여 있는 것은 22건이다. 누락은 아니었다. 보도자료 붙임은 데이터셋을 형식별로 쪼개 행을 세었고, 창구는 데이터셋 단위로 등재했다. 다섯 팀 모두 정확히 대응한다.

어긋나는 곳은 다른 데 있었다. 같은 데이터셋의 건수와 용량과 형식을 두 공식 기록이 서로 다르게 적는다. 민원 상담 음성 데이터는 창구 머리 필드에 「3,434시간」으로 적혀 있는데, 같은 페이지 아래쪽 통계는 음성이 514시간이라고 밝힌다. 3,434는 시간이 아니라 전사 파일의 개수다.

총량도 무엇을 받게 되는지는 알려 주지 않는다. 11TB 가운데 82.8%가 영상 두 건에 몰려 있고, 반대로 개방 이틀째 조회가 가장 많이 몰린 것은 목록 반대편의 작은 텍스트들이었다. 다 합쳐도 용량의 1%가 안 된다.

그리고 스물두 건 어디에도 이용조건이 적혀 있지 않다. 이용허락이나 라이선스를 적어 두는 칸 자체가 없다. 만든 기관의 담당자 이름과 전화번호는 스물두 건 모두에 적혀 있는데, 무슨 조건으로 쓸 수 있는지는 한 줄도 없다. 안심존을 거쳐야 하는 안전성 데이터 두 건은 그 신청을 심사하고 승인하는 주체가 데이터를 만든 기업 자신이다.

공개 데이터셋은 총량으로 도착하지 않는다. 항목으로 도착하고, 항목마다 조건이 다르다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

On 27 August, Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT and the National Information Society Agency opened 29 training datasets built with public money by the elite teams of the country's Sovereign AI Foundation Model project. The portal that hosts them lists 22. Nothing is missing. The annex to the press release counted rows split by format; the portal registers datasets whole. All five teams reconcile exactly.

The divergence sits somewhere else. Where the two official records describe the same dataset, they give different counts, different sizes and different formats. A speech dataset drawn from public-service call centres carries "3,434 hours" in the portal's headline field, while the statistics further down that same page report 514 hours of audio. The 3,434 is a count of transcript files.

Nor does the total tell you what you would be taking. Two video datasets hold 82.8% of the volume, while the items that drew the most traffic in the opening days sit at the other end of the list, together under one percent.

And nowhere across the 22 detail pages is there a licence or any statement of what the data may be used for. The name and phone number of the responsible engineer appear on every one of them. For the two safety datasets, which carry an application button rather than a download button, the party that reviews and approves that application is the company that built the data.

An open dataset does not arrive as a total. It arrives as items, and the conditions differ item by item.

## Facebook (EN)

"3,434 hours."

That is what sat at the top of the page describing a speech dataset collected from public-service call centres. Scroll a little further down the same page and a statistics table reports 514 hours of audio.

The 3,434 was never a duration. It was the number of transcript files.

A few days ago the Korean government opened a body of AI training data built with public money. Any Korean national, the announcement said, could download it free of charge. The announcement arrived as three totals, and each of them was internally correct.

But what someone assembling a corpus needs is not a total. It is the unit, the size and the terms of each item. That is exactly where the two official records parted ways.

"Is 'opened' the same word as 'usable'?"

Across all 22 datasets there was no statement of terms. There is no field for a licence at all. The name and phone number of the engineer responsible appear on every one of them.

What we do at Pebblous with a dataset is plain enough. We open it, count what is actually there, reconcile the units, and leave the result in a ledger. This time we ran that procedure not on a dataset but on the portal itself.

Doing so left me thinking that there is still an empty seat here, one you might call acceptance inspection. Measuring how much was opened, and how quickly, is a mature practice. The layer above it, whether what was opened arrived in a state anyone can actually use, has no agreed name and no agreed measure.

If publicly funded training data is going to keep arriving at this window, naming that empty seat seems likely to matter more, not less, as the openings multiply.
