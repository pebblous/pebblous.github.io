소장을 열면 다투는 자리가 설계와 수집입니다. 몬태나 소장 ¶258(c)가 위 설계 조항으로 걸고, 네브래스카의 연령 적합 온라인 설계 코드법은 규율 대상 기능에 이름을 붙여 목록으로 둡니다. 처음 그 목록은 무한 스크롤과 푸시 알림 같은 소셜미디어의 어휘였는데, 7월 17일 발효한 개정이 "사람과 이야기하고 있다는 착각을 통해 이용을 늘리면서 친밀감을 끌어내려 하는 기능"을 그 옆에 넣었습니다. 켜진 날로부터 제소까지 89일입니다. 다만 네브래스카 소장 원문은 구하지 못했고, 소장이 그 호를 집었는지는 확인하지 못했습니다.

벌금을 세는 단위도 발언이 아니라 수집 한 건입니다. 몬태나 소장 ¶261은 미성년의 데이터를 법에 어긋나게 수집할 때마다 그것이 별개의 위반이 된다고 적습니다. 소장들이 요구하는 단가는 위반 한 건에 5천 달러부터 5만 달러까지 벌어지는데, 같은 회사의 같은 기능인데도 주 경계를 넘으면 가격표가 열 배가 됩니다. 그러면서 어느 소장도 그 건수가 실제로 몇 건이었는지는 적지 않습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

"Keeping young users engaged longer" reads like a phrase a reporter chose. Open Montana's privacy act and it is sitting there in the statute: using a system design feature to significantly increase, sustain, or extend a minor's use. Without consent, that is prohibited.

On October 14 attorneys general in several US states sued OpenAI, each in their own state court. Even the count of states differs by announcement. The Arkansas attorney general's office lists nine by including Utah, the wire stories say eight, and the Utah attorney general's newsroom carries no such announcement. What is confirmed is that the lists disagree.

Open the complaints and the ground being fought over is design and collection. Montana pleads that clause at ¶258(c), and Nebraska's Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act names its covered design features and enumerates them. That list began in the vocabulary of social media and games, infinite scroll and push notifications. An amendment effective July 17 set beside them "a feature that increases usage through the illusion of talking with a human being that seeks to elicit feelings of intimacy from the user." Eighty-nine days separate that day from the filings. We never obtained the Nebraska complaint, though, so whether it picks up that subsection is unconfirmed.

The multiplier on the penalty is not a statement either. It is a single act of collection. Montana's complaint at ¶261 says that every single time minors' data is collected in violation of the privacy act, that is a separate violation. The per-violation figures sought run from $5,000 to $50,000, so the same feature in the same product carries a price tag ten times higher once you cross a state line. And no complaint says how many instances there actually were.

Where the evidence comes from is the substance of this case. The age labels built to screen children out are cited in the Arkansas complaint as proof the company knew, and the model evaluation scores it published voluntarily are cited in the West Virginia complaint as a half-truth for not having been disclosed sooner. Measure, and the measurement becomes a disclosure item; skip it, and failing to run a data protection impact assessment is itself one of Montana's counts. Every allegation here is the plaintiffs' and none of the cases has reached an answer. Pebblous diagnoses the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. The record these complaints ask for sits outside that diagnostic sheet: what was collected from minor users, when, and whether you can count it out one instance at a time.

## Facebook (EN)

On August 18 OpenAI released a version of ChatGPT for thirteen- to seventeen-year-olds, kept separate from the rest.

No one is asked to verify their age up front. The stated age is the starting point, read alongside account history and usage patterns, and minors are picked out and routed over automatically. A path to confirm age with a face photo opens only when an adult has been caught by mistake.

It is a device built to screen children out.

On October 14, that device turned up in a complaint.

Attorneys general in several US states sued OpenAI that day, each in their own state court. The Arkansas complaint says the company maintains data classifying users by stated, predicted and verified age, and offers that classification as evidence it collected while knowing.

A label built to screen children out becomes proof that they were recognized.

The other side is not empty either. In October 2025 OpenAI measured and published, on its own initiative, how its models respond in sensitive conversations. The West Virginia complaint calls the failure to disclose those figures sooner a half-truth, and says a half-truth is deception.

Nor is declining to measure the safe option. One of the nine branches Montana pleads under its privacy act is the failure to conduct a data protection impact assessment.

Measure, and the measurement becomes something you were obliged to disclose. Do not measure, and the absence of measurement becomes evidence that due care was not taken.

There is no quiet seat to take in a structure like that.

Look at the unit the penalty counts in and the position gets sharper. Montana's complaint says that every single time minors' data is collected in violation of the privacy act, that is a separate violation. The multiplier is not users and not messages. It is how many times you gathered.

"Could you pull up, as a single number, how many collections you made from minor users last month?"

Our DataClinic outputs still have no proper field recording where a record came from, when it arrived, and whether it can be deleted now. We have never pulled the records of minor users apart and counted them.

Korea has no clause aimed at that seat yet. The six dark-pattern types its e-commerce act prohibits all point at design that makes people spend more. Nothing points at design that makes them stay longer, and the shutdown law that once regulated time spent directly was repealed in 2021.

Everything above is what the plaintiffs wrote in their complaints, and none of the cases has reached an answer.

And while that clause stays empty, the records of minor users keep accumulating, one day at a time.
