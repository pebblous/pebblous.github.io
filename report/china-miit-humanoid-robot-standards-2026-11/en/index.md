# China

_Training-data and grading rules apply now; only summaries are public_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT, 工业和信息化部) has put five humanoid robot standards into force on the day it announced them, yet the text of the standards is not available through any official channel. On 16 November 2026, MIIT Notice No. 30 of 2026 approved 274 industry standards. Items 118 to 122 in the notice's annex are the humanoid set: training-ground construction (SJ/T 12353), training data management (SJ/T 12354), intelligence capability grading (SJ/T 12355), electrically driven integrated joints (SJ/T 12356) and dexterous hands (SJ/T 12357). The effective-date column reads "in force from the date of publication" for all five. The only primary material anyone can read today is the notice itself and a summary of two or three sentences per standard.

> The L0-to-L5 intelligence scale is not in the annex. The annex says only that SJ/T 12355 specifies a capability structure, a grading framework, grade definitions and an assessment method. The four assessment dimensions (perception, decision-making and learning, execution, collaboration and interaction) and the six levels come from a report in The Paper (澎湃新闻) that opens by crediting the secretariat of the standards committee, MIIT/TC8. The sentences describing the levels carry no attribution of their own. No source defines L1 through L4 or says how they are tested. A separate document, the association standard T/CIE 298-2025 issued in May 2025 by the Chinese Institute of Electronics (中国电子学会), uses the same four dimensions but has five levels, L1 to L5. If a quote for a Chinese-built robot says "L3", the first questions are which document, which edition, and who did the grading.

> For teams that build or audit robot learning data, the standard with the most direct bearing is SJ/T 12354. The annex lists seven lifecycle stages, from collection to destruction. Chinese press coverage lists eleven, adding annotation, quality evaluation, security and compliance, and continuous improvement. Even before the clauses appear, a supplier can organize collection conditions, lineage, usage rights and quality measurements into the evidence forms that ISO/IEC 5259-2, -3 and -4 describe in their public sections. There is no basis for saying the Chinese and ISO standards map onto each other or are mutually recognized.

## MIIT Notice No. 30 puts five humanoid standards in force on the day of publication

MIIT's Department of Science and Technology (科技司) issued [Notice No. 30 of 2026](https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/gg/art/2026/art_eb2325cb1a844fe99958eb8d104a34fc.html) on 16 November 2026; the document itself is dated 10 November. It approves 274 industry standards: 92 in electronics, 65 in telecommunications, 41 in light industry, 28 in textiles, 23 in machinery and 18 in automotive, among others. The body of the notice does not name the humanoid standards, give their numbers, or say when they take effect.

Those details sit in Annex 1, 「274项行业标准编号、名称及主要内容等一览表」 (a table of numbers, titles and main contents of the 274 industry standards). It is a 79-page scanned PDF with no extractable text, so we rendered pages 34 and 35 as images and read them. The table has six columns: item number, standard number, title, main contents, standard replaced, and effective date. The five humanoid standards are items 118 to 122, where the electronics section begins.

### 1.1. Annex 1, items 118–122: the effective-date column says 「自发布之日起实施」

For all five, the "standard replaced" column is empty and the effective-date column carries the same phrase.

In force from the date of publication
                        自发布之日起实施 · Annex 1, pp. 34–35, items 118–122, 实施日期 column ([annex PDF](https://www.miit.gov.cn/cms_files/filemanager/1226211233/attach/202611/6d2b9d84f80c435fb9c2feff84529863.pdf))

So the five are new standards, and they took effect with the announcement on 16 November 2026. Chinese press reports say the same: 「均自发布之日起实施」 (all in force from the date of publication). Other rows in the same annex carry specific dates instead. Among the rows we checked, items 1 to 3 take effect on 1 February 2027 and 1 September 2027, item 116 on 1 November 2028, and item 117 on 1 February 2027. We did not count how many of the 274 share the "from publication" wording.

The annex gives each standard two or three sentences. They say what the standard covers and where it applies; they contain no required values and no test conditions. Here is what the annex says about the five.

Three phrases in the scope column matter in practice. ### 1.2. SJ/T means a recommended electronics-industry standard, with no procurement use found yet

The SJ prefix marks a Chinese electronics-industry standard, and /T marks it as recommended (推荐性) rather than mandatory. What the notice settles is approval, number, title, summary and effective date. As of 28 November 2026 we found no case of these five being cited as a requirement in government procurement, subsidies or certification review. Nor did we find any public statement that a product had been tested or graded against them.

According to Chinese press reports, the committee responsible is MIIT's technical committee for humanoid robot and embodied intelligence standardization (MIIT/TC8). It was set up on 26 December 2025, and its secretariat is housed at the Chinese Institute of Electronics. MIIT's November 2023 《人形机器人创新发展指导意见》 (Guiding Opinions on the Innovative Development of Humanoid Robots) promised to speed up 「国家标准、行业标准和团体标准的研制」 (the development of national, industry and association standards). These five fall into the industry-standard category that document anticipated.

The training grounds that SJ/T 12353 addresses already exist in numbers, but the counts vary by source. Science and Technology Daily (科技日报), running a CCTV News app (央视新闻客户端) story on 19 August 2026, put it at more than 70 in operation and more than 40 under construction or planned. Roundups by other outlets later that month said about 100. The two figures use different counting bases, so we neither add nor compare them.

### 1.3. Where the source texts stand: no final full text for any of the five

We looked for final full texts in five official places: the notice, its annex, the committee's website ([miittc8.cie.org.cn](https://miittc8.cie.org.cn/)), the National Public Service Platform for Standards Information (std.samr.gov.cn) and the Industry Standards Information Service Platform (hbba.sacinfo.org.cn). The committee site's project, plan, report and release pages all showed 「暂无数据」 (no data yet). The national platform returned nothing for SJ/T 12354 or 12355. The industry platform loads its search results dynamically, and we could not retrieve the results table, so whether the standards are listed there remains unconfirmed. We found no sales page or price either. We did not pay for access or work around any access restriction.

## The L0–L5 grades in SJ/T 12355 come from a report in The Paper that opens with a secretariat source

On 17 November 2026, the day after the notice, Yu Yan (喻琰) of The Paper reported the release of the five standards, crediting that news to the secretariat of MIIT/TC8. Later in the same article she set out the four assessment dimensions of SJ/T 12355 and its L0–L5 framework. The sentences describing the levels carry no attribution of their own. Of the articles we checked, this is the earliest to carry the framework.

Built around four dimensions (perception and cognition, decision-making and learning, execution, and collaboration and interaction), it establishes an intelligence capability grading framework running from L0 to L5.
                        围绕感知认知、决策学习、执行表现和协作交互四个维度，建立从L0到L5的智能化能力分级框架 · 澎湃新闻, 2026-11-17 (NetEase repost; the secretariat is credited only in the article's opening sentence)

The annex row for SJ/T 12355 contains none of these numbers. It says the standard specifies 「架构、分级框架、等级定义及评定方法」 (a structure, a grading framework, grade definitions and an assessment method). It does not say how many levels there are or what the dimensions are. All the annex establishes is that grade definitions and an assessment method exist inside the standard.

### 2.1. From "preset execution" to "self-evolution": Sina Finance's account of 19 November

Sina Finance (新浪财经) filled in the two ends of the scale on 19 November, in a piece under Wen Meng's (文猛) byline. By this account the levels follow 「智能自主性」 (intelligent autonomy) as their main axis, climbing through six steps starting from 「预设执行」 (executing what has been preset) up to a top level that 「具备较高自主性和自我进化能力」 (has high autonomy and the ability to evolve itself). The article does not label 「预设执行」 as L0. The same article says results are reported in three forms: a capability profile, a radar chart showing strengths and weaknesses across the four dimensions; a base grade, used for broad classification and described as a 「安全兜底」 (safety floor); and an overall grade, which reflects overall balance so that products can be compared.

The article names the technical committee but not who gave the explanation. We therefore treat the three result forms as unattributed reporting. A Sina Tech (新浪科技) piece the same day simply repeats the annex wording and never mentions L0–L5. On 18 November, 21jingji (21经济网), reposting an Economic Information Daily (经济参考网) article, carried the four-dimension and L0–L5 sentence and added that the standard turns abstract intelligence into capability indicators that can be 「描述、比较」 (described and compared). It too gives no attribution. One article opened with a standard title that does not appear in the notice, so we left it out.

Put what each source says into one table and every cell in the row for level-by-level definitions is empty. Without the full text we have not built a grade-definition table.

### 2.2. The Chinese Institute of Electronics' T/CIE 298-2025: the same four dimensions, levels L1–L5

On 27 May 2025 the Beijing municipal government website announced an association standard, 《人形机器人智能化分级》 (Humanoid robot intelligence grading, T/CIE 298-2025). The Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Center led the work. The announcement described it as 「四维五级」 (four dimensions, five levels). The four dimensions carry the same names as in the SJ/T 12355 reports; the levels run L1 to L5.

No primary document confirms that one descends from the other. Press reports do confirm that the Chinese Institute of Electronics, which issued T/CIE 298, hosts the TC8 secretariat, but a shared secretariat does not make the documents the same. So we do not move T/CIE 298's 22 first-level indicators or its 「通用安全底线」 (general safety baseline) into the SJ/T 12355 column. The SJ/T 12355 reports say the base grade serves as a safety floor, which looks similar, but we do not treat the two as one. Nor do we line the levels up against driving-automation levels or functional-safety ratings; nothing supports such a comparison.

### 2.3. Four questions to ask when a quote says "L3"

The annex puts 「应用选型」 (product selection) inside the scope of SJ/T 12355, which suggests buyers may start receiving quotes with grades attached. Because the grade definitions are unpublished, the same number from two different vendors cannot yet be compared. When a quote or product sheet carries a label such as "L3", ask the following. These are Pebblous's practical recommendations, not clauses of the standard.

1. Source document and edition: T/CIE 298-2025, SJ/T 12355-2026, or an in-house scale?
2. Which result: the base grade or the overall grade that the reports describe? Are per-dimension values (the capability profile) available?
3. Test conditions: which tasks, which environment, how many trials?
4. Who graded it, and when: the manufacturer itself or a third-party test lab?

## SJ/T 12354 training data management and ISO/IEC 5259: a gap in what is public

Item 119 of the annex says SJ/T 12354 sets management requirements across the whole lifecycle of humanoid training data. The annex names seven stages.

This document specifies management requirements for activities across the full lifecycle of humanoid robot training data: collection, processing, storage, transmission, use, circulation and destruction. It applies to the management of humanoid robot training data; management of other kinds of data may use it as a reference.
                        本文件规定了人形机器人训练数据的采集、处理、存储、传输、使用、流通和销毁全生命周期活动中的管理要求。本文件适用于人形机器人的训练数据管理，其他类型数据管理可参考使用 · Annex 1, p. 34, item 119

### 3.1. Seven stages in the annex, eleven in press reports, with annotation only in the reports

21jingji's article of 18 November presented the same standard as eleven stages (环节): collection, preprocessing, annotation (标注), quality evaluation (质量评价), storage, transmission, circulation, use, security and compliance (安全合规), destruction, and continuous improvement (持续改进). It added that the standard pushes toward unified 「数据格式、标注规则和质量评价方法」 (data formats, annotation rules and quality evaluation methods) and strengthens data security, personal-information protection and process traceability (溯源). The article does not say where this came from. Sina Finance's 19 November article also lists eleven stages. The Paper's 17 November article lists eight, including annotation and quality evaluation.

Annotation review and quality evaluation, the first things a data supplier asks about, appear only in the press report, not in the annex summary. A sentence such as "SJ/T 12354 regulates annotation and quality evaluation" needs the report as its source, not the annex. Side by side, the two lists differ by four stages.

### 3.2. ISO/IEC 5259-2, -3 and -4: what the public previews show

ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, the joint committee of the International Organization for Standardization and the International Electrotechnical Commission, published the ISO/IEC 5259 series on data quality for analytics and machine learning in 2024. Parts 3 and 4 appeared in July 2024 and Part 2 in November 2024, all as first editions. The texts are paid, but free previews from authorized distributors show the foreword, scope and table of contents. Everything below comes from that public portion.

Part 2 states in its scope that it specifies a data quality model, quality measures and guidance on quality reporting. Its table of contents lists traceability among the quality characteristics, which is where lineage connects. Part 3 sets requirements and guidelines for data quality management. It gives each of eight lifecycle stages (motivation and conceptualization, specification, planning, acquisition, preprocessing, augmentation, provisioning, decommissioning) a subclause on work products, and Clause 9 covers data quality management in the supply chain. Part 3 also says plainly in its scope that it does not prescribe detailed methods.

This document does not define a detailed process, methods or metrics.
                        ISO/IEC 5259-3:2024, Clause 1 Scope

Part 4 is a process framework for the quality of training and evaluation data used in supervised, unsupervised, semi-supervised and reinforcement learning and in analytics. Its introduction describes it as the detailed process for meeting Part 3's requirements. Clause 8 covers labeling, including quality checks on the results (8.4.8) and correction of the results (8.4.9). Clause 9 defines six roles: data planner, producer, collector, engineer, holder and user. Clause 11, on reinforcement learning, has a separate subclause, 「11.5.2 Data recording」. The detailed requirements inside these clauses sit in the paid text, and we did not see them.

Ask both standards the same questions and a difference appears. The ISO previews show where each answer would sit; for SJ/T 12354 there are only press-level answers. The table below records that difference in public coverage. It is not a mapping between the two standards.

### 3.3. SAMR's GB/Z 218.1 and 220: separate documents, three months earlier

On 27 August 2026 the State Administration for Market Regulation (国家市场监督管理总局) and the Standardization Administration of China (国家标准化管理委员会) announced 60 national standardization guidance technical documents. Two of them are GB/Z 218.1-2026 《人工智能 具身智能数据质量规范 第1部分：真实数据》 (AI, embodied intelligence data quality specification, Part 1: real-world data) and GB/Z 220-2026 《人工智能 具身智能数据生成平台技术要求》 (AI, technical requirements for embodied intelligence data generation platforms). Sina Finance reprinted the notice's list on 4 September.

GB/Z 218.1 and SJ/T 12354 both concern the quality of robot learning data, so the names look like they overlap. They are separate documents, though, with different issuing bodies, a different document type and different dates. We could not confirm whether either refers to the other, and we did not obtain GB/Z 218.1's full text either. Nothing from GB/Z 218.1 is used to fill SJ/T 12354's gaps. What is established is only that between August and November 2026 standards for robot learning data appeared at both the national and the industry level. YD/T 6770-2026, a telecom-industry standard, covers benchmark test methods for embodied intelligence. Xinhua reported on 1 April 2026 that it had been approved recently (「近日」) and would take effect on 1 June. That is a different job from SJ/T 12355's grading.

### 3.4. Questions raised by human-demonstration, teleoperation and simulation data

The annex says only "humanoid training data". Neither the annex nor the press says whether requirements change with how the data was produced: by human demonstration, by teleoperation or in simulation. The only standard whose annex summary mentions simulation is SJ/T 12357, the hand standard. Drawing on problems that published research has already exposed, we list the questions to ask when data changes hands. All four groups are practical questions, separate from any clause of the standard.

<!-- stat-card -->
**Human demonstration** — Is there a record of the rules used to retarget an operator's hand video or motion capture into the robot's joint space? Is there a basis for handling the operator's personal data, and their consent? Stanford's HumanPlus (2024) collected training data by having a humanoid shadow human motion in real time.

<!-- stat-card -->
**Teleoperation** — Is each episode marked with operator skill, latency and the stretches where a human intervened? Were failed episodes discarded or kept? OmniH2O (2024) collected data through whole-body humanoid teleoperation; DROID (2024) had many institutions gather teleoperation data with the same hardware setup and collection protocol.

<!-- stat-card -->
**Simulation** — Do the simulator version, physics parameters and randomization ranges travel with the data? If it was mixed with real-world data, is the ratio recorded? Within the annex, SJ/T 12357 is the only standard that mentions modeling and simulation guidance.

<!-- stat-card -->
**Reuse across robot models** — When data comes from a robot with a different hand and joint layout, is there a rule for aligning the action spaces? Are the collecting robot's model and end effector kept as metadata? Open X-Embodiment (2023) dealt with this while pooling data from 22 robot types into one format.

The ISO side has a subclause specifically about recorded data: 「11.5.2 Data recording」 in Part 4's reinforcement-learning clause. Whether SJ/T 12354 treats data recorded through demonstration or teleoperation in similar depth will only be known once the text is public.

## A connector-insertion task on an electronics line separates judging the robot from judging its data

Take connector insertion on an electronics assembly line. A humanoid picks a wire-harness connector from a tray and seats it in a socket on a circuit board. It uses both hands, the positional tolerance is tight, and it shares the work cell with human workers. For this one task the buyer has two separate judgments to make. Can this robot do the job? And is it acceptable to train the job on this data?

Evaluating the robot looks at the machine. According to the annex, SJ/T 12357 covers the grasping and manipulation ability of the hand that picks the connector, and SJ/T 12356 covers the performance of the electric joints outside the hand, both down to test methods. So a buyer asking for component-level test reports at least has standard numbers to cite. SJ/T 12355, which grades the robot as a whole, has no published grade definitions or assessment method. For now, then, a contract should name this task's success rate, cycle time and number of human interventions rather than a grade number, because those are what can be checked on the floor.

Judging data quality and reuse looks at the data that taught the robot. The training ground where connector-insertion demonstrations were collected falls under SJ/T 12353's construction requirements, and the processing, handover and destruction of the collected data fall under SJ/T 12354. What is public about SJ/T 12354 stops at the names of its stages. Whether to accept delivered data therefore has to rest on the lineage, usage rights and quality measurements the supplier hands over rather than on clauses of the standard. Whether the same data can be reused on a robot with a different hand also depends on the collecting robot's model and end effector having been recorded.

The two judgments ask different things, lean on different standards and are made by different people. Within what the annex summaries show, they split like this.

|  | Evaluating the robot | Judging data quality and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The question | How well does this robot perform connector insertion? | Can this data train connector insertion, and can it be reused on another robot? |
| Relevant standards (annex scope) | SJ/T 12355 capability grading; SJ/T 12356 joint performance and tests; SJ/T 12357 hand grasping, manipulation and tests | SJ/T 12354 management from collection to destruction; SJ/T 12353 the training ground that produces the data |
| What can be known now | The annex says SJ/T 12356 and 12357 specify test methods. SJ/T 12355's grade definitions and assessment method are unpublished | SJ/T 12354's list of stages (annex 7 / press 11). Clause-level requirements are unpublished |
| What the buyer should write into the contract | This task's success rate, cycle time and number of human interventions, instead of a grade label | Lineage, usage rights and quality measurements for the delivered data |
| Who judges | A test lab, or the buyer's own on-site trial | The data supplier's internal review and the buyer's acceptance inspection |

The "contract" and "who judges" rows are Pebblous's practical recommendations, not clauses of any standard. YD/T 6770-2026 does not set the acceptance criteria for this task either.

Two robots with the same grade label give no grounds for expecting the same performance in another factory if their training data was collected under different conditions and has different lineage. A grade looks at the outcome; data management looks at the inputs that produced it. The figure places the five standards along that flow. Without the full texts we could not confirm whether the standards refer to each other, so the lines show positions in the workflow; it is an interpretive diagram.

The left side is the data. Data collected at a training ground (SJ/T 12353) passes through the stages of training data management (SJ/T 12354) and into training the model and robot. The right side is the machine. Joints (SJ/T 12356) and hands (SJ/T 12357) feed into the robot through component testing, and the finished robot is assessed by capability grading (SJ/T 12355). Only the grading box is dashed, because its level-by-level definitions are unpublished.
