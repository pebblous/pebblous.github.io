미국 맥도날드 매장 13,706곳 가운데 95%는 본사가 소유하지 않은 별개 법인이 운영한다. 10월 2일 일리노이 북부연방지법에 접수된 소비자 집단소송은 그 별개 법인들의 비공개 매출 자료가 본사의 가격 추천 엔진 한 곳으로 모였다고 주장한다.

사흘 사이에 문서가 셋 나왔다. 9월 29일 로이터가 그 엔진을 취재해 실었고, 10월 1일 맥도날드가 "AI는 맥도날드의 메뉴 가격을 정하지 않는다. 사람이 정한다"는 반박문을 냈고, 다음 날 소장이 접수됐다.

소장에서 가장 센 대목은 알고리즘을 설명한 자리가 아니다. 프랜차이즈 공개문서의 조항 둘을 나란히 옮겨 적은 자리다. 가맹점은 본사가 소유한 판매시점관리 시스템을 쓰도록 요구받고 거래 단위 기록은 본사 서버로 가는데, 그 자료를 가맹점끼리 보여 주는 것은 본사 승인 없이는 금지된다. 소장은 그 둘을 묶어 맥도날드가 "한 가맹점의 자료가 경쟁자의 가격에 영향을 줄 수 있는 유일한 통로"라고 적는다.

아직 정해진 것은 없다. 기각 신청도 집단 인증도 나오지 않았다. 같은 종류의 주장을 두고 제9연방항소법원은 각하를 확정했고 제3연방항소법원은 청구를 되살렸는데, 두 사건에 쓰인 소프트웨어는 같은 회사의 같은 제품이었다. 갈린 것은 코드가 아니라 소장에 적힌 자료의 경로였다.

경쟁사의 비공개 자료를 모델에 넣기 전 얼마나 묵혀야 하는지에는 이미 적힌 답이 있다. 미 법무부가 2025년 RealPage 동의명령에 쓴 값은 열두 달이다.

그 전까지 쓰이던 석 달짜리 안전지대는 기계학습의 발전을 이유로 2023년에 폐기됐다. 바깥 소송을 읽기 전에 안에서 셀 것은 하나다. 지금 운영하는 모델의 학습 데이터에 서로 경쟁 관계인 둘 이상의 당사자가 들어 있는가. 결측률도 분포도 그 사실을 알려 주지 않는다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Of the 13,706 McDonald's restaurants in the United States, 95% are owned and run by separate legal entities. A consumer class action filed on October 2 in the Northern District of Illinois alleges that the confidential sales data of those entities was pooled into a single pricing recommendation engine operated by the franchisor.

Three documents landed in three days. Reuters published its investigation of the engine on September 29. McDonald's answered on October 1 with a rebuttal that closes on one line: "AI does not set McDonald's menu prices. People do." The complaint followed the next day.

Its strongest passage is not the one describing the algorithm. It is the one that sets two franchise disclosure clauses side by side. Franchisees are required to run the company's point-of-sale system, transaction-level records travel from it to McDonald's servers, and franchisees may not disclose those records to anyone else without approval. Reading the two together, the complaint writes that McDonald's is "the only channel through which one franchisee's data can inform a rival's price."

Nothing has been decided. There is no ruling on a motion to dismiss and no class certification. The Ninth Circuit affirmed dismissal of a comparable claim while the Third Circuit revived one, and the software in those two cases was the same product from the same vendor. What differed was the data flow described in the pleadings.

How long competitors' confidential data must sit before it enters a model is a question American enforcers have already answered with a number. In the 2025 RealPage consent decree, the Justice Department wrote twelve months.

The three-month safety zone that preceded it was withdrawn in 2023, with advances in machine learning given as the reason. Before reading anyone else's lawsuit, there is one thing to count at home. Does the training data behind your model contain two or more parties who compete with each other? No missing-value rate and no distribution will tell you.

## Facebook (EN)

Two McDonald's in Fresno, two miles apart, sell the Big Mac at $5.69 and $6.89.

Reading that line in a Reuters story, I found myself curious about something other than the price. Where did those numbers come from?

(The story is careful here: it could not confirm that the gap came from the pricing engine, and both restaurants are company-operated rather than franchised.)

Three documents landed in three days at the end of September. The Reuters investigation on the 29th. A McDonald's rebuttal on October 1. A consumer class action in Chicago federal court on October 2.

The rebuttal closes on a single line. "AI does not set McDonald's menu prices. People do."

The complaint does not claim that AI set them either. What it lays out is two clauses from the franchise disclosure document. Franchisees must run the company's point-of-sale system, and the transaction-level records it generates travel to McDonald's servers. Meanwhile those same records may not be shown to another franchisee without the company's approval.

Reading the two together, the complaint arrives at one sentence. "McDonald's is the only channel through which one franchisee's data can inform a rival's price."

What I could not get past came after that.

The sales records inside that engine are not bad data. They come straight off the point of sale, one row per transaction, cleaner than most.

"But what are the owners of these rows to each other?"

No missing-value rate answers that. Neither does a distribution or an outlier scan. You can open the data all day without seeing it; it surfaces in the org chart and in the contract.

So I have come to see two tests where I used to see one. Quality asks whether the values are clean. Eligibility asks whether these values belong inside one model together.

Pebblous runs many clients' data through the same pipelines and the same tools. What DataClinic inspects is mostly the values. The second test is not yet in our tooling either. That is why I wrote this one up at length instead of filing it away as another company's trouble.
