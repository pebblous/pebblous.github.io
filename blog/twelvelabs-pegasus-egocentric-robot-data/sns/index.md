## LinkedIn (KO)

트웰브랩스가 10월 6일(현지시간) 1인칭 영상만 따로 다루는 비전언어모델 페가수스 1.6을 내놨다. 영상 1시간당 1.75달러를 받고 사람이 하던 라벨 작업을 대신하겠다는 제품이다.

그 작업이 얼마나 비쌌는지는 공개 데이터셋 Ego-Exo4D가 보여 준다. 1,286시간 분량 영상에 라벨을 붙이는 데 누적 20만 시간 넘는 주석 노력이 들어갔다. 원본 1시간마다 사람 손이 약 155시간 붙은 셈이다. 그러니까 로봇을 가르칠 영상이 모자란 게 아니었다. 찍어 둔 영상에서 쓸 것을 골라내고 설명을 붙일 사람이 모자랐다.

페가수스 1.6이 가져가겠다는 일은 다섯 가지인데, 그중 셋은 생성이 아니라 선별이다. 어떤 클립이 학습에 쓸 만한지, 어느 구간이 개인정보에 걸리는지를 모델이 정하고 그 판정이 다음 단계로 그대로 넘어간다.

벤처비트는 이 발표에서 바깥이 확인할 수 없는 대목을 함께 적었다. 이름을 밝힌 로봇 고객이 아직 없다. 경쟁 제품과 견줄 공개 벤치마크도 나오지 않았고, 17년치를 18시간에 처리했다는 주장에는 어떤 계산 자원을 썼는지가 빠져 있다.

사람이 라벨을 붙이던 시절에는 작업자가 누구였는지, 어떤 지침을 받았는지가 기록으로 남았다. 모델이 그 자리에 들어서면 결과는 남고 근거는 남지 않는다. 155시간을 1.75달러로 바꾸는 이득의 상당 부분이 그 근거를 만들지 않는 데서 나온다. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data를 말할 때 앞에 두는 전제도 여기 걸린다. 선별의 근거가 기록되지 않으면, 걸러 낸 데이터가 왜 좋은 데이터인지 사람에게 설명할 수가 없다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

TwelveLabs released Pegasus 1.6 on October 6, a vision-language model built for one kind of footage: first-person video. It prices the work at $1.75 per hour of video going in.

The work it is pricing has been expensive. Ego-Exo4D, a public dataset, holds 1,286 hours of egocentric video, and labeling it took more than 200,000 cumulative hours of annotation. That is roughly 155 hours of human effort for every hour of source footage. The shortage in robot training data was never the footage. It was the people to sort it and write it up.

Pegasus 1.6 claims five jobs, and three of them are selection rather than generation. The model decides which clips are worth training on and which stretches carry personal information, and those verdicts pass downstream as they are.

VentureBeat set out what cannot be checked from outside. No robot customer has been named. No benchmark against competing products was published, and the claim that 17 years of footage was processed in 18 hours arrived without the compute behind it.

When people did the labeling, the record held who annotated the clip and what guidelines they worked from. Seat a model there and the outcomes remain while the grounds do not. Much of what turns 155 hours into $1.75 comes from the grounds never being produced. That is the premise Pebblous puts first in AI-Ready Data: if the basis for the filtering is not recorded, there is no way to show anyone why the filtered data is good data.

## Facebook (EN)

Washing dishes. Fixing a bicycle. Cooking a meal.

That is what most robot training footage actually looks like, filmed from a camera worn on someone's head.

Collecting it is not the hard part. Describing it is.

Ego-Exo4D, a public dataset, runs to 1,286 hours of this kind of video. Labeling it took more than 200,000 cumulative hours of annotation work, roughly 155 hours of human effort for every hour of footage.

On October 6 TwelveLabs released Pegasus 1.6, which offers to do that work for $1.75 an hour of video.

The orders of magnitude are not close. Read only those two numbers and there is no reason to hesitate.

But of the five jobs the model takes on, three are not about producing anything. They are about sorting footage that already exists into what gets kept and what gets dropped. I have started holding that half apart in my head, as "automatic selection," because it behaves differently. The outcome survives and the reasoning does not.

When people did this work, there was somewhere to put the question. Who annotated this. What guidelines they worked from. How closely two people agreed after watching the same clip.

Seat a model there and you still learn that a clip scored low and was dropped. You do not learn why.

Much of what turns 155 hours into $1.75 comes from never producing that why.

"If we filtered this data automatically, where is the record that explains to a person why what is left is good?"

I am not arguing against automatic selection. Nobody is going to keep paying 155 hours of human work for one hour of video.

But receiving the filtered output, and receiving it together with the reason each clip was kept or dropped, are different things. The difference shows up later, when a regulator or a customer asks how the training set was chosen.

There is a premise Pebblous puts first whenever AI-Ready Data comes up. On data whose origin was never recorded, verification is not really verification. Hand the filtering to a model and one more line attaches to that sentence.
