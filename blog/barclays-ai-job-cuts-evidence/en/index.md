# Barclays Ties Its Job Cuts to AI, but Which Jobs Go First?

_About 5,000 roles go this year, around 60% of group actions sit in support functions including developers, and every number Barclays attaches to AI measures adoption_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> This article reads the job cuts the British bank Barclays announced alongside its third-quarter results on October 22, 2026. Around 5,000 roles go this year, about 5% of the workforce, and roughly 60% of the actions sit in support functions, software developers among them. What this article looks at is not the size of the cut but the question of which numbers the company could offer if it wanted to call this cut an AI story.

> Two executives described it differently on the same day. Chief Financial Officer Anna Cross named AI directly, saying there would be fewer developers as AI makes people more productive. Chief Executive C.S. Venkatakrishnan called AI "the icing on the cake, not the cake," and traced the gains so far to newer platforms laid in, systems merged together and steps cut out of the work. Meanwhile the AI figures Barclays has actually published are adoption figures, such as a target of covering half its developers with a coding tool by year-end. A few figures comparing the same work at two points in time did come up on the call. Not one of them was attributed to AI.

> Sections 1 through 3 follow what the announcement and the public filings say. Section 4, which lays this over the work of validating data, is this article's own.

## Where the 5,000 Roles Come From

The announcement came in a good quarter. Third-quarter profit before tax reached £2.3 billion, up 12% on the same period last year, and equities trading income jumped 47%. Fixed income, currencies and commodities fell 19%, which Venkatakrishnan singled out as a disappointment, but across the group this was not a quarter in which the money ran short. Barclays announced a share buyback of up to £500 million on the same day.

![Barclays world headquarters building, One Churchill Place, in Canary Wharf, London](./image/img-01-barclays-hq.jpg)
*▲ One Churchill Place, Barclays' world headquarters in Canary Wharf, London | Source: [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Barclays_Bank_PLC_world_headquarters.JPG) (Public Domain)*

The location of the roles going was disclosed fairly clearly. Cross, the chief financial officer, said about 60% of the actions related to support functions, developers included. What the company said is that support functions account for 60%, not that developers account for 60%. Developers are one occupation inside that bundle, and how many of them there are is not in the announcement.

The sentence Cross used on the results call ran like this. "These actions involve an expected reduction of circa 5,000 full-time employees. Around 60% of the group actions relate to support functions, including fewer developers as AI makes our people more productive." The 60% attaches to actions, not to people. Reporting carried it over as 60% of 5,000, a little more than 3,000 roles, and this article follows the same rough arithmetic, but the company never pinned the 60% to a headcount.

Gathering the items that carry a figure in the announcement and the results material gives this.

| Item | Figure given |
| --- | --- |
| Roles going this year | 5,000 (about 5% of the workforce) |
| Already gone in the first half | About 1,000 |
| Remaining before year-end | About 4,000 |
| Share sitting in support functions | Around 60% of group actions (developers included) |
| Restructuring cost this year | £307 million from January to September, about £300 million more by year-end |
| Gross efficiency savings expected in 2027 | More than £1.1 billion |
| Third-quarter profit before tax | £2.3 billion (up 12% year on year) |

Reading the table downward, one thing stands out. How many roles go, what that costs, and how much the bank means to save are all written as figures. There is no row for how many of those roles went because AI took the work. Filings do not ask for such a row, so its absence is unremarkable in itself.

Causation was therefore explained in words rather than figures, and the weight shifted with the speaker. Cross said the changes in the third and fourth quarters related both to the simplification of the organization and to some of the impacts the bank was starting to see from AI. Venkatakrishnan was more guarded. He said Barclays would employ AI at scale and then added that "AI is the icing on the cake, not the cake," since the infrastructure has to be built before the benefits arrive. What had driven the efficiency gains so far, he said, was platform modernization, system consolidation and simpler processes.

The icing image had evidence behind it. The improvements the two of them cited on the same call did carry before-and-after figures. On the mortgage broker platform, application times fell from 45 minutes to under 15 minutes, and broker net promoter score moved from minus 51 to plus 70. Customer-facing technology cut call center volumes by 10% over the past year, and a change to the branch staffing model brought employees per branch down by around 10% over the same period.

The first thing those four figures share is that they compare a before with an after. The second is attribution. The mortgage gain was put down to technology investment, the call center to customer-facing technology, the branches to a change in the operating model. Not one of the four was presented as an achievement of AI. That is the context for the chief executive's remark about building the infrastructure first. It also means the AI column is not blank because this is an organization that fails to keep records.

> [!callout]
> In the same announcement the chief financial officer named AI as a cause and the chief executive cut AI's share down. Neither of them was lying. The call did carry comparisons. It is that every improvement carrying a figure was credited to something other than AI, and the places where AI was named as the cause came with nothing to compare.

## The Same Occupations Were in Barclays' Own Report

A month before the cuts, on September 22, 2026, Barclays Research published a report analyzing the effect of AI on labor. According to the reporting, it is called the AI Automation and Amplification Framework, or 3A Framework. Its distinguishing move is to take skills rather than whole occupations as the unit of analysis, mapping 104 cross-occupational skills from the US Department of Labor database onto some 830 occupations and scoring exposure to cognitive AI and physical AI separately.

The message the report led with ran the other way, toward reassurance. Only about 20% of core work skills are ones AI can substitute for at a high level, it said, and the story of wholesale replacement is overdrawn. What spreads far more widely, in its reading, is amplification: AI assisting human work and raising productivity.

The same report also drew the distribution of that exposure. Occupations with high cognitive AI exposure cluster in computing, engineering and science, and those with high physical AI exposure cluster in construction, manufacturing, installation and maintenance. A month later, the 60% share of the actions the company said it would take sat in the first of those clusters, the support functions that include developers.

Barclays has never said this report fed into the decision on headcount, and it has offered no account linking the two. This article does not claim the report directed the cuts either. The report itself is not open to the public, so what is set out above rests on reporting. Even so, it is worth recording that one company issued an analysis saying replacement is overdrawn and, a month later, an announcement cutting roles in the category that analysis had marked as highly exposed.

Both documents can be true at once. Even if the substitutable skills are 20% of the whole, a concentration of that 20% in particular occupations will shrink those occupations. The difficulty is that neither document shows "AI did this work instead" as a measured value. One is an estimate of exposure, the other a plan for headcount. No observational record bridging them has ever been published.

## Every Number Barclays Puts on AI Counts Usage

The place where Barclays set out AI figures in the most detail was not the cuts announcement but three weeks before it. On October 1, 2026, Anthropic announced an expansion of its work with the bank and wrote the internal deployment and the plans out as numbers. Three values are public.

| Figure published | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Claude Code reaching 50% of the developer population by year-end, and the majority of software engineers in 2027 | How much they have agreed to use |
| An internal knowledge assistant used by more than 16,000 UK colleagues, with more than a million searches since 2025 | How many people used it, how often |
| Around 120,000 client emails a day triaged, enriched and routed in Global Markets | How much volume went through |

All three measure the size of the usage. How many people use it, how many items passed through, what percentage it will reach next. What is nowhere in the announcement is the comparison with how the same work went when people did it: how much the time to handle one item fell, how errors and rework shifted, how a team using it compares with one that does not. The same goes for a contract value and a saving.
