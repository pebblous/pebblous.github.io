# Korea

_The first of 19 proposed amendments FKI sent to the Ministry of Science and ICT asks that AI used only by employees and affiliates be marked off from services sold outside_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> The Federation of Korean Industries (FKI), the business group that speaks for the country's large companies, said on October 29 that it had gathered its members' views and sent the Ministry of Science and ICT 19 proposed changes to the AI Basic Act. The 19 fall into five areas, and the item that opens them is AI built for in-house work. A company that builds its own AI and lets only its employees or its affiliates use it has no way to tell whether it carries the same operator duties as a commercial service sold to outside customers.

> FKI is not the only one asking. The AI Basic Act support desk, opened by the ministry when the law took effect, took 552 inquiries over the ten weeks from January 22 to March 30, and among the online inquiries alone, 19.6 percent asked whether the company's own AI counts as high-impact AI. At the end of March the ministry went on to publish a collection of worked cases. Seven months later, a business group has brought the same question back.

> Sections 1 through 4 follow the submission FKI released in full and the text of the AI Basic Act and its Enforcement Decree. Section 5, which carries this gap over to the question of records, is this article's own reading.

## Nine Months In, 19 Items Land at the Ministry

On October 29 FKI said it had collected its members' views and put 19 proposed improvements to the AI Basic Act before the Ministry of Science and ICT. The statute at issue is the Framework Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of a Foundation for Trust, in force since January 22 of this year and nine months into its run.

FKI sorted the 19 into five areas.

- Clarifying who and what the AI rules apply to
- Making the duty to disclose and label AI use workable
- Spelling out the rules for high-performance and high-impact AI
- Tidying up the standards for using training data
- Making any widening of the rules predictable

Open the contents page of the document FKI attached to its release and the weight of each area shows. Four items sit under operator status and scope of application, seven under transparency duties for generative AI, five under high-performance and high-impact AI, two under training data, and one under the predictability of any widening.
