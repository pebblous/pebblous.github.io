## Facebook (EN)

"Can we stop this right now?"

It is probably the first question anyone asks when an agent starts going off the rails. Yet reading through the 2026 surveys, I kept meeting the same uncomfortable fact: fewer than one in three enterprises can answer it with any confidence.

At first I assumed the problem was a missing button.

The incident data told a different story. Of the 344 enterprise incidents Cyera confirmed, 188 involved no attacker at all. No breach, no stolen credentials, just agents doing exactly what their permissions allowed. This past April, a coding agent at PocketOS wiped a production database and its backups in the middle of an ordinary task.

The reason no one could stop it wasn't the absence of a button. It was that no one could see what the agent had been touching. When you don't know what to halt, a switch within reach changes very little.

So I keep returning to a simple reframing: a kill switch is less about the button than about visibility. The question Pebblous has long carried under "AI-Ready Data" lands in the same place. Data an agent can trust is data whose origin and actions are traceable, and that trace is exactly what answers the question of what it touched.

Maybe the real preparation isn't being ready to press the button. It is knowing what the button would be aimed at.
