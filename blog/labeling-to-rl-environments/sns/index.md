## LinkedIn (KO)

가장 비싼 인간 데이터 작업이 더는 정답 붙이기가 아니다. 이미지에 상자를 치고 문장에 라벨을 다는 일이 흔해지고 값이 떨어지는 사이, 프런티어의 일감은 에이전트가 실제 소프트웨어를 다뤄 보는 강화학습 환경과 그 시도를 채점하는 검증기를 만드는 쪽으로 넘어갔다. 앤트로픽은 이 환경에만 향후 1년 10억 달러가 넘는 지출을 논의하는 것으로 전해지고, Mercor는 도메인별 환경을 팔며 연환산 매출을 20억 달러까지 끌어올렸다.

환경은 라벨과 성질이 다르다. 라벨은 정답 한 장으로 끝나지만, 환경에는 기업의 실제 업무 흐름과 미공개 모델의 행동, 채점 기준에 담긴 훈련 전략이 함께 새겨진다. 그래서 값어치가 소프트웨어가 아니라 채점 기준을 짜는 전문가에게서 나온다. 라벨링에서 앞서 있던 회사들이 그대로 위층을 차지하는 이유다.

다만 시장은 아직 거칠다. 에이전트가 과제를 풀지 않고 보상만 챙기는 리워드 해킹이 흔하고, 특정 앱에 묶인 환경은 화면이 바뀌면 쉽게 낡는다. 승부처가 옮겨 간 것은 분명하되, 그 위에서 누가 이길지는 아직 열려 있다.

노출이 깊어진 만큼 중립성은 있으면 좋은 조건이 아니라 공급자를 고를 때 먼저 따지는 자격이 됐다. AI-Ready Data의 다음 정의가 정적 데이터셋이 아니라 검증 가능한 환경이라면, 그 환경을 누가 소유하고 어떻게 중립성을 보증하느냐가 새 해자다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The most expensive job in human data is no longer attaching answers. As drawing boxes and labeling sentences grew common and cheap, the frontier work moved to building reinforcement learning environments, where an agent handles real software step by step, and the verifiers that grade each attempt. Anthropic is reported to be weighing more than a billion dollars in RL-environment spending over the next year, and Mercor has pushed annualized revenue to two billion by selling domain-specific environments.

The shift is really about the nature of the data. A label is one artifact; an environment inscribes a company's real workflows, the behavior of unreleased models, and the training strategy baked into its grading rubrics. Its value comes less from the software than from the experts who write the criteria, which is why the labeling incumbents are the ones moving up a rung.

The market is still rough. Reward hacking, where an agent banks the reward without solving the task, is common, and environments tied to one app age fast. The battleground has clearly moved; who wins on it has not been settled.

What has settled is this: with exposure that deep, neutrality is no longer a nice-to-have but the first thing a buyer checks. If the next definition of AI-Ready Data is a verifiable environment rather than a static dataset, then who owns it, and how its neutrality is guaranteed, becomes the new moat.

## Facebook (EN)

A label used to be the whole job. Draw one box on an image, attach one answer to a sentence, and you were done.

For a while, that was the most valuable thing a human could do for an AI. But as that work grew common and its price fell, I read that the expensive work had quietly changed seats.

The frontier job now is not attaching answers. It is building the environments where an agent actually tries a task and gets graded, one step at a time. One founder called it building an extremely boring video game.

When I saw that Anthropic is reportedly weighing more than a billion dollars on these environments in a single year, what stayed with me was not the number.

A label is one artifact. An environment holds far more: how a company really works, how a not-yet-public model behaves, what a lab is quietly trying to get good at. It matters less who touched a label than what environment your agent grows up in, and against what it is graded.

In 2025, a supplier everyone had trusted as neutral took a stake from one Big Tech firm, and its rival customers walked away almost overnight. It looked like a contract problem then. It reads like a structural one now.

"Is this environment really neutral? Where does the strategy it trained leak?"

If the next definition of AI-Ready Data is a verifiable environment rather than a static dataset, one question remains. Who owns that environment, and what guarantees its neutrality? That is roughly the ground Pebblous has spent a long time looking at.
