휴머노이드 회사가 상장 서류의 실적 자리에 올린 것은 매출이 아니라 시간이었다. 디짓이 고객 현장에서 일한 6만 5,000시간.

애질리티 로보틱스가 기업인수목적회사 처칠 캐피털 XI와의 합병을 11월 4일 마치고, 다음 날 나스닥에서 AGLT로 거래를 시작했다. 미국 증시에 오른 첫 순수 휴머노이드 회사다. 중국 유니트리가 8월 상하이에 먼저 올랐으니 세계 최초는 아니다.

합병 직전 지분가치는 25억 달러였고, 실제로 손에 쥔 현금은 약 4억 6,500만 달러다. 인수목적회사 보통주 주주의 61.5%가 합병에 남는 대신 상환을 택하면서 6월에 내다본 금액에서 내려왔다.

실적을 앞세울 처지는 아니었다. 2025년 매출은 178만 달러인데 매출원가가 447만 달러였다. 로봇을 내보낼수록 손해가 났다는 뜻이다. 디짓 5를 공급하는 3억 달러짜리 수주가 있지만 상대는 한 곳이고, 등록서류 자체가 당기 매출의 척도가 아니라고 적어 두었다. 일반 공급은 2027년 말부터다.

그래서 6만 5,000시간이 그 자리에 놓였다. 최고경영자 페기 존슨은 이것을 데이터 플라이휠이라고 부른다. 시연 영상 속 다른 로봇보다 느려 보여도 디짓은 GXO와 쉐플러, 토요타의 현장에서 실제로 일하고 있고, 그렇게 쌓인 기록이 다음 세대를 더 빨리 만든다는 주장이다.

매출에는 회계기준이 있고 감사인이 따로 본다. 운영 시간에는 둘 다 없다. 이 시간을 로봇 몇 대가 나눠 쌓았는지, 그 가운데 사람이 원격으로 조작한 몫이 얼마인지, 전원만 켜져 있던 시간과 실제로 일한 시간이 어디서 갈리는지가 서류에 없다. 고객 시설에서 모인 데이터의 귀속을 다룬 조항도 위험 요인에 들어가 있지 않다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A humanoid robot company listed on Nasdaq this week and put something other than revenue where the results go: 65,000 hours of its robot working at customer sites.

Agility Robotics closed its merger with the blank-check company Churchill Capital Corp XI on November 4 and began trading as AGLT the next day. It is the first pure humanoid business on a U.S. exchange. China's Unitree reached Shanghai in August, so this is a first for the United States rather than the world.

The equity value set just before the merger was $2.5 billion, and the company took in about $465 million. Roughly 61.5 percent of common shareholders in the blank-check vehicle redeemed instead of staying in, which pulled the proceeds below what June's projection had assumed.

There was not much of a results line to lead with. Revenue in 2025 was $1.78 million against $4.47 million in cost of revenue. A $300 million order for Digit 5 units sits on the books, but the buyer is a single party and the filing itself states the figure is not a measure of current-period revenue. General availability comes at the end of 2027.

So the 65,000 hours went into that place instead. Chief executive Peggy Johnson calls it a data flywheel. Digit may look slow beside the demo reels, her argument runs, but it is working at GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota sites, and the record piling up there builds the next generation faster.

Revenue has accounting rules and an auditor. Operating hours have neither. The filing does not say how many robots shared the total, how much of it a person was driving by remote, or where hours powered on end and hours doing work begin. The risk factors carry no clause on who owns data gathered inside a customer's facility.

65,000 hours is true as far as activity goes. The claim resting on top of it is still outside the filing.

## Facebook (EN)

Finding the results in a listing document usually takes a moment. There is a line where revenue goes.

In Agility Robotics' filing, time had been put in that line.

Sixty-five thousand hours. Everything a two-legged robot called Digit has done inside other people's warehouses and factories, added up.

The company began trading on Nasdaq on November 5, the first pure humanoid business on a U.S. exchange.

Leading with revenue was never really an option. Last year the robots cost more than twice as much to build as they brought in.

So the hours went there instead. The chief executive calls them a data flywheel. Digit may be slower than the demo reels, but it is doing real work, and the record left behind is what builds the next machine.

I read that part as welcome news. Data has finally turned up on a balance sheet.

Something arrives with it, though.

Revenue has a standard for what may be counted, and an auditor who checks that the standard was followed. Operating hours have neither yet.

'A total with nothing underneath it.'

Without knowing how many robots shared the hours, how many of those hours had a person at the controls, or where waiting with the power on ends, there is nothing an outsider can do with the line except copy it down.

"How far does this number reach, and where does it stop?"
