## LinkedIn (KO)

구글이 10월 1일 저궤도에 올리는 AI 칩은 15분 돌고 나면 식히려고 꺼진다.

스페이스X 트랜스포터-18 공동 탑승 임무로 떠나는 냉장고만 한 시제 위성이다. 우주에 데이터센터가 생겼다는 제목이 여럿 붙었지만, 태양전지가 내는 전력은 1킬로와트 남짓이고 실린 칩은 네 개다. 올라가는 것은 데이터센터가 아니라 서버 한 대다. 구글이 공식 글에 적은 이번 임무의 목적도 연산이 아니다. 자사 TPU가 우주 비행의 물리적 스트레스와 방사선·열 극한을 "어떻게 견디는지에 대한 궤도 데이터"를 모으는 것이다.

저궤도에서는 같은 태양전지가 지상보다 최대 여덟 배 많은 전기를 만든다고 같은 글은 밝힌다. 다만 그 값은 단위면적당 발전량이지, 칩을 몇 시간 돌릴 수 있는지를 말해 주는 값이 아니다. 둘 사이를 가로막고 선 것이 열이다. 진공에는 열을 실어 나를 공기가 없어, 라디에이터가 적외선으로 버리는 속도가 곧 연산 시간의 상한이 된다.

다음 단계를 보면 구글이 무엇을 병목으로 보는지 드러난다. 2027년에 올릴 두 기가 시험하는 것은 더 많은 연산이 아니라 위성끼리 빛으로 데이터를 주고받는 광 링크다. 계산을 어디에 둘지는 전력과 대역폭이 정하고, 열이 그 옆에 상수처럼 붙어 있다.

이 임무가 1년간 쌓는 것은 계산 결과가 아니라 칩이 늙어 가는 기록이다. 그런데 표본은 한 기다. 태양 활동이 어느 국면이었는지, 라디에이터 코팅이 어느 배치에서 나왔는지가 값과 함께 남아 있지 않으면, 2027년 측정값과 나란히 놓아도 차이가 어디서 왔는지 가릴 수 없다. 페블러스가 데이터셋을 진단할 때 항목 수보다 먼저 확인하는 것이 그 수집 조건이다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The AI chips Google is sending into low Earth orbit on October 1 will run for about 15 minutes at a stretch and then switch off to cool.

The craft is a refrigerator-sized prototype riding SpaceX's Transporter-18. Plenty of headlines have announced a data center in space; the solar panels put out about a kilowatt and four TPUs sit inside. What goes up is one server. The purpose Google wrote into its own post is not compute either. The satellite is there to gather in-orbit data on how its TPUs handle the physical stress of spaceflight and the radiation and thermal extremes of space.

In that orbit, the same post says, satellites can generate up to eight times more solar power than on Earth. The figure is energy per unit of area, not hours a chip can run. Heat stands between the two. A vacuum has no air to carry heat away, so the rate at which a radiator sheds infrared sets the ceiling on how long the chips compute.

The next step shows what Google treats as the bottleneck. The two satellites going up in 2027 will test optical links between craft rather than more compute. Power and bandwidth decide where a computation sits, and heat sits beside them like a constant.

What the mission accumulates over a year is not results but a record of chips growing old. The sample is a single craft. Unless the phase of solar activity and the batch a radiator coating came from are written down next to the values, setting them beside the 2027 measurements settles nothing. ## Facebook (EN)

When a laptop gets hot, the fan is the first thing you hear.

It is not that the work got heavier. The heat it made has to go somewhere. When the fan cannot keep up, the machine quietly slows itself down.

The refrigerator-sized satellite Google puts into low Earth orbit on October 1 cannot carry that fan.

A vacuum has no air to carry heat away. Fans have nothing to push and coolant loops have nowhere to reject into, which leaves one method: a radiator panel shedding infrared into space, slowly.

So the AI chips aboard run a workload for about 15 minutes and then switch off to cool. That is how Travis Beals, senior director of Google's research team, described the operation. How long the cooling takes has not been published.

In that orbit, Google says, the same panel makes up to eight times the electricity it would on the ground. Eight times the power still has to pass through the place where power becomes computation, and heat is waiting there.

"What actually shuts down after 15 minutes? The chip, or the plan somebody built by counting a full day of it?"

What this satellite sends home over a year is not the output of a computation.

It is a year of "failure history."

Vibration through the launch. Cumulative radiation dose. How often a value flipped in memory. The radiator temperature curve, drawn once every time the craft goes around. Those are the numbers Google will reason from when it designs the next satellite, not the answers Gemini gave in orbit.

The sample, though, is one craft. Unless it is written beside the values whether the sun was quiet that year and which batch the radiator coating came from, setting them next to the two satellites of 2027 will not tell you whether a difference came from a better design or from a calmer year. At Pebblous, that record is what we look at before the number of fields. A laboratory gets its answer by controlling conditions; a field site gets its answer by recording them.

The first news a year from now may not be that the satellite survived. A table showing how many minutes a day it actually ran will arrive first, and the next design starts by reading that table as a map.
