아마존이 데이터센터를 유치할 때 지자체와 맺어 온 비밀유지계약을 더 쓰지 않겠다고 했다. 10월 2일 AWS 최고경영자 맷 가먼이 자사 블로그에 직접 올린 글이다.

계약 중단과 함께 나온 것이 'Built Together'다. 데이터센터가 들어선 지역에 5년간 10억 달러를 쓰는 프로그램이고, 전력과 물 사용량, 무탄소 전력 비중을 해마다 공개하겠다는 약속도 같이 적었다.

발표 시점이 한가하지 않다. 사흘 전 미국 하원 법제사법위원회 간사 제이미 래스킨이 아마존·구글·메타·오라클에 공개 질의서를 보냈다. 첫 질문은 미국에 보유하거나 운영하거나 제안한 데이터센터를 빠짐없이 열거하고 시설마다 완전 가동 시 전력과 물 사용량을 적어 내라는 것이었고, 답변 기한은 10월 13일이었다.

약속 목록에 오르지 않은 항목이 있다. 세금이다. 마이크로소프트가 올해 1월 지방 재산세 감면을 더 요청하지 않겠다고 선언했을 때 아마존은 같은 약속을 하지 않았다. 사업하는 거의 모든 지역에서 자사가 최대 납세자라는 이유였다.

숫자 쪽은 이렇게 된다. Built Together가 한 해에 쓰는 돈은 평균 2억 달러이고, 아마존이 2025년 인디애나주 한 주에서 받은 판매·사용세 감면은 5억 6,100만 달러다. 앞은 아마존이 쓰는 돈이고 뒤는 공공이 포기한 세수라 그대로 빼서 손익을 계산하면 틀린다. 그래도 주민이 두 금액을 한 화면에서 본 적은 거의 없다.

공개 약속에서 정해진 것은 항목과 주기다. 어느 시설까지 쪼개 내놓을지, 물과 무탄소 전력을 어떤 규칙으로 셀지, 받아서 맞는지 확인할 기관을 누가 맡을지는 아직 비어 있다. 감시단체 Good Jobs First 집계로는 데이터센터 세제를 둔 주가 적어도 36곳인데, 약속한 일자리와 실제로 생긴 일자리를 함께 보고하는 주는 하나도 없다.

싸움의 모양이 데이터센터를 받을지 말지에서 데이터센터가 무슨 숫자를 내놓는지로 옮겨 가는 중이다. 숫자는 틀렸다고 말할 수 있다. 그 숫자가 어디서 왔고 누가 손댔는지가 함께 남아 있을 때만.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Amazon says it has stopped signing nondisclosure agreements with the local governments it works with on data center projects.

The announcement came on October 2 from AWS CEO Matt Garman. Alongside the end of those agreements he introduced Built Together, a commitment of more than $1 billion over five years to the communities where Amazon's data centers sit, and a promise to publish power use, water use, and the share of carbon-free electricity every year.

The timing was not idle. Three days earlier, Representative Jamie Raskin, ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee, sent public letters to Amazon, Google, Meta, and Oracle. The first question asked Amazon to list every data center it owns, operates, or has proposed in the United States, with the projected power and water use of each facility at full operation. The response deadline was October 13.

One item never made the list of promises. Taxes. When Microsoft declared in January that it would stop asking for local property tax abatements, Amazon declined to match it, saying it is already the largest taxpayer in nearly every community where it operates.

The figures land this way. Built Together averages $200 million a year, and the sales and use tax Amazon did not pay in the single state of Indiana in 2025 came to $561 million, more than ten times the prior year. Subtracting one from the other gives the wrong answer, because the first is money Amazon spends and the second is revenue the public forgoes. Residents have still rarely seen the two amounts on one screen.

What Amazon has settled is the list of items and the cadence. Scope, method, and auditor are open: how far down the figures get broken out, what rules count water and carbon-free power, and which body receives the numbers and confirms them. The watchdog group Good Jobs First counts at least 36 states with tax programs aimed at data centers, and not one of them reports the jobs a company promised next to the jobs it created.

The fight is moving from whether to accept a data center to what numbers a data center produces. A number can be called wrong, but only when the record of where it came from and who touched it travels with it.

## Facebook (EN)

There are things a town learns only after the digging starts.

Which company moved in next door. How much electricity and water the site will draw. How much tax it will not be paying.

In South Bend, Indiana, the city's public works director and city engineer signed a nondisclosure agreement with Amazon in December 2023. It expires this December.

On October 2, AWS CEO Matt Garman said the company is done with that practice. No more nondisclosure agreements with local governments, plus more than $1 billion over five years for the communities where its data centers sit, plus a promise to publish power and water use every year.

What caught my attention was not the content of the promise but its blanks.

What gets published and how often are settled. How far down the figures get broken out, what rules count water and carbon-free power, and which body receives the numbers and confirms them are not.

The phrase I keep reaching for is a "self-graded report card." The party producing the number and the party checking it are sitting in the same chair.

"Who receives the electricity and water figures for the site in my county, and confirms that they are right?"

Indiana already showed that the question is not idle. Amazon claimed $561 million in state sales and use tax exemptions there in 2025, and the state did not volunteer that figure. It surfaced after a local broadcaster spent months chasing it and a watchdog group kept pressing. In Texas, 138 data center projects have been certified, 20 have reached a fifth-year review, and six of those were found non-compliant.

The slow work of tagging a figure with where it came from is close to what Pebblous has spent its time on. Unless the facility, the month, the aggregation rule, and whoever confirmed it travel alongside the number, an improvement cannot be separated into the site getting better and the counting changing.

The nondisclosure agreements are leaving. Numbers are moving into the space they vacate. The chair for whoever receives them is still empty.
