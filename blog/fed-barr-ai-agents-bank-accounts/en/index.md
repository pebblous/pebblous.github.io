# Michael Barr: Banks Can

_Speaking in Milan on November 16, the Fed governor warned that a deposit run could be over in minutes once AI assistants start moving money on their own._

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> The American central bank has put on the public record that AI agents are already moving inside other people's accounts. The occasion was a speech by Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr on November 16, 2026, at a finance AI conference in Milan. He said large banks are reporting AI agents that try to do something inside the accounts of individual customers — and he attached one condition to that. The bank cannot verify that the agent is genuine, that the customer consented, or that the action matches what the customer asked for.

> Barr argued that if the gap stays open, the speed at which deposits leave a bank changes. Once an agent takes over the step a person used to perform — the transfer itself — it can move the money on a difference in rates the moment it reads one, and an outflow that fast outruns both the bank's liquidity management and the point at which a supervisor could step in. The agents inside the accounts are a fact banks have reported. The run that could finish in minutes has not happened. Elsewhere in the speech sit the debt figures of the AI companies.

> Sections 1 through 4 are what the speech says. Section 5 is this article's reading. It takes up the warning where the record of delegation is empty.

## The One Sentence a Central Bank Put on the Record

The speech is titled "Some Potential Financial Stability Implications of Artificial Intelligence," and the venue was the seventh ACM International Conference on AI in Finance. The body breaks into six parts: the AI investment boom, leverage in the data center buildout, unintended consequences of AI in wholesale securities trading, market herding and a possible stampede, AI and cyber risk, and AI in consumer finance. The passage this article holds on to is in the last part.

That part opens with the good side. Alternative data sources such as rental payments, utility bills, or employment patterns may expand access to credit for people whose thin files keep pushing them out of underwriting. Then the risks arrive, and they catch on the same data. Those alternative sources may inadvertently serve as proxies for protected characteristics like race or national origin, Barr wrote, and systems leaning on incomplete or biased data may generate inaccurate information. The warning that a recommendation may rest not only on the investor's own circumstances and objectives but also on factors that advance a firm's commercial interests belongs here too. Every risk this part names points back at data.

After the data, a single sentence reports what banks are already seeing.

"Major banks now report finding AI agents attempting to take actions in retail customer accounts, without the bank being able to verify authenticity, consent, and alignment with customer instructions."

Governor Michael S. Barr, Milan, November 16, 2026, in the section on AI in consumer finance.

The weight of the sentence rests on "now report finding." A clause looking ahead at what might happen would have carried a different tense. Barr wrote that banks are living through this and reporting it. The agents turn up in retail customer accounts rather than corporate ones, which is to say in the accounts of individuals. And the verb is "attempting." Nothing in the sentence says the action ran to completion. What the bank saw is the trace of a try.

Barr went on to set out what would have to be in place. AI in financial services can only succeed long term with appropriate guardrails, he said, and he counted four: strict authentication, explicit consent verification for specific actions, technical controls limiting agent capabilities, and constraints grounded in both rules and values. Saying that four things must be built points at the same spot as saying that all four are currently empty.

A similar scene reached paper first in another industry. United Airlines amended its contract of carriage on November 11 to bar unauthorized personal AI assistants from booking and ticketing flights ([related article](/blog/united-airlines-blocks-ai-agent-booking/en/)). That, though, was one company drawing a line in its own terms. This time a governor of the central bank that runs monetary policy and supervises banks wrote the same gap into a public speech, and the subject was the banking system as a whole.

## Three Questions the Data Cannot Answer

The three words Barr lined up in one sentence are authenticity, consent, and alignment. Most coverage has carried them as a single bundle, but each is a different question and each asks for different data.

Authenticity asks whether the program that sent this request really is the assistant that customer uses. In front of a bank server today, a person and a program arrive in the same shape. An assistant handed an ID and a password is indistinguishable from the customer at the login screen. The moment one set of credentials changes hands, everything that account can do travels with it.

Consent asks whether the customer agreed to this action. The qualifier Barr attached when he listed the guardrails, "for specific actions," catches here. A blanket agreement clicked once at sign-up explains nothing about the single transfer that went out this afternoon. The question only gets an answer if consent is kept as an event that happens per action rather than a setting parked on the account.

Alignment asks whether the action stayed inside what the customer asked for. "Move the spare cash to whichever account pays more" and "send today's entire balance to another bank" are different instructions. If an assistant that heard the first did the second, authentication was not broken and consent was not missing. It is a case that cannot be sorted out because nothing on record says how far the delegation reached.

Set the three side by side and the common thread shows. Not one of them asks about model performance. An assistant that compares rates better, or calculates more accurately, leaves these three fields exactly as empty as before, because the request that reaches the bank server does not carry that information.
