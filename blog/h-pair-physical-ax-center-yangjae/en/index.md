# H-PAIR Is Building a Real Home in Seoul Where Robots Practice

_Seoul National University and the city of Seoul will open a 338-square-meter living lab with a living room, kitchen and bedroom in Yangjae in the first half of 2027_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> A real home where robots can practice care and everyday assistance is going up in Yangjae, Seoul. H-PAIR, the national research laboratory at Seoul National University's College of Engineering, signed an agreement with the Seoul city government on October 30 to build a 612-square-meter Physical AX Center inside Seoul AI Hub. The first phase takes a little over half of that, a 338-square-meter everyday-support living lab, and it opens in the first half of 2027.

> The order matters more than the floor area. The space is finished in the first half of 2027, but testing of the robots' performance, safety and user acceptance only begins in the second half of that year. The stage goes up first and the actors come later. Conditions inside the living lab are not fixed, and the environment can be rearranged at any time to stand for a different household; about five companies a year will share the space.

> Sections 1 and 2 follow what Seoul and SNU announced, and Section 4 follows the Physical AI Training Center plan the Ministry of Science and ICT presented the same week. Who owns the records that pile up in that living room, and in what format they are kept, is answered nowhere in either announcement. Three parts are ours: Section 3, which reads the choice of a home as a data problem, the table in Section 4 that gathers the two programs into one comparison, and Section 5, which raises that question.

## A House Goes Up on the Fourth Floor in Yangjae

On October 30, Seoul National University's College of Engineering and the Seoul city government signed a business agreement on physical AI at the city's Seosomun Building 2. Kim Young-oh, dean of the College of Engineering, signed for the university, and Kim Hyun-woo, head of the city's economic affairs office, signed for Seoul. Seven people attended the ceremony, among them Cho Kyu-jin, director of the SNU Robotics Institute. The agreement runs three years and can be extended if both sides agree.

Space is the substance of the agreement. Seoul will build a 612-square-meter Physical AX Center at the main building of Seoul AI Hub in Yangjae-dong, Seocho-gu, and as the first phase will fit out a 338-square-meter H-PAIR everyday-support living lab on the fourth floor. The number 338 is the size of the first room to open, not of the whole center. Let that distinction slip and the center reads as roughly half the size it is.

Inside the living lab go a living room, a kitchen and a bedroom, laid out at full scale along the paths people actually walk. Lighting can be altered, objects can be pushed out of place and fresh obstacles can be set down, so the differences that run from one home to the next are produced deliberately. The space is to be finished by the first half of 2027, and verification of care robots, mobility-assist robots and other living robots begins in the second half. Half a year separates the day the space opens from the day the robots arrive.

The announcement names five kinds of robot for testing: strength-assist robots, mobility-assist robots for people who have trouble getting around, wearable robots, care robots and serving robots. There are three axes of measurement as well: performance, safety and user acceptance. The first two look at what the robot does; the third asks whether the person using it will accept it.

> [!callout]
> At the signing, dean Kim Young-oh said physical AI is "the core technology by which artificial intelligence moves beyond digital space to interact with humans in a real physical environment and create new value."

## What H-PAIR Plans to Do Over Ten Years

H-PAIR is an interdisciplinary body built around the SNU Robotics Institute, drawing in 16 departments and graduate programs and 41 full-time professors. It was selected as a national research laboratory in 2026 and receives ₩95 billion in state funding across the ten years from 2026 to 2035. Some outlets have put the figure at up to ₩100 billion. Its research runs along three branches, everyday support, body-augmenting wearables and intra-body robots, and this living lab takes on the first of them. Wearable robots also sit among the five kinds of robot listed for testing, so the branches do not divide cleanly; the announcement sets the two lists down separately and does not say how they relate.

A division of labor sits behind the split between Gwanak and Yangjae. The H-PAIR laboratory on the Gwanak campus draws on the university's research resources and holds the core of fundamental technology work, while the Physical AX Center in Yangjae becomes the forward base that connects what comes out of there to companies and pushes it through verification, field demonstration and commercialization. That is how the agreement announcement explains it. Seoul takes the space and the cooperation structure, SNU the technical verification and startup incubation. The reason for a separate site in Yangjae lies in that division.

The route companies take in is organized under the name Physical AX Consortium. Care centers, senior housing and welfare centers under the Seoul city government put forward the problems they run into on site, and companies develop AI and robot technology against those problems. What they build goes through technical advice and verification at the H-PAIR living lab, then returns to the field as a pilot. One consortium is to run as a trial in 2027, and from then on about two projects are added each year.
