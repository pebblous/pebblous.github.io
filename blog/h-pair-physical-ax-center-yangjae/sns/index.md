## LinkedIn (KO)

서울 양재에 거실과 주방, 침실을 갖춘 집 한 채가 2027년에 들어선다. 사람이 들어가 살 집이 아니라 돌봄 로봇이 살림을 연습할 집이다.

서울대학교 공과대학의 H-PAIR 국가연구소와 서울시가 10월 30일 협약을 맺었다. 서울 AI 허브 메인센터에 612제곱미터 규모의 'H-PAIR 피지컬 AX 센터'를 조성하고, 먼저 여는 한 칸이 338제곱미터짜리 일상지원 리빙랩이다.

면적보다 눈여겨볼 것은 순서다. 공간은 2027년 상반기에 완성되지만 로봇 검증은 그해 하반기부터 시작한다. 무대를 먼저 짓고 배우를 나중에 들인다.

안에서 바꿀 수 있는 것이 많다. 조명을 바꾸고 물건의 자리를 흐트러뜨릴 수 있게 설계했다. 어제 없던 장애물도 새로 놓는다. 어느 한 가정을 그대로 본떠 두는 것이 목적이었다면 필요 없는 기능이다. 같은 공간에서 조건이 다른 기록을 여러 벌 만들어 내겠다는 뜻으로 읽힌다.

비어 있는 칸도 있다. 해마다 기업 다섯 곳 안팎이 이 거실을 쓰는데, 거기서 쌓인 영상과 궤적을 누가 소유하고 어떤 규격으로 남기는지는 발표문에 없다. 협약 하루 전 과학기술정보통신부가 내놓은 피지컬 AI 트레이닝센터 계획에도 같은 칸이 비어 있다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단한다. 공간을 함께 쓰는 일과 데이터를 함께 쓰는 일은 같지 않고, 앞엣것이 뒤엣것을 자동으로 만들어 주지도 않는다.

5년이면 스물다섯 곳이 같은 거실을 거쳐 간다. 서로 읽히지 않는 기록이 그만큼 남을지 다음 기업이 이어 쓸 한 벌이 남을지는, 첫 컨소시엄이 고르는 포맷에서 갈린다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A house with a living room, a kitchen and a bedroom will go up in Yangjae, southern Seoul, in 2027. Nobody will live in it. Care robots will practice housework there.

Seoul National University's College of Engineering and the Seoul city government signed the agreement on October 30. The city will build a 612-square-meter Physical AX Center inside the Seoul AI Hub, and the first room to open is a 338-square-meter living lab for everyday assistance.

The sequence matters more than the floor area. The space is finished in the first half of 2027, and robot testing begins in the second half. The stage goes up first and the cast arrives later.

Much of the interior is meant to move. Lighting can be changed and objects can be left out of their usual places. Obstacles that were not there yesterday can be set down. None of that would be needed to copy one apartment faithfully. It reads as an intention to produce several different records of the same room.

One column is blank. About five companies a year will work in that living room, and neither the agreement nor the briefing says who owns the video and the trajectories that accumulate there, or in what format they are kept. The Ministry of Science and ICT announced its own Physical AI training center the day before, with the same column left empty.

Twenty-five companies will pass through that living room over five years. Whether what remains is twenty-five records nothing can read together, or one that the next company can pick up, is settled by the format the first consortium chooses.

## Facebook (EN)

Who will own the moment a robot first catches its foot on a doorsill at home?

That question stayed with me while I read about the living lab going up in Yangjae.

Seoul National University's College of Engineering and the Seoul city government signed an agreement on the thirtieth of last month. Inside the Seoul AI Hub they will build a house, with a living room, a kitchen and a bedroom laid out at the distances of an actual home, and from the second half of 2027 they will bring care robots and mobility assistance robots into it for testing.

One line in the design held me.

The lighting can be changed, and the objects can be moved out of their usual places.

You would not need that to reproduce a single apartment faithfully. I read it as an intention to produce several records of the same room under conditions that differ each time.

Which makes the place less a house than 'a stage whose conditions can be rearranged.'

Once a stage stands, records accumulate on it. There is the care center that brought the problem in from the field, the company that brought the robot, and the institute that runs the testing. While the sensors turn, three parties are writing at once.

"Whose record is it, of the three? Can the company that arrives next year pick it up and keep going?"

The announcement puts numbers on the size of the space, on the month the doors open, on how many companies will be supported each year. Only the column for ownership and format is empty.

Two recordings made in the same living room, by the same sensors, stay unreadable to each other if the labeling scheme, the coordinate frame and the definition of failure do not agree. A record needs a grammar too.

Pebblous quantifies the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. What goes missing on that work is not usually poor data. It is the data nobody kept. The clip where the robot catches the doorsill and drops the cup is the most expensive material you have for teaching the next robot, and a test report usually keeps only whether it passed.

The doors of that living room open in the second half of 2027.

I hope one more thing opens with them. A column, filled in beforehand, saying what bones the records coming out of that room will be given.
