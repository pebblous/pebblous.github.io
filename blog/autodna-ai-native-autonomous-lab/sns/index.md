## LinkedIn (KO)

자연어로 적어 넣은 요청 한 줄에서 여드레짜리 DNA 실험이 시작됐다.

상하이교통대 연구진이 11월 4일 네이처에 실은 AutoDNA다. 인공지능 에이전트 여섯이 자연어로 의논해 실험 절차를 정하고, 장비를 움직일 제어 코드까지 직접 쓴다. 사람이 중간에 절차를 승인하거나 코드를 손보는 자리가 설계상 없다.

가장 긴 시연은 디지털 정보를 DNA에 넣었다가 다시 읽어 내는 왕복 실험이었다. 하드웨어 조작 1만 1,382단계가 약 203.5시간 이어졌고, 처음 넣은 문장이 그대로 복원됐다.

이 길이를 버티게 하는 것은 코드를 바로 장비에 내리지 않는 설계다. 가상 실험실에서 먼저 돌려 액체의 양을 따라가고 용기가 제자리에 있는지 확인한 뒤에야 실제 장비로 간다. 어느 실험에서는 시료를 넣기도 전에 형광값을 읽으려 한 코드가 그 단계에서 걸러졌다.

'사람 개입 없음'에는 단서가 붙는다. 그 여드레 동안 시약을 채우고 폐기물을 치운 것은 사람이고, 기록된 하드웨어 오류 열넷 가운데 여섯은 사람이 손을 대야 했다. 연구진이 직접 적어 둔 한계는 따로 있다. 모델의 무작위성이 실험 절차의 재현성에 영향을 줄 수 있다는 대목이다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단한다. 1만 1,382단계는 기계가 한 일이자 기계가 남긴 기록이고, 그 기록이 다음 모델의 학습 데이터가 된다.

무인 실험실이 늘어날수록 데이터 품질을 묻는 자리는 결과값에서 로그 설계로 옮겨 간다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

One request typed in plain words set off a DNA experiment that ran for eight days without anyone directing it.

AutoDNA, built by researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, appeared in Nature on November 4. Six AI agents settle the procedure in conversation and write the code that drives more than 20 instruments. There is no point in the design where a person approves a step or edits the code.

The longest demonstration put digital information into DNA and read it back out. It came to 11,382 hardware steps over about 203.5 hours, and the original sentence returned intact.

What carries a run of that length is a design that keeps code off the bench at first. Everything runs through a virtual laboratory that tracks liquid volumes and checks that vessels sit where they should. In one experiment that stage caught code attempting to read a fluorescence value before the sample had been loaded.

The phrase no human intervention comes with a qualifier. People topped up reagents and cleared waste across those same eight days, and of the fourteen hardware errors logged, six needed a person's hands. The limit the team wrote down for itself sits elsewhere: randomness in the model can affect the reproducibility of a procedure.

Pebblous weighs the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Those 11,382 steps are both work a machine did and a record a machine wrote, and the record is what the next model learns from.

As laboratories run unattended, the place where data quality gets asked about moves from the output values to the design of the log.

## Facebook (EN)

On the eighth day somebody still walks into that room.

They top up the reagents that ran low, clear the waste that piled up, and walk out again.

Nobody in between decided what would be done or how.

This is AutoDNA, which researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University published in Nature on the fourth. Six AI agents settle the procedure among themselves in plain language and write the code that moves the instruments. One round trip that put digital information into DNA and read it back out took 11,382 hardware steps, and the sentence that went in came back the same.

What lasted longest out of a lab notebook was never the numbers. It was why the buffer was changed, which paper the choice rested on.

That place is not empty in AutoDNA. What the six say to one another stays behind, denser in form than a human notebook, and the team put those exchanges on Zenodo along with the sequencing data.

The trouble is that the limit the paper sets for itself cuts into the value of that record.

If randomness in the model can shake the reproducibility of a procedure, then the reason for choosing this condition, as written in the log, might be the causation that actually operated, or an account composed plausibly afterward.

The same suspicion attached to notebooks people wrote. The difference is that the writer could be asked, and the writer answered for the judgment.

Pebblous scores the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. The verdict lands on data already through the door, so how that data came about is usually settled business by then. With records that accumulate on their own, whether the route survives at all is decided where the log is designed.

"In the automatically generated records you take in, who is there to ask why this condition was chosen?"

A small instance sits inside the news itself. For the same experiment on the same system, the team's project page reads 11,386 steps and the published Nature version reads 11,382. The project page still points at the preprint, which looks like where the gap comes from.

Neither record is wrong. Neither one says beside it which version it belongs to.

A record left by a machine does not gather into one set of its own accord.
