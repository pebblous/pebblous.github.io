# Korea

_Effective March 9, 2027 — the draft decree now out for comment sets four conditions, from naming an officer to deleting the data when the need ends_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Where pseudonymized or anonymized data will not carry an AI project, a path opens to train on personal data in raw form. The document that spells out the terms of entry is now public. On November 23, 2026 Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission [opened public comment on a draft amendment to the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act](https://www.pipc.go.kr/np/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?bbsId=BS074&mCode=D010030000&nttId=12663). These are the detailed standards for the AI exception that was promulgated on September 8, 2026 and takes effect on March 9, 2027.

> The draft asks for four things. Appoint an officer for the personal data used under the exception and put an internal management plan into operation; block any use beyond the scope of the Commission's resolution; limit and control access rights; delete the data once the need to use it ends. On top of that, four situations — among them any use of sensitive or unique identifying information — now call for a risk factor assessment before the review itself. The question has moved from whether personal data may train AI to what a company must prove before it does.

> The statute is settled; the decree is still a draft. Comments are open until January 4, 2027, and review by the Ministry of Government Legislation follows. Sections 1 through 5 set out the promulgated Act, the draft as pre-announced, and the facts carried in same-day reporting. Section 6, which asks where these record-keeping duties overlap with data quality governance, is this article's own reading.

## The Act is settled, the details are still a draft

The parent statute behind this notice has already cleared the legislature. The amended Personal Information Protection Act passed the National Assembly plenary on August 20, 2026, went to the government on August 28, and was promulgated on September 8 as Act No. 21910. It takes effect six months later, on March 9, 2027. The amendment inserts a new Section 5 into Chapter 3 for "special provisions on the processing of personal information for the development of artificial intelligence technology," running from Article 28-12 to Article 28-15.

Here is the path the Act opens. Personal data that was lawfully collected may be used for AI development beyond the purpose for which it was gathered. Improving the performance of a model already built counts as development. What a controller cannot do is make that call alone. The Commission has to review and resolve the request, and every requirement written into the Act has to be met.

Two of those requirements were left blank. The Act did not say which safeguards a controller must have in place, nor which cases call for a risk factor assessment first; it handed both to the Presidential Decree. The draft pre-announced on November 23 is the document filling in those blanks. Comments are due by January 4, 2027, through three channels — the government's public legislation portal, the Commission's email address, and ordinary post. The Personal Information Protection Policy Division is handling it.

One distinction is worth making here. The statute is final; the decree is a draft. A legislative pre-announcement is a comment period, and review by the Ministry of Government Legislation and approval by the Cabinet still lie ahead. The Commission says it will take in comments, clear those steps, and finish the amendment in time for the Act's effective date. So the list of duties below is what the published draft contains, and the fine print may read differently when the final version is promulgated.
