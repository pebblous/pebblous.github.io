## LinkedIn (KO)

AI 사용을 금지한다고 지침에 적어 둔 연구에서, 지급 결정이 기록된 60건은 한 건도 빠짐없이 승인되고 돈이 나갔다. 거절은 없었다.

10월 7일 arXiv에 올라온 논문이다. 펜실베이니아대와 하소플라트너연구소 연구진이 연구 참가자 모집 플랫폼 Prolific의 제출 기록 712,930건을, 참가자들이 직접 기증한 ChatGPT 대화 기록과 같은 시간축 위에 놓고 맞춰 봤다. 설문으로 물어보거나 글만 보고 판정하는 탐지기를 돌린 것이 아니라, 챗봇을 실제로 연 흔적과 과제가 돌아간 시각을 대조한 관측이다.

AI 보조가 확인된 제출은 1.00%였고, 3년이 넘는 관측 기간에 늘어나는 추세는 잡히지 않았다. 승인 단계에서도 둘은 갈리지 않았다. AI가 확인된 제출은 99.6%, 그렇지 않은 제출은 99.70%가 승인됐고 논문은 두 값을 통계적으로 구분되지 않는 수준으로 본다.

제동이 걸리지 않은 이유는 검사가 닿는 자리에 있다. 플랫폼이 제공하는 LLM 탐지 기능은 연구자가 직접 켜야 하고 자유서술형 문항만 본다. 그런데 AI 보조가 확인된 제출의 52.6%에는 객관식이나 평점처럼 고르거나 적어 넣기만 하면 되는 응답이 들어 있었다. 거절 기준을 적은 플랫폼 안내 문서도 유효한 사유를 다섯 가지로 한정하는데, AI를 썼을 것 같다는 심증은 그 목록에 없다.

과장해 읽을 자리도 분명하다. 60건은 저자들이 연구 지침을 직접 열어 금지 조항을 확인할 수 있었던 범위에서 나온 수이고, 논문도 금지된 사용의 전체 규모는 추정할 수 없다고 적는다. 1.00%라는 비율 역시 ChatGPT 기록만 받아 온 수치이면서 동시에 챗봇을 주 1회 이상 쓰는 사람들에게서 나온 수치라, 논문은 치우침의 방향을 알 수 없다고 미뤄 둔다. 확실한 것은 비율이 아니라 결과의 방향이다. 들여다본 건 가운데 제동이 걸린 것이 없었다.

사람이 만든 데이터를 사다 쓰는 쪽이 여기서 가져갈 것은 1%라는 낮은 비율이 아니다. 금지 조항도 있었고 탐지 도구도 있었고 거절 규정까지 있었는데, 들여다본 건 가운데 걸러진 것이 하나도 없었다는 사실이다. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data를 말할 때 데이터만 들여다봐서는 그 값어치를 정할 수 없다고 하는 이유도 여기에 있다. 규칙이 문서에 적혀 있는지와 그 규칙이 대금을 치르는 단계에서 실제로 걸리는지는 서로 다른 기록이고, 둘 다 데이터를 모으기 전에 정해진다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

In studies whose written instructions prohibited AI use, every one of the 60 submissions that reached a payment decision was approved and paid. None were rejected.

The finding comes from a paper posted to arXiv on October 7. Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania and the Hasso Plattner Institute laid 712,930 submission records from Prolific, a platform that recruits participants for online research, over ChatGPT conversation histories that participants had donated, on a shared clock. Nobody was asked to self-report and no detector was run across the text. The comparison ran between the trace of a chatbot actually being opened and the minutes a task was underway.

AI assistance turned up in 1.00% of submissions, with no detectable rise over more than three years. The approval gate did not separate the two groups either: assisted submissions were approved at 99.6% and unassisted ones at 99.70%, figures the paper reports as statistically indistinguishable.

Why nothing was stopped comes down to where the checking looks. Prolific's LLM detection feature is switched on by the researcher and covers open-ended questions only, while 52.6% of assisted submissions contained bounded responses that are picked or rated from a set. The platform's own guidance on rejection lists five valid grounds, and a suspicion that AI was used is not among them.

The limits deserve stating plainly. That 60 counts only the cases where the authors could open the instructions, confirm a prohibition and find a payment decision on record, and the paper says the overall scale of prohibited use cannot be estimated. The 1.00% rests on ChatGPT histories alone and on 408 people selected for using ChatGPT at least weekly, so the paper leaves the direction of the bias open. What the 60 settle is a direction rather than a rate: among the cases that could be checked, nothing was stopped.

For anyone buying data made by people, the figure to carry away is not the 1%. It is that a prohibition, a detection tool and a rejection policy were all in place, and among the cases that could be checked nothing was filtered out. This is the ground Pebblous has argued from in AI-Ready Data: the worth of data is not settled by looking at the data alone. Whether a rule is written down and whether that rule bites when the money moves are two different records, and both are decided before collection starts.

## Facebook (EN)

A survey page holds two kinds of box.

The one you write into, and the one where you pick an option or set a rating.

On Prolific, a platform that recruits participants for online research, the feature offered for checking whether AI helped with an answer reads only the first kind.

A paper posted to arXiv on October 7 puts numbers on that mismatch.

Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania and the Hasso Plattner Institute took 712,930 submission records from the platform and laid them over ChatGPT conversation histories that participants had donated, on a shared clock.

Nobody was asked. The records were matched. Did the minutes a chatbot was open overlap the minutes a task was underway?

AI turned up in 1.00% of submissions, and across more than three years that share did not grow.

A low number. The two beside it are the ones I read again.

Approval rates of 99.6% and 99.70%. The first for submissions with AI, the second for submissions without.

The paper reports them as statistically indistinguishable. Whether AI was used did not decide whether a submission was approved.

The picture sharpens in the studies whose instructions prohibited AI use. Of those, the 60 submissions with a payment decision on record were all approved. None were rejected.

No rule was missing here.

The prohibition was written into the instructions. The detection feature sat on the platform. A document defining when a researcher should reject existed too.

What those three cover and where the AI did the answering did not overlap.

The detector reads open-ended boxes, and 52.6% of assisted submissions contained responses that only had to be picked or filled in from a set. The document on rejection lists five valid grounds, and a suspicion that AI was used is not among them.

"Does the checking we run only read the boxes a person wrote into?"

This is the condition behind what Pebblous means by AI-Ready Data. The quality of data cannot be read off the data alone; the range it was reviewed across has to be recorded alongside it.

A box nothing checks stays quiet.

The quiet does not mean nothing happened there. It means nobody was looking.
