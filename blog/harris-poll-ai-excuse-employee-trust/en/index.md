# Harris Poll: Workers Doubt the Reason When It Is AI

_In a Grossman Group survey of 2,000 employees and 1,003 managers, 41% say a decision their company attributed to AI had another reason behind it_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> When a company explains a decision by pointing to AI, 41% of employees think the real reason was something else. That comes from The Harris Poll, which surveyed 2,000 non-manager employees and 1,003 managers at companies with more than 500 staff. The Grossman Group, an organizational communication consultancy, commissioned the work and released it on November 19. The study is called the Six Gap Study.

> The same decision drew different reactions depending on the reason given for it. 32% of employees strongly distrusted an explanation that gave AI as the reason, and 15% strongly distrusted one that gave economic conditions. Neither 41% nor 32% is a majority. The finding is not that most employees distrust their employer, but that among the explanations on offer, the AI one gets doubted first.

> Sections 1 through 4 report the published findings and what the coverage carries. Section 5 is the reading Pebblous draws from them. What the numbers point at is not the performance of the AI, but where the grounds for the decision are kept.

### Key Numbers

Four numbers summarize this survey: the size of the doubt, how that doubt splits by the reason given, how far managers and employees read the same thing differently, and the point where distrust turns into an intention to leave.

Source: the Harris Poll Six Gap Study release commissioned by The Grossman Group (November 19, 2026), and the [FinancialContent](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/gnwcq-2026-11-19-new-study-suggests-employee-distrust-is-a-hidden-risk-to-companies-ai-returns) page carrying it.

## Distrust More Than Doubles When the Explanation Points to AI

The survey asked about moments when a company explains a decision that lands on employees, such as a restructuring or a shift in priorities. 47% of employees believe they get the full story when a leader explains one. That is under half, and on its own it is not news. The part that stands out arrives when the reason is narrowed to AI. Only 40% believe they get the full story then.

Asked how strong that distrust is, the difference widens. 32% strongly distrust an explanation that gives AI as the reason, against 15% for one that gives economic conditions. The economy can be checked in the news and happens outside the company. AI happens inside it, and who looked at which data to reach which judgment never comes out. The reason that cannot be checked drew the heavier doubt.
