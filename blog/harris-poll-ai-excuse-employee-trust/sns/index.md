## LinkedIn (KO)

같은 결정이라도 이유를 AI로 대면 강하게 의심하는 직원이 두 배를 넘었다. AI를 이유로 든 설명은 32%, 경기 상황을 이유로 든 설명은 15%였다.

미국 조사기관 해리스폴이 조직 커뮤니케이션 컨설팅사 그로스먼 그룹의 의뢰로 500인 이상 기업의 비관리직 직원 2,000명과 관리자 1,003명에게 물었고, 결과를 11월 19일 내놓았다. 조사 이름은 'Six Gap Study'다.

회사가 AI를 이유로 든 결정을 두고 실제 이유는 따로 있었다고 본 직원은 41%였다. 리더가 그런 결정을 설명할 때 전체 이야기를 듣는다고 믿은 직원은 47%였는데, 이유를 AI로 한정하자 40%로 내려갔다.

두 이유를 가른 것은 설명의 내용이 아니라 확인 경로다. 경기는 회사 밖에서 벌어지고 뉴스로 대조할 수 있다. AI는 회사 안에서 누가 어떤 데이터를 보고 무엇을 판단했는지가 밖으로 나오지 않는다.

불신은 생각에 머물지 않았다. 리더의 설명을 의심해 일하는 방식을 바꾼 적이 있다는 직원이 75%였고, 39%는 아이디어나 우려를 꺼내지 않았다. 업무에 AI를 적극 쓴다는 직원 34% 가운데 27%는 리더에게 알리지 않고 쓴다고 답했다.

조심해서 읽을 대목도 있다. 41%도 32%도 과반은 아니다. 표본은 미국에서 직원 500명이 넘는 회사에 다니는 사람들이고, 조사를 의뢰한 쪽은 사내 커뮤니케이션을 파는 회사다. 두 수치가 왜 갈렸는지는 공개된 자료에 적혀 있지 않다.

리더십을 탁월하다고 평가한 직원은 48%가 AI를 적극 쓰는데, 가장 낮게 평가한 직원에서는 26%였다. 신뢰가 낮은 조직에서 AI 사용은 줄어드는 것이 아니라 보이지 않는 곳으로 내려간다. 회사가 집계한 도입률이 낮게 나올 때, 적게 쓴 것과 알리지 않은 것은 같은 칸에 적힌다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Employees doubt the same decision twice as hard when the reason given for it is AI. Strong distrust ran at 32% when AI was named as the reason and 15% when economic conditions were.

The Harris Poll surveyed 2,000 non-manager employees and 1,003 managers at US companies with more than 500 staff, on commission from The Grossman Group, an organizational communication consultancy. The findings were published on November 19 as the Six Gap Study.

Asked whether a decision their company attributed to AI had really been made for another reason, 41% said yes. 47% believe they get the full story when a leader explains a decision at all; narrow the reason to AI and that falls to 40%.

The gap between the two reasons is not in what they say. It is in whether someone outside the company can check them. The economy happens beyond the company walls and can be matched against the news. An AI judgment happens inside, and who looked at which data to arrive at what never leaves the building.

The doubt did not stay in people's heads. 75% of employees say they have changed how they work after doubting a leader's explanation, and 39% held back an idea or a concern. Among the 34% who say they use AI heavily at work, 27% use it without telling their leaders.

A few cautions sit in the same paragraph. Neither 41% nor 32% is a majority. The sample is people at US firms with more than 500 employees, and the study was paid for by a firm that sells internal communication. The release published the two figures without saying why they diverged.

48% of employees who rate their leadership excellent use AI heavily, against 26% of those who rate it lowest. Where trust is low, AI use does not shrink; it drops out of view. When an adoption rate comes back low, light usage and unreported usage land in the same cell.

## Facebook (EN)

Picture a manager walking out of a room where a reorganization has just been explained. The words came out, a couple of questions were taken, and on the way back to the desk it feels like the message landed.

This survey set that feeling beside what the room reported back. When AI is given as the reason for a decision, 67% of managers believe employees are hearing the full story. Among employees, 40% feel they are.

A second mismatch points the opposite way. 69% of managers think employees feel their jobs are insecure as the company leans further on AI, while 43% of employees say so. Twenty-six points. The fear gets read larger than it is, and the message gets counted as received because it was sent.

62% of employees say they turn to their direct manager when they suspect a leader is not telling them everything. So the place people reach for first is the same place both gaps sit in.

The two gaps answer different questions and cannot be added together. What they share is the direction they lean.

Where is the record that would show how far last month's explanation actually travelled?

88% of employees said they would rather hear bad news directly, including whatever part AI played in it. Nothing restful comes from withholding it.

Pebblous sets a number against the quality of AI training data. DataClinic writes that down. A diagnosis reads what arrived, never what somebody meant to send.

Those twenty-six points are not a measure of how little managers care. They are a distance invisible from the sending end, and closing a distance like that starts with having some way to see it at all.
