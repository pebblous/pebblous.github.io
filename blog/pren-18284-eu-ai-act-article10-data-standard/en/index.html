Executive Summary

prEN 18284, the European standard meant to spell out the training data requirements of EU AI Act Article 10, is still a draft. According to a November 27, 2026 entry on the standards tracker Standards Drift, the document sits with Working Group 3 (WG3) of CEN-CENELEC Joint Technical Committee 21, has not been cited in the Official Journal of the EU, and therefore offers "no presumption of conformity yet." Obligations for Annex III high-risk AI systems apply from December 2, 2027 under the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.

EN 18286, the quality management system standard from the same committee, was published in the Official Journal on October 14, 2026. It is the first harmonised standard cited under the AI Act, and providers that follow it are presumed to meet the Article 17 quality management requirement. Article 10, which asks whether training, validation and testing data are "sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible, free of errors and complete," has no such standard. Providers must decide for themselves how they measured data quality and back that choice with evidence.

The application dates and the EN 18286 citation are settled by law and the Official Journal. The stage and timeline of prEN 18284 come from a private tracker and could not be checked against the official CEN portal. Until the standard arrives, the measurement framework available in practice is the already published ISO/IEC 5259 series. It has not been cited in the Official Journal as a harmonised standard for Article 10, though, so it carries no legal presumption.

Digital Omnibus 2026/1744 Pushes Annex III High-Risk Duties to December 2, 2027

The EU has postponed the high-risk obligations of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) once. The amending regulation known as the AI Omnibus, (EU) 2026/1744, was published in the Official Journal on July 24, 2026 and entered into force on July 27. More than ten compliance outlets, including Obligara, GlobalSuite Solutions and Praxikon, report the same timeline. The application date for Annex III high-risk systems, originally August 2, 2026, moved 16 months later to December 2, 2027.

AI built into products already governed by EU product legislation, such as medical devices and machinery (Annex I), moved a year, from August 2, 2027 to August 2, 2028. The prohibited practices (Article 5) and the obligations for general-purpose AI models already apply. For Annex III systems, the Article 10 data requirements covered here take effect on December 2, 2027. Counted from this article's publication date of November 29, 2026, that leaves 368 days. Standards Drift, counting from November 27, gives the same interval as 370 days.

The two rows in the table below were delayed by different amounts: the first by 16 months, the second by 12. This article concerns the first row. It covers systems used for a high-risk purpose in their own right, without relying on another product regulation.

Scope Original date After the Omnibus
Annex III high-risk systems (hiring, credit scoring, biometrics, etc.) 2026-08-02 2027-12-02
AI embedded in Annex I regulated products (medical devices, etc.) 2027-08-02 2028-08-02

Late standards were behind the postponement. Throughout 2025, observers warned that the harmonised standards needed to turn high-risk requirements into practice would not be ready by the original date, and Pebblous covered that story in "On August 2, the EU AI Act's switches aren't the ones you think." Ten of the 16 extra months have now passed, and the standard meant to support Article 10 is still a draft.

Annex III Providers and Deployers in Hiring, Credit Scoring and Biometrics Are Covered

Annex III of the AI Act lists high-risk uses in eight areas: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education and vocational training, employment and worker management, access to essential services such as credit scoring and pricing of life and health insurance, law enforcement, migration and border control, and the administration of justice and democratic processes. Companies outside the EU, including Korean ones, are covered when they place such systems on the EU market or when the system's output is used inside the EU.

The party directly bound by Article 10 is the provider, which builds the system and places it on the market. Article 16 requires providers to meet every requirement in Chapter III, Section 2, and Article 10 sits there. Deployers, which bring a system in and use it, are not exempt either. Article 26(4) requires deployers, to the extent they control the input data, to ensure that data is relevant and sufficiently representative for the intended purpose.

In one place the existence of a harmonised standard changes the procedure itself. Under Article 43(1), a provider of an Annex III point 1 biometric system may choose self-assessment based on internal control only if it has applied harmonised standards or common specifications. If no standard exists, or it was applied only in part, the system must be assessed by a notified body. With no standard covering the data requirements today, the self-assessment route for the Article 10 portion is closed to biometric providers.

3

EU AI Act Article 10 Requires Representative, Complete, Error-Free Training, Validation and Test Data

Article 10(3) of the AI Act states that training, validation and testing data sets "shall be relevant, sufficiently representative, and to the best extent possible, free of errors and complete in view of the intended purpose." They must also have the appropriate statistical properties with regard to the persons or groups the system is meant to be used on. Paragraph 4 requires the data to reflect the geographical, behavioural and functional setting in which the system will be used. For systems that do not involve training, paragraph 6 applies these requirements only to the testing data sets.

Paragraph 2 lists eight governance practices to apply to data sets. In the order of the text, they are:

  • (a) the relevant design choices about the data
  • (b) data collection processes and origin, and for personal data, the original purpose of collection
  • (c) preparation operations such as annotation, labelling, cleaning, updating, enrichment and aggregation
  • (d) the assumptions about what the data is supposed to measure and represent
  • (e) an assessment of the availability, quantity and suitability of the needed data sets
  • (f) examination of biases that could affect health, safety, fundamental rights or lead to discrimination
  • (g) measures to detect, prevent and mitigate the biases found under (f)
  • (h) identification of data gaps or shortcomings that prevent compliance, and how they are addressed

The text says only what must be done. It does not state which metric measures "sufficiently representative" or what value counts as enough. Recital 67 explains that the qualifier "to the best extent possible" acknowledges the need to balance against competing constraints such as privacy protection. prEN 18284 was planned as the document that fills that blank.

The Article 10 requirements also connect to technical documentation. Article 11 and Annex IV point 2(d) require the technical documentation to include datasheets describing the training methods and data sets: the data's provenance, scope and main characteristics, how it was obtained and selected, labelling procedures and cleaning methods. Under Article 99(4), breaching Article 10 can bring an administrative fine of up to 15 million euros or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

4

prEN 18284, Started September 2024, Has Been a Draft at JTC 21 WG3 for 26 Months

The official title of prEN 18284 is "Quality and governance of datasets in AI." The "pr" prefix marks a European standard that is not yet final. According to Standards Drift, its work item number is JT021037 and it is assigned to WG3 of CEN-CENELEC JTC 21. It entered the drafting stage on September 5, 2024. Its current stage reads "Draft standard: in drafting," it has no Official Journal citation, and its next milestone is "not announced."

The same record shows no movement for this reference since the site began its day-to-day list comparisons on September 28, 2026. Public enquiry is the step at which a draft is opened to the public and national standards bodies submit comments, and prEN 18284 has not reached it. Only after enquiry and a formal vote does it become an EN, and it takes legal effect only when the Commission cites its reference in the Official Journal.

The legal basis for the work is the Commission's standardisation request. Decision C(2025)3871 of June 23, 2025 replaced the 2023 request C(2023)3215 and asked CEN and CENELEC for standards supporting the requirements for high-risk AI systems. Praxikon describes the scope of prEN 18284 as dataset design choices, collection and preparation, and the metrics and methods used to assess representativeness, relevance, completeness and accuracy. That structure targets Article 10(2) and 10(3) almost directly.

Newsletters on AI standardisation and material from European research consortia say the technical report CEN/CLC/TR 18115, published in 2024, laid the conceptual groundwork, and that prEN 18284 is building normative requirements on top of it. The same sources say the draft was substantially rewritten in 2025 and 2026 around the data life cycle and a data quality model. This history comes from private secondary sources and was not confirmed against official CEN documents.

5

EN 18286 Reaches the Official Journal While Article 10 Data Has No Presumption of Conformity

Article 40(1) of the AI Act presumes that a high-risk system conforming to a harmonised standard whose reference is published in the Official Journal meets the requirements that standard covers. For companies the difference is real: if a regulator raises a concern, the burden of proof shifts to the authority. Without a standard, the provider must show from scratch that the method it chose meets the requirement.

Of the documents JTC 21 has produced, one has gained that presumption. The Commission adopted Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2274 on October 12, 2026, and on October 14 published EN 18286:2026, "Quality management system for EU AI Act regulatory purposes," in the Official Journal. The decision itself does not state which AI Act provisions the standard covers. The law firm Orrick and several compliance outlets read it as the basis for the Article 17 quality management requirement. As of October 17, it was the only AI Act standard with its reference in the Official Journal.

Within the same set of high-risk obligations, then, the side dealing with documentation and management procedures has a harmonised standard, while Article 10, which deals with the quality of the data itself, has none. A provider can build a quality management system under EN 18286 and still gain no presumption that the data sets that system manages are representative and complete. This asymmetry is Pebblous's interpretation. The status of each document is a confirmed fact, but reading the two side by side is a combination not found in the original sources.

The law also provides for standards that never arrive. Article 41 allows the Commission to set common specifications by implementing act when harmonised standards are missing or insufficient. As of late November 2026, we found no official announcement that common specifications for Article 10 had been adopted.

6

ISO/IEC 5259 and Third-Party Verification Offer Measurement Outside the Official Journal

JTC 1/SC 42, the joint committee of ISO and IEC, has been publishing ISO/IEC 5259, "Data quality for analytics and machine learning," since July 2024. Part 1 covers overview and terminology, Part 2 data quality measures, Part 3 data quality management requirements, Part 4 the data quality process framework, Part 5 data quality governance, and Part 6 is a technical report on visualization. Part 2 builds on the quality characteristics of ISO 8000 and ISO/IEC 25012 and 25024 and defines a formula for each characteristic, such as completeness, accuracy and representativeness.

According to the catalogue of the standards distributor iTeh, Parts 2 and 4 have already been adopted as European standards, EN ISO/IEC 5259-2:2025 and EN ISO/IEC 5259-4:2025. An EN number does not make a harmonised standard, however. Because the Commission has not cited them in the Official Journal in connection with Article 10, they carry no presumption of conformity under Article 40. Following ISO/IEC 5259 does not, by itself, create a presumption that Article 10 has been met.

The AI Act also mentions relying on third parties. Recital 67 says providers should be able to rely on third parties offering certified compliance services, including verification of data governance, data set integrity, and data training, validation and testing practices. Recitals do not bind like articles, but this one shows the legislator expected external verification results to be offered as evidence of Article 10 compliance.

Pebblous's interpretation: while no harmonised standard exists, the most defensible combination is to adopt ISO/IEC 5259 as the internal data quality benchmark and document its results with third-party test reports. Before a regulator, "values measured under an international standard and confirmed by an outside body" are easier to explain than "a standard we set ourselves." The limit remains: this combination does not create a legal presumption either.

Eight Records Data Teams Should Keep Now, Mapped to Article 10(2)

The eight items of Article 10(2) become mandatory on December 2, 2027 whether or not a standard appears. The list below translates them into records a data team can keep. It is Pebblous's summary, not the wording of the law. The parentheses at the end of each line give the legal basis.

  1. Design rationale document: which data was chosen, why, and what was left out. (Art. 10(2)(a))
  2. Collection source and method log: collection date, path and license for each source, and the original purpose of collection for personal data. (Art. 10(2)(b), Annex IV 2(d))
  3. Preparation change history: versions of labelling guidelines, and dates and owners of cleaning, enrichment and aggregation work. (Art. 10(2)(c))
  4. Assumptions about what is measured: a note on what labels and features are taken to represent. (Art. 10(2)(d))
  5. Prior assessment of representativeness and completeness: comparison of the target population with the data distribution, and missing-value rates. (Art. 10(2)(e), 10(3), 10(4))
  6. Bias examination and mitigation record: which attributes were checked for what, and what action was taken. (Art. 10(2)(f)–(g))
  7. Data gaps and response plan: known shortcomings, their effect on performance, and the remediation schedule. (Art. 10(2)(h))
  8. External benchmark measurements and verification: values computed with ISO/IEC 5259-2 measures, and third-party test reports. (Art. 10(3), Recital 67)

Seven of the eight are hard to produce except while the data is being built. Collection paths and preparation history are difficult to reconstruct later, and a bias review only means something alongside the data version it was run on. Once prEN 18284 is final, its metrics and document formats may differ. If the underlying records exist, they can then be reformatted and used as evidence, and that is the reason to start keeping them now.

How to keep labelling-stage records as audit evidence is covered separately in "Every Click on the Labeling Screen Becomes Audit Evidence."

Omnibus and EN 18286 Are Settled, prEN 18284 Is a Draft

The points above fall into three levels of confirmation. Settled means confirmed by legislation or the Official Journal, or consistent across several independent sources. Secondary record means accounts from private trackers and newsletters. Interpretation means a judgment Pebblous formed by combining facts.

Why Pebblous Is Watching This

Pebblous has used the ISO/IEC 5259 series as the benchmark for its data quality diagnostics. Our "ISO/IEC 5259 Series" overview and an analysis mapping 5259-2 measures to DataClinic diagnostic items are that work. Depending on how prEN 18284 defines its metrics, that mapping may need to be realigned. When public enquiry opens, we plan to check first how far the draft's metrics overlap with 5259-2.

The regulation and standards strand of our data quality series (DQ-3) has followed record-keeping duties on the data side. "Korea's PIPA AI Exception Lets You Train on Raw Data If You Can Prove It" was a case where the effective date and conditions were set, and "Under Korea's Data Benefit Sharing, Who Puts a Price on Your Data?" was a case with a plan but no law. EU AI Act Article 10 is a case where the law and its date are settled but the measurement standard is missing. How to measure label quality is covered in the DQ-2 strand, in "Label Noise Audit Counts Wrong Labels a Model Already Memorized." The whole series is on the data quality series hub.
