어린이집에서 점심을 차리고 치우는 동안 장치가 알아서 남긴 식판 사진 13만 6,517건이 학술지 논문의 분석 자료가 됐습니다.

연세대 식품영양학과 함선옥 교수팀과 푸드테크 기업 누비랩이 9월 15일 발표한 공동 연구입니다.

창원시 어린이집 영유아 170명의 기록을 같은 아동 단위로 이어 붙여, 서비스 이용 초기와 약 1년 뒤를 나란히 놓은 설계입니다. 발표에 따르면 처음 제공된 음식의 절반도 먹지 않았던 아동의 평균 섭취율이 그 사이 8.8%포인트 올랐습니다.

영유아 식생활 연구가 오래 막혀 있던 곳은 측정입니다. 24시간 회상법처럼 어른의 기억에 기대는 조사로 같은 아이를 1년간 반복해 재려면 인력과 시간이 크게 듭니다. 이번 연구는 새 측정 도구를 만들지 않았습니다. 급식 운영 화면에 뜨던 숫자가 종단 분석의 관측값 자리로 옮겨 앉은 셈입니다.

한계는 연구팀이 먼저 밝혔습니다. 대조군이 없으니 같은 기간의 자연스러운 성장이나 어린이집의 식단 개선만으로도 섭취율은 오를 수 있습니다. 섭취량은 식판 이미지에서 추정한 값이라, 무게를 직접 달아 맞춰 보는 검증도 아직 남은 숙제입니다. 초기 섭취가 낮았던 집단의 변화는 평균으로의 회귀 가능성과 함께 읽어야 한다는 단서도 저자들이 초록에 적어 둔 대목입니다.

페블러스가 이미 쌓인 데이터를 AI가 쓸 수 있는 상태로 만들면서 가장 자주 마주치는 것도 이런 부산물 기록입니다. 기록이 연구 자료가 되는 문턱은 양이 아닙니다. 같은 잣대로 쟀는지, 같은 아이를 1년 뒤에도 다시 이을 수 있는지가 문턱입니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

136,517 lunch tray photographs, left behind by a scanner while daycare centers served and cleared lunch, have become the analysis data of a journal paper.

The study is joint work by a Yonsei University team led by Professor Ham Sunny and the foodtech company Nuvilab, announced on September 15.

Records from 170 children at daycare centers in Changwon, South Korea, were stitched together per child to compare each child's first weeks in the service with the same child about a year later. According to the announcement, children who had eaten less than half of what they were first served gained 8.8 percentage points of average intake over that year.

Measurement is where research on young children's diets has long been stuck. Methods such as 24-hour dietary recall run on an adult's memory of what a child ate, and repeating that for the same child across a year costs more staff and time than most studies can carry. This work built no new instrument. It moved values that were already accumulating inside daycare operations into the observation slot of a longitudinal analysis.

The research team named the limits first. With no control group, ordinary growth or an improved menu over the same period could raise intake on its own, and because intake is estimated from tray images, validation against direct weighing is still outstanding. The authors also wrote in the abstract that the clearer gain in the low-intake group has to be read alongside regression toward the mean.

Records of this kind are also what we meet most often at Pebblous, where the job is making data that already exists usable by AI. The threshold for turning an operational byproduct into research data is not volume. It is whether one yardstick did the measuring, and whether the same child can still be found a year later.

## Facebook (EN)

In the corner of a daycare kitchen sits a scanner that looks at a child's tray twice: once before the food is eaten, once before the tray is cleared.

The numbers it leaves behind were meant to describe today's lunch. Which room sent back most of the soup. Which menu came back almost untouched.

On September 15, 136,000-odd of those records turned up as the analysis data of a journal paper. A Yonsei University team led by Professor Ham Sunny, working with Nuvilab, stitched the records of 170 children at daycare centers in Changwon together per child and set each child's first weeks against the same child a year later. Among the children who had eaten less than half of what they were first served, average intake rose 8.8 percentage points. Across all the children it went from 63.9% to 68.5%.

The figures are not the striking part. The fact in front of them is. Nobody measured anything new for this study. The measuring had already happened.

"When exactly did a number on an operations dashboard become research data?"

I have started calling records like these "byproduct records." Nobody designed them for research or for training, and they pile up anyway while the work runs. Logs, inspection histories, support tickets all sit in the same place. This is also the kind of material we meet most often at Pebblous, where the job is making data that already exists usable by AI.

Crossing from byproduct record to research data takes clearing a few thresholds. This case cleared three. One device measured under one set of rules, one child at the start stayed linked to the same child a year later, and the records accumulated for 170 children across a full year. Two were not cleared, and the research team named both first. There is no control group, and intake is estimated from tray images without ever being checked against a scale.

The last two are a different kind of problem. Neither can be fixed in hindsight. A control group is decided the moment you choose who gets the feature and when, and the gap between an estimate and a measurement cannot be recovered if nobody ever weighed anything. So the question to ask about our own logs may not be how much has gathered. It may be closer to what we are quietly becoming unable to ask.
