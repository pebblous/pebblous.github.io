연방법원이 국방부의 앤트로픽 공급망 위험 지정을 위법이라고 판단했습니다. 인정된 기술적 근거는 한 건도 없었습니다.

캘리포니아 북부지구 연방지방법원의 Rita Lin 판사는 8월 27일 판결에서 이 지정을 수정헌법 1조가 금지하는 보복으로 봤습니다. 딱지가 붙은 계기는 모델의 결함이 아니라 계약에 적혀 있던 두 개의 선이었습니다. 완전 자율무기에 쓰지 않을 것, 미국인을 대상으로 한 대량감시에 쓰지 않을 것. 국방부가 그 선을 지우라고 요구했고, 앤트로픽은 거부했습니다. 그러자 주로 적대국 계약업체에 쓰이던 범주가 미국 기업에 적용됐습니다.

국방부의 설명은 정반대입니다. 이 갈등은 살상무기나 대량감시에 관한 것이 아니며, 민간 기업이 정부의 기술 사용 방식을 지정할 수는 없다는 것입니다.

판사가 자의적이라고 본 근거는 정부 자신의 행동이었습니다. 같은 시기 장관은 이 회사에 방위생산법 적용을 제안했고, 국방부는 계약도 계속 추진하고 있었습니다.

판결까지 여섯 달이 걸렸고, 그사이 벌어진 일은 되돌아오지 않습니다. 지정 닷새 만에 한 벤처 투자사의 포트폴리오사 열 곳이 국방 용도의 클로드를 교체하는 절차에 들어가 있었습니다. 교체를 결정한 파트너는 이것이 클로드의 결함을 반영한 것이 결코 아니라고 CNBC에 밝혔습니다.

벤더 심사표의 위험 항목은 원래 기술적 사실을 적는 자리입니다. 이 사건에서 그 자리에 적힌 것은 협상에서 벤더가 물러서지 않았다는 기록이었고, 심사표를 넘겨받아 읽는 쪽에서는 둘이 구분되지 않습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A federal court has struck down the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk. Not one technical finding sat behind the label.

Judge Rita Lin of the Northern District of California ruled on August 27 that the designation was retaliation of a kind the First Amendment forbids. What brought it on was not a defect in the model but two lines written into a contract. Claude would not be used for fully autonomous weapons, and it would not be used for mass surveillance of Americans. When the department asked Anthropic to erase those lines and the company refused, a category normally reserved for suppliers tied to hostile states was applied to an American firm.

The Pentagon's account points the other way. Officials there say the fight is not about lethal weapons or mass surveillance, and that a private company cannot dictate how the government uses technology it has bought.

What made the decision arbitrary, in the judge's reading, was the government's own conduct. Over the same stretch the secretary proposed applying the Defense Production Act to Anthropic, and the department kept pursuing a contract with it.

The ruling took six months, and what happened in that window does not come back. Five days after the designation, ten portfolio companies at a single venture firm were already replacing Claude for defense work. The partner who made that call told CNBC the switch in no way reflected a perceived shortcoming of the product.

The risk column on a vendor scorecard is meant to hold technical facts. What went into it here was a record of a vendor refusing to move in a negotiation, and to whoever inherits the scorecard the two are indistinguishable.

## Facebook (EN)

A company can call a product excellent and remove it in the same breath.

In early March, ten startups working with the Defense Department began replacing Claude with another service. The partner at J2 Ventures who backs them told CNBC the switch in no way reflected a perceived shortcoming of Claude, and that most of them called the situation lamentable because the product itself is excellent.

Five days earlier, the Pentagon had designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk.

The cause was neither performance nor failure. It was two lines written into a contract. Claude would not be used for fully autonomous weapons, and it would not be used for mass surveillance of Americans. The department asked for those two lines to be erased, and Anthropic refused. Officials have disputed that the fight is about lethal weapons or mass surveillance at all. Their position is that a vendor cannot decide how the government uses a model it paid for.

On Thursday evening, August 27, Judge Rita Lin of the Northern District of California ruled that the designation was unlawful. One sentence from the ruling stays with me. "The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics."

Reading this case, I found a phrase for it: a grade that hardens as it travels down.

To whoever assigns it, a risk grade is a judgment. They know where the negotiation broke and what the reasoning rested on. But as the grade moves to a contractor, and then to the contractor below that, the reasoning falls away and one line survives. This vendor is classified as a risk.

The ten companies did not move because they had concluded something was wrong with Claude. They were reading a prime contract requirement strictly.

"What is the entry in our own risk column actually resting on?"

A vulnerability report or an outage history is a record you can check. A judgment that grew out of a contract negotiation or a policy disagreement is something else. The two get written into the same cell in the same color, and their shelf life and their answerability are nothing alike.

The problem Pebblous meets over and over in data quality work has this same shape. If you never record where a value came from, there is no way to check it once time has passed. The risk grade on a vendor scorecard is data too.

The ruling took six months, and the swaps finished in that window did not come back with it. The suit in Washington, D.C. is still open.

Recording where a grade came from may matter more than filling the grade in.
