"옵트인이었다면 아무도 옵트인하지 않았을 것입니다." 트위치 최고제품책임자가 공개 방송에서 AI 학습 기본값을 켜 둔 이유를 이렇게 설명했다.

8월 12일 계정 설정에 "Training for Generative AI"가 추가됐다. 스트림과 VOD, 클립, 채팅, 채널에 올린 사진이 아마존 전반의 생성형 AI 학습에 쓰인다. 스위치는 크리에이터 대시보드가 아니라 계정 설정 아래쪽 보안 항목에 있어, 방송을 준비하는 동선에서는 마주치지 않는다.

같은 방송에서 시청자가 자기 영상이 이미 학습에 쓰였는지 물었을 때, 같은 임원은 아마존이 무엇을 썼고 무엇을 쓰지 않았는지 자신은 모른다고 답했다.

핵심은 이 답에 있다. 동의를 계정 설정의 참·거짓 값으로 두면 지금 상태만 남고, 어느 방송이 언제 어떤 동의 아래 수집됐는지는 남지 않는다. 그래서 토글은 앞으로만 작동한다.

문서에는 이전 수집분을 확인하거나 특정 학습 실행에서 제외했다고 통지하는 절차가 없다. 채팅의 학습 여부도 그 문장을 쓴 사람이 아니라 채널 소유자의 설정이 정한다. 되돌릴 수 없는 1차 원인은 재학습 비용이 아니라 그 기록의 부재다.

옵트아웃 기능은 언제든 붙일 수 있지만, 수집 시점에 적지 않은 동의는 나중에 복원되지 않는다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

"If it was opt in, nobody would opt in." That is how Twitch's chief product officer explained, on a live stream, why the company shipped its new AI training setting switched on.

The setting appeared in account settings on August 12. It covers streams, VODs, clips, stream chat, and photos posted to a channel, and it sits under security and privacy rather than in the Creator Dashboard, off the path a creator walks to go live.

Asked on that same stream whether a viewer's footage had already been used, the same executive said he does not know what Amazon used and what it did not.

That answer is the story. When consent is held as a true/false value in account settings, only the present state survives, and which stream was collected when, under what consent, does not. So the toggle can only work forward.

The documentation describes no way to check earlier collection or to be told your content was excluded from a given training run, and chat is governed by the channel owner's setting rather than by the person who typed the sentence. The first reason this cannot be undone is not the cost of retraining. It is the missing record.

Bolting on an opt-out is always possible, but consent that was not written down at collection time cannot be reconstructed later.

## Facebook (EN)

A streamer with years of broadcasts already sitting on Amazon's servers opens account settings on August 12 and finds a switch they have never seen. It is called Training for Generative AI. It is already on.

Turning it off takes a second. The question that arrives right after stays longer.

"So what happens to everything I already streamed?"

A viewer asked exactly that on a live stream. Twitch's chief product officer answered that he does not know what Amazon used and what it did not.

I read that less as an executive holding something back than as an executive with nowhere to look it up.

A switch in account settings holds one thing: whether this account permits it right now. Change the value and the old value is gone, along with the moment it changed.

While consent exists only in that shape, deciding to undo something never gets you as far as what should be undone.

The phrase I kept returning to while writing this was a "consent field." Not a switch on a settings screen, but a value attached to each piece of content: when this stream was collected, and what the owner's consent state was at that moment.

None of that calls for new infrastructure. It is closer to adding a few values to a collection pipeline that already runs. The hard part is not storage. It is the discipline to write down something that never shows up in a benchmark score.

That is also why, when Pebblous talks about AI-Ready data, this record sits beside the quality. What gets deferred does not get reconstructed later, and what stays in its place is the sentence we heard this week.

Ask your own datasets the same question. Does the answer come back as a query result, or as somebody's memory?
