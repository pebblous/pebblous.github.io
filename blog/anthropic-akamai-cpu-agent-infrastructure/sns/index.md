## LinkedIn (KO)

앤트로픽이 7년간 116억 달러를 쓰기로 한 계약서에 붙은 이름표는 모델을 학습시키는 가속기가 아니라, 코드를 돌리고 웹을 여는 범용 칩이었다.

아카마이가 9월 24일 발표한 계약이고, 창사 이래 서명한 가장 큰 건이다. 넉 달 전 같은 두 회사가 맺었던 계약의 여섯 배가 넘는다.

돈과 지분이 서로 반대로 흐르는 것도 낯설다. 인프라를 파는 쪽이 AI 회사에 투자하는 익숙한 그림 대신, 이번에는 아카마이가 앤트로픽에 자사 지분 약 5%까지 살 수 있는 신주인수권을 줬다.

성격을 정하는 것은 금액이 아니라 전력 단위다. 앤트로픽이 봄에 맺은 설비 계약은 기가와트로 적힌다. 아마존과는 최대 5기가와트였다. 아카마이가 올해 서명한 클라우드 계약은 전부 합쳐도 95~105메가와트다. 같은 종류의 장비를 사는 두 계약에서 나오기 어려운 비율이다.

장비를 다루는 조항도 같은 방향을 가리킨다. 7년 동안 교체하거나 성능을 올려 줄 의무가 계약에 없다. 범용 칩은 세대가 바뀌어도 하던 일을 계속하지만, 학습용 가속기는 한 세대만 밀려도 쓸모가 크게 달라진다.

다만 아카마이는 앤트로픽이 이 자원으로 무엇을 할지 밝히지 않았다. 에이전트 수요와의 연결은 계약서가 아니라 업계 배경에서 나온 읽기다. 앤트로픽이 범용 칩을 처음 사는 것도 아니고, 가속기 수요가 꺾였다는 신호로 읽을 근거도 없다. JP모건은 아카마이가 올해 서명한 다년 계약의 93%가 한 고객에 쏠렸다며 투자의견을 중립에 뒀다.

그래도 이 계약이 남기는 질문은 칩 이름이 아니다. 에이전트가 하루 종일 파일을 열고 명령을 돌리고 응답을 기다리는 동안 늘어나는 비용은, 예산 문서에 적힌 모델 호출료 한 줄에 잡히지 않는다. AI 비용은 모델 안에서만 자라지 않는다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The label on the contract where Anthropic agreed to spend $11.6 billion over seven years is not the accelerators that train models. It is the ordinary chips that run code and open web pages.

Akamai announced the agreement on September 24, the largest it has signed in its history and more than six times the deal the same two companies struck four months earlier. The money and the equity also run in opposite directions. Instead of the supplier taking a stake in the AI company, Akamai issued Anthropic a warrant for up to about 5% of its own shares.

What settles the character of the deal is not the dollars but the unit of power. The compute contracts Anthropic signed this spring are written in gigawatts, up to 5 of them with Amazon. Every cloud contract Akamai signed this year adds up to 95 to 105 megawatts. Two purchases of the same kind of hardware rarely produce that ratio.

One clause about the equipment points the same way. Nothing in the contract requires a refresh or an upgrade across its seven years. General-purpose chips keep doing their job across generations, while a training accelerator loses much of its value once a single generation slips past.

Akamai never said what Anthropic will do with the capacity. The link to agent demand comes from the industry backdrop rather than the contract. These are not the first general-purpose chips Anthropic has bought, and nothing here says accelerator demand has fallen. JPMorgan, noting that one customer accounts for 93% of the multi-year commitments Akamai signed this year, kept its rating at neutral.

The question the deal leaves behind is not which chip. While an agent spends its day opening files, running commands and waiting on answers, the cost that grows does not fit on the single budget line that reads model calls. AI costs do not grow only inside the model.

## Facebook (EN)

I have sat watching a screen while an agent worked.

A line appears, then stops. A long pause, then the next line.

What was passing through that gap I had never thought about for very long.

On September 24 Akamai announced a seven-year, $11.6 billion cloud agreement with Anthropic, the largest contract in the company's history.

What caught me was not the figure. It was a single word.

The press release said the money supports Anthropic's CPU workload demands. Not the accelerators that train models. The ordinary chips that run code and open web pages.

The compute deals the same company signed this spring come in another unit entirely. Up to 5 gigawatts with Amazon. Every cloud contract Akamai signed this year adds up to something near 100 megawatts.

One set of capacity grows a model. The other puts it to work.

An AMD executive says that in the company's own testing, seven of the eight stages in a realistic agent pipeline ran entirely on general-purpose chips.

That is where my empty screen time had been going. Opening a file, waiting for a command to finish, starting the same round over when a format does not match. Not computation. The time it takes to reach the data.

"In our own budget, does the cost of building a model sit on the same line as the cost of putting one to work?"

What we write down first in a data diagnosis at Pebblous runs into that same line. With no record of where the data sits, what format it is in and who may open it, an agent repeats the same search from scratch every time.

People remember after the first attempt. An agent holds no such memory unless somebody builds it one.

That repetition does not arrive wearing the face of a quality problem. It arrives as longer runtimes and a bill swollen with retries.

The waiting has no name yet. No invoice carries a line item for it.
