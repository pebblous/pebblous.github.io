## LinkedIn (EN)

California has become the first U.S. state to ask that the profits of AI be shared with the workers it displaces.

An executive order Governor Gavin Newsom signed on May 21, N-6-26, directs state agencies to study — alongside safety nets and retraining — mechanisms for sharing the gains AI companies create with workers and the public. This is a different kind of demand from the AI labor rules that came before it. A duty to give advance notice of layoffs can be met from personnel records a company already keeps; a demand to share the gain cannot, because it first requires knowing how large the productivity gain from automation is and where it went.

The order is a non-binding research directive, and the profit-sharing review is not due until late 2026. But the direction is unmistakable: the question is moving from when workers are cut to who owns what automation creates.

What companies should prepare is not paperwork. To answer a demand to share the gain, you first have to be able to explain and measure it.

이 요구가 지금까지의 규제와 다른 지점이 있습니다. "언제 해고하는지 미리 알리라"는 요구는 회사가 이미 가진 기록으로 답할 수 있습니다. 하지만 "그 이익을 나누라"는 요구 앞에서는, 먼저 그 이익이 얼마인지부터 세어야 합니다.

## Facebook (EN)

"AI is taking jobs" is a headline we've all gotten used to.

There's a question we rarely ask right after it.

Where did the gains from those vanished jobs actually go?

This spring, California wrote that question into policy. An executive order Governor Newsom signed in May asks state agencies to study how the profits AI creates might be shared with workers. What we need, he said, is ownership, not charity — not cash handed out, but a stake handed over.

There's a quiet asymmetry in that demand. Asking a company when it will lay people off can be answered from records it already keeps. Asking it to share the gain cannot, until someone first counts how large that gain is.

And we are not very good at counting it yet. We know the top 10% of U.S. households hold about 93% of directly owned stock. We do not know how much of the productivity automation creates flowed where, because most companies have never traced it.

Talking about how to divide something we cannot yet measure — that gap is why we at Pebblous read this less as a regulatory story than as a data one. To share a gain, you first have to be able to explain it.

The regulation hasn't arrived yet. The question already has.
