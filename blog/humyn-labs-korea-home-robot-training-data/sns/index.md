미국 로봇이 배울 집안일 영상을 모으는 주요 거점 세 곳 가운데 하나가 한국이다.

인도 벵갈루루의 Humyn Labs가 11월 18일 액셀이 이끈 시리즈 A로 4,700만 달러를 모았다고 밝혔다. 카메라 두 대를 단 장비를 사람에게 쥐여 주고, 그 사람이 평소 하던 집안일을 하는 동안 찍는다. 연출한 동작이 아니라 원래 하던 일을 찍는다는 점을 회사는 강조한다. 그렇게 모인 영상에 손동작과 물체 접촉의 꼬리표를 달아 프런티어 AI 연구소와 로봇 회사에 넘긴다.

수집은 라틴아메리카와 아시아 20여 개국에서 이뤄지고, 매출의 약 90퍼센트는 미국 고객이 낸 돈이다. 영상이 나오는 곳과 그것을 사 가는 곳이 다르다. 로봇팔이 수건을 개려면 사람 손이 그린 궤적을 아주 많이 봐야 하는데, 그런 자료는 웹에 쌓여 있지 않다. 누군가의 부엌에 카메라를 들여놓아야만 생긴다.

빈칸은 한국 쪽이다. 어떤 가정을 어떻게 찾아 무엇을 찍는지가 원 기사에도 국내 보도에도 없다. 찍히는 사람이 무엇에 동의하고 얼마를 받는지도 마찬가지다. 인도 전자정보기술부는 11월 중순 가사 서비스 플랫폼들을 불러 세 가지를 물었다. 노동자의 동의, 화면에 함께 담기는 가족의 사생활, 나라 밖 AI 개발사로의 반출. Humyn Labs는 그 자리에 없었지만 질문 자체는 회사를 가리지 않는다.

품질이 높은 자료일수록 더 사적인 공간에서 나온다. 그 품질을 만드는 쪽은 장비를 쥔 사람이다. 영상이 누구의 자산이 되는지, 그것으로 학습한 모델이 팔릴 때 무엇이 돌아가는지를 적은 줄은 아직 없다. 동의 범위는 수집하는 순간에 기록해 두지 않으면 나중에 복원할 수 없다. 페블러스가 이 소식을 품질 문제로 읽는 이유가 거기에 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

One of the three main sites where household footage is gathered for American robots is Korea.

Humyn Labs, based in Bengaluru, said on November 18 that it had raised a $47 million Series A led by Accel. The company hands people a two-camera rig and films them doing the chores they would have done anyway, insisting the motion be unstaged. Software then labels each scene, tracking hand movement and object contact, and the footage goes to frontier AI labs and robotics firms.

Collection runs across more than 20 countries in Latin America and Asia. About 90 percent of revenue comes from US customers. For a robot arm to fold a towel, it needs to watch a great many human hands trace that path, and no amount of web scraping produces that. Someone has to put a camera in a kitchen.

What is missing is Korea. Neither the original report nor domestic coverage says which households are recruited, what is filmed, what the people on camera agreed to, or what they are paid. India's IT ministry convened home services platforms in mid-November and asked three questions: whether workers truly consented, how the privacy of family members caught on camera is handled, and whether the footage leaves the country for foreign AI developers. Humyn Labs was not in that room. The questions are not company-specific.

The better the data, the more private the room it comes from. The person holding the rig is the one who makes that quality, and nothing published says whose asset the footage becomes or what returns to them when a model trained on it sells. Consent scope cannot be reconstructed after the fact; it has to be written down at collection. That is why Pebblous reads this as a data quality problem rather than an ethics footnote.

## Facebook (EN)

I was washing dishes last night and thought about the fact that there is now a market that puts a price on this motion.

A company in Bengaluru called Humyn Labs closed a Series A last week.

They hand someone a two-camera rig and record while that person does the chores they would have done anyway. The company is firm that the motion must be unstaged.

The footage is then labeled for hand movement and object contact, and it goes to AI labs and robotics companies in the United States.

Their three main collection sites, they say, are Brazil, India and Korea.

What stopped me was not seeing Korea on that list. It was that nothing anywhere says which households are approached, what is filmed, what the people on camera agreed to, or what they are paid.

I have started calling this the unconsenting background.

The person who takes the rig and signs the agreement knows exactly what they are joining. The family member who walks past while they are at the sink signed nothing. Both end up inside the same recording, holding very different kinds of consent.

"The back of someone crossing my living room: whose data is that?"

India's government has already asked. In mid-November its IT ministry called in home services platforms and pressed them on worker consent, on the privacy of family members caught in frame, and on whether the footage leaves the country. Humyn Labs was not in that room. The questions are not addressed to any one company.

In Korea no one has asked yet, officially. That silence is not the same thing as the question being unfounded.

When Pebblous looks at data, this is what comes before the number. Whose consent is this footage standing on. Does that consent travel with it through resale and the next training run. If it is not written down at collection, there is no way back to it later.

The better the data, the more private the room it comes from. The scenes you can only get in a kitchen or a laundry room are the expensive ones.

Which makes it worth asking, alongside everything else, who that price should reach.
