월드모델 회사들에 3D 학습 데이터를 대는 회사의 대표가, 고객이 무엇을 만드는지 모르는 채로 데이터를 만들고 있다고 말했습니다.

테크크런치 AI 에디터 러셀 브랜덤이 9월 18일 전한 내용입니다. 얀 르쿤이 세운 AMI 랩스는 2026년 3월 10억 3천만 달러를 시드로 받았습니다. 페이페이 리가 세운 월드 랩스는 누적 12억 3천만 달러를 모았습니다. 돈과 화제는 넉넉한데 그 기술을 어디에 팔 것이냐는 물음 앞에서는 말이 흐려집니다. AMI 랩스에서 월드모델을 맡은 마이클 라바는 패널에서 이야기할 준비가 되면 이야기하겠다고 답했습니다. 이메일로 다시 물었을 때도 아직 연구하고 만드는 단계라는 답이 돌아왔습니다.

침묵의 이유는 둘로 읽힙니다. 어디에 쓸 기술인지 먼저 밝히면 시장으로 가는 길이 드러나고, 그 길이 보이면 경쟁사도 그리로 자금을 모을 수 있습니다. 브랜덤은 류츠신 독자에게 익숙한 암흑의 숲 상황이라고 적었습니다. 여기에 몇 해를 버틸 자금이 붙으면 지금 당장 팔아야 할 압박도 사라집니다. 회사 경계 안에서 보면 합리적인 계산입니다.

경계 밖에는 그 회사가 무엇을 만드는지에 맞춰 일해야 하는 사람들이 있습니다. 피지클의 대표 알렉스 드 비강이 브랜덤에게 한 말은 하소연이 아니라 사양 이야기에 가깝습니다. 같은 창고 장면을 3D로 지어도 쓰임새에 따라 사양이 갈립니다. 로봇 팔이 물건 집는 법을 배울 용도라면 접촉면의 마찰과 물체의 질량이 맞아야 합니다. 영상이나 게임 환경 용도라면 겉모습과 조명, 장면의 다양성이 먼저입니다. 어느 쪽인지 듣지 못하면 양쪽에 조금씩 모자란 자산이 나옵니다. 어느 자산이 좋았는지 확인할 길도 함께 사라집니다.

양이 병목이라는 기존 진단에도 근거는 단단합니다. 스케일 AI는 2025년 9월 글에서 그 시점 공개 데이터셋을 다 합쳐도 로봇 상호작용 데이터가 5,000시간 남짓이라고 밝혔습니다. 목적을 공유했을 때 데이터 품질이 실제로 얼마나 좋아지는지를 보여 주는 수치는 아직 어디에도 없습니다.

그래도 확인된 장면 하나는 남습니다. 만들 물량과 능력을 갖춘 공급사가 조준할 곳을 모른 채 일하고 있습니다. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data를 말할 때 붙드는 구별도 그 앞에서 시작합니다. 학습에 쓸 수 있게 준비됐다는 판정은 무엇에 쓸 데이터인지가 정해진 다음에야 내려집니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The chief executive of a company that sells 3D training data to world model startups says he does not know what his customers are building.

Russell Brandom, AI editor at TechCrunch, reported the exchange on 18 September. AMI Labs, founded by Yann LeCun, took $1.03 billion as a seed round in March 2026; World Labs, founded by Fei-Fei Li, has now raised $1.23 billion in total. The money and the attention are there, and the answers go vague at the question of who will buy the technology. Michael Rabbat, who leads world models at AMI Labs, told Brandom's panel the company would talk about it when it was ready to talk about it. An emailed follow-up said AMI Labs is still in a research and building phase and does not discuss product plans or timelines publicly.

Two reasons sit behind the silence. Naming the use for a technology exposes the road to market, and a rival can then raise money to travel it; Brandom called it a dark forest scenario familiar to readers of Cixin Liu. A seed round large enough to cover several years removes what pressure remained. Inside the boundary of the firm, the arithmetic holds.

Outside that boundary are the people who have to build against whatever the firm is making. What Alex de Vigan of Physicl told Brandom reads less as a complaint than as a question of specification. Build the same warehouse bay in 3D, and a robot arm learning to grip needs accurate friction on contact surfaces and accurate mass; a video or game environment needs appearance, lighting and variety of scene first. Without being told which, the asset falls a little short on both sides, and the supplier also loses any way of knowing which of its assets was the good one.

The older diagnosis, that the shortage is one of volume, still has evidence behind it. Scale AI wrote in September 2025 that every usable public dataset at the time added up to roughly 5,000 hours of robot interaction data. And no figure anywhere yet shows how much data quality improves once a purpose is shared.

One scene survives all of that. A supplier with the volume and the skill in place is working without a target. It is the distinction Pebblous keeps returning to with AI-Ready Data, moved one step earlier: the judgment that data is ready to train on can only be made after someone has said what it is for.

여러분이 최근에 밖으로 넘긴 사양서 첫 장에는 무엇이 적혀 있었습니까.

## Facebook (EN)

In the 3D warehouse scenes Physicl builds, the friction of the contact surfaces and the mass of each object are written in alongside the geometry.

The people making them have not been told what the scenes are for.

If a robot arm will learn to pick things up from it, the friction on the contact surfaces has to be right, and so does the mass of each object and the way it deforms under a grip. If a video or a game environment will be built from it, appearance and lighting and the sheer variety of scenes come first. Matching both at the top level costs several times as much, and matching neither produces an asset that falls a little short on both sides.

Alex de Vigan runs Physicl, a French company that makes 3D data for physical AI. He met the TechCrunch reporter in a hallway at the All-In conference, where Brandom had just run a panel on world models. What he said was that he still does not know exactly what his customers are building. More information, he added, would mean more useful data.

"Which task is this data for?"

Neither Yann LeCun's AMI Labs nor Fei-Fei Li's World Labs has answered that. Naming the use would show rivals the road to market. With a seed round covering several years, there is no hurry to speak.

Scarcity is not the problem here. The volume is there, the skill is there, and what is missing is the thing the work should aim at. "Data without a target" is the phrase that keeps coming back.

The distinction Pebblous keeps returning to with AI-Ready Data begins earlier than that. Having data and having data ready to train on are different things. But the judgment that something is ready can only be made once someone has said what it is for. With the use left blank, readiness has nothing to be measured against.

The question does not stay with world model companies. A spec sheet handed to an outside data supplier usually carries file formats, a label schema, inspection criteria, a delivery date. It rarely carries a sentence about what the data is meant to help judge.
