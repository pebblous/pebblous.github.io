Executive Summary

Korean businesses were running 3,164 scraping setups that sign in to government websites with their customers' IDs, passwords and digital certificates. South Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC) released the survey on November 26 at the 18th National Policy Coordination Meeting, chaired by the prime minister, together with a plan for the safe transmission of personal data. The scraping came from 754 businesses and reached 129 websites run by 35 public agencies.

Of the 41 types of data these businesses pulled from Gov24, the main government services portal, 30 were already available through a safe transmission channel. The PIPC will not stop scraping right away. Instead, each agency will move to APIs and other formal channels during 2027 and 2028.

Logging in to someone's account on their behalf and collecting records is also how many AI agents work today. Services built on Korean public data may find their supply channel changing from 2027.

PIPC Counts 3,164 Scraping Setups by 754 Businesses

The PIPC surveyed scraping of public agency websites from June through August 2026. It found 754 businesses running 3,164 scraping setups across 129 websites belonging to 35 agencies.

In scraping, a service provider takes a user's ID, password or digital certificate, signs in to a website as that user, and pulls information with automated tools. The PIPC described the problem this way: the business, not the citizen, decides "what information is sent, when, where, how and why." Users face misuse of their data and leaked credentials. Public agencies carry the load and security risk of constant automated requests. When something goes wrong, it is unclear who is responsible.

By industry, financial firms such as banks and insurers made up the largest share at 45%, followed by tax, health management and platform businesses. By purpose, the uses included loan and guarantee screening and credit scoring, identity and customer verification, and tax, accounting and social insurance filing on clients' behalf. The data collected included resident registration records, income and tax certificates and vehicle registration files, ranging from birth and family relations to health and taxes.

2

Top 10 Websites Draw 81.5%; Most Gov24 Data Had a Safe Channel

Scraping clustered on a handful of websites. The top 10 websites, including Gov24, the National Tax Service's Hometax, the National Health Insurance Service and the traffic services portal eFine, accounted for 81.5% of all scraping setups.

Gov24 stands out in the survey. Of the 41 types of data businesses scraped from it, 30 (73.2%) could already be sent through a safe transmission channel. The PIPC said businesses "were using scraping out of habit even though safe transmission methods already existed."

More data was scraped out of habit, through sites that already had a formal channel, than for lack of one. By the government's own count, the obstacle to switching is less technology than habit and cost.

3

APIs From 2027 to 2028, No Immediate Ban

The legal basis is an amendment to the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act, approved by the Cabinet on February 10, 2026. It extends the right to have one's personal data transmitted, previously limited to healthcare and telecommunications, to every sector, and allows transmission through a representative only by methods agreed in advance. API connections are the rule. In the short term, scraping is allowed on a limited basis, and only for representatives that have completed prior consultation.

To keep services running, the PIPC will not block scraping at once. It plans the change in three stages.

  • • Stage 1 (2026, prior consultation): Agencies first process the consultation requests already filed. Uncontested cases are handled from late November, and businesses whose cases are agreed may keep scraping by the agreed method for a set period.
  • • Stage 2 (2027–2028, new transmission system): The PIPC issues safety standards that vary with each service's risk level, and each agency picks a channel such as an API and rebuilds its systems in turn from 2027.
  • • Stage 3 (2029 onward, wider use): Experience from the public sector is extended to the private sector. Korea's private-sector MyData program for personal data transmission expands in February 2027.

Businesses that must change their services in Stage 1 get enough time to switch. The government expects users to use services without worrying about leaks, agencies to face less website overload and security risk, and industry to gain room for new services as data silos open. The announcement does not say which agency opens which API, or when. Those schedules are set in Stage 2.

Each agency will keep a standing prior-consultation desk, and issues between agencies go to a pan-government MyData council. Services with legal restrictions or strong public concern get a separate review by the PIPC and the agency on whether to allow them. The PIPC also issued six rules for users and eight for businesses. PIPC Chair Song Kyung-hee expressed hope that "the successful adoption of the personal data transmission system will invigorate a MyData ecosystem that guarantees citizens' right to control their personal information."

Editor's Note: The Same Pattern as AI Agents Logging In for Users

A person hands over login credentials, and whoever receives them signs in as that person and takes information. When a user lets an AI agent into their accounts to gather information from other services, the steps are the same. So is the gap in the record: the data subject is left with no log of what was taken, when, or how much. The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) finalized a rule on October 22, 2024 requiring financial institutions to share personal financial data directly through safe means. The Korean government has now counted the scale of proxy logins in its own public sector.

Teams that take in data to build models or run analyses have two things to check. Can they document whose consent each dataset rests on and which channel it came through? And, as with Gov24, are they scraping data that already has a formal channel? As Korean agencies rebuild their systems from 2027, data supplies that depend on scraping may change format or stop.
