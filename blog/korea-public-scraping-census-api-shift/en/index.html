Executive Summary

Korea now has a number for how often a business logs into a public website as you and takes what it finds there. The count is 3,164. Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC) released the figure on November 26, 2026, at a government policy coordination meeting chaired by the Prime Minister, where it discussed a plan with other ministries for moving personal data to its owner safely. The survey ran from June through August of this year and covered businesses: 754 of them were using this route against 129 websites operated by 35 public agencies. The label the press release puts on the 3,164 is "prior consultation applications (scraping status)." Businesses declared these themselves when they applied, so the figure reads as a floor rather than a full audit.

The concentration is more striking than the total. The top 10 websites account for 81.5% of all cases, among them Government24, Hometax, the National Health Insurance Service and the traffic penalty portal eFine. Take Government24 alone and 30 of the 41 record types businesses were scraping, or 73.2%, already had a safe transfer route in place. Nothing was missing on the technical side. The old path simply stayed in use. From 2027 the government plans to have each agency rebuild these connections around APIs and similar transfer methods.

Sections 1 through 3 and section 5 are facts confirmed in the press release, same-day reporting and the statute. Sections 4 and 6, which read this collection method as structurally the same as an AI agent signing into someone else's service with a person's account, are this article's own interpretation. The phrase "AI agent" appears nowhere in the government's announcement.

1

3,164 cases across 129 public websites

Between June and August of this year the PIPC asked businesses what scraping they were running. The answer came back as 754 businesses, 35 agencies, 129 websites and 3,164 cases. One business scrapes several websites and one website is scraped by several businesses, so the case count runs more than four times the number of businesses.

The survey also published what the scraping was for. Loan and guarantee screening, credit assessment, identity and customer verification, tax and accounting work, and filing on clients' behalf for the four major social insurance programs account for most of it. What moved across was largely the paperwork public agencies issue: resident registration certificates in full and abridged form, income and tax payment certificates, vehicle registration records. From birth and family relations through health and medical records to income and tax, in the press release's phrase, information covering an entire life was in transit. Sorted by business type, banks, insurers and the rest of the financial sector make up 45% of the 754. The remainder spread across tax, health care and platform services, and no ranking within that remainder was published.

The concentration is steeper still. The top 10 websites hold 81.5% of all cases. Government24, Hometax, the National Health Insurance Service and eFine, all on that list, are counters most of the country uses. Scraping is not happening in some quiet corner of the public web. It sits on the busiest paths.

The weight of this survey lies less in how large the numbers are than in the fact that numbers now exist. Scraping was known to be widespread, but how many firms were scraping which sites how many times had never been published. The press release says as much where it states the survey's significance: it confirmed a practice that had been running below the surface. Without a measure of the scale, no transition schedule can be set.

2

The business decides what gets taken

The definition the PIPC uses runs like this. A service provider receives the user's ID, password, certificate and the like, logs into the website of a public agency or other sender on the user's behalf, and then, through automated tools, collects the user's information indiscriminately and unilaterally. Two handovers sit inside that sentence. One is the credential. The other is the authority to decide what will be taken with it.

How far the second handover reaches is something the press release states outright in a sentence of its own.

Scraping is a method in which the service provider, not I, decides on my behalf what personal data is transmitted, when, where to, how and why.

What stands out is that the sentence counts five things separately. The annex breaks the five out as the data transmitted, the purpose of use, the destination, the start and duration of use, and the transmission method, and says the business decides all of them arbitrarily "on behalf of" the person. The same table lists the data subject and the sender side by side as parties with little room to intervene. The agency handing over the data stands outside this structure too.

From the receiving agency's side, what arrived is the user. The login is valid, so there are no grounds to refuse it, and nothing distinguishes a person from an automated tool at the door. That is why the agency cannot set the scope of what leaves. Which of the items visible on screen get taken is decided entirely by the business's code.

API transfer reverses the order. The data subject requests a transfer and the agency sends only the items that were agreed in advance. In the diagram below, the upper row is scraping as it works today; the lower row is what the government wants in place from 2027.

For anyone who works with data, the difference in what record survives matters more. Information that arrives by scraping has one piece of provenance behind it, a login event. Which item was received under which consent is not recorded per transfer, so when someone later asks where the data came from, the only evidence available is the business's own internal log. And the harms the press release lists are not confined to the data subject. Queries that arrive at any hour without regard for system load leave agencies with overload and security exposure, and when something goes wrong, the first argument is about who is responsible.

3

At Government24, seven in ten record types were already replaceable

The most quoted line in the survey is the Government24 case. Businesses were scraping 41 types of records from that site, and 30 of them already had a safe transfer route available. That is 73.2%. Put another way, seven in ten were records that could have been received without scraping at all.

This is not missing technology. It is the inertia of a choice. Once a safe route opens, changing an existing integration still costs development time and verification work. When code is running and the alternative returns the same result, the case for moving is hard to make. Deferred migrations piled up, and the pile reads as 73.2%.

One thing is worth keeping straight when the number is quoted. The 73.2% counts record types scraped at Government24 alone, not a share of the 3,164 cases. The universal claim in the press release is phrased differently: it says that a significant share of the information at the top 10 websites moved by scraping out of habit even though a safe transfer route already existed, and the 30-of-41 figure is the single worked example attached to that phrase. Ratios for the other websites were not published.

4

The decree calls this an agent

In the status box of the press release, the 754 businesses carry a label: "applicant firms and institutions (agents)." Agent is not a metaphor this article reached for. It is the term the regime uses, and the term comes from a clause.

The survey did not appear out of nowhere. It follows a provision already in force. The Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act (Presidential Decree No. 36121), promulgated on February 19, 2026 and effective August 20, 2026, widened the right to request transfer of one's own data from health care and telecommunications to every sector, and attached one condition to anyone exercising that right on someone else's behalf.

Where an agent who has had the data subject make a transfer request under Article 38(1) of the Act uses an automated tool, the method shall be one agreed in advance between the sender of the data and that agent, in order to secure the safety of the personal data.

That is Article 42-6(3)1(b) of the decree. Item (a) of the same subparagraph requires the transfer to be encrypted with a safe cryptographic algorithm, so the two bind together. Scraping is one of the automated tools meant here. Paragraph 5 of the same article states that an agent who carries out a transfer must deliver the personal data to the person without storing it. Pass it through; do not keep it. The dates differ by sector: August 20, 2026 for public system operating institutions and others, February 20, 2027 for the private sector. The private threshold is not revenue alone. On top of average annual revenue above 180 billion won, the entity must handle sensitive or unique identifying information for 50,000 or more data subjects, or personal data for 1 million or more data subjects.

Read the clause for the scene it describes and it comes to this. An automated tool enters someone else's system with a person's authority and takes data out. So the side coming in and the side handing over are told to agree on the method beforehand, and the thing taken is not to be held.

The annex draws the after picture with the same five slots from section 2. What, why and where to are set by the data subject and delegated to the agent; when and how are agreed in advance between the agent and the sender. The five that one party held alone are split in two, one half returned to the person and the other half put on a negotiating table.

The same scene is appearing again under a different name. An AI agent that signs in with a user's account to carry out tasks is structurally the same thing: an automated tool holding delegated credentials, taking data out of someone else's service. The two requirements the decree places on agents, a method agreed in advance and no storage, carry over cleanly into agent design. This reading is ours. The PIPC's announcement and the clause itself say nothing about AI agents, and we found no evidence that the agent provision was drafted with them in mind.

5

Transition through 2028, and one route already blocked this summer

The plan has three stages. This year each public agency opens a prior consultation desk and clears the uncontested applications first, starting at the end of November. Cases that complete consultation may keep scraping in the agreed form for a set period, and where a service change is unavoidable, a transition window follows. One caveat is attached: for services that are legally restricted or raise significant public concern, the PIPC and the agency involved consult separately on whether to permit them at all. From 2027 through 2028 the PIPC issues tiered safety standards graded by service risk, and each agency reviews API and other transfer methods and rebuilds its systems. From 2029 the stage shifts to widening data use. The private-sector data transfer regime takes effect more broadly in February 2027.

Coordination between agencies falls to the Government-wide MyData Council. On paper the roadmap is smooth, but the cost of breaking a habit already showed once this summer.

The Supreme Court took the position that it was not subject to the amended decree's prior consultation, and gave advance notice that from August 20 it would block the system through which family relation certificates were scraped from the online issuance screen, on the ground that scraping that certificate is contrary to law in the first place. The decision rested on existing statute rather than the amended decree, by its own account. With work that depends on that certificate about to stop, including remote mortgage screening, the PIPC formally asked the Supreme Court on August 11 for a reprieve, and the next day the Financial Services Commission convened a review meeting. On that same day the National Court Administration announced a temporary reprieve for financial institutions and others that applied for one by August 31. August 31 was the deadline for applying, and how long the reprieve runs was left to the point at which each applicant finishes moving to an alternative route. The decision did not treat the block as wrong. It bought time to prepare.

That was the fallout from cutting one certificate at one institution. From 2027 the scope to be converted is 129 websites. We read that as the reason the transition schedule stretches to 2028.

6

Why Pebblous is watching this

The route by which data came in sets the range of what the data can be used for. Pebblous has covered why training data mixed into one bucket gets caught in an audit, and the case of Reddit closing its public counter, which left contracts and approvals as the only remaining path. This survey shows the same problem on the public side, with numbers.

None of this says the information that arrived by scraping is wrong. The values may well be identical. But asked to prove where a value came from, under what consent and on what date, the scraping route has thin paperwork to offer. As section 2 showed, nothing is recorded per transfer. The same paperwork is demanded where data gets priced, something already visible in the debate over data benefit sharing.

There is no need to invent a checklist. The "Eight Safety Rules for Scraping Businesses" published with this announcement contains two items that bear directly on data work. The sixth says that when a layout change causes the wrong value to be collected, collection must stop immediately and the value be corrected or deleted. That is a government document stating that values arriving by scraping can be wrong. The eighth says users must be able to see, at a glance, the connected institutions, the items collected, the purpose of use, the collection interval, the most recent collection timestamp and whether credentials are being retained. Those six fields are what provenance and consent proof require, and as section 2 showed, those six fields are exactly what the current route fails to leave behind.

So while the transition window is still open, three lines are worth checking. Does your service take a user's ID and password and sign into another institution on their behalf? If so, has each of those routes been through prior consultation? Is it built to pass the received data through without storing it? Fill all three and 2027 is a scheduling problem. Leave one blank and that line will be hard to fill once the deadline arrives. Articles on the legal foundations of data are collected in the Data Economy hub.

Editor's Note

The survey figures (754 businesses, 35 agencies, 129 websites, 3,164 cases; finance at 45%; the top 10 websites at 81.5%; 30 of 41 record types at Government24, or 73.2%), the survey period, the definition of scraping and the harms listed, the three-stage plan, the prior consultation desk and the Government-wide MyData Council, and both sets of safety rules were confirmed by downloading the full text of the PIPC and Government-wide MyData Taskforce press release attachment, eight pages. The body of the press release page itself is empty. Same-day reporting by ZDNet Korea and Digital Daily matched these figures. The denominators of the three percentages follow the press release's own labeling: 3,164 is counted as "prior consultation applications (scraping status)," 45% is a share of the 754 businesses by type, and 73.2% is the worked example attached to the phrase "a significant share." Remarks by Chairperson Song Kyoung-hee also appear in the attachment but were left out as formal courtesies. The decree's promulgation and effective dates, the presidential decree number, the wording of Article 42-6(3)1(a) and (b) and paragraph 5, the two effective dates and the private-sector threshold were cross-checked against statutory databases and commentary rather than compared character by character with the original on the National Law Information Center. The Supreme Court's blocking notice and its legal position, the PIPC's request for a reprieve on August 11, the National Court Administration's temporary reprieve notice the following day and the Financial Services Commission's review meeting were confirmed in reporting from the same period. All sources for this article are in Korean; the quoted definition of scraping, the decree clause and the titles of the two sets of safety rules are our translations of the Korean originals. The interpretations in this article that are not in the press release are these: the reading in section 2 that the two routes leave different records; the paragraph in section 3 reading 73.2% as the inertia of a choice; the claim in section 4 that the agent provision and AI agents share a structure; the reading of the sixth and eighth safety rules as provenance-proof fields; the final paragraph of section 5; and all of section 6.
