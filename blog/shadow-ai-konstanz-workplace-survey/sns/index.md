## LinkedIn (KO)

직장에서 AI를 쓴다는 사람에게 가장 자주 쓰는 도구 하나를 짚어 보라고 했습니다. 그것이 회사가 공식적으로 들여온 것이냐는 물음에 55%가 그렇다고 답했습니다. 나머지는 각자 알아서 들고 들어온 도구입니다.

독일 콘스탄츠대 Future of Work Lab이 9월 8일 공개한 3차 조사입니다. 2026년 5월에 독일 재직자 1,105명이 답했습니다.

헤드라인은 다른 문항이 가져갔습니다. 업무 중 AI를 쓴다는 응답이 1년 사이 35%에서 38%로 올랐고, 보고서 제목도 성장 둔화였습니다. 그런데 도입이 느리다는 이야기와 도입 경로가 회사 바깥에 있다는 이야기는 서로 다른 문제입니다. 보고서가 인용한 독일 ifo 연구소는 고용주가 들여오는 길과 직원이 들여오는 길이 동시에 열리는 것을 이중 확산이라고 부릅니다. 한쪽 길만 세면 나머지 절반이 통계에서 사라집니다.

그 두 번째 길에 붙어 있어야 할 것이 없습니다. 규모가 작은 조직에서 AI 교육을 받았다는 응답은 11%, 구속력 있는 이용 규칙이 있다는 응답은 10%였습니다. 사다리를 끝까지 올라가 직원 1,000명이 넘는 조직을 봐도 규칙 쪽은 31%에서 멈춥니다.

숨기고 쓰는 장면과는 다릅니다. 3차 조사에서 처음 물은 문항에 따르면 자기 사용을 드러내기 꺼리거나 불편하다고 답한 쪽은 9%였습니다. 회사가 모르는 이유는 직원이 숨겨서가 아니라 말하고 물어볼 자리가 없어서에 가깝습니다.

수치에 따라붙는 단서도 같이 옮깁니다. 45%는 보고서가 적은 값이 아니라 55%를 뒤집어 계산한 값입니다. 표본은 독일 재직자이고, 같은 방향을 가리킨 다른 두 조사는 보안 제품을 파는 회사가 낸 자체 자료입니다. 그래도 남는 것은 이 구조입니다. 도구를 몇 명이 쓰는지 세는 일과 그 도구에 무엇이 들어갔는지 아는 일은 다릅니다. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data를 말할 때 데이터 계보를 앞에 두는 것도 그 차이 때문입니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Asked to name the single AI tool they reach for most often at work, 55% of German employees who use AI said it had come through their employer's official adoption. The rest run on tools each person carried in.

The figure comes from the third wave of the Konstanz AI study, published on 8 September by the Future of Work Lab at the University of Konstanz. It draws on 1,105 employees in Germany, surveyed in May 2026.

A different question took the headlines. Use of AI at work rose from 35% to 38% over the year, a climb slow enough that the report's own title calls the growth slowed. Slow adoption and adoption that arrives from outside the company are separate problems. The report cites work from the ifo Institute that names the pattern dual diffusion: the employer's route and the employee's route open at the same time, and counting only the first leaves the other half out of the statistics.

The second route arrives without the paperwork a tool normally travels with. In small organizations, 11% have had AI training and 10% report binding rules for AI use. Climb to the top of the ladder, above 1,000 employees, and the rules figure stops at 31%.

This is not concealment. Asked for the first time in this wave how openly they discuss their own AI use, 9% said they were reluctant or uncomfortable. Employers are unaware less because the use is hidden than because no place has been made to raise it.

The caveats travel with the numbers. The 45% is 55% turned over rather than a figure the report prints, the sample is German, and the two surveys pointing the same way were published by companies that sell security products. The structure survives all three. Counting how many people use a tool and knowing what went into it are different exercises, which is why data lineage comes first when Pebblous talks about AI-Ready data.

## Facebook (EN)

There is a meeting transcript to clean up before the end of the day, and a tool that already feels familiar.

The company never bought it. Someone tried it alone at some point, found it easier, and kept going.

The University of Konstanz asked 1,105 employees in Germany where that tool came from. Those who use AI were asked to name the single tool they reach for most often, and whether the employer had introduced it officially. 55% said yes. The remaining share had been filled in without anyone signing off.

Employees reaching for a tool first is not new. Technology has always spread that way. The survey also asked what sits alongside that route.

In organizations of fewer than 50 employees, about one in ten had received any AI training, and about one in ten reported rules they were bound to follow.

No rules means no prohibition, which is not the same as freedom. Nobody has said what may go in and what may not, and when something goes wrong the person who decided alone is the one left holding it.

The phrase 'shadow AI' suggests someone working in secret. This survey unsettles that picture. Only 9% said they were reluctant or uncomfortable about discussing their own use. Employers are in the dark less because people are hiding than because nobody has opened a place to say it out loud.

A tool the company brings in has somewhere for the record to sit. Terms for handling data in the contract, access logs, a retention period, a deletion procedure. A transcript or a customer's phone number pasted into a personal account skips all of that. It reads less like an accident than like a design; that route was never built to leave a record. This is where data lineage comes in when Pebblous talks about AI-Ready data.

"Can we name the AI tools in use across this company, together with where the data went?"

Writing one more prohibition may matter less than starting that list.
