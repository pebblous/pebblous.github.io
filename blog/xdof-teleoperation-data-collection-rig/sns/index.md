스텔스를 나온 지 석 달 된 회사가 12억 달러 밸류로 시리즈 B를 협상하고 있습니다.

XDOF는 모델도 만들지 않고 로봇도 만들지 않습니다. 사람이 로봇 팔을 원격으로 움직여 남긴 기록을 모아 프런티어 AI 랩과 로보틱스 기업에 넘깁니다. 다시 조달할 계획이 없던 회사인데 연환산 매출이 5천만 달러에 가까워지자 벤처캐피털 쪽이 먼저 새 라운드를 제안했다고 테크크런치가 전했습니다.

그 몸값의 실물은 6월에 공개된 데이터셋에서 보입니다. 창업자 두 사람이 공저자로 이름을 올린 ABC-130K는 3,553시간짜리 양팔 조작 기록인데, 그 전부가 한 종류의 작업대에서, 세 면을 흰 벽으로 막은 인클로저 안에서 기록됐습니다. 배경 다양성이 좁아진다는 사실은 논문이 스스로 적어 두었습니다.

수집 조건은 이미 상품이 됐습니다. 팔을 만든 I2RT는 지금 같은 구성을 ABC 박스라는 이름으로 9,599달러에 팝니다.

공개된 시간이 그 팀이 가진 전부도 아닙니다. 내부 7,000시간 코퍼스로 사전학습한 정책이 공개분으로 사전학습한 쪽보다 네 과제 모두에서 나았고, 하위 과제 주석이 붙은 분량은 전체의 44%에 그칩니다.

페블러스가 데이터 품질을 진단할 때 먼저 묻는 것은 이 데이터가 어떤 조건에서 만들어졌는가입니다. 로봇 데이터에서는 그 조건이 곧 장비이고, 견적서의 총 시간은 그 조건을 보여 주지 않습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Three months out of stealth, XDOF is in talks for a Series B at a $1.2 billion valuation.

The company builds neither models nor robots. It collects records of people driving robot arms by remote control and sells them to frontier AI labs and robotics firms. It had no plans to raise again; as annualized revenue approached $50 million, investors came to it first, TechCrunch reported.

What that valuation rests on is visible in a dataset released in June. ABC-130K, co-authored by both XDOF founders, is 3,553 hours of bimanual manipulation. All of it was recorded on one kind of workbench, inside an enclosure walled off on three sides in white. The paper states plainly that this narrows background diversity.

The collection conditions are already for sale. I2RT, which makes the arms, now lists that same configuration as the ABC Box at $9,599.

The released hours are also not everything the team held. A policy pretrained on their internal 7,000-hour corpus beat one pretrained on the public data across all four fine-tuning tasks, and sub-task annotations cover 44% of the release.

When Pebblous audits data quality, the first question is what conditions produced the data. In robotics that condition is the rig, and a total hour count on an invoice does not show it.

## Facebook (EN)

A banner sits on the front page of a robotics parts shop. "ABC Box," $9,599.

It is one workbench with two arms on it. A piece of hardware named after a dataset, now something you can add to a cart.

The dataset behind that name, ABC-130K, is 3,553 hours of robot manipulation released in June. XDOF, which sells the pipeline that collects records like these, is negotiating a Series B at a $1.2 billion valuation three months out of stealth.

What stayed with me was not the valuation but a passage in the paper's appendix. Every one of those hours was recorded inside an enclosure walled off on three sides in white. The authors wrote down themselves that this narrows background diversity. It is the price paid for evaluations that hold still.

"Which walls was the robot data we are buying made inside of?"

An invoice lists total hours. But 3,553 hours gathered inside white walls and 3,553 hours gathered in an actual store are hard to price the same. The team kept a separate internal corpus of 7,000 hours for development, and sub-task descriptions cover 44% of the release. Neither shows up in an hour count.

When Pebblous looks at a dataset, the first question is what conditions produced it. With robot data those conditions seem to be the rig itself. Which arms, which camera placement, which walls. If none of that is in the spec, there is nothing to trace back to later.

"Collection conditions turned catalog item." I cannot yet tell how far that goes. What is a little clearer is what belongs in writing before the money moves.
