# When Korea

_The cross-government data pipeline reaches 848 public bodies, yet at the November 6 plenary a treatment date alone could not say whether insurance covers another dental cleaning_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Pooling public data in one place and building the AI service citizens will use are two different jobs. That point was made at the third plenary meeting of the National AI Strategy Committee, held on November 6 at Seoul Square in Jung-gu, Seoul. Bae Kyung-hoon, Minister of Science and ICT and deputy prime minister for science and technology, reported that about 30 AI transition projects built on public data are running across ministries and are being wired into Moduui AI, the government's AI-for-everyone service due in mid-December. In the same room, Cho Jun-hee, chairman of the Korea Software Industry Association, said that securing data and developing a service are separate jobs.

> The example Cho gave is a citizen asking whether health insurance will cover another dental cleaning. The date of the last visit on its own does not answer that. The rule setting how soon the procedure becomes eligible again has to be there beside the date before the question has an answer. The pooling side, meanwhile, is already far from small. The Ministry of the Interior and Safety's cross-government data pipeline reaches 848 public bodies, and 54,768 shared datasets are registered on it.

The public data evaluation scores Section 4 leans on come from outside this meeting, from a separate 2025 assessment by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety.

## The Connection Is Under Way, the Service Is Not

The third plenary meeting of the National AI Strategy Committee, held at Seoul Square on November 6, was the first full meeting Ha Jung-woo chaired after taking office as standing vice-chair. About 60 people, government and private-sector members and heads of related agencies, gathered and approved three items: a legislative framework for artificial intelligence in Korea, a plan for forming and running the committee task forces behind the national AI core tasks, and a report on the state and use of the cross-government data pipeline. The third item is the one this article follows.

Bae reported that each ministry is running about 30 AI transition projects built on public data, and that work is under way to connect them so citizens can reach those services through Moduui AI. He added that connecting the individual datasets is no small task. Moduui AI is due in mid-December, and he has described it as a first step toward the agent era rather than a generative chatbot.

In the same room Cho Jun-hee, chairman of the Korea Software Industry Association, took up the other side. He chairs the committee's industrial AX and ecosystem subcommittee. Securing data and developing a service with that data, he said, are separate jobs. For Moduui AI to become something other than an overseas big-tech service, it has to use public data to actually produce better answers, and that requires each ministry to have development capacity, budget and staff of its own.

> [!callout]
> One side reported progress on connection. The other spoke about the work that remains once connection is done. The two remarks do not contradict each other. They point at different stretches of the same job, and what lies between them is the question the next section asks.

## Can I Get Another Dental Cleaning on Insurance?

This is the example Cho raised at the meeting. When a citizen asks whether health insurance will cover another dental cleaning, what does an AI need in order to answer? At first glance the treatment record looks like enough. The date of the last cleaning is written there.

The date, though, is not the answer. It is the material for one. What tells you where to hold that date against is the health insurance benefit rule: how long before the procedure becomes eligible again, whether age or condition changes the terms, and when the clock restarts. None of that is in the treatment record. It is written in the policy document. Both have to be in one place before yes and no can be told apart.
