뉴욕타임스가 11월 2일 제소한 상대는 모델을 만드는 회사가 아니라 브라우저를 만드는 회사였다.

맨해튼 연방법원에 같은 날 소장 두 건이 들어갔다. 한 건은 뉴욕타임스가 단독으로, 다른 한 건은 뉴욕 데일리뉴스·시카고 트리뷴·올랜도 센티널이 함께 냈다. 피고는 브레이브 소프트웨어 한 곳뿐이다. 광고를 막아 주는 브라우저로 알려진 회사인데, 자체 검색엔진과 그 엔진이 쓰는 300억 페이지 규모의 웹 색인을 함께 운영한다.

소장이 겨누는 것은 그 색인이다. 원고들은 브레이브가 기사를 긁어 색인에 담은 뒤 앤트로픽·퍼플렉시티·미스트랄·xAI·코히어에 유료 API로 되팔았다고 주장한다. 증거로는 돌아온 산출물의 분량을 들었다. 어느 뉴욕타임스 기사는 그 API의 한 엔드포인트를 거쳐 1,295단어 가운데 1,291단어가 그대로 나왔다는 것이 소장의 서술이다.

청구는 다섯 갈래로 쌓였다. 직접·기여·대위 저작권 침해가 긁어 담고 되판 행위를 겨누고, DMCA 기술보호조치 우회 청구는 브레이브 브라우저의 페이월 차단 기능을 건드린다. 다섯 번째인 부당이득은 뉴욕주법에 근거한 것인데, 복제나 배포가 인정되는지와 무관하게 성립한다고 소장이 못 박았다.

눈여겨볼 자리는 피고 명단이다. 색인을 사 간 다섯 회사는 고객으로 이름만 올랐을 뿐 피고가 아니다. 지금까지의 AI 저작권 소송이 모델을 학습시키고 서비스하는 쪽을 겨눴다면, 이번 피고석에 앉은 것은 그 앞 단계인 공급자다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터를 정량 진단한다. 검색이나 그라운딩 API를 사다 쓰는 계약에서 면책 조항은 대개 가장 얇게 쓰인다. 분쟁이 났을 때 공급사가 지기로 한 책임이 전송과 가용성에서 끊기는지, 아니면 그 색인을 처음 모은 단계까지 닿는지는 계약서를 펴 보기 전까지 확인되지 않는다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The New York Times went to court on November 2 against a browser company, not a model company.

Two complaints were filed in Manhattan federal court that day, one by the Times alone and one jointly by the New York Daily News, the Chicago Tribune and the Orlando Sentinel. Both name a single defendant, Brave Software, known for a browser that blocks ads and also the operator of its own search engine and the web index of more than 30 billion pages behind it.

That index is what the complaints go after. The plaintiffs say Brave scraped their articles into it and resold access through a paid API to Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI and Cohere. Their evidence is the length of what comes back out: one Times article, by their account, emerged from a single endpoint with 1,291 of its 1,295 words intact.

Five claims are stacked on that structure. Direct, contributory and vicarious copyright infringement cover the scraping and the resale, while a DMCA circumvention claim reaches the paywall-blocking feature in Brave's browser. The fifth, unjust enrichment under New York law, is pleaded to stand whether or not copying and distribution are found.

The defendant list is the part to read closely. The five AI companies appear by name as customers and go no further than that. Earlier suits of this kind went after the firms that train and serve the models; this one stops one link upstream, at the supplier.

Pebblous ranks the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Indemnity is usually the thinnest clause in a search or grounding API contract. Whether the supplier's liability ends at delivery and uptime, or reaches back to how the index was gathered in the first place, is a question the contract answers only when somebody wrote the answer in.

## Facebook (EN)

You open a news article, read a few paragraphs, and a subscription notice slides up the screen.

That wall was built with a person in mind. Reach for your wallet or close the tab, it says, and those are the choices on offer.

Two complaints filed in Manhattan federal court on November 2 describe the other side of the same wall. The New York Times and three other newspapers sued the browser company Brave, saying it holds their articles inside a web index of more than 30 billion pages and sells access to that index to five AI companies through a paid API.

The complaints also take aim at the paywall-blocking feature in Brave's browser, and to the plaintiffs the two are not separate matters. Articles that come over the wall raise the quality of the index, and a higher-quality index sells for more.

One of the exhibits the plaintiffs reach for is a blog post Brave wrote itself, which noted that Anthropic had used the Brave Search API while training Claude 2. A customer story posted as a point of pride came back years later as material for an infringement claim.

'Quality that arrived from behind a wall'.

Pebblous records the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Most datasets will yield a score to anyone who asks for one. Far fewer carry a line naming the supplier each batch came from and the agreement it arrived under.

"The material that lifted the score this time: on what terms did it arrive?"

Putting that question into a contract as one line takes very little. Reconstructing the answer years later, with the line never written, takes a great deal.
