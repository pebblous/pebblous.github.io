캘리포니아 의회가 자동화 시스템만 보고 직원을 자르는 일을 막았다. 그런데 이 법이 고용주에게 실제로 요구하는 것은 윤리 규정이 아니라 로그다.

SB 947, 별칭 「No Robo Bosses Act」다. 8월 말 상·하원을 지나 9월 4일 등록됐고, 시행일은 2027년 7월 1일이다. 작년에 같은 취지의 법안이 주지사 거부권에 막혔고, 올해는 보호 대상과 통지 의무를 덜어 낸 판본이 다시 올라갔다. 서명 여부는 아직 정해지지 않았다.

실무 부담은 금지 자체보다 거기 붙은 절차에서 나온다. 시스템 출력에 주로 의존해 징계나 해고를 결정했다면, 사람이 그 출력을 만들 때 수집되거나 쓰인 데이터로 결정을 뒷받침해야 한다. 뒷받침하지 못하거나 검토자가 그 출력이 부정확하다고 판단하면 그 출력은 결정에 쓸 수 없다. 노동자는 자기 데이터에 대한 의미 있고 객관적인 설명을 요구할 수 있고, 결정에 시스템이 쓰였다는 사실이 밝혀지고 나면 절차를 지켰음을 증명할 쪽은 고용주다.

정작 그 데이터를 얼마나 보존하라는 규정은 조문 어디에도 없다. 보존 기간을 정해 주지 않은 채, 보존해야만 이행할 수 있는 의무만 세워 둔 구조다. 벌금은 위반 1건당 500달러이지만 부담의 실체는 그 숫자가 아니다.

컴플라이언스의 경계는 1년 전 성과 점수를 지금 다시 만들 수 있는 조직과 그러지 못하는 조직 사이에 그어진다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

California's legislature has decided that software alone cannot fire anyone. The prohibition is the easy half. The other half arrives after the decision, when an employer has to show its work.

SB 947, known as the No Robo Bosses Act, cleared both chambers at the end of August and was enrolled on September 4. It would take effect on July 1, 2027. A predecessor bill was vetoed last year, and this version passed after shedding its pre-use notice requirement and narrowing who it protects. The governor has not signed it yet.

The operative burden comes from the procedure attached to that ban. Where an employer relied primarily on a system's output to discipline or fire someone, a human has to corroborate that decision using the data collected or used to produce the output. If the employer cannot corroborate it, or the reviewer finds the output inaccurate, the output cannot be used at all. Workers may request a meaningful, objective description of their own data. Once it is shown that a system was involved in the decision, proving compliance is the employer's job.

The text sets no retention period. It never says how long to keep any of this, only that duties exist which cannot be performed unless the data is still there. The civil penalty runs $500 per violation, though the exposure is not really in that figure.

The compliance line falls between companies that can reconstruct a performance score from a year ago and companies that cannot.

## Facebook (EN)

If someone asked you to explain a personnel decision made a year ago, what would you reach for?

The approval trail is probably still there. Whatever score the system produced that week, and whatever records that score was built from, is another question.

I kept returning to that scene while reading a bill California's legislature passed at the end of August and sent to the governor's desk this month. SB 947, the No Robo Bosses Act, stops an employer from disciplining or firing someone on an automated system's output alone.

The line that held me was not the ban. It was the sentence right after it. Where the decision leaned primarily on that output, a person has to corroborate it using the data collected or used to produce it. Fail to corroborate, and the output cannot be used in the decision at all.

There is a second duty stacked on top. A worker can ask for a description of their own data, and the personal information of colleagues and customers has to be anonymized on the way out. Which means knowing, value by value, what came from this person and what came from the desk next door.

And yet no provision says how long any of it must be kept. I have started calling that gap "the quiet retention duty." It was never written down, and every other duty in the statute collapses without it.

"Could the logs we keep today reconstruct the grounds of a decision we made last year?"

That has always been the slow part of the work at Pebblous too. Deciding how to connect a pipeline usually settles quickly. Attaching to each value where it came from, when, and by which route takes much longer to take hold.

The law becomes operative in July 2027. The records anyone reaches for that day are either accumulating now, or they are not.
