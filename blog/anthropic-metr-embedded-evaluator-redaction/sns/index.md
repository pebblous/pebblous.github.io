## LinkedIn (KO)

앤트로픽이 10월 15일 비영리 평가기관 METR의 직원 여섯 명을 사내에 들이고, 이들이 쓸 보고서의 게재를 막을 권리를 내려놓았습니다.

회사 하드웨어와 사무공간을 주고, 내부 도구 접근은 자사 위험 평가팀이 가진 것과 대략 비슷한 수준으로 열었습니다. 직원들에게는 모델이 사람의 의도에서 벗어나는 위험을 함께 다루는 동료에게 하듯 이들에게도 정보를 공유하라고 권합니다.

거부권을 내려놓는다는 문장 옆에 조건이 함께 적혀 있습니다. 정보가 미리 정해진 범주에 들어가면 그 부분을 가려 달라고 요구할 수 있습니다. 발표문이 든 범주는 고객에 관한 데이터, 변호사 비밀유지 대상 정보, 모델 구조에 관한 정보입니다.

앞의 둘은 평가와 다투지 않습니다. 세 번째가 다릅니다. 모델이 왜 그렇게 행동했는지를 설명하려면 구조를 짚게 되는 일이 많습니다. 같은 발표문은 평가자에게 필요한 접근 조건으로 모델이 어떻게 훈련됐는지와 안전장치가 어떻게 작동하는지까지 볼 수 있어야 한다고 적었습니다.

그리고 그 세 범주 앞에는 "예를 들어"가 붙어 있습니다. 셋이 전부라고 적은 문장은 공개된 글 어디에도 없습니다.

거부권 포기가 등급을 가른 것도 아닙니다. 승인 없이 게재하는 권한은 2단계 평가자인 액센츄어에게 이미 있었고, 특정 범주를 빼 달라고 요구할 수 있다는 조건도 2단계에 그대로 있습니다. 이번에 넓어진 쪽은 보는 범위입니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Anthropic opened its internal systems to six employees of the nonprofit evaluator METR on 15 October and gave up its right to block what they publish.

They receive company hardware, office space, and access to internal tools at roughly the level Anthropic's own risk assessment teams reach. Staff are encouraged to share information with them as they would with a colleague working on alignment risk.

A condition sits beside the sentence that gives up the veto. When information falls into a predefined category, Anthropic can ask for that part to be removed. The categories the announcement names are data about customers, attorney-client privileged information, and information about model architectures.

The first two do not fight with evaluation. The third does. Explaining why a model behaved as it did often means pointing at how it is built, and the same announcement writes down what an evaluator needs as visibility into how models were trained and into how the company's safeguards function.

An "e.g." sits in front of those three categories. No sentence in the published text says the three are all of them.

Nor is the veto what separates the tiers. Publishing without approval was already open to Accenture at tier two, and so was the condition allowing a demand to remove specific categories. What widened this time is the range of sight.

Where verification stops is set by the boxes left out, not by the boxes opened.

## Facebook (EN)

Two lists get written whenever data leaves a building.

One for what goes out. One for what is held back.

The first runs long. The second usually ends in a single line about sensitive information and the like.

On 15 October, Anthropic brought six employees of METR, a nonprofit evaluator, inside the company.

They were given company hardware and office space, and internal tool access at roughly the level Anthropic's own risk assessment teams reach.

And Anthropic wrote that it cannot block the analysis METR publishes.

That is where most of the reading stopped.

A condition sits directly beside that sentence. When information falls into a predefined category, the company can ask for that part to be removed.

The announcement names three. Data about customers, attorney-client privileged information, and information about model architectures.

The first two do not fight with evaluation. Customer data belongs to third parties, and attorney-client privileged material is redacted in any audit of any company.

The third is a different kind of removal. Explaining why a model behaved as it did often means pointing at how it is built. A conclusion can go out while part of what holds it up does not.

The same announcement writes down separately what an evaluator needs to reach. Visibility into how the models were trained, and into how the company's safeguards function.

What an evaluator needs and what the company may ask to have removed sit side by side in the same document.

And an "e.g." sits in front of those three categories.

No sentence in the published text says the three are all of them.

The open side is written down to the numbers. The withheld side is written down only as examples.

"The things we folded into 'sensitive information and the like' last time: counted one by one, how many were they?"
