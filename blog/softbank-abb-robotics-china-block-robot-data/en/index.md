# China Blocks the SoftBank ABB Robotics Deal Over Robot Data

_SoftBank received the notice on November 4. The review_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> China's government has barred SoftBank from taking over ABB's robotics business inside China. SoftBank agreed last October to buy the whole of that business at an enterprise value of $5.375 billion, and the Chinese antitrust review cleared the deal this February. One gate was left, the foreign investment security review, and that is where it turned over.

> What the review caught on was not a plant or a patent. It was data. The issue, as reported, was that the production and motion records ABB robots have built up in Chinese factories could be moved out of the country and used to train AI models. SoftBank's standing as a major investor in OpenAI is cited as enlarging that concern. China is the largest market for ABB's robotics business at about 30 percent of its revenue, so the arithmetic of the deal does not hold with that one country taken out.

> Sections 1 through 4 follow the facts and figures confirmed by news reporting and official announcements. Section 5, on what a company gathering or receiving robot data should write down at the contract stage, is this article's reading.

## One Notice Stopped a $5.4 Billion Deal

ABB had planned to carve its robotics division out and list it separately. SoftBank came forward last year offering to buy all of it, the plan turned into a sale, and on October 8, 2025 an agreement was announced at an enterprise value of $5.375 billion. It was the largest piece in the portfolio Masayoshi Son has been assembling since naming physical AI as his next frontier.

The year or so that followed belonged to the reviews. The competition authority's antitrust review cleared in February, and the industry expected the remaining procedures to finish without trouble. The brake came at the last gate.

The date was November 4. SoftBank received notice that day from the security review authority that it could not take over ABB's robotics business inside China. The Financial Times reported it first a day later, and ABB confirmed the facts. SoftBank carried the same content in an official press release on November 6, adding only that it would announce whatever it decides from here as its disclosure obligations require. Laid out by date, the course since the agreement was announced runs as follows.

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| October 8, 2025 | SoftBank announces an agreement to acquire the whole of ABB's robotics business (enterprise value $5.375 billion) |
| February 2026 | Chinese antitrust (competition) review clears the deal |
| November 4, 2026 | SoftBank receives notice from the Office of the Working Mechanism for Foreign Investment Security Review barring the acquisition inside China |
| November 5, 2026 | The Financial Times reports it first and ABB confirms the notice. SoftBank's shares fall 6.77 percent on the Tokyo exchange |
| November 6, 2026 | SoftBank issues an official press release carrying the notice |
| November 7, 2026 | Bloomberg reports that the two companies are examining a plan to carve the China business out |

The date of receipt is the one SoftBank gave in its November 6 announcement, and it falls a day apart from the date of first reporting.

The market answered at once. SoftBank's shares gave up 6.77 percent on the Tokyo exchange the day of the report and were pushed down 11 percent across the week. Technology shares were weak together that week, though, so the whole of the fall is hard to charge to this event. ABB's shares fell a little over 1 percent on the day and more than 5 percent on a weekly basis. The ABB robotics division being sold employs about 7,000 people, and its 2024 revenue of $2.3 billion is around 7 percent of group revenue.

The two companies issued only a short joint statement. It said they are in close dialogue and working constructively on the transaction. Nothing in it said they would drop the deal, and nothing said they would look for another route.

## The Review's Target Was the Records the Robots Keep

The notice came from the Office of the Working Mechanism for Foreign Investment Security Review (外商投资安全审查工作机制办公室). Its secretariat is housed within China's National Development and Reform Commission (国家发展和改革委员会), and it takes in and reviews, ahead of time, deals in which foreign capital enters and acquires real control in areas such as national defense, energy, important equipment manufacturing and core technology. The governing rules, the Measures for the Security Review of Foreign Investment (外商投资安全审查办法), took effect in January 2021 and run in two stages: a general review that moves to a special review when a deal is judged capable of affecting national security.

No official reason has been made public. The issue carried in news reports is consistent. The production and motion records ABB robots have piled up while running in Chinese factories could pass to Japan and be used to train AI models. The Financial Times noted that SoftBank's position as a major investor in OpenAI gave the worry extra weight. That draws a picture of records from Chinese manufacturing floors flowing into the models of a Western AI company.

The record an industrial robot leaves is thicker than it sounds. The angle at which a welding torch went in, the force with which a gripper closed and opened, the places where the same process went off course over tens of thousands of repetitions — all of it remains as coordinates and timestamps. This becomes the teaching material for a robot. It is a channel through which the skill a plant has learned can enter a model while the plant stays where it is, and that is where the review fixed its attention.
