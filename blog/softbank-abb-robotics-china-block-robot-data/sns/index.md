## LinkedIn (KO)

7조 원이 넘는 로봇 인수가 반독점 심사까지 통과하고 마지막 관문에서 멈춰 섰다. 막은 것은 설비도 특허도 아니라 데이터였다.

중국 외국인투자 안보심사 업무메커니즘 판공실이 소프트뱅크의 ABB 로보틱스 인수를 중국 안에서는 허용하지 않겠다고 통보했다. 소프트뱅크가 통보를 받은 날은 11월 4일이고, 파이낸셜타임스가 이를 전한 것은 하루 뒤였다. 기업가치 53억 7,500만 달러에 합의한 거래이며, 반독점 심사는 올해 2월에 이미 끝나 있었다.

심사가 붙잡은 것은 중국 공장에서 ABB 로봇이 돌며 쌓아 온 생산·동작 기록이다. 용접 토치가 들어간 각도, 집게가 쥐었다 놓은 힘, 같은 공정이 수만 번 반복되는 사이 틀어진 자리가 좌표와 시간으로 남는다. 이 기록이 국외로 나가 AI 모델을 학습시키는 데 쓰일 수 있다는 것이 쟁점이었다고 복수 매체가 전했다. 공장을 옮기지 않고도 그 공장이 익힌 공정을 모델에 담을 수 있는 통로이기 때문이다.

중국을 빼고 거래를 맞추기도 어렵다. ABB 로봇 사업 매출의 약 30%가 중국에서 나오고, 중국 고객에게 파는 로봇의 90% 이상이 현지에서 만들어진다. 다만 공식 거부 사유는 공개되지 않았고, 양사가 검토 중이라고 전해지는 중국 사업 분리안도 합의된 구조가 아니라 협상 중인 선택지다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단한다. 기업 인수에서 데이터는 오랫동안 설비에 딸려 오는 부속품으로 처리됐다. 나머지가 다 통과한 뒤에 데이터 하나가 거래를 멈춰 세운다면, 실사 목록에서 데이터는 따로 한 줄을 받아야 하는 항목이다.

설비에는 값이 매겨져 있었고 기록에는 매겨져 있지 않았다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A $5.4 billion robotics deal cleared antitrust in February and then stopped at the last gate. What stopped it was not the plant or the patents. It was data.

China's foreign investment security review office notified SoftBank that it may not take over ABB's robotics business inside China. SoftBank says the notice arrived on November 4. The Financial Times reported it a day later, and ABB confirmed receiving it.

The records at issue are the ones ABB robots have accumulated while running in Chinese plants. The angle a welding torch went in at, the force a gripper closed and released with, the point where a cycle drifted somewhere inside tens of thousands of repetitions. All of it lands as coordinates and timestamps. Several outlets report that the review turned on whether those records could leave the country and train AI models, which is a way of carrying what a factory has learned without moving the factory. SoftBank's position as a major investor in OpenAI added weight to the concern.

China is also hard to leave out of the arithmetic. About 30 percent of ABB robotics revenue comes from there, and more than 90 percent of the robots sold to Chinese customers are built locally. No official reason for the refusal has been published, and the China carve-out the two companies are reported to be weighing is an option under negotiation rather than an agreed structure.

Pebblous gauges the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. In acquisitions, data spent a long time being treated as an accessory that came along inside the equipment. When everything else clears and one piece of data stops the deal, data becomes an item that needs a line of its own on the diligence list.

The equipment had a price on it. The records did not.

## Facebook (EN)

A factory can be moved.

You take the machines apart, put them on a ship, and stand them up again somewhere else. Difficult, but it has been done many times.

What was supposed to be unmovable is what the factory had learned. The angles and the forces and the timings that only come out of years on one line sit inside the people and the equipment, and no contract has been able to lift them out.

Robots unsettled that assumption.

An industrial robot leaves records while it works. The angle the welding torch went in at. The force the gripper closed and released with. The place where the cycle drifted, somewhere inside tens of thousands of repetitions. Coordinates and timestamps, accumulating.

Move the records and what the factory learned goes with them. The factory stays where it is, and the skill becomes the portable part.

China has now stopped a deal at that exact point. SoftBank agreed last year to buy ABB's robotics business, and antitrust cleared in February. The plant, the patents, the brand all went through. What remained at the end was whether records kept by robots in Chinese factories cross a border.

In buying and selling companies, data was long treated as something that came along inside. You price the plant and the machinery, the patents and the trademarks, the people and the contracts, and the data is assumed to be in there somewhere.

Pebblous gauges the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Lifting quality and establishing whether the data may be used at all are separate questions. Where it came from, where it sits, who may use it for what: with none of that written down, the first answer will not stand in for the second.

"On our own asset list, which line holds the data? Is it on the list at all?"

Beijing has never given an official reason, and the account that the issue was data rests on reporting. Even so, the place where the deal came to a stop is visible without the reason being published.
