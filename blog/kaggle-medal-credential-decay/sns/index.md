## LinkedIn (KO)

캐글 메달의 예측력은 딴 지 1년이 지나면 사실상 사라집니다. 생성 AI가 자격의 값을 무너뜨렸다는 통념과 달리, 이 감쇠는 챗GPT가 나오기 한참 전부터 있었습니다.

워싱턴대 세인트루이스의 야오 송 교수가 캐글 참가 기록 444,698건을 패널로 묶어 잰 값입니다. 딴 지 1년이 안 된 메달 하나는 다음 대회 리더보드 백분위를 10점에서 12점 끌어올렸고, 1년을 넘긴 메달의 기울기는 메달이 없는 경우와 구분되지 않았습니다.

생성 AI 책임론이 걸린 자리는 업로드형 대회 메달입니다. 예측값만 제출하고 그것을 만든 과정은 확인하지 않는 형식인데, 이 메달 재고는 AI 시대에 예측력의 82%를 잃었습니다. 같은 기간 코드를 제출해 플랫폼이 직접 실행하는 코드형 메달의 예측력은 오히려 올랐습니다.

손실을 분해하면 절반에서 4분의 3은 AI의 재평가가 아니라 발급 중단이 원인이었습니다. 캐글은 검증되지 않은 제출과 리더보드 조작 문제 때문에 생성 AI가 나오기 몇 해 전부터 코드형으로 옮겨 갔고, 남은 업로드형 재고는 소지자의 실력이 변하지 않은 채로 늙었습니다. 저자는 이 상태를 좌초 자산에 빗댑니다.

플랫폼의 표시 방식도 같이 검사받았습니다. 캐글의 Expert, Master, Grandmaster 등급은 생애 누적 메달 수만 보기 때문에 손에 든 정보의 13%에서 16%를 쓰지 않고 버립니다. AI 이전 데이터로만 학습해 그대로 얼려 둔 최근성 가중 인덱스가, AI 시대 성적조차 공식 등급보다 잘 맞혔습니다.

자격의 값은 발급이 아니라 갱신에서 나옵니다. 페블러스가 데이터 자산에서 보는 장면도 같습니다. 라벨링 데이터셋과 평가 벤치마크의 신뢰도는 만들어진 시점이 아니라 마지막으로 갱신된 시점에서 정해지는데, 재고의 크기를 세는 지표는 그 노화를 보여 주지 못합니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A Kaggle medal stops saying much about a data scientist's next result once it is a year old. The decay was already there long before ChatGPT, which is awkward for the belief that generative AI drained the value of credentials.

Song Yao at Washington University in St. Louis built a panel of 444,698 contest entries to measure it. A medal earned within the past year lifted a competitor's finishing percentile by 10 to 12 points. Past that mark, the slope was indistinguishable from holding no medal at all.

The format carrying the generative-AI story is the upload competition, where entrants submit predictions and nobody checks how those predictions were produced. That medal stock lost 82% of its predictive power in the AI era. Medals from code competitions, which the platform executes on held-out data, gained over the same stretch.

Decompose the loss and half to three-quarters of it is not a repricing. Kaggle had been retiring the upload format for years before ChatGPT, over unverified submissions and leaderboard gaming. The stock stopped being replenished and aged along the curve it had always followed. Yao calls it a stranded asset. The plant did not get worse; the grid moved.

The platform's own display came out of the audit no better. Expert, Master and Grandmaster tiers are a function of lifetime medal counts, so they discard 13 to 16% of the information sitting in the same record. An index that weights recent medals, trained only on pre-AI data and then frozen, predicted AI-era results more accurately than the official tier did.

A credential is worth what its renewal is worth, not what its issuance was. Data assets behave the same way at Pebblous, where a labeled dataset or an evaluation benchmark earns trust on the strength of its last update rather than its creation date. Metrics that count the size of the stock say nothing about that aging.

## Facebook (EN)

If you have ever written "Kaggle Master" on a résumé, or hired someone partly because that line was on theirs, one question tends to surface for a second.

When was the medal earned?

Song Yao at Washington University in St. Louis put that question to 444,698 Kaggle contest entries.

Whatever a medal says about the next result, it says almost all of it within the first year. Past a year, holding one is hard to tell apart from holding none.

That was true well before ChatGPT.

What stayed with me was what came next.

Upload competitions, where you submit predictions and nobody inspects how you arrived at them, saw their medals lose 82% of their predictive power in the AI era. Take the loss apart, though, and half to three-quarters of it has nothing to do with AI making those medals cheap.

Kaggle had been retiring that format for years, over leaderboard gaming, and a stock that nobody was replenishing simply aged at the rate it had always aged.

Yao calls the result a stranded credential. Less a power plant that got worse, more a grid that moved away from it.

"Did the credential lose its value, or did the institution issuing it disappear first?"

No evidence turned up that the people holding the medals had changed. What changed sat on the side that issues the signal and displays it.

We keep meeting a version of this in data assets at Pebblous. A carefully labeled dataset, a well-built evaluation benchmark: both are trusted on the date they were last renewed, not the date they were made. And what we usually count is the size of the corpus.

Size cannot show you aging.

The date something was issued is written everywhere. The date someone last touched it rarely is.
