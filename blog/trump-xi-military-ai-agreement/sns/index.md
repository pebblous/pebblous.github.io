## LinkedIn (KO)

트럼프 대통령과 시진핑 주석의 군사 AI 합의를 알린 것은 두 정상이 아니라 미 재무장관이다.

스콧 베선트 재무장관이 11월 20일 CNBC에 나와, 두 정상이 어떤 군사적 결정에도 사람이 늘 관여해야 하고 대규모 군사 행동을 AI가 단독으로 하게 둬서는 안 된다는 데 합의했다고 말했다. 두 정상이 만난 것은 하루 전, 중국 선전에서 열린 APEC 정상회의에서다.

공동선언문은 실제로 나왔다. 다만 보도로 전해진 항목은 유엔 개혁과 연료 공급 확대 같은 것이고, 핵 위험에 대해서는 원론적 약속에 그친다. AI 문구는 없다. 백악관 공식 결과문도 이 합의를 담지 않았다. 지금까지 공개된 근거는 회담에 배석한 각료 한 사람의 방송 발언뿐이다.

비교 대상은 2년 전이다. 2024년 리마 APEC에서 바이든 대통령과 시진핑 주석은 핵무기 사용 결정을 AI가 아니라 사람이 통제한다는 문장을 백악관 공식 결과문에 넣었다. 이번 선언문에는 그 조항이 빠졌다. 문서로 남은 쪽이 오히려 줄었다.

같은 약속을 확인 가능한 형태로 옮기려는 시도는 미 상원에 있다. 군사위원회의 마크 켈리 의원이 낸 법안은 자율무기체계에 대해 지휘관과 운용자가 감독하고 개입하고 중단시킬 수 있는 능력을 궁극적 인간 책임으로 규정한다. 사람이 관여했다는 문장과 달리, 시스템을 열어 보면 답이 나오는 물음이다.

기업의 에이전트 승인 절차에도 같은 조건이 걸린다. 감사가 열리면 그 결정은 AI가 아니라 사람이 했다는 설명이 먼저 나오고, 다음 질문은 늘 같다. 누가, 언제, 무엇을 보고 승인했나. 기록이 없으면 선언문의 문장과 사내 정책의 문장은 같은 종류다. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data 작업에서 되풀이해 마주치는 자리가 여기다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The Trump-Xi understanding on military AI was made public by neither leader, but by the US Treasury Secretary on television.

Scott Bessent told CNBC on November 20 that the two had agreed humans should always be involved in any military decision, and that AI should not be left to run a large-scale military action on its own. The leaders had met the day before, at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China.

A joint statement did come out of that meeting. What reporters described in it runs to items like UN reform and expanded fuel supply, and on nuclear risk it goes no further than a general pledge. No AI language. No official White House readout covers the understanding either. The only basis made public so far is one cabinet member's remark on a broadcast.

The comparison is two years old. At the 2024 APEC summit in Lima, Biden and Xi put into an official White House readout the position that decisions on the use of nuclear weapons stay under human control rather than with AI. That clause is absent from this year's statement. Less is on paper now than then.

An attempt to turn the same promise into something checkable sits in the US Senate. A bill from Senator Mark Kelly of the Armed Services Committee defines ultimate human responsibility for autonomous weapons as the ability of commanders and operators to supervise, intervene in, and terminate. Unlike a sentence saying a person was involved, that is a question you answer by opening the system.

The same condition applies to approval steps inside companies. When an audit opens, the first explanation offered is that a person made the call, not the AI, and the question that follows never changes. Who approved it, when, and on the basis of what they saw? Without a record, a line in a summit statement and a line in an internal policy are the same kind of line. That is the point Pebblous keeps running into in its AI-Ready Data work.

## Facebook (EN)

"A person made that call, not the AI."

It is the first thing said when an audit or an incident review opens. And the question that follows is always the same.

"Who approved it, when, and on the basis of what they saw?"

This week the same question landed on a summit room.

Scott Bessent, the US Treasury Secretary, went on television on November 20 and said that President Trump and President Xi had agreed not to leave large-scale military action to AI alone.

The two had met the day before, at the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

The joint statement from that meeting says nothing of the kind.

What stays with me more is 2024. At the Lima APEC summit that year, Biden and Xi put into an official White House readout the line that decisions on the use of nuclear weapons stay with people. This year even that line is gone. Two years on, less is written down, not more.

I have started calling this kind of promise control left in speech. That is not to say it is untrue. It means there is no place to open months later. It stops at the memory and the phrasing of the person who carried it.

The same thing is spreading inside companies. AI agents are placing orders, approving refunds, and changing account permissions, and the explanation that a person decided has grown more common. To stand behind that explanation, you have to settle in advance what gets written at the moment of approval. Who approved it, what screen they actually saw, which policy and model version were in force, and whether the record stayed unedited afterward.

That is exactly the ground Pebblous keeps returning to when it talks about AI-Ready Data.

Take one thing an AI agent handled last month that is marked as approved by a person. Can you trace whose screen that approval came from, and at what hour?

If the record does not reach that far, our own human control is standing where the sentence in the summit statement stands.
