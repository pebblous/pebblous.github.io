## LinkedIn (KO)

넷앱이 7월 16일 산 것은 AI 데이터 스타트업 하나가 아니라, 데이터를 다루는 순서 전체다.

지금까지 기업 데이터는 저장소에 있었고, AI는 그것을 GPU가 있는 곳으로 대량 복사해 와야 학습을 시작했다. 넷앱이 인수한 DataPelago의 Nucleus 엔진은 그 복사 단계를 지운다. 데이터가 놓인 자리에서 CPU와 GPU로 곧바로 질의하고 변환한다. 넷앱은 이를 '제로카피 활성화'라 부르며 기존 컴퓨팅 대비 최대 10배 빠르고 비용은 3분의 1이라 밝혔다.

넷앱만의 승부수도 아니다. Dell, VAST Data, Everpure가 저마다 같은 방향을 향하고, 2026년 1분기 스토리지 지출은 전년 대비 22.7% 뛰었다.

주의할 점은 남는다. 저장 계층이 데이터를 빨리 활성화한다고 해서, 그 데이터가 맞는지 틀리는지 판정하는 일까지 대신하지는 않는다. 오히려 활성화가 빨라질수록 잘못된 데이터가 더 빨리 퍼진다.

'AI-레디 데이터'가 파이프라인의 한 단계에서 저장 계층 자체의 속성으로 올라서는 장면이다. 그럴수록 무엇을 준비할지 먼저 가려내는 진단의 값이 오른다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

What NetApp bought on July 16 wasn't a storage startup. It was a change in the order of how data gets used.

Until now, enterprise data sat in storage, and AI could only begin once that data had been bulk-copied to where the GPUs live. DataPelago's Nucleus engine, now NetApp's, erases that copy step: it queries and transforms data on CPUs and GPUs right where it is stored. NetApp calls this "zero-copy activation" and claims up to 10x the speed of legacy compute at a third of the cost.

This isn't NetApp alone. Dell, VAST Data, and Everpure are moving the same way, and first-quarter 2026 storage spending rose 22.7% year over year.

A caution remains. Activating data faster doesn't mean judging whether it's right, and the faster activation gets, the faster bad data spreads.

What's really happening: AI-ready data is being promoted from one step in a pipeline to a property of the storage layer itself. The faster that happens, the more the diagnosis of what to prepare is worth.

## Facebook (EN)

I once watched a team spend half its day just moving data.

Pull it out of storage, copy it over to where the GPUs live, then check all over again that the copy hadn't drifted from the original. The training never started until that move was done.

When NetApp acquired a company called DataPelago on July 16, the idea it put forward was to erase that move entirely. Don't relocate the data. Use it for AI right where it sits. NetApp called this "zero-copy activation."

What struck me is that this isn't NetApp's story alone. Dell, VAST Data, and Everpure are each walking a different road toward the same place. The spot where data rests has itself become the field of competition.

And yet one question kept staying with me.

"If we can use the data faster now, who decides whether that data is right or wrong?"

Infrastructure lifts the burden of moving data. It does not take over the work of judging whether the data is fit to use, whether the number can be trusted. If anything, the faster activation gets, the quieter and higher the value of knowing what to prepare first.

That is why Pebblous keeps returning to "diagnose first, then refine": surface what the problem is, and only then fix it. The more the storage layer takes preparation onto itself, the more the work of checking that it's preparing the right data moves to the front, rather than disappearing.
