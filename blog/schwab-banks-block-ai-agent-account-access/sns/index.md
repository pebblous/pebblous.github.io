찰스 슈왑 최고경영자 릭 워스터가 10월 15일 3분기 실적 발표에서 외부 AI 비서의 계좌 로그인을 허용하지 않겠다고 말했습니다. 막는 대상은 AI가 아니라 들어오는 방식입니다. 고객에게서 아이디와 비밀번호를 받아 사람인 척 화면을 조작하는 경로입니다.

다음 날 블룸버그 텔레비전 인터뷰에서 규모도 밝혔습니다. 큰 에이전트 기준으로 한 주에 열 몇 건에서 스무 몇 건을 막고, 그 건수를 주간 보고로 받아 본다고 했습니다. 워스터는 이 수를 사람들이 AI를 좋아하기는 해도 아직 금융 계좌를 통째로 넘길 생각은 없다는 뜻으로 읽었습니다.

슈왑만의 결정이 아니었습니다. 10월 13일부터 16일까지 다섯 곳이 각자의 선을 그었습니다. JP모건은 외부 에이전트를 자사 시스템에 들이지 않겠다고 하면서 제3자 도구에 계좌 조회는 이미 열어 두고 있습니다. 뱅크오브아메리카는 돈이 나가는 자리에 사람의 승인이 있어야 한다는 조건을 걸었습니다. 씨티는 로그인과 직접 거래를 모두 허용하지 않는다고 밝혔습니다. 웰스파고는 차단을 말하지 않고 온라인 이용약관을 고쳤고, 새 약관은 11월 24일 시행됩니다.

공개적으로 든 이유는 책임 소재입니다. 외부 프로그램이 고객 자격증명으로 들어와 돈을 옮겼을 때 누가 물어내는지에 미국 법은 아직 답을 주지 않습니다. 같은 보도에 다른 숫자가 함께 실립니다. JP모건·뱅크오브아메리카·웰스파고가 이자를 주지 않고 받아 둔 예금이 1조 6천억 달러이고, 아폴로 수석이코노미스트 토르스텐 슬록은 여기에 연 3% 이자가 붙는다고 가정해 세 은행의 연간 추가 부담을 470억 달러로 추산했습니다. 전액이 옮겨 갈 때를 가정한 상한이지 예측치가 아니고, 이 합산에 슈왑은 들어 있지 않습니다.

워스터 본인은 이 해석을 먼저 집어 부인했습니다. AI가 퍼지면 고객 현금이 줄어들리라는 걱정을 안다면서, 슈왑에서는 AI를 쓰는 고객일수록 주식을 활발히 사고팔아 현금을 더 들고 있다고 했습니다. 바깥에서 확인할 자료는 없습니다. 남는 사실은 안전한 선택과 조달 비용에 유리한 선택이 같은 방향을 가리킨다는 것입니다.

슈왑은 막기만 하지 않았습니다. 외부 AI 모델이 잔고 조회와 리서치를 할 수 있는 MCP 서버를 준비하고 있고, 돈을 옮기는 일은 거기서 뺐습니다. 그 경계가 읽기와 쓰기 사이에 그어져 있는 것도 아닙니다. 아메리칸 뱅커에 따르면 일부 대규모 언어모델은 이미 슈왑에 연결해 주식 거래를 체결하고 있고, 챗GPT도 거기에 들어갑니다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단합니다. 현장에서 "이 데이터를 외부에 어디까지 내보낼 수 있느냐"는 질문의 답은 대개 담당자 머릿속이나 주고받은 메일 안에 있습니다. 사람이 사람에게 넘기던 동안에는 그걸로 굴러갔습니다. 에이전트가 요청하기 시작하면 그 답이 시스템이 읽을 수 있는 자리에 적혀 있어야 합니다.

막힌 것은 AI가 아니라 슈왑이 고르지 않은 AI입니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Charles Schwab will not let outside AI assistants log into client accounts, chief executive Rick Wurster said on the firm's third-quarter earnings call on October 15. What Schwab closes is the way in, not AI itself: the route where a client hands over an ID and a password and an outside program works the screen as though it were a person.

The next day, on Bloomberg Television, Wurster put a size on it. The big agents are turned away in the tens and twenties each week, and he gets the count in a weekly report. He read the number as a sign that people like AI but are not yet ready to hand over a financial account whole.

Schwab was not alone. Over the four days from October 13 to 16, five firms drew their own lines. JPMorgan said it will not let outside agents into its systems, while third-party tools already have account viewing open. Bank of America set a condition instead, that a person must approve money leaving an account. Citi said agents may not log in or trade directly. Wells Fargo announced no block at all and rewrote its online access agreement, effective November 24.

The reason given in public is liability. U.S. law still has no clear answer for who pays when an outside program enters on a client's credentials and moves money. A different number sits in the same reporting: JPMorgan, Bank of America and Wells Fargo hold $1.6 trillion in deposits that pay no interest, and Torsten Slok, chief economist at Apollo, assumed 3% on that sum to estimate $47 billion a year in additional cost for the three banks. It is a ceiling that assumes every dollar moves rather than a forecast, and Schwab is not in the total.

Wurster named that reading himself and denied it. He knows the market fears AI will pull cash out of accounts, he said, and at Schwab the opposite shows: clients who use AI trade more actively and hold more cash. It cannot be checked from outside. What remains is that the safe choice and the choice that protects funding costs point the same way.

Schwab did not stop at blocking. It is preparing an MCP server that will let outside AI models reach balances and research, with money movement left out. Nor does that boundary fall between reading and writing. According to American Banker, certain large language models already connect to Schwab and execute trades, ChatGPT among them.

While people were handing data to people, that was enough. Once agents start asking, the answer has to sit somewhere a system can read.

What is blocked is not AI. It is the AI Schwab did not pick.

## Facebook (EN)

To get an AI assistant to read my brokerage balance, I would start by handing it my ID and my password.

On October 15, that way in closed.

On Charles Schwab's third-quarter earnings call, chief executive Rick Wurster said the firm will not allow an agent to come to its site and log in on behalf of a client.

The first thing I noticed was where the line fell. Not on AI. On the way in.

He gave a size for it the next day, on Bloomberg. Big agents, turned away in the tens and twenties each week. He gets the count in a weekly report.

Not hundreds, not thousands. Wurster read that number as a sign that people like AI but are not yet ready to hand over a financial account whole.

JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi each drew a line of their own the same week.

The firm that went furthest was not one of the banks that announced a block. It was Wells Fargo, which rewrote its agreement: a request arriving through a third-party AI tool may be handled as the customer's own, and the duty of managing that tool belongs to the customer.

Schwab did not bolt the door shut either.

It is preparing a separate channel for outside AI models to read balances and do research, with money movement left out of it.

That boundary does not fall between reading and writing.

According to American Banker, certain large language models already connect to Schwab and execute trades, ChatGPT among them. The writing that buys and sells stock is open. Only the writing that sends money out of the account is shut.

"Stock orders open, transfers shut. In our own data, who draws that line?"

An agent handed a password can do everything the client can do, because it inherits the client's authority whole. An agent arriving through a channel can do only as much as whoever opened the channel decided in advance.

The balance is my data. The decision to let my AI assistant read it is, in principle, mine. Whether that is actually possible is Schwab's to decide.

It is hard to say the banks took a right away from their customers. Handing a password to an outside program really is precarious, and there are grounds for closing that route.

What remains is that in closing it, the same side also took the authority to set the width of the new one.

The seat where data access gets designed in the age of agents is not sitting empty. The party holding the data is already in it, and its own interests are sitting there too.
