능력을 잰 쪽은 구글이 아닙니다. 취약점 수리 시험 CWE-bench v1은 Collinear AI가 만들어 직접 돌리고 리더보드로 공개합니다. Argon의 68%에는 오픈AI의 GPT-6 Astra와 xAI의 Grok 4.7이 같은 점수로 나란히 서 있습니다.

그 능력을 누구에게 풀어 줄지는 구글이 혼자 정합니다. 프로그램 안내 페이지는 신청 자격과 승인 후 보안 의무를 꽤 상세히 적어 두었습니다. 다만 어느 선을 넘어야 통과인지, 누가 그 판정을 내리는지, 떨어진 조직이 사유를 듣는지는 어느 문서에도 없습니다. 지금까지 몇 곳이 통과했는지도 공개돼 있지 않습니다.

측정하는 일에는 밖에서 돌리는 시험과 모델을 줄 세운 공개 표가 있는데, 배분하는 일에는 그런 것이 하나도 없습니다. 기록되지 않은 판단은 나중에 꺼내 볼 수 없습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A model that finds and fixes software vulnerabilities on its own has arrived, and Google decides who gets to use that ability without limits.

Gemini 4 Argon, announced September 30. The public release carries cyber guardrails that refuse to reason about exploitation or write attack code. The build with those guardrails stripped out goes first to Google's own teams and to security organizations that clear the Fairwind program. Fairwind launched September 2 with more than 650 members.

Google did not measure the capability. CWE-bench v1, a vulnerability-repair test, belongs to Collinear AI, which runs the models itself and publishes the leaderboard. Argon's 68% is a three-way tie with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and xAI's Grok 4.7.

Google alone decides who receives the unguarded build. The program page is detailed about who may apply and what an approved organization owes afterward. It says nothing about how strong a record has to be to pass, who issues that judgment, or whether a rejected applicant learns why. The number of organizations cleared so far has not been published either.

Measuring the ability has an outside test and a public table of scores. Distributing it has neither. A judgment nobody wrote down cannot be pulled back out later.

## Facebook (EN)

You have probably filed an access request and then waited.

If it clears, that is the end of it. If it does not, you rarely find out why.

Reading Google's September 30 announcement of Gemini 4 Argon, that wait came back to me.

The model is described as able to discover, verify and fix software vulnerabilities on its own. The build that can use that ability without limits is not part of the public release. It goes to Google's internal teams and to security organizations that clear a program called Fairwind.

Google did not measure the ability. The vulnerability-repair test behind the 68% was built and run by an outside company, which publishes the standings. Two models from two other companies sit on the same score.

Up to here, anyone can open the table.

The other side has no table. Who may apply, and what an approved organization owes afterward, is set out in real detail on the program page. How strong a record has to be to pass, who issues that judgment, whether a rejected organization is told why: none of it is there. I have started calling this an unrecorded approval. One publication read the same gap and asked it in a single line.

"Who audits the gatekeeper?"

The work we do at Pebblous ends up near that question. We see it often enough: the standard lives in a document, the decisions live in one person's memory. A judgment that goes unrecorded never becomes an asset. Data quality and model access permissions are probably no different on this point.

Could your organization list every permission it opened last quarter, and do it before the day is out?
