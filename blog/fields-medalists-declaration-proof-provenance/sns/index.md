9월 8일 오픈AI는 나비에-스토크스 방정식의 유한시간 특이점 증명을 166쪽 원고와 Lean 형식화 저장소로 함께 공개했다. 누구나 내려받아 기계로 다시 검사할 수 있다. 사흘 뒤 선언이 문제 삼은 것은 정확성이 아니라 발표 방식이었다. 서둘러 나온 발표는 제대로 된 작성과, 새 방법의 추출과, 남의 선행 연구를 인용할 시간을 남기지 않는다는 것이다.

끝까지 확인되지 않은 것은 증명이 아니라 출처였다. 뉴욕대 트리스탄 버크마스터는 1년치 초고를 코덱스 세션에 넣어 두고 일했고, 그 세션이 학습에 쓰였는지 물었지만 답을 받지 못했다고 적었다. 오픈AI는 특정 사용자 데이터에 접근하지 않았다고 밝히면서도, 제품 사용에서 파생된 비식별 데이터가 모델 개선에 도움이 됐을 가능성은 배제하지 못했다.

Lean 증명서는 결과가 참인지를 보증할 뿐 그것이 어디서 왔는지는 적지 않는다. 검증 가능성과 출처 추적성은 다른 물건이고, 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data에서 출처 추적성을 품질 항목으로 따로 세워 둔 이유가 여기에 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The declaration signed by 25 Fields Medalists never said OpenAI's proof was wrong.

On Sep. 8 OpenAI published its finite-time blowup proof for the Navier-Stokes equation as a 166-page manuscript alongside a Lean formalization repository that anyone can download and re-check by machine. Three days later the signatories objected not to correctness but to the manner of release. Solutions announced in a rush, they wrote, leave no time for a proper writeup, for isolating new methods, or for citing the previous work of others.

The proof was never the unverified part. Its provenance was. Tristan Buckmaster of NYU wrote that he and his collaborator had kept a year of drafts inside Codex sessions, that he asked whether the model had been trained on them, and that no answer came. OpenAI said it accessed no specific user data, while adding that it could not rule out that de-identified data derived from their product usage had helped improve its models.

A Lean certificate attests that a result is true. It records nothing about where the result came from. Verifiability and provenance are different properties, and the first does not deliver the second. That is why Pebblous carries provenance as its own quality dimension in AI-Ready Data.

## Facebook (EN)

If you pasted a draft into a coding agent last night and then closed the laptop, this one is not somebody else's problem.

Tristan Buckmaster at NYU and Levent Alpöge at Anthropic spent close to a year on a fluid equation. For that entire year, every draft they wrote lived inside Codex sessions.

On Sep. 8, OpenAI published a proof of the same equation: a 166-page manuscript and the Lean code to check it.

Three days later, 25 Fields Medalists signed a declaration. It never said the proof was wrong.

One of the questions Buckmaster asked on the Sep. 6 call never got an answer.

"Was the model trained on our sessions?"

He asked again, he wrote, and nothing came back.

OpenAI said it had accessed no specific user data. It also said it could not rule out that de-identified data derived from their product usage had helped improve its models.

I have started calling this the provenance gap.

Whether a proof is true, a machine answers in hours. Where that proof came from, nobody outside the company can check. A Lean certificate carries no record of origin.

"We can verify it" and "we know where it came from" turned out not to be the same sentence.

That is why Pebblous carries provenance as its own quality dimension in AI-Ready Data. Whether data is clean and where data came from are different questions, and the second one disappears at the moment you fail to write it down.

So how much unpublished material from your team is sitting in some tool's logs tonight? I am not sure how many organizations hold that list as data rather than as a clause in a contract.
