## LinkedIn (KO)

반도체를 팔던 회사가, 그 반도체가 들어갈 데이터센터를 짓는 회사의 주주가 됐다.

삼성 계열사 여섯 곳이 9월 29일 헬릭스에 10억 달러를 함께 넣는다고 발표했다. 헬릭스는 사모펀드 KKR이 올해 세운 회사로, 데이터센터를 짓고 굴리는 일에 더해 그 시설에 전기를 대는 발전 설비와 전기를 끌어오는 송배전 설비까지 한 계약에 담아 넘긴다. 발표문에는 돈을 댄 곳들이 각자 맡을 일도 함께 적혀 있다. 반도체와 냉각은 삼성전자, 설계와 시공은 삼성물산, 구축과 운영은 삼성SDS, 무정전 전원 장치와 배터리는 삼성SDI다.

왜 전기까지 묶어서 파는가. 헬릭스 최고경영자 아담 셀립스키는 회사가 출범한 날 방송에 나와, 짓겠다고 발표만 해 놓고 이행되지 않는 데이터센터 사업이 넷 중 하나를 넘고 그 몫도 계속 커진다고 말했다. 우드매킨지 조사에서 대형 변압기는 주문에서 납품까지 평균 128주가 걸렸다. 병목의 이름을 전력이라고 부른 쪽은 기사가 아니라 삼성 자신의 발표문이었다.

다만 삼성이 산 것은 헬릭스라는 회사의 지분이고 데이터센터의 소유권이나 운영권이 아니다. 계열사가 받은 자리도 공급과 시공과 운영 서비스다. 여섯 곳 가운데 삼성생명과 삼성화재는 투자자 명단에만 이름이 있고 맡을 일이 적혀 있지 않다.

그 자리들 가운데 성격이 다른 하나가 운영이다. 반도체와 냉각기와 배터리는 납품하고 나면 손을 떠나지만, 운영은 서버가 몇 시간 돌았고 어느 랙이 언제 멈췄는지가 시설이 돌아가는 내내 쌓이는 자리다. 이번 발표문 어디에도 삼성이 그 운영 데이터를 가져간다는 말은 없고 계약 내용도 공개되지 않았다. 그래도 남는 질문은 이것이다. AI 산업에서 오래 값이 나가는 자산은 칩인가, 그 칩이 돌아간 기록인가.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A company that has been selling chips bought equity in the company that builds the data centers those chips go into.

Six Samsung affiliates announced on September 29 that they are putting a combined $1 billion into Helix, an AI infrastructure company KKR set up earlier this year. Helix does not sell the building alone. The generation that feeds the site and the transmission equipment that carries the power in go over in the same contract. The release also writes down what each investor will do: semiconductors and cooling from Samsung Electronics, engineering and construction from Samsung C&T, build and operation from Samsung SDS, uninterruptible power supplies and batteries from Samsung SDI.

Why bundle the power. Adam Selipsky, the chief executive of Helix and formerly of Amazon Web Services, said on the day the company launched that more than one in four announced data center projects is not delivering, and that the share keeps growing. Wood Mackenzie put the average wait for a large power transformer at 128 weeks from order to delivery. The party that named power as the bottleneck was not the press but Samsung's own release.

What Samsung bought, though, is equity in Helix, not ownership or operating rights over any data center. The roles written into the release are supply, construction and operating services. Two of the six affiliates, Samsung Life Insurance and Samsung Fire & Marine Insurance, sit on the investor list with no stated role at all.

One of those seats differs in kind from the rest. Chips, cooling units and batteries leave your hands once delivered, while operation continues for as long as the facility runs, and that is where hours logged, racks failed and power drawn accumulate. Nothing in the announcement says Samsung takes that operating data, and the contract was not disclosed. The question it leaves is still worth asking. In the AI industry, which asset holds its value longer: the chip, or the record of the chip running?

## Facebook (EN)

I read that a large power transformer now takes 128 weeks on average to arrive after you order it, and then went to count it out on a calendar.

Two and a half years.

A data center building goes up inside that window with time to spare. The building comes first. What runs late is the part that puts electricity into it.

Last week six Samsung affiliates announced a combined $1 billion going into a company called Helix.

What that company sells is not the building.

On top of developing and operating the data center, it hands over the generation that feeds the site and the transmission equipment that carries the power in, all in one contract. Negotiate the power separately and completion slips, or the project never happens at all. I have started thinking of this as a data center sold with the electricity attached.

The party that named power as the bottleneck was not the press either. It was Samsung's own release.

What held me in the announcement was not the figure, though. It was the list of seats Samsung took inside that company.

Semiconductors, cooling units and batteries leave your hands once delivered. Operation does not. How many hours the servers ran, which rack went down and when, how much power it drew: that keeps accumulating for as long as the facility runs.

From the seat where you sell a part, what the part endured in the field is mostly invisible. The seat where you operate is closer to keeping an eye on the site itself.

Nothing in this announcement says Samsung takes that operating data. Ownership and use of data are settled by contract, and the terms were not disclosed. Everything above is a general point about operation producing a record where it happens.

The scene Pebblous runs into often, working on data quality, sits with that general point. Budget gets approved for bringing the equipment in. No budget gets approved for collecting what the equipment leaves behind in a form anyone can pull out later.

A few years on, what remains is an impression rather than a log.

"Are the records our own equipment leaves behind piling up in a form we can actually pull out later?"

A chip falls behind the next generation in two or three years. The record of what that chip endured is worth more the longer it accumulates.

The seat where the value sits seems to be moving over, quietly, by one.
