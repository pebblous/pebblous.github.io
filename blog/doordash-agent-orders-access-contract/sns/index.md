도어대시가 외부 AI 비서를 거쳐 받은 주문은 지금까지 다 합쳐 수백 건이다. 공동창업자이자 최고경영자인 토니 쉬가 10월 29일 3분기 실적 발표에서 애널리스트 질문에 답하며 말했고, 같은 분기 도어대시 전체 주문은 9억 9,300만 건이었다.

이름이 거론된 비서는 메타의 뮤즈, 스피어스트리트의 인스팅트, 와조의 포 셋이다. 보도 대부분은 이 수치를 하루 300만 건이라는 숫자와 맞댔는데, 그 값은 도어대시 대셔 리포트에 실린 2024년 미국 내 배달 집계다. 같은 분기 공시로 분모를 맞추면 하루 평균은 약 1,080만 건이 되고, 간격은 좁아지는 것이 아니라 더 벌어진다.

도어대시는 이 비서들을 막은 적이 없다. 토니 쉬는 기술을 두고 중력과 싸우려 들면 안 되지만 그렇다고 망상에 빠져서도 안 된다고 했고, 에이전트를 만드는 회사들과 건강한 관계를 맺고 싶다고 덧붙였다. 자기 쪽 도구는 부지런히 늘렸다. 프롬프트와 사진으로 주문하는 챗봇을 6월에 내놓았고, 기업 주문을 다른 챗봇에서도 넣을 수 있도록 모델 컨텍스트 프로토콜 서버도 열어 두었다.

같은 가을에 다른 두 회사는 선을 그었다. 아마존은 9월 20일 승인받지 않은 비서의 접속이 이용 약관 위반이라며 접근을 끊었고, 월마트는 10월 21일 승인된 비서만 지나가는 전용 창구를 열어 고객이 프로필과 한도를 걸게 했다. BCG가 같은 날 낸 조사에서 세계 상위 소매업체와 브랜드 가운데 열에 여덟은 이미 입장을 정해 두었고, 전면 차단을 택한 곳은 6%로 셋 중 가장 드물었다.

숫자가 작은 까닭을 비서 쪽 완성도에서 찾는 목소리도 있다. 아마존 최고경영자 앤디 재시는 같은 날 자사 실적 발표에서 지금의 쇼핑 비서 경험이 가게에 직접 가는 것보다 낫지 않다고 말했다. 이 비서들이 모두 올해 나온 신생 서비스라 이용자 자체가 적다는 사정도 같은 숫자를 낳는다. 공개된 자료만으로는 두 설명 중 어느 쪽이 더 큰 몫인지 가를 수 없다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

DoorDash has taken several hundred orders in all through outside AI assistants. Co-founder and chief executive Tony Xu gave the figure on the October 29 third-quarter earnings call, answering an analyst's question, in a quarter when DoorDash itself handled 993 million orders.

The assistants he named were Meta's Muse, Spear Street's Instinct and Wajo's Fo. Most coverage set the figure against 3 million deliveries a day, a number that comes from DoorDash's Dash Report and counts deliveries completed inside the United States in 2024. Match the denominator to the same quarter's reported total and the daily average is closer to 10.8 million, which widens the gap rather than closing it.

DoorDash has never blocked these assistants. Xu said you never want to fight gravity on anything to do with technology, while adding that you do not want to be delusional either, and that he wants a healthy relationship with the companies building agents. Its own tools kept arriving: a chatbot that takes orders from a prompt or a photo in June, and a Model Context Protocol server that lets corporate orders be placed from other chatbots.

Two other companies drew lines the same autumn. Amazon cut access off on September 20, telling users that an unapproved assistant's access breaches its Conditions of Use. Walmart opened a dedicated gateway on October 21, admitting only approved assistants and letting customers attach a profile and a spending limit. In a BCG survey published that same day, eight in ten of the world's largest retailers and brands had already settled a position, and an outright block was the rarest of the three at 6%.

There is also a reading that puts the small number down to the assistants themselves. Amazon chief executive Andy Jassy said on his own earnings call that same day that the shopping-assistant experience is not yet better than going to the store. These assistants all launched this year and have few users to begin with, which produces the same figure. What has been disclosed is not enough to say which explanation carries more weight.

## Facebook (EN)

"Get me dinner."

More people have said that to an AI assistant this year than last.

Last week DoorDash counted how many times a sentence like that actually turned into an order on its platform.

Several hundred, all told.

Over those same three months, close to a billion orders passed through DoorDash. 993 million, to use the filing.

My first thought was that the assistants are simply still clumsy.

Then I read that DoorDash has never blocked them.

Chief executive Tony Xu said you never want to fight gravity on anything to do with technology, and that he wants a healthy relationship with the companies building agents.

The door was not shut. The orders still did not come.

From the store's side, these assistants arrive with no dedicated route, on the same screens and the same phone numbers people use. Whether the menu changed, whether the restaurant is open right now, whether an item is still in stock: none of it is handed over as a feed. It has to be read off the screen.

Two other companies drew their lines that same autumn.

Amazon, on September 20, told users that an unapproved assistant's access breaches its Conditions of Use, and cut it off.

Walmart, on October 21, opened a counter that only approved assistants pass through, with a profile and a spending limit the customer sets.

DoorDash is at neither of those places.

An undecided door.

Not shut, not open, a third condition. And that is a condition too. With no sentence written down, the judgment made on the day an assistant shows up becomes the policy.

"When an outside assistant arrives, what do we hand over and what do we refuse? Is it written anywhere on our side?"

Pebblous quantifies the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. That work begins by listing what is actually inside a dataset. The same list is what you reach for when you separate what goes outside from what stays in.

Where to draw the line already has a proposal on the table. BCG recommends leaving product, inventory and price for assistants to read, while the store keeps customer identity and purchase history.

In that same survey, 87% said they have never handed customer data to an assistant.

The line is already being drawn.

Several hundred is, for now, small enough to ignore.

Which is exactly why now is when the line can still be drawn. Walmart could open a counter because approval and limits were defined first, and Amazon could cut access overnight because a sentence was already in its terms.

When DoorDash writes its own version of that sentence, I do not know.

I would rather it not be after several hundred has become several million.
