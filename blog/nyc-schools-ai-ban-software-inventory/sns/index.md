9월 2일 맘다니 시장과 사무엘스 교육감이 내놓은 정책입니다. 2-K부터 8학년까지 약 60만 명, 시 전체 공립학교 등록생의 3분의 2가 대상입니다. 학생이 직접 쓰는 생성형 AI를 한 학년도 동안 막고, 컴패니언 챗봇은 학년을 가리지 않고 금지합니다.

집행하려면 목록이 필요한데 그 목록이 아직 없습니다. 시 관계자들은 1,600개 학교의 AI 사용 실태 자료를 거의 갖고 있지 않다고 인정했습니다. 학교를 상대로 돌린 설문의 결과 공개는 거듭된 요청에도 거부했습니다. 이름이 알려진 제품은 둘뿐이고, 그중 Houghton Mifflin Harcourt는 자사 초등 읽기 교육과정에 학생용 AI가 없다고 반박했습니다.

벤더를 심사하는 절차가 없어서 벌어진 일은 아닙니다. 뉴욕시 교육청에는 ERMA라는 승인 절차가 있고, 뉴욕주 교육법 2-d에 따라 벤더는 어떤 데이터를 수집하고 어떻게 보호하는지 설명해야 합니다. 다만 그 대장이 확인하는 것은 데이터 취급이지, 소프트웨어 안에 생성형 AI가 들어 있는지가 아닙니다.

교사에게 걸린 규칙은 한 겹 더 어렵습니다. 수업 준비와 행정에는 AI를 쓸 수 있고 채점과 학생에 관한 결정에는 쓸 수 없는데, 같은 챗봇이 같은 계정에서 두 가지로 쓰입니다. 제품 목록으로는 그 둘을 구분할 수 없습니다.

규칙을 걸기 전에 대상을 셀 수 있어야 합니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

New York City Public Schools said it is disabling AI features on 38 software programs already running in its classrooms. It has not said which 38.

The policy was announced on September 2 by Mayor Mamdani and Chancellor Samuels. It covers students from 2-K through eighth grade, close to 600,000 of them, or two-thirds of the district's enrollment. Student-facing generative AI is off limits for the school year, and companion chatbots are prohibited across every grade.

Enforcing that requires a list, and the list does not yet exist. Officials acknowledged they hold little hard data on how much AI is actually in use across the district's 1,600 schools, and they declined repeated requests to release a survey they had run on the question. Only two products have been named, and one vendor, Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, says there is no student-facing AI in its elementary reading curriculum at all.

This is not a district without a vendor review. The Education Department runs an approval process called ERMA, and under New York State Education Law 2-d every vendor must document what data it collects and how it protects it. What that register confirms is data handling, not whether generative AI sits inside the product.

The rule for teachers is harder still. They may use AI for lesson planning and administrative work but not for grading or decisions about students, and the same chatbot serves both purposes from the same account. A product register cannot tell those two apart.

Before you can apply a rule, you have to be able to count what it applies to.

## Facebook (EN)

"Which 38 programs?"

The question survives the whole story. Chalkbeat reported that the city would not say which companies or which products they were.

On September 2, New York City Public Schools announced that student-facing generative AI would be off limits for the school year. From 2-K through eighth grade, close to 600,000 students. The largest school district in the country.

The city said it was disabling AI features on 38 software programs already in use. It did not name them.

Only two products have surfaced so far, and one of the two vendors, Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, replied that its elementary reading curriculum has no student-facing AI in it. The buyer says it is turning something off. The supplier says there is nothing there to turn off.

None of this happened for lack of a review.

The Education Department runs an approval process called ERMA. Nothing reaches a child until it has been through that review, and every vendor must set out what data it collects and how it protects it. The register exists. It simply has no field that asks whether generative AI sits inside the product.

A ban list is an asset register. That is the sentence I kept returning to while writing.

Deciding what to prohibit can be done in an announcement. Knowing what you are already running can only be done in a register. The first wall Pebblous runs into when diagnosing data quality is usually the same one. When there is no answer to what exists and where it lives, the work starts with a survey rather than a measurement.

An institution that had positioned itself as a cautious adopter found that the moment it moved to block adoption was the moment it had to count what it was already running.

A policy can be announced in a day. A register cannot be built in one.
