로봇 손이 시간당 1,500개를 집는다는 수치가 지난 주말 내내 인용됐습니다. 그 값이 어느 과제에서 나왔는지를 적어 둔 곳은 회사도 언론도 아닌 투자사였습니다.

에카 로보틱스(Eka Robotics)는 10월 20일 a16z와 F-Prime이 공동 주도한 1억 5천만 달러 시리즈 A를 발표했습니다. 투자 후 기업가치는 14억 5천만 달러입니다. 창업자는 MIT 교수 풀킷 아그라왈과 구글 딥마인드 출신 연구자 투오마스 하르노야입니다.

모델 이름은 VFA입니다. 시각과 힘과 행동을 묶었습니다. 최근 몇 년 로봇 분야의 표준어였던 VLA에서 가운데 자리를 차지하던 언어를 힘으로 바꾼 것입니다. 아그라왈의 설명은 이렇습니다. "시각은 물체가 대략 어디에 어떤 자세로 있는지를 알려 주지만, 실제로 쥐는 일과 실제로 더듬는 일은 힘을 통해 일어난다." 학습 데이터도 사람 시연을 베끼는 대신 물리 시뮬레이션 안에서 만듭니다.

같은 날 a16z가 올린 투자 공지는 1,500개를 실험실 인덕션과 빈피킹 과제에서 보고된 값으로 적었습니다. 그러고는 교대 근무 전체를 통틀어 그 성능을 유지하는 것이 다음 시험이라고 덧붙였습니다. 그리퍼 하드웨어는 현재 500만 사이클 정격이고, 표준 산업용 로봇 팔이 맞춰 둔 2천만 사이클은 아직 목표입니다.

수집 방식이 바뀌면 품질을 재는 축도 바뀝니다. 사람이 만든 시연 데이터에서는 출처와 라벨 일관성이 품질의 축이었고, 시뮬레이션이 데이터를 만들면 연습한 마찰과 무게와 자세의 범위가 실제 라인을 덮는지가 축이 됩니다. 페블러스가 물리 기반 합성 데이터를 만드는 일과 학습 데이터를 진단하는 일을 한자리에서 보는 이유도 여기에 있습니다. 조건이 적힌 수치는 검증할 수 있고, 조건이 지워진 수치는 그럴 수 없습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A robot hand picking 1,500 objects an hour was the figure that traveled all weekend. The party that recorded which task produced it was neither the company nor the press. It was the investor.

Eka Robotics announced a $150 million Series A on October 20, co-led by a16z and F-Prime, at a post-money valuation of $1.45 billion. It was founded by Pulkit Agrawal, a professor at MIT, and Tuomas Haarnoja, formerly a researcher at Google DeepMind.

The model is called VFA, after vision, force and action. Robotics has spent the past few years on VLA, where the middle letter is language; Eka swapped that slot for force. "Vision tells you roughly where the object is, what the pose is, but the actual grabbing, the actual searching is happening through forces," Agrawal told TechCrunch. The training data follows the same logic: rather than copying human demonstrations, it is generated inside a physics simulation.

The a16z post published the same day places the 1,500 figure in laboratory induction and bin-picking tasks, then adds that holding that performance across an entire shift is the next test. Eka's gripper hardware is currently rated for 5 million duty cycles, and the 20 million cycle bar set by standard industrial arms remains a target.

Change how data is made and you change what quality means. With human demonstrations, the axis was provenance and label consistency. With a physics engine, the axis moves to coverage: whether the range of friction, weight and pose a policy practiced against covers the range on an actual line. That is why Pebblous treats building physics-grounded synthetic data and diagnosing training data as one job rather than two. A number with its conditions written next to it can be verified. A number with its conditions stripped off cannot.

## Facebook (EN)

Pulling items one at a time out of a jumbled bin is called bin picking. The baseline for a person picking objects runs at about 600 an hour.

That is one every six seconds.

The robotics announcement I read this week was, in effect, the claim that those six seconds are now 2.4.

Eka Robotics builds the part at the end of a robot arm, the hand that grips, inserts and turns things. Fifteen hundred picks an hour. Two and a half times a person.

The model is called VFA, after vision, force and action. For the past few years the term heard most often in robotics has been VLA, where the middle letter stands for language: a person gives an instruction in words, and the model looks and moves. Eka swapped that middle slot for force.

Pulkit Agrawal, one of the founders, puts it simply. Vision tells you roughly where the object is and what pose it is in, but the actual grabbing happens through forces. One image in the announcement shows the hand closing around a lit candle in a setting where the eye has very little to go on.

For several days the number 1,500 was all that traveled.

What held my eye was not the number. It was the condition written beside it.

The party that recorded that condition most precisely was neither the company nor the press. It was a16z, which co-led the round. The post places the figure in laboratory induction and bin-picking tasks. Then it adds one more line: holding that performance across an entire shift is the next test.

I have started separating the two as "a laboratory scorecard" and "a shift scorecard."

The first records a personal best. The second records time not spent stopped.

Which leaves one question.

"What data proves that a score holds through a full shift?"

Answering it takes more than a count of items picked. It takes the type of each failed pick and the condition of the object at the time, how long a retry took to succeed, and how the success rate moves as duty cycles accumulate.

That is why Pebblous treats building physics-grounded synthetic data and diagnosing training data as one job. A number with its conditions attached can be held against the conditions on your own line. A number with its conditions stripped off cannot.

None of this is an argument against Eka. The researchers who saw the robot up close were clear about what they saw, and nearly every condition here was written down by the company and its investor themselves.

What went missing was not the performance. It was the part of the journey the number took to reach us.
