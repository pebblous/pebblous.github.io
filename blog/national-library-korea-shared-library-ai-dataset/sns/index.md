## LinkedIn (EN)

The AI training data the National Library of Korea opened on September 17 is announced as 38.33 million items. The part of it counted in books and articles is 3,974 works.

The venue is a site called the Shared Library, searchable and downloadable without an account. It holds modern magazines from the 1930s and 1940s, government publications and school textbooks from the 1940s through the 1960s, and open access scholarly articles licensed for AI training. It is the first time the country's national library has packaged its own holdings in a form models can train on.

Some 99.93 percent of the announced total comes from a single line item called the character dataset. Each entry is one character cut out of a scanned page as a picture, labeled with the character it shows. That is material for teaching a recognition model to read old type, not prose for a language model to swallow. Nothing is wrong with the addition. Books, fragments of picture and single characters were simply added up on one line.

The recount already exists inside the platform. Open the same holdings in integrated search and the top of the screen reads 25,459. That is the count with the character dataset set aside. Only the headline figure that traveled outward skipped the arithmetic.

What was selected was selected by copyright status, and a selection rule settles what falls out as much as what goes in. Cut the text holdings by publication year and everything from 1970 through 1999 comes to 94 items. This is why, at Pebblous, we ask about the conditions and units of collection before the volume. Fixing a catalog costs far less than building the data again.

## Facebook (EN)

For as long as libraries have counted things, the unit has been the book.

One volume, two volumes. So many to a shelf.

On September 17 the National Library of Korea opened, for the first time, the AI training data it had built from its own holdings. The site is called the Shared Library, and anyone can search it and download from it without an account.

The number attached to the announcement was 38.33 million items.

My first thought, seeing it, was of shelves.

Then it turned out that 99.93 percent of that total is pictures of single characters, each one cut out of a scanned page on its own. Work back from the counts listed on each individual item and one item comes out to one character. Nowhere on the platform did we find that spelled out in a sentence. What the line accounting for almost the entire announced total is counting is left for a reader to reverse-engineer.

It is material for teaching a model to read old type, not prose for a language model to learn sentences from.

The part counted in books and articles is 3,974 works.

Nothing is wrong with the addition. Books, fragments of picture lifted off a page, and single letters were added together on one line. A mixed total, if we need a name for it.

"What did 38.33 million count? Books, or letters?"

The recount already exists inside the platform. Open the same holdings in integrated search and the top of the screen reads 25,459. That is the count with the character dataset set aside. Only the headline figure that traveled outward skipped the arithmetic.

What decided the contents was copyright status. Rights were cleared before release, which is reassuring news for anyone on the using end. But a selection rule settles what falls out as much as what goes in. Cut the text holdings by publication year and everything from 1970 through 1999, thirty years of it, comes to 94 items.

The modern Korea a model raised on this data ends up knowing is not a sample of the writing produced in that era. It is a sample of the writing whose rights were settled first.

A catalog is a map of the data. When the map holds a single total and nothing else, whoever receives that dataset has to work out its size and its composition again from the beginning. When we run a data diagnosis at Pebblous, the first question is not how many items there are but what one item is.

None of which is a reason to run this release down. For a researcher who could not lay hands on training material because of the copyright burden, 3,974 works is no small gift, and for anyone raising a character recognition model, 38.3 million items of character material is an asset that existed nowhere else in the country.

Had one line recorded how much of what is inside, unit included, that value would have carried more accurately than it does now.

Fixing a catalog costs far less than building the data again.
