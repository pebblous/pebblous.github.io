## LinkedIn (KO)

2027년 상장을 준비하는 AI 클라우드 기업 Lambda의 주문 잔고가 석 달 만에 150억 달러에서 500억 달러가 됐다. 늘어난 350억 달러는 8월 말 앤트로픽 한 곳과 맺은 계약의 규모와 같은 수다.

테크크런치는 10월 6일 이 회사가 투자 전 기업 가치 145억 달러에 최대 40억 달러를 조달 중이며 코튜 매니지먼트와 블랙스톤이 라운드를 이끈다고 전했다. 비상장사라 공시된 재무제표가 없는 자리에서, 수요가 늘고 있다는 주장을 떠받치는 가장 구체적인 수가 이 잔고다. 주식 투자자에게는 미래 매출의 증거로, 데이터센터 건설 자금을 빌려주는 쪽에는 상환 능력의 근거로 같이 쓰인다.

그 500억 달러를 고객별로 쪼개면 앤트로픽 몫이 70%다. 두 수가 같다고 해서 석 달 동안 다른 계약이 하나도 없었다는 뜻은 아니다. 기존 잔고 가운데 일부는 그사이 실제 매출로 빠져나갔을 테니, 얼마가 나가고 얼마가 새로 들어왔는지는 총액만 적힌 줄에서 읽히지 않는다.

같은 업종의 상장사 CoreWeave는 분기마다 최대 고객의 매출 비중을 증권거래위원회 보고서에 적는다. 2026년 2분기에 36%였고 한 해 전인 2025 회계연도에는 67%였다. 쏠림이 더 작아서 다른 자리가 아니라, 그 수를 밖에서 열어 볼 수 있어서 다른 자리다. Lambda의 구성은 기자가 투자자 서한을 입수한 뒤에야 드러났다.

고객 열 곳에 고르게 퍼진 500억 달러와 한 곳에 70%가 쏠린 500억 달러는 완전히 다른 장부인데, 합계로 적는 순간 두 장부는 같은 모양이 된다. 평균이 분포를 가린다는 경계는 익숙한데 합계 쪽은 대체로 그냥 받아들인다. 페블러스가 DataClinic으로 데이터 품질을 정량 진단할 때 점수 한 줄과 함께 그 점수가 쪼개진 단위를 적는 것도 같은 문제를 겨눈다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The order backlog at Lambda, the AI cloud company preparing to list in 2027, went from $15 billion to $50 billion in three months. The $35 billion of growth is the same figure as one contract it signed with Anthropic in late August.

TechCrunch reported on October 6 that the company is raising up to $4 billion at a pre-money valuation of $14.5 billion, in a round led by Coatue Management and Blackstone. With no filed financials to read, that backlog is the most concrete number standing behind the claim that demand is rising. It serves equity investors as evidence of future revenue and the lenders financing data centre construction as evidence of capacity to repay.

Split by customer, Anthropic accounts for 70% of the $50 billion. Two matching numbers do not mean no other contract was signed across those three months. Some of the existing backlog will have converted into revenue in that period, and how much left and how much arrived cannot be read off a line written as a total.

CoreWeave, a listed company in the same business, writes its largest customer's revenue share into its SEC filings every quarter: 36% in the second quarter of 2026, and 67% in fiscal 2025 a year earlier. What separates the two companies is not a smaller concentration but a visible one. Lambda's composition surfaced only after a reporter obtained the investor letter.

A $50 billion spread evenly across ten customers and a $50 billion with 70% in one place are entirely different books, and the moment they are totalled the two take the same shape. Guarding against an average that hides a distribution is familiar; a total tends to be taken as it comes. When Pebblous puts numbers on data quality through DataClinic, it records the unit a score comes apart in alongside the score itself, for the same reason.

An order backlog is the sum of contracts signed but not yet booked as revenue. Less what a company earned today, more the reservation book it is holding.

With no filed financials to read, that one line is the most concrete number standing behind the case that demand is rising.

$50 billion.

Split by customer, Anthropic alone holds 70% of it.

Two matching numbers do not mean no other contract was signed in between. Some of the existing backlog will have converted into revenue, and how much left and how much arrived cannot be read off the total.

Before the split and after it, the book is the same. The number on it is the same $50 billion.

What changes is only how much the reader ends up holding.

A $50 billion spread evenly across ten customers and a $50 billion with 70% in one place are entirely different books, and the moment they are totalled the two take the same shape.

That an average hides a distribution is a familiar caution. That a total does the same thing is less familiar.

A total looks as though no calculation happened, which may be why.

This is not an argument that Lambda is in danger.

CoreWeave, listed and in the same business, had 67% of its revenue coming from a single customer in fiscal 2025, a year ago. What sets the two apart is not a smaller concentration but a quarterly filing anyone can open.

"Can you split the total in front of you right now, by customer, by source, and write it down?"

Pebblous runs DataClinic to put numbers on the quality of AI training data. A line reading 95% accuracy says nothing about the distribution it stands on, and a label reading one million training records does not say how many sources those million came from.

The trouble is not a wrong number. It is a correct number travelling all the way to the conclusion without ever being split.

The picture after the split cannot be drawn in later.

It is there only in the books someone decided to keep that way.
