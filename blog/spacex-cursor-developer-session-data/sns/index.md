여기서 계산이 맞지 않습니다. 컴퓨트는 계약으로 넘길 수 있는 자원이고, 커서는 이미 4월부터 그 계약으로 스페이스X 인프라를 쓰고 있었습니다. 반대편 자산은 커서가 다섯 달 전에 공개한 학습 방식 문서에 적혀 있습니다. 실사용자 상호작용에서 나온 토큰을 보상 신호로 정제해 다섯 시간마다 새 체크포인트를 내보내는데, 그 신호 가운데 하나는 에이전트가 만든 편집이 코드베이스에 그대로 남았는지 여부입니다.

그 고리에 무엇이 들어가는지는 프라이버시 모드라는 설정 하나로 갈립니다. 모드를 끄면 코드베이스 데이터와 프롬프트, 편집기 동작을 저장해 모델 학습에 쓸 수 있다고 커서 문서에 적혀 있습니다. 5만 곳 넘는 고객사 각각의 개발자 계정이 지금 어느 쪽에 있는지는 도구를 도입한 조직만 확인할 수 있습니다.

사내에 코딩 에이전트를 붙일 때 확인해야 할 것은 기능 비교표가 아니라 로그의 소유와 반출 조건입니다. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data를 값의 정확도가 아니라 소유와 반출 조건까지 포함하는 문제로 다루는 이유가 여기에 있습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The company with the most GPUs on earth spent its last $60 billion on the way people edit code.

SpaceX closed its all-stock acquisition of the coding agent Cursor on August 14. Cursor does not stay independent; its people and operations move into SpaceXAI. The reason Cursor led with in its own announcement was access to the largest GPU fleet in the world.

That reason does not add up on its own. Compute is a thing you can hand over by contract, and Cursor had been running on SpaceX infrastructure under exactly that kind of contract since April. The asset on the other side of the trade is written down in a Cursor engineering post from five months earlier. Billions of tokens from interactions with real users are distilled into reward signals, and a new checkpoint ships roughly every five hours. One of those signals is whether the edit the agent made stayed in the codebase.

What enters that loop turns on a single setting called privacy mode. With it off, Cursor's own documentation says codebase data, prompts, and editor actions can be stored and used to train models. Which side each developer account sits on, across more than 50,000 customer organizations, is something only the adopting company can check.

Before you wire a coding agent into your own work, the thing to settle is not the feature list. It is who owns the logs and where they are allowed to go. That is why Pebblous treats AI-Ready Data as a question of ownership and export terms, not only whether the values are correct.

## Facebook (EN)

"Keep this one. Undo that one."

It is a call you make a dozen times a day, skimming what the agent just rewrote. It passes without feeling like a record of anything.

Then I read the engineering post Cursor published back in March, and learned where that call goes.

Whether the edit the agent made stayed in the codebase is used as a reward signal to revise the model.

Signals from live sessions are gathered, and a new checkpoint ships roughly every five hours.

On August 14, SpaceX finished acquiring Cursor for $60 billion in stock. The announcement led with the largest GPU fleet in the world. But compute is a thing you can lend by contract, and Cursor had been running on SpaceX infrastructure under one of those contracts since April.

So I have come to read this deal as a purchase of "work trajectories." No amount of GPUs produces them. They exist only while a person is using the tool.

What stayed with me afterward was not a question about Cursor. It was a question about us.

"When our developers undo what the agent wrote, does that record stay on our side too?"

If it does not, the same session becomes material for the vendor's next model and, for us, just another day that already happened. When the only thing on the table is a feature comparison, the asset that carries the price is sitting outside the table.
