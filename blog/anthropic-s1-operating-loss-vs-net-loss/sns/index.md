앤트로픽이 10월 26일 나스닥 상장을 신청했다. 같은 문서가 적은 반년 순손실은 807억 달러이고, 같은 문서의 영업손실 칸은 6억 9300만 달러다.

둘 다 맞는 숫자다. 차액의 대부분인 723억 8800만 달러는 전환사채와 SAFE의 공정가치가 오르며 장부에 잡힌 비현금 비용이다. 투자자가 이 회사를 비싸게 볼수록 커지는 항목이라, 몸값이 오르면 장부의 순손실도 같이 불어난다. 통장에서 빠져나간 현금은 없다.

장사 쪽만 떼어 놓으면 방향이 다르다. 상반기 매출 162억 8600만 달러는 지난해 같은 기간의 열네 배이고, 2분기에는 영업이익 9억 7400만 달러가 났다. 영업 기준으로 흑자가 난 첫 분기다.

그림자도 같은 문서 안에 있다. S-1의 약정 주석은 금액과 기간이 고정된 컴퓨팅 약정만 6월 30일 기준 2656억 달러로 적는다. 학습 데이터에 대해서는 그 사용이 공정이용에 해당한다고 선의로 믿는다고 쓰면서, 법원이 달리 판단하면 사업에 부정적 영향이 있을 수 있다고 덧붙였다.

어느 쪽이 장사의 성적인지를 가른 것은 계산이 아니라 손실을 어느 칸에 세우느냐였다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Anthropic filed to go public on Nasdaq on October 26, and the same document reports two losses for the same six months. The net loss is $80.7 billion. The operating loss line reads $693 million.

Neither figure is wrong. Most of the gap is $72.388 billion of non-cash cost, booked as the fair value of convertible notes and SAFEs rose with the company's valuation. The more investors are willing to pay for the company, the larger the loss on its books. No cash left the account.

On the operating line the direction is different. First-half revenue of $16.286 billion is fourteen times the same period last year, and the second quarter closed with operating income of $974 million, the first quarter in the black on operations.

The filing carries its own shadows. The commitments note counts $265.6 billion of compute obligations fixed in amount and term as of June 30. On training data, the company writes that it believes in good faith its use qualifies as fair use, and that a court deciding otherwise could harm the business.

Which number is the record of the business was settled not by arithmetic but by which column the loss was booked in.

## Facebook (EN)

Two losses for the same six months went past me yesterday, both belonging to the same company.

One story said $80.7 billion. The other said $693 million.

I opened them side by side expecting to find a typo, and both numbers turned out to have been copied from one document: Anthropic's registration statement, filed with the SEC on October 26.

Most of the distance between them is a $72.388 billion change in fair value. Investors hold a right to convert into shares later, accounting treats that right as a liability, and the liability is priced again at the close of every period. When the company is worth more, what it owes later is worth more, and the increase goes down as a cost. No cash left the account.

'A loss the valuation wrote.' That is the phrase that came to mind while I read the line. It grows when things are going well.

That pairing is not confined to financial statements. Read the labels again more carefully and the disagreement count climbs. Count the missing values honestly and the missingness rate looks worse. The team that did the work properly comes off badly in the report.

"Is there a column on your dashboard that gets uglier the better the work is?"

Pebblous puts a figure on the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. What gets settled first there is direction, not score: which way a number should move once someone has done the job well.

Finding that column is probably the easy part. The harder part is arguing, after it has been found, that the number should be left where it stands.
