# Meta Is Selling Stock, and Its Chip Suppliers Are the Buyers

_Meta is raising $97.5 billion in equity for AI facilities, and the cornerstone buyers are Nvidia at $3 billion and AMD at $1 billion, both of which sell Meta chips._

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Meta has told the stock market it wants money for AI facilities. The filing it put before the Securities and Exchange Commission on October 26 carried a figure of $95 billion, and within a day the figure had grown to $97.5 billion. Demand for the common stock came in more than five times the offering, so Meta lifted that slot from $22.5 billion to $25 billion. As a single trip to the equity market, this is a size the company has never attempted before.

> What drew attention was not the figure but the list of names. The cornerstone investors written into the filing as buyers of the common stock are Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom, and all three sell Meta the chips and systems it builds with. The next day Nvidia and AMD each named an amount, and Broadcom has yet to say anything. The two together come to $4 billion, 16% of the $25 billion common stock offering.

> Sections 1 through 4 follow what Meta's filings, the companies' own announcements, and the reporting that carried them record. The sorting in Section 5, which splits these numbers into what the market priced, what the parties to the deal wrote down, and what was never disclosed at all, and the reading in Section 6, which says that sorting is the same work data quality asks for every day, belong to this article.

### Key Numbers

Four numbers carry this announcement. The first three are amounts written into filings and company statements. The last one is a slot where nothing has been written.

## A Day Later, the Target Grew

The filing Meta made on October 26 had two slots. First, about $35 billion raised through a public offering: roughly $22.5 billion of common stock, plus about $12.5 billion of preferred depositary shares that convert into common stock when their date arrives. The remaining $60 billion is to be filled by selling shares at market prices, a little at a time, from the first quarter of 2027 onward. That program opens only after the new non-voting shares have started trading.

The next day the first slot grew. Orders came to more than five times the stock on offer, so Meta raised $22.5 billion to $25 billion, and the price was set at $625 a share. The preferred depositary shares sell at $50 each, 250 million of them. Add the two and you get $37.5 billion; add the $60 billion of at-market sales and you get the $97.5 billion total of the plan.

The structure printed next to the figure deserves as much attention as the figure. On the common stock side, Meta is not issuing shares and selling them today. According to the filing, it is entering forward sale agreements with Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs. Meta picks the day it hands over the shares and collects the money, any time up to November 19, 2027, and settles at the offering price less the underwriting discount. Which is to say that the day $25 billion reaches Meta's account has not been fixed yet.

Other terms were not all written down on the first day either. The dividend rate on the preferred depositary shares was left to pricing in the October 26 filing, and the figure that it settled at 6.50% a year came out in the reporting that followed. There is also a separate allotment the underwriters may take up within 30 days: $3.375 billion of common stock and 37.5 million depositary shares. So $97.5 billion is not a ceiling either.

Why equity, then. Meta has been paying for its facilities out of the cash it earns. Capital spending planned for this year runs from $135 billion to $145 billion, close to double last year's $72.2 billion. As of September 30 the company held $82.45 billion in cash and equivalents against $80.92 billion of long-term debt. Free cash flow for the third quarter came in at negative $2.12 billion, the first negative reading since 2012. Money left the books faster than it came in.

How that happened is written plainly in the quarterly results. Cash from operations in the third quarter was $32.786 billion, and capital spending was $34.91 billion. The gap between the two is the negative free cash flow. Widen the window to January through September and cash from operations stands at $96.873 billion, which is still thick. Meta chose to sell stock anyway. Over those same nine months it bought back none of its own shares, where a year earlier it had repurchased $26.248 billion. The company that was buying its stock back has moved to the seat where it sells it.

## Nothing Requires the Three Chip Sellers to Buy Meta Stock

Three companies appear in the filing under the heading of cornerstone investors: Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom. The filing records that the three have, severally and not jointly, indicated an interest in buying up to $5 billion of the common stock at the same price and on the same terms as every other investor.

Two things have to be kept apart here. First, this is an indication of interest and not a commitment to buy. The filing states outright that it carries no legal obligation, that any of them may buy more, buy less, or buy nothing at all, and that the underwriters may adjust allocations as well. Second, there is no discount. Where cornerstone investors usually come in a few percent below the market, these three pay the same $625 a share as everyone else.

No discount, though, means only that these three were not marked down separately. It does not mean $625 was the going price. That level sits 2.7% below the close on the Friday before the announcement. Everyone who came into the offering bought that much below the market, and the underwriting discount applies to the shares handed to the three chip companies just as it does to the rest.

On October 27 two of the three named their amounts. Nvidia put in $3 billion and AMD $1 billion. Broadcom is on the cornerstone list by name only and has not disclosed a figure. The filing caps the three together at $5 billion and $4 billion has been disclosed, which leaves $1 billion of room, but because an indication of interest binds no one, that subtraction cannot settle what Broadcom will buy.

How large a place that $4 billion holds in the order book can be estimated. With the offering more than five times oversubscribed, more than half of the allocated shares went to long-only funds and existing shareholders, according to the reporting. The fifteen largest recipients took roughly 70% of the deal. Split that share evenly across fifteen and each comes to a little over $1.2 billion. Nvidia alone disclosed $3 billion, more than double that average.

What the three have in common sets the character of the list. Every chip and system going into Meta's AI facilities comes from one of these three.

## Supply Deals and Equity Checks in One Week

What separates this from an ordinary share purchase is that the investment news arrived on the same day as the supply news. Nvidia disclosed its $3 billion alongside word that deployments of its Vera Rubin systems and Vera CPUs at Meta are growing, and added that it is widening its work on Muse, Meta's AI agent. AMD disclosed its $1 billion alongside word that its Helios racks are installed at Meta and running Muse, with EPYC processors beside them. Both are continuations of supply agreements signed in February of this year.

Inside one week, then, money moved in two directions. When Meta buys chips, that is revenue for Nvidia and AMD. When those two buy Meta stock, that is capital for Meta.
