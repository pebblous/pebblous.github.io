유럽연합이 연구자에게 내줄 AI 자원의 한 층을, 기업과 재단 일곱 곳이 적어 낸 자발적 약속으로 채우기로 했습니다.

집행위원회가 10월 15일 더블린 과학 속 AI 정상회의에서 RAISE 약정 사업을 시작했습니다. 구글은 유럽 연구자 최대 200명에게 클라우드 크레딧 300만 달러어치와 딥마인드 프런티어 모델 접근을, 노보노디스크재단은 AI 전용 슈퍼컴퓨터 제피온의 연산 능력을 내놓기로 했습니다.

공적 예산을 기업 돈으로 갈아 끼운 것은 아닙니다. 호라이즌 유럽에는 RAISE 몫으로 2026~2027년 1억 700만 유로가 이미 배정돼 있고, 기업 약정은 그 위에 한 층 얹혔습니다.

눈에 걸리는 것은 집행위가 약정이 될 수 있다고 적어 둔 범주입니다. 연산과 모델만이 아니라 데이터셋과 소프트웨어 라이선스가 함께 들어 있습니다. 그리고 이 약속은 해마다 다시 검토합니다. 몇 해짜리인지, 중간에 거두려면 어떤 절차를 밟아야 하는지는 공개된 문서에 적혀 있지 않습니다.

연산 크레딧은 다 쓰고 나면 결과가 손에 남지만, 데이터는 결과가 나온 뒤에도 계속 열려 있어야 합니다. 페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단합니다. 재 본 값이 오래 쓰이려면 그 데이터를 몇 해나 다시 열 수 있는지도 자료 옆에 적혀 있어야 합니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The European Union has decided to fill one layer of the AI its researchers use with promises written down by seven companies and foundations.

The European Commission opened the RAISE pledging initiative at the AI in Science Summit in Dublin on October 15. Google is giving up to 200 European researchers $3 million in cloud credits along with access to Google DeepMind's frontier models, and the Novo Nordisk Foundation intends to open the computational capacity of Gefion, an AI-dedicated supercomputer.

This is not public budget swapped out for corporate money. Horizon Europe has €107 million dedicated to RAISE across 2026 and 2027, and the pledges were laid on top of that.

What stands out is the list of what the Commission counts as a pledge. Next to compute and models it names datasets and software licenses. The initiative is reviewed once a year, and no pledge term, and no procedure for withdrawing one, appears in anything published so far.

Compute credits get spent and the result stays in your hands. Data has to remain open after the result is published, which makes the lifespan of a permission the window in which the work can be checked. ## Facebook (EN)

"Is that dataset still open?"

Anyone who has gone back to check a number in a paper knows the question, and the login screen that sometimes arrives instead of the file.

On October 15, in Dublin, the European Commission opened the RAISE pledging initiative.

Companies and foundations write down what they will give European researchers, and the Commission publishes the list.

Seven went first. Google put up $3 million in cloud credits and access to Google DeepMind's frontier models. The Novo Nordisk Foundation intends to open the computational capacity of Gefion, an AI-dedicated supercomputer.

What kept me there was not an amount. It was a category.

Here is what the Commission counts as a pledge. Funding, access to compute, models, cloud services, scholarships, fellowships, training programmes. And datasets. And software licenses.

Compute credits get spent, and the result stays in your hands.

Data is different. It has to still be there after the result is published, because checking a number in a paper means opening that data again.

Borrowed ground, perhaps.

The pledges come up for review once a year. How long any one of them runs, and what a provider does to withdraw it, is not written in anything published so far.

"Can a result produced in 2026 from a pledged dataset still be checked in 2029?"

Pebblous checks the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. For that measurement to hold its worth, how many years the data can be opened again belongs beside it.

Anything built on borrowed ground might also carry a note saying how long the ground was promised for.
