## LinkedIn (KO)

정답률 공동 1위였던 GPT-5.5가 종합 순위에서는 5위로 내려앉았다.

미시간대가 7월 공개한 인과 추론 벤치마크 CausalDS는 여섯 개 데이터 과학 AI에게 상관관계 계산을 넘어 '무엇이 원인인가', '이 데이터로 그 질문에 답할 수 있기는 한가'를 물었다. 정답을 맞히는 능력과 자기 답을 얼마나 믿어도 되는지 아는 능력은 서로 다른 축이었고, GPT-5.5는 뒤쪽에서 무너졌다. 명목 95% 신뢰구간의 실제 커버리지가 42.9%에 그쳤기 때문이다.

이 벤치마크는 '모른다'고 물러서는 판단까지 점수로 매겼다. 답할 수 없는 질문을 알아차려 기권하는 능력은 감점이 아니라 1급 성적으로 채점됐다.

정답률 하나만 보고 모델을 고르면, 42.9%짜리 확신을 95%로 착각한 채 의사결정에 넣게 된다. 데이터가 깨끗한 것과 인과적으로 답할 수 있는 것은 다르다. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data를 정확도만이 아니라 정직한 불확실성까지로 넓혀 보는 이유가 여기에 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

GPT-5.5 tied for the top accuracy score on a new causal-reasoning benchmark. It finished fifth overall.

Michigan's CausalDS, released in July, asked six data-science AI agents to move past correlation and answer a harder question: what causes what, and can this data answer that at all. Getting the answer right and knowing how far to trust it turned out to be separate abilities. GPT-5.5 matched Claude at 82.4% accuracy, but the true coverage of its nominal 95% confidence intervals was only 42.9%.

The benchmark also scored something most tests ignore: the judgment to say "I don't know." Recognizing a question that cannot be answered in principle counted as a first-class result, not a penalty.

Pick a model on accuracy alone and you fold 42.9% confidence into a decision while reading it as 95%. Clean data and causally answerable data are not the same thing, which is why we treat data quality as more than accuracy — honest uncertainty belongs in the definition.

## Facebook (EN)

I kept coming back to one row on the scoreboard.

The model tied for the highest accuracy had somehow landed fifth overall.

What Michigan's researchers asked six data-science AI agents was not really about correlation. It was about cause. What makes what happen, and, before that, whether this data can answer the question at all.

That is where a quiet fracture opened. Getting an answer right and knowing how much to trust it turned out to be two different abilities. One model announced 95% confidence and then held less than half of it.

The part that stayed with me was different. This benchmark did not punish a model for saying "I don't know." Recognizing a question that cannot be answered, and stepping back, counted as a skill of its own.

So what makes data good? Data that returns a precise answer, or data that is honest about where the answers run out? The reason we at Pebblous think about data quality as more than accuracy, as honest uncertainty too, probably lives in that same question.
