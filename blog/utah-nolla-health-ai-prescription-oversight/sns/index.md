## LinkedIn (KO)

미국의 한 주가 의사의 서명 없이 AI가 처방을 내도 좋다고 문서에 적었다. 전에 받던 약을 이어 주는 갱신이 아니라 처음 내는 처방까지 들어간다.

유타주 인공지능정책국과 전문면허국이 9월 22일 스타트업 놀라헬스와 규제 완화 합의서에 서명했다. 대상은 만 18세 이상 유타 거주자의 가벼운 여드름부터 중간 정도까지다. 환자가 앱에서 문진에 답하고 얼굴을 촬영하면 AI가 중증도에 점수를 매긴다. 통과한 환자에게는 사전에 승인된 바르는 약 목록에서 하나가 골라져 약국으로 전송된다. 먹는 약은 전부 빠져 있다.

눈여겨볼 대목은 AI의 정확도가 아니라 감독의 설계다. 합의서는 사람이 처방을 들여다보는 방식을 세 단계로 나눠 두었다. 처음 100명까지는 의사 두 명이 약국에 보내기 전에 전부 검토한다. 누적 500명까지는 AI가 먼저 발급하고, 의사가 적어도 일주일에 한 번 모든 건을 다시 본다. 그다음부터는 한 달에 한 번 전체의 10%를 표본으로 본다.

감독이 처음 느슨해지는 자리는 10%로 떨어지는 지점이 아니다. 검토가 사전에서 사후로 넘어가는 지점이다. 사후 검토는 이미 나간 처방을 되돌리지 못하고, 다음 환자에게 같은 일이 반복되는 것만 막는다.

단계가 저절로 오르지는 않는다. 주 정책국의 서면 승인이 필요하고, 의사와 AI의 처방 일치율이 95% 이상이어야 한다. 놀라헬스가 안전성 근거로 내건 수치는 최근 실제 처방 1,000건에서 약 96%인데, 그 집계는 회사가 했다. 두 수의 분모가 같은 것인지는 공개된 자료로 확인되지 않는다.

회사의 최고의료자문역 자이드 파둘은 발표에 붙여 이렇게 말했다. 의료에서 AI의 진짜 시험대는 얼마나 똑똑한가가 아니라, 회사 말고 다른 누군가가 그것을 멈출 수 있는가라는 것이다. 그는 동시에 이 시범사업을 감독하는 의사 팀의 일원이다.

"사람이 감독한다"는 문장은 비율을 적지 않는다. 유타 합의서는 적었다. 100%, 100%, 10%.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A US state has put in writing that an AI may issue a prescription with no physician signature. Not just refills that continue a drug someone already prescribed, but first-time prescriptions.

Utah's Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy and its Division of Professional Licensing signed a regulatory mitigation agreement with the startup Nolla Health on 22 September. It covers Utah residents aged 18 and over with mild to moderate acne. A patient answers an intake questionnaire in the app and photographs their face, the AI scores the severity, and one topical drug is picked off a pre-approved list and sent to a pharmacy. Every oral drug is excluded.

The part worth watching is not how accurate the AI is but how the oversight was designed. The agreement splits human review into three stages. Through the first 100 patients, two licensed physicians read every prescription before it reaches a pharmacy. Up to 500 cumulative patients, the AI issues first and a physician re-reads every case at least weekly. After that, a physician reads a 10% sample once a month.

The first loosening is not the drop to 10%. It is the move from reviewing before to reviewing after. A review that happens after the fact cannot pull back a prescription already filled. It can only stop the same thing from happening to the next patient.

Stages do not advance on their own. The state office has to approve each move in writing, and physicians and the AI have to agree on at least 95% of prescriptions. The figure Nolla Health offers as safety evidence is roughly 96% across its last 1,000 real prescriptions, counted by the company. Whether the two figures share a denominator cannot be checked against anything public.

Zaid Fadul, the company's chief medical adviser, said alongside the announcement that the real test for AI in healthcare is not how smart it is but whether someone other than the company can shut it off. Fadul also sits on the physician team overseeing this pilot.

"A human stays in the loop" is a sentence with no percentage in it. Utah's agreement wrote one down. 100%, 100%, 10%.

## Facebook (EN)

Have you ever looked at the name printed on your own prescription?

I almost never have. You hand the slip over and the drug comes back, and the fact that a person is standing behind that piece of paper is not something you stop to think about.

I thought about that slip while reading an agreement the state of Utah signed last month. It lets one startup have an AI issue acne prescriptions directly, with no physician signature. Not only refills that continue a drug someone already prescribed, but first-time prescriptions too.

What surprised me was not the AI.

The agreement writes down, in advance, how much of this a human reads, and it writes it in three stages. At the start, two physicians read every prescription before it goes to a pharmacy. In the next stretch, the AI issues first and a physician reads afterward, once the drug has already gone out. Past a few hundred patients, a physician reads one case in ten, once a month.

So the schedule by which the human share shrinks was written before the first patient arrived. I have started calling that a loosening set on a timer.

And the gate that permits it is a single thing: how often physicians and the AI pick the same prescription. The company's own count sits a hair above that gate, though nothing public shows the two were measured the same way. The company is also the party that does the counting and files it with the state.

One sentence from the company's chief medical adviser has stayed with me. The real test for AI in healthcare, he said, is not how smart it is but whether someone other than the company can shut it off. He also sits on the physician team overseeing this pilot.

There is a premise we repeat at Pebblous when we talk about AI-Ready data: the context in which data was produced has to be recorded. This agreement moves that premise over to oversight. The fact that a human reviewed an AI output is data too. When they looked, what they disagreed with, how the disagreement was classified: unless those are kept alongside, there is nothing usable to go back to.

"When you say a human stays in the loop, what percentage is that human actually reading?"

Where only a principle is declared, no number gets made that could answer this. Utah wrote the number down, and because it is written, the shrinking is visible as well. I wonder what that share is on your team right now, and whether it is recorded anywhere at all.
