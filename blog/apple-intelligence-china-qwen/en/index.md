# In China, Alibaba

_The feature reaches iPhones in China in December with iOS 27.3. Alibaba says user requests and the answers that come back will not train its models_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> On iPhones sold in China, the questions Siri cannot handle will be answered by Alibaba's Qwen (千问). Apple announced the Qwen-powered version of Apple Intelligence from its China newsroom on October 31, and the software reaches a developer beta on November 4 before shipping in December as iOS 27.3. What decides whether the feature appears is not who the user is but where the device came from and where it is registered. An Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone bought in China qualifies, and so does one whose device location and Apple Account country are both set to China. A device matching neither of those two paths never sees it.

> Alibaba has said it will not use the requests users send or the responses generated for them to train its models. Outside China, the same place in Apple's architecture is held by Private Cloud Compute, a structure that enforces in technology that nothing is retained once a computation ends, and that leaves the server code open for independent experts to audit. Cloud queries from users in China do not reach those servers; they go to Alibaba's infrastructure. The sentence reads the same on both sides. On one side it is tied to machinery that can check it, and on the other it stands as an announcement.

> Sections 1 through 3 follow what can be confirmed in Apple's China newsroom, in Alibaba's announcement, and in the reporting that carried them. Reading the guarantee as changing in character from one country to the next, in section 4, and carrying that split over into a service's own terms for handling data, in section 5, are this article's interpretation.

### Key numbers

There are four. The first two mark how long the feature took to reach China and what makes it appear; the last two mark the regulatory step that opened the door and the gap still standing after that step.

## Three Places Qwen Sits Inside the iPhone

They are Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence. When Siri takes a request its own model cannot process, it calls on Qwen's broader knowledge and answers on the spot. Writing Tools gains a new entry called Compose with Qwen, which produces original text and images on any subject. The features opening alongside them include live translation in Messages, FaceTime and phone calls, Genmoji, Clean Up for taking distractions out of photos, mail summaries, priority ordering for notifications, and question-and-answer over photos and PDFs.

What is missing is just as clear. The next-generation Siri that Apple showed at WWDC this year is not part of this update. What users in China receive in December is the earlier generation of the Apple Intelligence feature set. iPad and Mac have not finished the approval process and have no date at all, so this release covers the iPhone only. The features ship switched off, and the user has to turn them on. Turning them on means signing in with an Apple Account or the user's own Qwen account, and the system asks for consent each time before a question, a file or an image is passed to Qwen.

Two paths lead to the feature. Either the Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone was bought in China, or the device location and the country or region on the Apple Account are both set to China. On either path, Siri and the device language have to be English or Simplified Chinese. It is less a feature for people who live in China than a feature that appears when the device came from China or stands at coordinates registered there. Nowhere is there a field asking for the user's nationality or address. The supported models are iPhone 15 Pro and Pro Max, every iPhone 16 and later, iPhone Air and iPhone Duo.

> [!callout]
> One line is left over. Alibaba has said it will not use the requests users send or the responses generated for them to train its models. Of everything on the feature list, that sentence alone speaks about where data goes rather than what the software can do. Set it beside the way Apple keeps the same promise elsewhere, and what the sentence rests on turns out to differ inside China and outside it.

## Why Apple Spent Two Years Looking for a Partner

Apple Intelligence was unveiled at WWDC in June 2024 and switched on first in the US that October, but on the Chinese mainland the launch itself was blocked. China's Interim Measures for the Management of Generative AI Services require anyone offering a generative AI service to the general public in China to pass a government review and register, and Apple's cloud model sat outside that gate. What Apple needed urgently, then, was not a better model but a local one already through the gate.

The search did not end on the first try. December 2024 brought reports that Apple was in talks with Tencent and ByteDance. Work with Baidu, which had started earlier, ran aground on data. Baidu wanted to collect and analyze iPhone user queries to improve its own models, and Apple's privacy policy would not allow that collection, as reporting at the time explained. The two also differed on how an answer gets made. Baidu wanted to answer out of training data, while Apple wanted answers shaped by the context of that person's iPhone use. Further reports said other candidates including DeepSeek were considered, and none of those pairings came together.

Those two years sit behind the fact that the no-training sentence appeared in the front row of this announcement, right next to the feature descriptions. Baidu, though, is not out of the picture. The main model seat went to Alibaba, and Baidu is reported to have stayed on for search and vision.

The gate opened in July 2026. The notice published by the Cyberspace Administration of China on July 15 carried seven generative AI services built into mobile phones, Apple Intelligence among them. Handset AI from Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Samsung and Nubia was on the same list. Coverage described it as the first time the regulator cleared a batch of AI models built for phones. That is a different procedure from the one that had been screening apps and cloud services. Rather than a door opening for Apple alone, the procedure for handling AI embedded in a handset had only then settled into place. Laying the published dates end to end shows the shape of the gap.

From the start, the Chinese version of Apple Intelligence was drawn around two local companies. The October 31 announcement is the Qwen side of that taking shape first. Which features each company handles, though, has not been disclosed by Apple, by Alibaba or by Baidu. The account that Qwen handles language and multimodal generation while Baidu handles search and vision comes out of industry analysis and reporting, and neither party has confirmed that division. The opacity this article turns to later does not hang on data handling alone.

## Hard Questions Travel to a Different Company in Each Country

The basic design of Apple Intelligence is the same everywhere. Solve it on the device first, and pass to the cloud only what the device cannot manage. Where that handoff lands is not the same in every country. Outside China, Google Gemini holds the spot. Apple signed a custom model licensing deal with Google in January 2026 to replace OpenAI, reported at around a billion dollars a year. From iOS 27 on, settings let a user pick ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude as the default AI.

In China, Qwen and Baidu fill that spot. Reports that Apple trained its own model for China and reports that Qwen powers Siri look like they contradict each other, but the two sit at different layers. The model that receives a request at the base and decides where to send it is Apple's; when Siri gets something past its own range, such as general knowledge, long-form writing or image generation, it calls Qwen. The role Gemini plays outside China is the role Qwen plays inside it.
