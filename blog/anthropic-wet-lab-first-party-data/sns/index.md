신약을 만드는 곳은 아니라고 대변인은 선을 그었습니다. 제약사들은 앤트로픽의 고객이자 파트너이고, 이틀 전에는 노보 노디스크와 신약 개발 협업을 발표했습니다. 남는 자리는 기초 생물학이고, 거기가 데이터가 가장 얇은 곳입니다. 세포 안에서 무엇이 무엇을 일으키는지 사람이 조건을 통제해 확인한 기록은 논문에 실린 몇 줄을 빼면 대부분 어느 실험실 노트북 안에서 끝납니다.

가장 자본이 넉넉한 AI 회사가 데이터를 사는 자리에서 만드는 자리로 한 칸 옮겨 앉았습니다. 설비를 갖는 쪽과 빌리는 쪽의 비용을 세어 본 적이 없다면, 데이터가 부족하다는 진단은 해마다 같은 자리에서 반복됩니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A company that sells software now has a room with flasks and robotic arms in it.

Reuters reported on September 18 that Anthropic runs a biology lab in the San Francisco Bay Area, and Eric Kauderer-Abrams, its head of life sciences, confirmed it in an interview. The company did not say how large the lab is, how many people work there, what biosafety level it holds, or when it opened. It comes five months after Anthropic bought Coefficient Bio, a stealth biotech with fewer than ten employees.

A spokesperson drew one line. The lab is not for drug discovery. Pharmaceutical firms are customers and partners, and two days earlier Anthropic had announced a drug discovery collaboration with Novo Nordisk. What is left is basic biology, and that is where the data runs thinnest. Records of what causes what inside a cell, under conditions somebody deliberately controlled, mostly end in a lab notebook rather than on the open web.

What the model cannot do in that room is already on the record. In liquid-handling trials with Genentech, Claude read an error caused by foam as a software problem rather than a physical one. Anthropic wrote that Claude learned the physical world through text and images. Once a researcher explained it, the model held onto the fact for the rest of the run, and Genentech folded the correction into a reusable technique. A lab is both the exam and the place the answer sheets pile up.

This is why AI-Ready Data at Pebblous never points only at training data. In a domain where data is scarce, the asset is not the data but the procedure that keeps producing it.

The best-capitalized AI company moved one seat over, from buying data to making it. If the cost of owning that equipment has never been weighed against renting it, the finding that your data is insufficient will return to the same place every year.

## Facebook (EN)

Foam climbs the wall of one well on a 96-well plate.

To a person that is foam. To Claude it was an error code.

It happened during liquid-handling trials Anthropic ran with Genentech.

When the error came up, the model retried the same well with the settings changed, which shook the liquid harder and made more foam.

Only after a researcher told it to move to a clean well and mix fewer times did the model hold onto the fact for the rest of the run.

Anthropic wrote the reason into its own announcement. Claude learned the physical world through text and images.

It has read about foam many times and has never made any.

Put that scene next to the Reuters report of September 18 and the news reads differently.

Anthropic is running a laboratory in the San Francisco Bay Area where real biology experiments happen.

How large it is, how many people work there, what biosafety level it holds, when it opened: none of it was disclosed.

The company drew one line clearly. The lab is not for drug discovery. What is left is basic biology.

And basic biology is where the data runs thinnest.

Records of what causes what inside a cell, under conditions somebody deliberately controlled, mostly end in a lab notebook. A few lines survive in a paper.

I have started calling the other kind "measured data."

Not a document in which somebody else reports an observation, but a value you produced by changing a condition yourself.

The same announcement holds a small measure of the difference. Tetsuwan Scientific, a company building automated labs, ran 1,508 conditions across four liquids, and the prediction model fit to those values came out about 12% more accurate than the instrument maker's specification sheets on held-out experiments.

A spec sheet is a document somebody else wrote. The 1,508 conditions were made on their own instruments.

There was nothing to argue about. The side that measured won.

"When the data you need does not exist anywhere in the world, does a company have to own the equipment that makes it?"

This is why AI-Ready Data at Pebblous never points only at training data. In a domain where data is scarce, the asset is not the data but the procedure that keeps producing it. What was measured under which conditions, and who checked it when, has to survive alongside the value, or the data gets used once and thrown away.

Anthropic took that seat in April by buying a whole biotech. The reported price was around $400 million.

Few companies can give an answer of that size. For most organizations, sharing the equipment with an outside partner is the right call.

But if the sum has never been done, the finding that your data is insufficient comes back to the same place every year.

How much of the data on your desk today was measured by you?

There is no telling until somebody counts.
