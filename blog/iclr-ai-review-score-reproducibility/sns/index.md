뉴질랜드 오클랜드대 연구진이 ICLR 2025와 2026 논문 200편에 1만 4천 건 가까운 AI 심사를 붙인 실험입니다. 점수가 널뛴 것은 아닙니다. 열 번의 점수 폭은 대부분 1점 안쪽입니다. 평가를 통째로 뒤집는 것이 아니라, 인접한 두 칸 사이에서 계속 마음을 바꾸는 쪽입니다.

성가신 일은 두 논문 가운데 어느 쪽이 나은지 가를 때 생깁니다. 한 번 심사로 우열을 매기면 논문 쌍 셋 중 하나가 동점으로 끝납니다. 순서가 실제로 뒤집히는 경우는 드뭅니다. 한 번 심사의 약점은 틀린 순서를 내놓는 것이 아니라 순서를 아예 내놓지 못하는 것입니다.

그러면 채점 눈금을 더 잘게 쪼개면 될까요. 연구진이 소수점을 반드시 붙이도록 하자 흔들림은 실제로 줄었습니다. 대신 평균 점수가 0.693점 통째로 올라갔습니다. 합격선을 고정해 둔 조직이 채점 기준만 정교하게 바꾸면, 손대지 않은 그 선을 넘어오는 집단이 달라집니다.

더 까다로운 쪽은 고친 원고를 다시 채점할 때입니다. 똑같은 수정안을 한 모델은 평균 0.474점 올려 줬고 다른 모델은 0.348점 깎았습니다. 이 논문이 좋은 논문인가에는 두 모델이 대체로 동의하는데, 이 수정이 논문을 좋게 만들었는가에는 동의하지 않습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The same paper was sent back to the same reviewer ten times with nothing changed. The share that came back with an identical score every time was 56.5% for the steadiest setup, and the other two sat in the low thirties.

Researchers at the University of Auckland ran close to 14,000 AI reviews across 200 papers from ICLR 2025 and ICLR 2026. The scores did not swing wildly. The spread across ten repeats stayed inside a single point for almost every paper. This is not a reviewer overturning its verdict. It is a reviewer changing its mind between two adjacent marks.

The trouble starts when two papers have to be ranked against each other. Decide on a single review and one pair in three ends in a tie. Outright reversals are rare. The weakness of a single review is not that it produces the wrong order but that it produces no order at all.

The obvious fix is a finer rubric. When the researchers required scores to carry a decimal, the wobble did fall. The mean rose by 0.693 points at the same time. An organisation that holds its cut-off fixed and refines only the rubric will find a different group clearing the untouched line.

The harder case is scoring a revision. Handed the identical set of edits, one model raised the average by 0.474 points and another cut it by 0.348. The two mostly agree on whether a paper is good. They do not agree on whether an edit made it better.

Pebblous runs quantitative diagnosis on training data with DataClinic. Human review comes with a step that measures how far the assessors agree, and a low figure sends the rubric back for a rewrite. When AI takes over the scoring, that step is often the one thing that quietly drops out. Applicant screening, supplier document review and internal data quality grades all run on the same structure.

For anyone scoring with AI, the cheapest check available is to put the same input in twice.

## Facebook (EN)

"4.2. Below the line."

A single AI-generated score deciding a document is no longer an unusual thing to see.

What number comes back if the same document goes in again that afternoon?

Some people asked that question at the scale of 200 papers.

Researchers at the University of Auckland put papers from ICLR 2025 and 2026 in front of an AI reviewer ten times each. The prompt did not change. The paper did not change. The only thing that changed was which attempt it was.

Just over half came back with an identical score every time, and that was the steadiest setup of the three.

It was not wild swinging. Not 8 one time and 3 the next, but a reviewer changing its mind between 4 and 5.

The trouble sat one step further on.

Ask for a ranking of two papers from a single review, and one pair in three lands in the same box. Not the wrong order. No order.

So draw the marks finer. The researchers thought the same thing.

Requiring a decimal did reduce the wobble. And one more number appeared in the same table. The mean had moved up by 0.693 points.

The band narrowed while the whole ruler slid.

I wrote this down as "the moving ruler." The place where a rubric grows more precise and the reference point quietly travels.

"If the measure that reduced the variance also moved the reference point, can it still be called an improvement?"

In the revision experiment the gap was plainer still. Handed the identical set of edits, one grader raised the score and the other lowered it. The two models mostly agree on whether a paper is good. They do not agree on whether an edit made it better.

This is the part Pebblous watches. Human review carries a step that measures how far the assessors agree, and a weak figure sends the rubric back to be rewritten. When DataClinic runs a quantitative diagnosis on data quality, the first thing checked is not how high the score is but whether it comes back. That step is exactly the one that tends to vanish when AI picks up the scoring.

The cheapest check is not ten runs. It is two.

The distance between two scores on the same input. That distance is the thickness of the cut-off line you are using right now.

Whether anyone has measured that thickness is a question I have not answered yet.
