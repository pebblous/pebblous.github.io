회사가 쓰려고 만들어 둔 AI 에이전트 열에 일곱이 두 달째 한 번도 돌지 않았는데, 그 에이전트가 쥔 로그인 정보는 그대로 살아 있었다.

이스라엘 보안업체 아라키스 시큐리티가 고객사 운영 환경의 에이전트 수천 개를 12주 동안 지켜보고 정리한 '2026 자율 에이전트 위협 보고서'다. 켜 둔 에이전트 가운데 76%가 두 달째 한 번도 실행되지 않았다. 실행되지 않았다는 것과 권한이 끊겼다는 것은 별개였다. 자격증명은 여전히 유효했고 트리거도 그대로 발동했다.

아라키스 대표 탈 바론은 이유를 한 문장으로 말했다. 직원이 떠나면 그 사람의 접근 권한은 그날 회수되지만, 에이전트는 쓰이지 않게 돼도 아무것도 회수되지 않는다는 것이다. 대부분의 회사에서 그 에이전트에게는 주인이 없기 때문이다.

읽을 때 같이 봐야 할 조건도 있다. 표본이 에이전트 보안 제품을 이미 들여놓은 고객사들이라 모든 회사의 평균으로 옮기기 어렵다. 휴면 판정도 마지막 실행 시각 기준이어서, 분기마다 한 번 도는 예약 작업은 멈춘 것과 구별되지 않는다. 보고서를 낸 쪽이 그 공백을 메우는 제품을 파는 회사라는 점도 알고 읽는 편이 맞다.

데이터를 맡은 쪽에서 이 숫자는 하나의 질문으로 돌아온다. 사람의 계정은 인사 기록이 닫아 주는데, 에이전트의 생애에는 그 마지막 칸이 비어 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Seven out of ten AI agents a company had built for its own use had not run once in two months. Their logins still worked.

The finding comes from the 2026 Autonomous Agents Threat Report by Arrakis Security, an Israeli security firm that gathered twelve weeks of records from thousands of agents running in customer production environments. Among the agents still switched on, 76% had not executed once in two months. Not running and losing access proved to be separate things: the credentials stayed valid and the triggers still fired.

Tal Baron, who runs the company, put the reason in a sentence. When an employee leaves, their access is removed the same day. When an agent stops being used, nobody removes anything, because in most companies nobody owns it.

The conditions on the number deserve equal billing. The sample comes from firms that had already bought agent security tooling, and dormancy was judged by last execution time, so a job that runs once a quarter looks the same as one that has stopped for good. The company also sells the product that closes the gap it measured.

For anyone responsible for data, the figure arrives as one question. A person's account is closed by an HR record; an agent's life has no equivalent final entry.

## Facebook (EN)

There is an email that arrives on your last day at a job.

Your account has been closed, your access to internal systems has been revoked, here is the equipment to hand back. The wording is clerical. What it records is that a whole procedure ran to its end on one person's behalf.

That email stayed in my head while I read a study from Arrakis Security, an Israeli security firm. Over twelve weeks the company watched thousands of AI agents running in its customers' production environments, and found that 76% of the agents still switched on had not executed once in two months.

The logins those agents held were still good. They had stopped showing up for work without ever handing in the badge.

Tal Baron, who runs the company, explained it briefly. When an employee leaves, their access is removed the same day. When an agent stops being used, nobody removes anything, because in most companies nobody owns it.

An identity with no last day.

There is a procedure for bringing one into existence and none for ending it. A person's arrival and departure are both written down somewhere, and that writing is what tells the account system to act. An agent falling out of use is written down nowhere.

"Who in your organization holds the list of agents that are running? And are the stopped ones on the same list?"

Pebblous surveys the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Standing on the side that tends the data, you notice how few organizations keep any register of the non-human things that consume it. The further the work of making data usable by AI advances, the more visible the empty column next to it becomes: who, exactly, is the AI.

The numbers come with conditions. They were gathered inside companies that had already bought agent security tooling, and dormancy was decided by one signal, the time of last execution. Even so, what lasts here is not the percentage but the sentence about ownership. A tighter review cycle can bring the percentage down. An absence of owners is not the kind of thing an audit fixes.

It took a long while for human account management to reach its present shape, with HR records wired into identity systems. On the agent side, most organizations have not laid the first wire.
