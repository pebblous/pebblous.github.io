## LinkedIn (KO)

관측은 더 이상 병목이 아니다. 8월 18일 arXiv에 올라온 연구자 27명의 커뮤니티 성명서는 이 문장으로 문제를 정의합니다.

베라 루빈 천문대는 2026년 6월 10년짜리 관측을 시작했습니다. 로먼 우주망원경은 발사를 앞두고 있고, 유클리드는 이미 수천 평방도의 이미지를 보내오고 있습니다.

2027년이면 세 대가 같은 하늘의 상당 부분을 동시에 찍습니다. 세 서베이에 들어간 미국과 유럽의 공공투자는 60억 달러를 넘습니다. 그런데 그 픽셀을 함께 처리하고 서로 보정하는 일은 어느 임무나 기관의 위임 범위에도 없습니다. 저자들의 표현으로는, 실제 데이터로 그 작업을 수행할 자금 프로그램이 존재하지 않습니다.

역량의 문제가 아니라는 증거로 논문은 DES와 KiDS를 듭니다. 두 약한 중력렌즈 협업은 양쪽 팀이 모두 참여한 결합 분석을 해냈습니다. 그러면서도 균일한 시스템 오차 보정은 스코프 밖으로 규정했습니다. 전문성이 없어서가 아니라, 그 일이 애초에 어느 협업의 할 일로도 정해져 있지 않았기 때문입니다.

합치지 않으면 정밀도가 조금 떨어지는 데서 끝나는 과학이 있고, 아예 성립하지 않는 과학이 있습니다. 마이크로렌징 사건은 한 번 일어나고 반복되지 않습니다. 나중에 독립 카탈로그를 맞춰 보는 방식으로는 복구되지 않습니다. 다만 이 문서는 새 관측 결과가 아니라 커뮤니티 관점 논문이고, 저자들도 어느 프로젝트가 실패하고 있다고는 말하지 않습니다.

이들이 하필 지금이라고 말하는 이유는 비용의 비대칭입니다. 설계 단계에서 형식과 좌표계를 맞추면 규약에 한 번 합의하는 값으로 끝납니다. 어긋난 채로 굳으면 서베이 규모의 재처리로 되돌려야 합니다. 수집에는 예산이 붙고 정렬에는 붙지 않습니다. 페블러스도 산업 데이터의 품질을 진단하며 같은 구조를 만납니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The observations are no longer the bottleneck. That is how 27 researchers, in a community perspective posted to arXiv on August 18, define the problem they are raising.

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory began its ten-year survey in June 2026, the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is awaiting launch, and Euclid is already returning imaging over thousands of square degrees. By 2027 the three will be covering much of the same sky at once, backed by combined US and European public investment exceeding $6 billion. Processing that data at the pixel level together, and calibrating each survey against the others, falls inside no mission's or agency's mandate. No funded programme exists, the authors write, to do this on real data.

The paper's evidence that this is not a capability problem is DES and KiDS. The two weak-lensing collaborations completed a joint analysis with both teams at the table, and still ruled uniform systematics calibration out of scope. Not for lack of expertise, but because the work had never been assigned to either collaboration.

Some science degrades without joint processing. Some never happens at all. A microlensing event occurs once and does not repeat, so nothing recovers it by matching independent catalogues after the fact. The document is a perspective paper rather than a new result, and its authors are careful to say that no project here is failing.

What makes the timing argument work is an asymmetry in cost. Aligning formats and coordinate conventions during design costs one agreement on a convention; discovering the mismatch later costs a survey-scale reprocessing. Budget attaching to collection but not to alignment is a pattern Pebblous meets in the same shape while diagnosing industrial data quality.

이 창은 곧 닫히고 다시 열리지 않는다고 논문은 적었습니다. 우리 쪽 데이터에도 그런 창이 열려 있는지, 열려 있다면 얼마나 남았는지는 아직 세어 본 적이 없습니다.

## Facebook (EN)

Somewhere right now, an astronomer is logging into two separate archives to pull two matched cutouts of the same patch of sky, then reconciling the coordinate conventions by hand.

The paper's verdict on this is brief.

"It works, and it works by hand, one dataset at a time."

It gets stranger when you consider the scale that hand is working against. Public investment in Rubin, Roman and Euclid exceeds $6 billion, and by 2027 all three will be imaging much of the same sky at the same time.

The telescopes are finished.

What is missing is the place where those three images would be processed together, pixel by pixel. The public investment built the telescopes, the authors write, and what remains unbuilt is the layer between them. Nobody disputes the need for it, and nobody owns it. An empty layer.

The part that stayed with me is that the emptiness is not a shortage of experts. DES and KiDS completed a joint analysis with both teams present, and still left uniform systematics calibration out of scope. Not because the expertise was missing, but because the work had never been written into either collaboration's remit.

"If every expert is already in the room, who does the work that belongs to no one in it?"

I see the same room in industrial data. Each team knows its own records well. The joining of one team's records to another's is rarely written into anyone's objectives. Collection counts toward someone's results, alignment counts toward nobody's. It is a scene Pebblous keeps meeting while diagnosing data.

The reason the authors say now rather than later comes down to cost. The three surveys are early in their life cycles, so formats and coordinate conventions can still be settled at the design stage. Settled now, the price is one agreement. Left to harden apart, the price is reprocessing at survey scale.

The window closes soon and does not reopen, the paper says. Whether a window like that is still open on our own data, and how much of it is left, is not something I have counted.
