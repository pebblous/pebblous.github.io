알려진 효소와 서열이 30%밖에 겹치지 않는 단백질을 넣은 대장균이 페니실린 512 μg/mL를 넘겨서도 자랐다.

베이징사범대 연구진이 단백질 언어 모델 ESM2에 분류기 다섯 개를 묶어 공개 메타지놈 데이터베이스 MGnify를 훑은 결과다. 환자에게서 분리한 균이 아니라 바닷물과 하수를 통째로 읽은 자료다. 항생제를 깨뜨리는 효소 베타락타마제 후보가 4만 6,335개 나왔다. 지금까지 실험으로 성질이 밝혀진 것은 1,778개였다.

기존 방식은 이미 아는 효소 서열과 닮은 것만 건졌다. 같은 일을 하면서 서열이 크게 갈라진 단백질은 그물코를 그대로 빠져나갔다. 단백질 언어 모델은 글자가 얼마나 겹치는지가 아니라 어느 자리에 어떤 아미노산이 오는지의 규칙성으로 서열을 읽는다.

실험대에 올린 둘 중 하나는 활성슬러지에서 나온 Prot94다. 알려진 단백질과 27.4%만 겹치는데, 대장균에 넣자 페니실린이 512 μg/mL를 넘겨도 자랐다. 빈 벡터 대조군은 8이었다. 지하수에서 나온 Prot22는 아미노산 한 글자를 바꾸자 이미페넴 내성이 네 배로 올라갔다. 카바페넴은 다른 약이 듣지 않을 때 마지막으로 꺼내는 항생제다.

시료를 뜬 자리도 결과를 갈랐다. 사람 대변 시료에서는 Class A가 48.38%를 차지하는데 바닷물에서는 11.00%로 내려간다. 하수에서 나온 효소는 바다에서 나온 효소보다 바꿀 수 있는 자리를 더 많이 갖고 있었다.

다만 실험대에 오른 것은 Class C 후보 2종과 단일 치환 일곱 개뿐이고, 나머지 4만여 개는 아직 예측이다. 하수와 바다의 차이도 양성 선택의 직접 증거는 아니라고 논문이 못 박아 두었다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질에 수치를 달아 둔다. 어느 항목이 사람 손으로 확인된 것이고 어느 항목이 아직 모델의 답인지는, 그 수치 옆에 따로 적어 두지 않으면 남지 않는다.

확인된 둘과 확인되지 않은 4만여 개가 지금 한 목록 안에 있다. 그 목록을 맡아 확인할 쪽은 아직 정해져 있지 않다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A protein that shares barely 30% of its sequence with any known enzyme kept E. coli growing in penicillin past the top of the measured range.

The group behind it, at Beijing Normal University, stacked five classifiers on the protein language model ESM2 and pointed the ensemble at MGnify, a public store of metagenome proteins. The material was not bacteria from patients. It was seawater and wastewater, read whole. The sweep returned 46,335 candidate beta-lactamases, against the 1,778 characterized in a laboratory until now.

Until now a new enzyme turned up only when its sequence looked like one already on file, which is a way of finding more of what you have. A protein language model reads which amino acid sits at which position instead of counting shared letters, so a protein doing the same job from far away no longer slips through.

Two candidates went onto the bench. Prot94, from activated sludge, overlaps the closest known protein by 27.4% of its sequence, and in E. coli it grew past 512 μg/mL of penicillin against an empty-vector control of 8.

In Prot22, from groundwater, swapping a single tyrosine for phenylalanine took imipenem resistance from 4 to 16. Carbapenems are what is left when everything else has failed.

The place the sample came from moved the result as well. Class A takes 48.38% of the enzymes in human fecal samples and 11.00% in seawater. Enzymes from wastewater carried more positions open to change than enzymes from the ocean.

Only two class C candidates and seven single substitutions ever reached the bench. The rest are predictions, and the paper is explicit that the wastewater-ocean difference is no direct evidence of positive selection.

DataClinic, the tool Pebblous built for this, separates what has been verified in AI training data from what has only been predicted. A figure in a table rarely says which of the two it came from, and that distinction does not come back on its own.

Two verified entries and forty-six thousand unverified ones now sit in a single list. Nobody has been given the job of telling them apart.

## Facebook (EN)

When a drug stops working, the news almost always comes out of a hospital.

Someone isolates a strain from a patient, notes down which drugs it shrugs off, and sends the result up into a surveillance network. That is how GLASS, the system the World Health Organization runs, is fed.

This study went somewhere else for its samples.

It went through seawater and wastewater that had been scooped up whole, every microbial gene in them read at once. Out of that came 46,335 candidate enzymes that take antibiotics apart. The number of such enzymes ever characterized in a laboratory was 1,778.

The distance mattered more than the count.

A protein overlapping known enzymes by barely 30% of its letters went into E. coli and held off penicillin past the top of the measured range. Something that works was sitting in a place a search for lookalikes was never going to reach.

And yet only two of these candidates ever reached a bench. All the rest are still predictions.

"When two checked entries and forty-six thousand unchecked ones share one list, whose job is it to tell them apart?"

What a row of AI training data has been through is the thing Pebblous records in DataClinic. A number tells you the condition the material is in. It does not tell you which rows of it ever passed through a pair of human hands.

The paper's data availability clause is one sentence long. The data is available on reasonable request, and no deposit in a public repository is mentioned.

A list of risk that has not yet shown up in any patient is sitting behind that one sentence.
