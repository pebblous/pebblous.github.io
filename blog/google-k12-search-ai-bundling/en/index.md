# Google Won

_Gemini and Search both arrive switched off on K-12 school accounts, and a school that enables Search has no way to remove the AI that comes with it_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Google is turning AI off by default on the accounts it hands to schools. Chris Phillips, the company's vice president for education, wrote on the corporate blog on November 3 that Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search will all start in the off position on K-12 student accounts, and that only a school administrator can switch them on. The reason the company gives is that schools should hold control over AI in their own classrooms.

> The catch is the fourth name on that list. Phillips told the New York Times that Search and AI are deeply integrated, so students at a school that declines AI will not have access to search either and will see only a message telling them it is not available. As criticism built, Google added that Gemini and Search can be enabled separately. True enough, yet switching Search on also brings AI Mode, AI Overviews and Guided Learning along with it. The switches stop at the product line, and no setting keeps search while clearing the AI answers out of it. Bernie Sanders called the arrangement extortion, and Lina Khan, the former FTC chair, called it anticompetitive.

> Sections 1 through 4 follow what Google changed and why the design is drawing fire. Section 5, which carries the same structure over to how companies bring AI tools inside, is this article's reading rather than reported fact.

## Four Items Default to Off, and the School Decides

The announcement went up on Google's education blog on November 3, written by Chris Phillips, vice president and general manager for education. What changes is the default on K-12 student accounts, and four things are covered. Gemini. Gemini Notebook. Gemini in Classroom. Google Search. All four begin in the off position, and a school administrator has to enable them in the console before a student account can open them. Schools should hold control over AI in their classrooms, the post says.

None of this lands immediately. The change takes effect in early 2027 at the earliest, and Google said it would share details with schools over the coming months. The company also described the decision as the outcome of conversations with schools, teachers, researchers and policymakers.

It is also a reversal. On August 10, Google began rolling out a Gemini tab in Google Classroom that appeared by default. Not every school was swept in automatically, since the rollout covered schools that had already permitted the underlying services, but the direction was clearly toward on. Eighty-five days later the default moves the other way.

So far this looks like a company granting what schools have been asking for. Over the past year one district did stop generative AI in its classrooms, citing how student data is handled, whether the tools are safe for children, and whether they genuinely help learning. The trouble starts with the fact that one of the four items is not an AI product.

## Gemini Has a Switch of Its Own, the AI Inside Search Does Not

Phillips told the New York Times that Search and AI are deeply integrated. A student at a school that has not enabled AI therefore cannot reach search at all, and will see only a message from the school saying it is not available. That explanation never appeared on the official blog, and once the interview circulated the reaction turned.

Google soon posted a clarification on X. Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search can each be enabled or disabled on their own. That is accurate, and it misses where the criticism lands. The unit of division stops at the product, and nothing below that line can be separated.

Enable Search and the AI living inside Search comes on with it. That includes AI Mode, which answers in conversation, AI Overviews, which places a summary above the results, and Guided Learning, which responds when a query looks like homework. No setting keeps Search running while removing those three. A school that wants its students not to read AI-written answers is left with exactly one lever, which is to shut off search itself.
