Executive Summary

Google is turning AI off by default on the accounts it hands to schools. Chris Phillips, the company's vice president for education, wrote on the corporate blog on November 3 that Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search will all start in the off position on K-12 student accounts, and that only a school administrator can switch them on. The reason the company gives is that schools should hold control over AI in their own classrooms.

The catch is the fourth name on that list. Phillips told the New York Times that Search and AI are deeply integrated, so students at a school that declines AI will not have access to search either and will see only a message telling them it is not available. As criticism built, Google added that Gemini and Search can be enabled separately. True enough, yet switching Search on also brings AI Mode, AI Overviews and Guided Learning along with it. The switches stop at the product line, and no setting keeps search while clearing the AI answers out of it. Bernie Sanders called the arrangement extortion, and Lina Khan, the former FTC chair, called it anticompetitive.

Sections 1 through 4 follow what Google changed and why the design is drawing fire. Section 5, which carries the same structure over to how companies bring AI tools inside, is this article's reading rather than reported fact.

1

Four Items Default to Off, and the School Decides

The announcement went up on Google's education blog on November 3, written by Chris Phillips, vice president and general manager for education. What changes is the default on K-12 student accounts, and four things are covered. Gemini. Gemini Notebook. Gemini in Classroom. Google Search. All four begin in the off position, and a school administrator has to enable them in the console before a student account can open them. Schools should hold control over AI in their classrooms, the post says.

None of this lands immediately. The change takes effect in early 2027 at the earliest, and Google said it would share details with schools over the coming months. The company also described the decision as the outcome of conversations with schools, teachers, researchers and policymakers.

It is also a reversal. On August 10, Google began rolling out a Gemini tab in Google Classroom that appeared by default. Not every school was swept in automatically, since the rollout covered schools that had already permitted the underlying services, but the direction was clearly toward on. Eighty-five days later the default moves the other way.

So far this looks like a company granting what schools have been asking for. Over the past year one district did stop generative AI in its classrooms, citing how student data is handled, whether the tools are safe for children, and whether they genuinely help learning. The trouble starts with the fact that one of the four items is not an AI product.

2

Gemini Has a Switch of Its Own, the AI Inside Search Does Not

Phillips told the New York Times that Search and AI are deeply integrated. A student at a school that has not enabled AI therefore cannot reach search at all, and will see only a message from the school saying it is not available. That explanation never appeared on the official blog, and once the interview circulated the reaction turned.

Google soon posted a clarification on X. Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search can each be enabled or disabled on their own. That is accurate, and it misses where the criticism lands. The unit of division stops at the product, and nothing below that line can be separated.

Enable Search and the AI living inside Search comes on with it. That includes AI Mode, which answers in conversation, AI Overviews, which places a summary above the results, and Guided Learning, which responds when a query looks like homework. No setting keeps Search running while removing those three. A school that wants its students not to read AI-written answers is left with exactly one lever, which is to shut off search itself.

An asymmetry opens here. AI that stands on its own, like a chatbot, can be removed, and AI dissolved into another feature cannot. What decides whether a thing is removable is not whether it belongs in a classroom but which product it happens to be attached to.

What a school weighs is no longer whether AI helps students learn. It becomes whether search is a price the school can afford to pay for keeping AI out. Change the question and the answer changes with it.

3

Lawmakers Reach for the Word Antitrust

What turned this design into a political fight is how far it reaches. Around 50 million Chromebooks are reported to be deployed in schools, and more than 150 million students and teachers worldwide use Google Classroom. In many American classrooms the laptop a school lends out is a Chromebook, and the search engine loaded on it is Google. Saying you will simply use something else does not come easily under those conditions.

Senator Bernie Sanders called it extortion on X, reading the arrangement as a threat to cut off search from anyone who refuses AI. Senator Richard Blumenthal said Google appears more focused on pushing Gemini into schools than on student success.

Lina Khan, the former FTC chair who now chairs the board of the New York City Economic Development Corporation, used the word anticompetitive. She described it as a demonstration of what happens when an unregulated monopolist is left holding an essential service, and pointed to Google having lost antitrust cases repeatedly without changing course. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said no company should hold the power to shape how children are taught, and accused Google of using its monopoly position to force AI in.

Educators landed in a similar place. Josh Golin, executive director of the children's advocacy group Fairplay, read the strategy as tilted toward planting lifelong brand loyalty rather than toward learning. Randi Weingarten, president of the American Federation of Teachers, said Google had put commercial interest ahead of education.

Antitrust is a different argument from judging a product to be bad. The rebuttal that nobody has to buy it requires somewhere to go instead, and a school shedding Google has to replace its devices, its coursework management and its search engine at once. When that effort outweighs the effort of accepting AI, the choice exists in name only.

New York City had already decided for itself. On September 2 it paused generative AI from 2-K through eighth grade for a year, a measure covering about 600,000 students. This blog covered that decision at the time in New York City Turned Off AI in 38 School Programs and Won't Name Them. Gemini's chatbot and AI Mode are already disabled on the city's Chromebooks, and the education department has asked Google for more detail on the new announcement. The ground a district switched off itself and the ground a vendor now decides on its behalf have started to overlap.

4

DuckDuckGo Puts Another Option in Front of Schools

While the criticism ran, DuckDuckGo started selling to schools. Founder Gabriel Weinberg posted a document on X explaining how a school can change its default search engine to DuckDuckGo, and offered direct help. The company's line was that Google forces AI on students, paired with an argument that schools ought to keep a basic function like search while refusing the AI attached to it.

What DuckDuckGo offers is divided differently. Chat stands apart as Duck.ai, the AI assistant attached to search carries a setting that disables it, and a separate address at noai.duckduckgo.com strips the AI features out entirely. The company said app installs rose 30% this year and read that as users walking away from search with AI mixed into it.

DuckDuckGo has a stake in this fight and is selling its own product as it speaks. Allowing for that, one fact still stands. A product that keeps search and AI apart is running today. Deep integration sounds like a sentence about the limits of the technology, yet whether a summary appears above the results is at minimum the kind of thing a single setting can decide. The arrangement Google has put forward is not the only shape the technology permits.

5

Why Pebblous Is Watching This Story

Step outside the school gates and a familiar scene appears. AI arrives inside a tool your team already uses, and when you open the settings to switch it off, the unit of disabling is not the unit you had in mind. You want the summarization feature out of a document editor and the option on offer covers the whole collaboration suite. You want automated review paused on a code repository and pausing it also pauses search.

Organizations usually have clear reasons for wanting AI off. For schools it is student data and the protection of minors, and for companies it is contracts, regulation and trade secrets. Bundled features keep those reasons from reaching their conclusion. When declining AI costs you a function you were already relying on, most organizations end up accepting it. Consent accumulates without anyone being asked for it.

How AI placed inside search actually moves user behavior is something this blog has examined once, following a field experiment in Hiding Google's AI Answers Sent More Clicks Out to the Web. The question there was where an AI summary sends the clicks. The question now is who decides whether that summary appears.

If you sit where AI adoption gets decided, three things can be established before anyone signs.

  • What is the unit of disabling? A product or a feature? Can the one feature you are worried about be removed on its own?
  • What else goes dark when you disable it? Do you have the list of functions you forfeit, in writing?
  • Do you hear about a change in the default before it reaches you? In this case the default moved from on to off inside three months.

None of the three asks about performance or price. All three ask about a boundary. Write down where the line between removable and permanent falls before you adopt a tool, and when that line later moves you will be able to say what moved.

Editor's Note

In procurement reviews, the field left blank most often is the one for what can be switched off. Features you can use get a table of their own, while features you can disable get a single line saying it is available in the settings. Sales explains the turning on, and nobody is paid to explain the turning off.
