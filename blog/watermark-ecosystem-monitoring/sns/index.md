대니얼 서서, 존 틱스턴, 길리 비단 세 저자는 이 신호를 콘텐츠 하나가 AI 생성물인지 가려내는 판별기 대신, 미디어 생태계에 합성 콘텐츠가 얼마나 들어와 있는지 재는 계측 장치로 다시 정의합니다. 역학자가 개인의 병력을 캐묻지 않고 하수를 검사해 지역의 감염 확산을 읽는 방식과 같습니다.

핵심은 요구 조건이 달라진다는 데 있습니다. 개별 판정에서 위양성 5%는 스무 명 중 한 명이 억울해진다는 뜻이지만, 비율을 재는 자리에서 같은 5%는 보정하거나 감안할 수 있는 편향입니다.

대신 다른 숙제가 옵니다. 표본의 대표성과 시계열, 그리고 무엇을 한 건으로 셀지에 대한 합의입니다. 넷 중 가장 무거운 것은 마지막입니다. 이 논문에는 제안을 검증한 파일럿 계측 데이터가 아직 없습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A paper posted to arXiv on August 7 does not ask for more accurate watermarks. It asks for them to be moved to a different job.

Daniel Susser, John Thickstun, and Gili Vidan propose using the signal as an instrument that measures how much synthetic content is circulating in a media ecosystem, rather than as a detector that rules on whether a given piece came from a machine. The model is epidemiology, where testing wastewater reads the spread of infection in a community without interrogating anyone's medical history.

What changes is the requirement, not the technology. On an individual verdict, a 5% false positive rate means one wronged person in twenty. On a measurement of prevalence, the same 5% is a bias you can correct for.

Different chores arrive in exchange: a representative sample, a time series, and agreement on what counts as one instance. The last one is the heaviest, and the paper carries no pilot measurement data to test any of it yet.

Data quality teams know the same condition. The problem is rarely an absence of usable signal. It is the signal already on hand, discarded for falling short of a verdict. Making a signal more accurate and deciding what to count with it are two different jobs.

## Facebook (EN)

"92% likely AI-generated."

There is not much a person can do with that line when it appears on a grading screen or in a review queue.

Teachers, editors, and platform staff rarely get to treat the number as a probability. Ninety-two percent reads as guilty in the room where it lands, and the cost of the remaining eight percent is paid alone by whoever was misclassified.

A paper posted to arXiv last week suggests turning at exactly this point. Not toward more accurate watermarks. Toward a different place to put the signal.

An epidemiologist tests wastewater to read how an infection is spreading through a neighborhood, without asking anyone about their medical history.

That is the job Daniel Susser, John Thickstun, and Gili Vidan want to give watermarks. Instead of asking "did a machine make this piece," asking "how much of this market is machine-made." Ecosystem measurement, rather than forensics.

Fabricated citations went from one in every 2,828 papers in 2023 to one in 277 by early this year.

Nobody learned that by catching individual papers. It came from counting a proportion.

Which leaves the harder question. "What have we agreed to count as one instance?"

Is a fully generated piece the same instance as one where only the draft was generated? Without that line, two institutions publish two numbers that contradict each other.

What we run into on the data quality side has a similar shape. It is seldom that no usable signal exists. It is that the signal already on hand was set aside for falling short of a verdict.

While we wait for the signal to get more accurate, whatever the current one could have told us may be quietly going past.
