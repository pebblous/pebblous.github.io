## LinkedIn (KO)

빌 게이츠가 8월 26일, AI가 할 수 있는 일이라도 사람 몫으로 남겨 두자는 'Human Reserved'를 제안하면서 그 목록을 누가 어떤 기준으로 정하는지는 자신도 모른다고 적었다. 그는 답 없는 질문 네 가지를 나열했다. 누가 정하는가, 어떤 기준을 쓰는가, 몰래 로봇을 쓰는 기업을 어떻게 막는가, 한 나라만 규제하면 무역은 어떻게 되는가. 규모에 대해서도 스스로 상한을 낮췄다. 아주 극단적으로 가도 초기 일자리의 40%가 자신이 올릴 수 있는 최대치라고 액시오스에 말했다.

같은 발상을 3주 앞서 조문으로 옮긴 문서가 이미 있다. 8월 6일 그레그 카사 하원의원이 발의한 H.R. 10044는 파운데이션 모델에 물품세를 매기고 세율을 U-4 실업률에 연동한다. 다만 판정이 걸리는 곳은 세율이 아니라 과세 계기다. 모델을 밖에 파는 행위는 조건 없이 과세되지만, 기업이 안에서 쓰는 경우는 그 사용이 인력 감축을 "가능하게 하거나 초래할" 때만 과세된다. 무엇으로 그것을 입증하는지는 조문에 없다. 청구서가 남는 쪽만 자동으로 걷히는 구조다.

과세 표준 자체도 흔들린다. 앨런AI연구소 창립 CEO 오런 엣지오니는 토큰 과세를 키보드 타건에 세금을 매기는 일에 비유했다. AI 튜터를 쓰는 교실은 토큰을 쉬지 않고 소모하고, 고객센터를 통째로 정리한 모델은 적게 쓸 수 있다. 세금이 게이츠가 지키고 싶다고 말한 용도에 가장 무겁게 내려앉는 셈이다. 세율을 매단 실업률 쪽도 사정이 비슷하다. 스탠퍼드 관측에서 AI 노출도가 높은 직군의 22~25세 고용은 반사실 대비 19% 아래에 있지만, 7월 미국 공식 실업률은 4.1%에 머물렀다. 대체가 해고가 아니라 애초에 열리지 않는 채용공고로 오고 있기 때문이다.

페블러스가 지난달 다룬 캘리포니아·코네티컷의 AI 해고 신고 규정에서도 같은 빈칸을 봤다. 신고 의무는 확정됐고 판정 기준만 비어 있었다. 어떤 일을 사람 몫으로 남길지 정하려면, 그 일에서 AI가 이미 무엇을 얼마나 가져갔는지 세는 장부가 먼저 있어야 한다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Bill Gates proposed on August 26 that societies set aside certain jobs for people even when AI can do them, and then wrote that he does not know who should draw that line or by what standard. His essay lists four questions he cannot answer: who decides what stays human, what criteria apply, how to stop firms from quietly automating anyway, and what happens to trade when only one country reserves work. On scale he was blunt with Axios. In a very extreme form he could imagine 40% of jobs initially reserved, "but that's as high as I can get."

A bill introduced three weeks earlier taxes the same phenomenon without answering any of it. H.R. 10044, filed on August 6 by Rep. Greg Casar, would levy an excise on foundation models with rates indexed to U-4 unemployment. The hard call is not the rate but the trigger. Selling model access to an unrelated party is taxable on its face, while a company's own internal use is taxable only if that use "enables or results in a reduction in the workforce." The text never says what evidence establishes that, so the side that leaves an invoice is the side that gets collected.

The base itself is unstable. Oren Etzioni, founding CEO of the Allen Institute for AI, likens taxing tokens to taxing keystrokes. A classroom running an AI tutor burns them continuously; a model that retires a customer center may burn few. The tax lands hardest on exactly the uses Gates said he wants to protect. The unemployment signal the rates ride on has the same problem. Stanford's latest reading puts employment for 22-to-25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations 19% below where it would otherwise be, even as July's headline rate held at 4.1%. Displacement is arriving as postings that never open rather than as layoffs a rate can see.

We found the same blank last month, in the California and Connecticut rules that made AI-related layoffs a filing requirement without defining what counts as one. Before you can decide which work to reserve for people, you need a ledger of what has already moved.

## Facebook (EN)

"He could not say he was hungry. The caregivers always knew anyway."

Inside the long essay Bill Gates published on August 26 is the last stretch of his father's life. Alzheimer's, 2020, paid caregivers through the nights.

Whatever they gave him, Gates wrote, was irreplaceably human. No robot could have done it, and none should have.

Out of that sentence comes the proposal he calls "Human Reserved." Work we leave to people even when a machine could do it. He borrowed the name from nature reserves, land we could build on and choose not to.

What held me was not the proposal but the paragraph after it. Gates lists four questions he has no answer to. Who decides what stays human. What criteria apply. How do you stop a company from quietly automating anyway. What happens to trade when only one country draws the line.

They sound like ethics. In practice they collapse into one question.

"Can we actually count what AI has already taken from a given job?"

Read alongside the token tax bill filed in the House three weeks earlier, that blank gets sharper. Selling access to a model is taxable outright. A company running the model on its own staff is taxable only if the use enabled a reduction in the workforce. What proves it, the text does not say. So the side that leaves an invoice is the side that gets collected.

In July we looked at California and Connecticut turning AI-related layoffs into a filing requirement. The duty was settled. The test for what counts as an AI-caused layoff was not. A month later the same blank has moved, into a tax base and a list of protected occupations.

Before anyone can write the list of work to protect, someone has to keep the ledger of what has already crossed over.
