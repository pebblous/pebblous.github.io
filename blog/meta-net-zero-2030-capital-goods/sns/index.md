## LinkedIn (KO)

메타의 탄소중립 약속을 흔든 것은 데이터센터가 쓴 전기가 아니다.

11월 19일 나온 지속가능경영보고서에서 메타의 2025년 순배출은 1,180만 톤 CO2e, 전년보다 45% 늘었다.

늘어난 몫의 대부분은 자본재에서 나왔다. 서버와 칩과 네트워크 장비, 그리고 건물을 올린 철강과 콘크리트가 전체 배출의 73%를 차지했고 이 항목만 1년 사이 58% 늘었다.

두 종류의 배출은 해법이 서로 다른 곳에 있다. 전력 배출은 청정에너지 계약을 더 맺으면 줄고, 메타는 2020년부터 전력 사용량을 재생에너지 구매로 맞춰 왔다. 자본재 배출은 서버가 만들어진 공장과 철강을 녹인 용광로에서 이미 끝난 뒤에 메타의 장부로 들어온다. 줄어드는 속도를 메타가 정하지 못한다.

메타가 넘지 않겠다고 한 2021년 Scope 3 기준선은 577만 톤이었다. 2025년 실적은 1,186만 톤으로 그 2.05배다.

약속한 기한은 아직 남아 있는데 선은 이미 지나간 뒤다. 데이터센터를 맡은 레이철 피터슨 부사장과 넷제로 총괄 블레어 스위딘은 서문에서 2030년 넷제로 시한을 포함해 일정과 순서, 중간 목표를 다시 검토하고 있다고 적었다.

배출이 늘어난 회사가 메타뿐인 것도 아니다. 오라클은 같은 해 157% 늘었고 마이크로소프트 25%, 구글 18%, 아마존 16%가 뒤를 이었다. 다만 시한 자체를 다시 보겠다고 공개 문서에 적은 쪽은 아직 메타뿐이다.

같은 보고서에는 로케이션 기준으로 센 2,170만 톤도 메타가 공시한 2025년 숫자로 함께 실려 있다. 경계를 어디에 긋느냐가 약속의 성패를 가른다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

What put Meta's net-zero deadline back under review is not the electricity its data centres draw.

In the sustainability report it published on 19 November, net emissions for 2025 came to 11.8 million tonnes of CO2e, 45 percent above the year before.

Most of the increase sits in capital goods. Servers, chips and network gear, together with the steel and concrete in the buildings, made up 73 percent of the total, and that one category grew 58 percent in a year.

The split matters because the two kinds of emissions answer to different remedies. Power emissions fall when a company signs more clean energy contracts, and Meta has matched its electricity use with renewable purchases since 2020. Capital goods emissions are finished in the supplier's plant and the steelmaker's furnace before they ever reach Meta's ledger, and how fast they come down is not Meta's call.

The line the company promised not to cross was its 2021 Scope 3 figure of 5.77 million tonnes.

The 2025 figure is 11.86 million, 2.05 times that baseline, and the deadline has not arrived yet. In the foreword, Rachel Peterson, who runs data centres, and Blair Swedeen, who leads net zero, wrote that Meta is re-evaluating its timing, sequencing and interim targets, the 2030 net-zero date included.

Nor is Meta the only company whose emissions climbed. Oracle's rose 157 percent over the same year, with Microsoft at 25 percent, Google at 18 and Amazon at 16. It is alone, so far, in putting the deadline itself back on the table.

The same report carries a second figure for the same year: 21.7 million tonnes, counted on a location basis. Both numbers are Meta's own disclosure. Where the boundary sits decides whether the pledge survives.

## Facebook (EN)

When data centres and carbon turn up in the same sentence, my mind goes to electricity. Racks running, cooling plant, heat.

In the report Meta published on 19 November, the largest item is somewhere else.

Capital goods: 73 percent of the total. Servers and chips, and the steel and concrete that went into the buildings.

Which is to say most of that carbon was out in the air before the site drew its first kilowatt.

Electricity can be renegotiated. Buy enough renewable power and the line clears on a market basis.

A furnace that has already run leaves no line to clear.

What is finished during construction cannot be undone by running the place well.

Meta wrote in its foreword that it is re-evaluating when its commitments fall due, the 2030 net-zero date among them. The 2021 ceiling it promised not to cross had already been passed twice over.

On the day the report landed, Meta also announced deals with makers of green steel and low-carbon cement.

What it buys there is not the material itself but a certificate for the reduction attached to it. The ledger technique that has long covered electricity is now being extended to steel and concrete.

"If we wrote down what one model run cost us, what would go on that line besides the power bill?"

Diagnosing data at Pebblous turns on that question more often than on whether the values are clean: how far out did the counting go before it stopped.

In the same report the 2030 water target is described as on track. Of two pledges aimed at the same year, the one drawn around a smaller area is the one being kept.
