2001년부터 미국이 거래를 막아 둔 중국 대학이 엔비디아 H200을 손에 넣은 사실이 드러났다. 그 사실이 적혀 있던 곳은 세관 자료가 아니라 그 대학이 자기 홈페이지에 올린 낙찰 공고였다.

서북공업대학 경영대학원이 10월 21일 올린 공고에 H200 두 장이 87만 4,700위안으로 적혀 있었다. 파는 쪽은 엔비디아가 아니라 산시보쑤안네트워크테크놀로지라는 중국 회사였다. 로이터는 이 공급사의 연락처를 확인하지 못했다고 밝혔다.

산 기록보다 빌린 기록이 길다. 이 대학이 올해 맺은 연산 임대 계약은 다섯 건이고, 그중 둘은 H200 여덟 장이 올라간 서버를 일곱 달씩 쓰는 계약이다. 다섯 건을 합치면 10월에 사들인 두 장 값의 두 배를 넘는다. 여덟 장을 반년 넘게 돌리는 값이 한 장을 사는 값과 거의 같으니, 세는 단위가 장에서 계약으로 옮겨 간 셈이다.

제도가 비껴간 자리는 규정의 범위가 아니다. 대학이 돈을 준 상대는 중국 회사였고 칩은 이미 중국 안에 있었다. 미국 장부에 새로 적힐 줄이 한 줄도 생기지 않는 거래다. 하원이 1월 원격 접속을 통제 항목으로 넣는 법안을 369 대 22로 통과시켰지만 법안은 상원에 묶여 있고, 그 법이 통과돼도 이번 건에는 미국 당국에 서류를 낼 사람이 사슬 어디에도 없다.

로이터가 올해 중국의 공개 조달·기업 기록 26건을 훑어 같은 거래를 더 찾아냈다. 다만 칩이 어디서 왔는지, 빌린 서버가 어디서 돌아갔는지는 기록에 거의 없었다. 누가 썼는지는 남았고 어디서 왔는지는 남지 않았다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A Chinese university that the United States has blocked from trade for a quarter century turned up holding Nvidia H200 cards. The record that showed it was not customs data. The university had published it itself, as a winning-bid notice on its own site.

The notice, filed by Northwestern Polytechnical University's School of Management on October 21, priced two H200 cards at 874,700 yuan, about $123,000. The seller was a Chinese firm, Shaanxi Bosuan Network Technology, not Nvidia. Reuters said it could not reach the supplier.

The renting runs longer than the buying. The university signed five compute rental contracts this year, two of them for seven months each on a server carrying eight H200 cards. Together the five cost more than twice what October's two cards did. Running eight cards for half a year comes to roughly what owning one card costs, which is the whole reason the unit of the trade has shifted from cards to contracts.

Where the controls missed is not a question of their reach. The university paid a Chinese company and the chips were already inside China, so no American ledger gains a line. The House passed a bill in January, 369 to 22, that would bring remote access inside export controls, but it is stuck in the Senate, and even enacted it would find nobody in this chain who owes a US agency a filing.

Going through 26 of this year's Chinese procurement and corporate filings, Reuters watched the pattern repeat at other institutions. What the filings rarely held was any trace of where the chips came from, or where the rented servers physically sat. Who used them survived in writing. Where they came from did not.

## Facebook (EN)

On October 21, Northwestern Polytechnical University posted a winning-bid notice on its own website.

The item line read 'NVIDIA H200 141G PCIe'.

Two cards, 874,700 yuan, about $123,000.

The school has been on the US export blacklist since 2001.

I kept rereading the seller's line rather than the item line. It was not Nvidia. It was a Chinese company called Shaanxi Bosuan Network Technology. The chips were already inside China, and nowhere in this purchase is there a moment when anything crosses a border.

The renting goes further. Five compute rental contracts this year, and some notices priced in hours rather than in cards. One award covering compute for the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences set the rate at 10 yuan per chip per hour.

Ten yuan an hour. Nothing priced by the hour ever reaches a border where it could be counted.

And none of this surfaced through the machinery built to watch for it. It surfaced through China's procurement disclosure rules, which exist to show where public money went. After the Reuters story ran, the October notice came off the university's website.

Reuters read 26 records from this year and found the same arrangement at other universities. What the records almost never held was where the chips had come from. Who used them stayed in writing. Where they came from did not.

"The chips in the server you rent by the hour: where were they before they got there?"
