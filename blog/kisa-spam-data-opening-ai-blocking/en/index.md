# KISA Spam Data, the Citizen Reports That Train Samsung and LG

_KISA sorts citizen spam reports with AI and passes about 500,000 a month to Samsung, whose blocking service has stopped about 500 million malicious texts_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Spam texts that people in Korea reported to the government have ended up inside commercial blocking services, and the effect has now been published as a count. The figures came out at the [2026 Spam Data Opening Results Briefing](https://www.kisa.or.kr/402/form?postSeq=2658), held in Seoul on November 20 by the Korea Internet & Security Agency (KISA) and the Korea Media and Communications Commission (KMCC). News that public data has been opened is common. News that follows the data into a named service and counts what it stopped there is not.

> The largest number is 500 million. That is how many malicious texts Samsung Electronics' AI-based malicious message blocking service has stopped since it launched in March 2025, running on the spam report data KISA hands over. On the voice side, LG Uplus's AI call service ixi-O blocked about 12 million spam calls in the three months since that partnership began. The two figures cover different stretches of time and different media, though, so reading them as one combined result is a stretch.

> Sections 1 to 4 set out what the KISA and KMCC announcements and the coverage confirm. Section 5, which reads the case as a question about AI-Ready Data, is this article's own.

## The Text Never Shows, the Call Never Rings

There are two moments at which spam can be stopped. One is after it arrives, when you delete it. The other is before it arrives, when it never gets through. What KISA presented this time belongs to the second kind. None of the items in the count ever showed up on a screen, and none of the calls made a phone ring.

The AI-based malicious message blocking service Samsung Electronics runs with KISA launched in March 2025. It picks out texts that look like illegal gambling, loan offers, stock tips or smishing, and it judges them against KISA's spam report data. Since launch, the service has filtered about 500 million malicious texts before delivery.

The work with LG Uplus started in July 2026 and covers calls rather than texts. KISA shares voice spam data to sharpen how accurately the AI call service ixi-O identifies spam calls, and the spam sender numbers ixi-O pulls out of call analysis are wired back into KISA's real-time blocking system. In the three months after that work began, about 12 million spam calls were blocked before they rang.

Several outlets added the two into a single figure of 512 million blocked. The arithmetic holds, but one side covers 20 months and the other three, and one counts texts while the other counts calls. They read better apart.

> [!callout]
> Three items were on the agenda that day: the state of illegal spam in 2026, the progress of KISA's spam data opening, and the cases and results of using that data by sector. The first two are close to annual reporting. The third is the part that came with numbers attached this time.

## One Report Becomes One Label

KISA's job in this program splits in two. First it collects the spam that the public reports, then it classifies and analyzes that pile automatically with AI. Opening comes after that, when the classified data goes out to public bodies and to companies.

The private recipients named in the press release are mobile carriers, handset makers and others like them, and two of those have blocking counts attached to them in this announcement. The one case where the supply volume is spelled out is Samsung Electronics. Samsung receives sender numbers, risky links and message text from KISA and uses them to improve its blocking, and the volume handed over runs to about 500,000 items a month. Over a year that is a six-million-item sample of current spam flowing into one company's classifier.

What deserves more attention than the volume is how this data was made. A spam report is a record of an actual recipient marking, on their own phone, that this one is spam. It was not produced by commissioning a labeling job. It is a label applied by the person who was hit, at the moment they were hit. Collection and labeling finish in a single motion.

Timing matters too. Spam sender numbers and short URLs get swapped out within days. A list gathered last year will not stop what arrives today, which is why a supply that keeps coming month after month is worth more than one large dataset handed over once. Here the value of opening comes from the interval rather than the volume.

## One Way Out, and a Way Back

The same opening program connects the two companies differently. On the Samsung side, KISA gives and the company receives, and that is the whole path. On the ixi-O side there is one more leg, a return. The spam sender numbers ixi-O extracts from calls are fed into KISA's real-time blocking system, so a company's analysis refills a public blocklist.
