## LinkedIn (KO)

국민이 신고한 스팸 문자가 삼성전자의 차단 서비스로 들어갔고, 거기서 걸러진 악성 문자가 약 5억 건으로 집계됐다.

한국인터넷진흥원(KISA)과 방송미디어통신위원회가 11월 20일 서울에서 연 '2026 스팸 데이터 개방 성과 공유회'에서 나온 발표다. 국민 신고 스팸을 KISA가 AI로 분류해 공공기관과 민간기업에 나눠 주는 사업이다. 삼성전자로 넘어가는 분량은 발신번호와 위험 링크, 문자 내용을 합쳐 월평균 약 50만 건이다.

LG유플러스의 AI 통화 서비스 익시오 쪽에서는 협력을 시작한 지 3개월 만에 스팸 전화 약 1,200만 건이 수신 전에 끊겼다. 두 기업의 연결 방식은 다르다. 삼성전자 쪽은 KISA가 주고 기업이 받아 쓰는 한 방향이고, 익시오가 통화에서 뽑아낸 발신번호는 KISA의 실시간 차단 시스템으로 되돌아간다.

공공데이터를 열었다는 발표는 흔하다. 그 데이터가 어느 서비스로 들어가 무엇을 몇 건 막았는지까지 붙는 경우가 드물다. 재료의 성질이 여기서 갈린다. 스팸 신고는 피해를 본 사람이 자기 전화기에서 직접 붙인 판정이다. 라벨링을 따로 발주할 일이 없다. 그리고 발신번호가 며칠이면 갈리는 영역이라, 매달 끊이지 않고 도착한다는 점이 분량보다 크게 작용한다.

읽을 때 떼어 둘 대목이 둘 있다. 5억 건은 20개월 누적이고 1,200만 건은 3개월 치이며 막은 대상도 문자와 전화로 다르니, 더해서 하나로 읽을 값이 아니다. 그리고 같은 기간에 불법스팸 과징금을 최대 20억 원까지 물릴 수 있게 한 제재 강화가 함께 굴러갔는데, 줄어든 몫이 어느 쪽에서 나왔는지를 발표는 가르지 않았다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 셈해 진단한다. 이번 발표에서 오래 붙잡힌 자리는 차단 건수가 아니라 공급 간격이었다. 분기에 한 번 내려오는 덤프로는 주 단위로 바뀌는 현상을 쫓지 못한다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Spam that people in Korea reported from their own phones has been feeding a commercial text filter, and the filter has now stopped about 500 million malicious messages.

The figure came out on November 20 in Seoul, at a briefing on spam data opening held by the Korea Internet & Security Agency and the Korea Media and Communications Commission. KISA collects what the public reports, sorts it with AI, and passes the result to government bodies and to companies. Samsung Electronics receives sender numbers, risky links and message text at a rate of roughly 500,000 items a month, and the blocking service it feeds has filtered the 500 million since March 2025.

The voice side works differently. LG Uplus's AI call service ixi-O blocked about 12 million spam calls in the first three months of its arrangement with KISA. The sender numbers ixi-O pulls out of call analysis are wired back into KISA's real-time blocking system. Samsung receives; ixi-O receives and returns.

Announcements that public data has been opened are routine. Announcements that name the service the data entered and count what it caught there are not. The difference sits in the material. A spam report is a verdict applied by the person who was hit, at the moment it happened, so nobody commissioned the labels. And because sender numbers are swapped out within days, the fact that the supply arrives every month matters more than how much of it there is.

Two things should be read apart. The 500 million covers 20 months and the 12 million covers three, one counts texts and the other calls, so they are not a single total. And Korea raised illegal spam penalties to as much as 2 billion won over the same months; the briefing leaves that split undone.

Pebblous puts a date on AI training data as well as a score, and that is what DataClinic reports. What held attention in this announcement was not the blocking total but the delivery interval. A dump that arrives once a quarter cannot track something that turns over weekly.

## Facebook (EN)

I could not tell you how many spam texts reached me last month. Deleting one takes less than a second.

Where that second goes afterwards had never occurred to me.

On November 20 in Seoul, Korea's internet agency and its media and communications commission held a briefing on what has come of opening the country's spam reports. The public reports spam, KISA sorts the pile with AI, and the sorted data goes out to government bodies and to companies.

A Samsung filter running on that supply has stopped about 500 million malicious texts before they reached a screen, counting from its launch in March 2025.

The supply itself is around 500,000 items a month.

What I kept rereading was not the figure with eight zeros. It was the two words "a month."

Spam sender numbers and short links are swapped out within days. A list assembled last year will not stop what arrives this morning. So the worth of this data is not in how much of it has piled up. It is in how often it turns up.

Freshness, here, is the quality.

The same shape sits in places nobody calls open data. A fault a technician logged on the factory floor. A damage claim recorded in a warehouse. A false positive an operations team corrected last night. In every case someone at work has already written down a verdict, the verdict is recent, and the record never leaves the department that holds it.

"Do the records we are stacking up arrive as often as the problem we want to solve changes?"

What Pebblous asks of a training set through DataClinic is not only how clean it is but how old. Gaps and duplicates sit inside the file and can be counted there. When a record arrived usually has to be fetched from somewhere else.

A dump that lands once a quarter and 500,000 items arriving every month can look alike on a storage bill. Put either one into a model and the difference shows up in the first week it has to catch something new.
