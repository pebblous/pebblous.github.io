## LinkedIn (KO)

앤트로픽이 기업 고객에게 보낸 유출 공지에는 이름과 이메일, 계정 ID와 조직 ID 밑에 두 항목이 더 적혀 있었다. 요금제 등급, 그리고 사용량과 지출 지표다.

국내에서 범위에 든 기업용 계정은 112개다. 뉴스1과 이투데이가 적은 수이고, 다른 보도는 "100여 개"로 적었다. 사람 수가 아니라 계정 수다.

경위는 앤트로픽 바깥이 아니라 안쪽에서 시작됐다. 미국 보안 스타트업 캐시드럴이 앤트로픽이 업무에 쓰던 외부 소프트웨어의 취약점으로 내부 시스템에 들어갔고, 앤트로픽은 이틀 만에 접근을 막았다. 한국 신고는 그로부터 3주 뒤, 개인정보보호위원회의 정식 조사 착수는 다시 열흘 뒤다.

가장 많이 인용된 문장은 프롬프트와 AI 답변, 결제정보에는 접근이 없었다는 대목이다. 틀린 말은 아니다. 다만 그 문장이 덮지 않는 자리에 요금제와 사용량·지출이 있다. 대화 한 줄도 담기지 않았지만 그 대화가 몇 번 오갔고 거기에 돈이 얼마나 들어갔는지는 담긴 기록이고, 계정 ID와 조직 ID, 담당자 이름과 업무 이메일에 붙어 한 묶음으로 나갔다.

공지는 이 항목들을 "유출이 확인된 정보"가 아니라 "접근 가능했던 정보"로 분류했다. 누가 무엇을 열어 봤는지는 앤트로픽도 모른다는 뜻이다. 범위를 적은 쪽과 범위를 다투는 쪽의 설명도 아직 갈려 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The breach notice Anthropic sent its enterprise customers carries two fields that are easy to scroll past. Plan tier. Usage and spend metrics.

Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission opened a formal investigation on November 10 into the exposure of data from 112 Korean corporate Claude Enterprise accounts. That is the figure used by News1 and Etoday; other outlets wrote about 100, and it counts accounts rather than people.

The entry point was not a Claude product. Cathedral, a US security startup, used a flaw in third-party business software that Anthropic ran internally, and Anthropic cut the access off two days later. The filing in Korea came three weeks after that, and the formal investigation ten days after the filing.

The line quoted everywhere is that prompts, model outputs, and payment details were never reached. That line holds. What it does not cover is the rest of the list. Plan tier and usage and spend metrics hold no sentence anyone typed, and they do hold how often the typing happened and what it cost. And they left in one bundle with the account and organization IDs and with the name and work address of whoever runs the account.

The notice also files those fields as information that may have been accessed rather than information confirmed to have left, which is a way of saying Anthropic cannot tell who opened what. Anthropic and Cathedral still describe the scope of the intrusion differently.

## Facebook (EN)

When a breach notice lands, most of us go looking for one sentence first. The one that says the content is safe.

That sentence is in Anthropic's notice. Prompts, model answers, payment details: none of it was reached.

The fields underneath are where this one gets interesting. Names and work emails. Account IDs and organization IDs. Plan tier. Usage and spend metrics.

Not one line of anyone's conversation. A full account of how often those conversations happened.

A plan tier tells you how far into AI an organization has gone. Usage carries time: a month where the line jumps is a month where something inside the company began. Spend is the budget riding on that change. The account ID and the organization ID decide which company and which team the numbers belong to, and a name with a work email attaches the team to a person.

The notice files all of it as information that may have been accessed, not information confirmed to have left. Which is a careful way of saying nobody knows who opened what. The same document tells customers there is no reason to believe they are at heightened risk, and the researcher who published it pointed out that both sentences sat in the same document.

There is a difference between not knowing and being fine.

"When the notice arrives, is anyone on our side assigned to keep reading after the sentence that says the content is safe?"
