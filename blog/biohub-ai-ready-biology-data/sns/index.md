## LinkedIn (KO)

18억 달러짜리 발표에서 바이오허브에 맡겨진 동사는 '만든다'가 아니었다. '표준화한다'였다.

2026년 10월 7일, 바이오허브가 미 에너지부·국립보건원과 구글 딥마인드·메타 등을 모아 AI가 바로 학습할 수 있는 생물 데이터에 18억 달러를 약정했다고 발표했다. 이 가운데 이날 처음 공개된 약정은 8억 달러다. 나머지는 바이오허브가 지난 4월 출범 때 이미 내건 금액과, 국립보건원이 과거 연방 예산으로 만들어 둔 데이터의 접근권을 다시 합산한 몫이다.

병목이 어디로 옮겨 갔는지가 낱말에 드러난다. 반년 전 출범 발표문에는 'AI-ready'도 'standardize'도 나오지 않는다. 10월 발표문은 첫 두 문단 안에서 두 낱말을 다 쓰고, 서로 다른 실험실이 같은 유전자를 다른 이름으로 부르는 일을 없애는 공유 표준과 공통 식별자를 바이오허브의 몫으로 적었다. 4월에 모인 쪽이 데이터를 만드는 기관들이었다면, 10월에 들어온 쪽은 그 데이터를 학습에 쓸 기관과 이미 쌓아 둔 기관이다.

공개 조건은 균일하지 않다. 상업 자금으로 만든 데이터에는 엠바고가 붙어 자금을 댄 쪽이 먼저 쓰고, 정부 자금으로 진행되는 작업에는 그런 제한이 없다. 표준화가 선의로 모여서 되는 일이 아니라 먼저 쓸 권리라는 값을 치러야 돌아가는 일로 설계됐다는 뜻이다.

돈으로 데이터를 만들 수는 있다. 서로 맞물려 학습에 쓰이게 만드는 일도 같은 방식으로 되는지는 첫 데이터셋이 나오는 1년 뒤에 드러난다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

In a $1.8 billion announcement, the verb handed to Biohub was not generate. It was standardize.

On 7 October 2026 Biohub said it had pooled $1.8 billion with the U.S. Department of Energy, the National Institutes of Health, Google DeepMind, Meta and Isomorphic Labs to produce biological data that AI models can train on directly. The total splits four ways, and $800 million of it was disclosed for the first time that day. The rest is the sum Biohub pledged back in April, counted again, plus access to datasets the NIH built with more than $500 million of earlier federal money.

The wording shows where the bottleneck moved. Neither 'AI-ready' nor 'standardize' appears anywhere in the April launch release. The October release uses both inside its first two paragraphs and assigns Biohub the shared standards and common identifiers that stop two laboratories from calling the same gene by different names. April gathered the institutions that make data. October brought in the ones that will train on it and the ones already sitting on decades of it.

The terms of release are not uniform. Data funded by companies carries an embargo that lets the funders work on it first, while the government-funded work running alongside carries no such restriction. Standardization here was not designed to happen because participants showed up in good faith. It was designed to run on a price.

Money can make data. Whether it can make that data mesh well enough to train a model is a question the first dataset will start answering a year from now.

## Facebook (EN)

Hundreds of millions, and trillions.

Those are the two numbers Alex Rives, Biohub's head of science, gave Reuters. The first is the scale of the cell data that exists today. The second is the scale a predictive model will eventually need.

Reading them side by side, what came to mind first was not the size of the gap. It was a quieter question: once those trillions of cells are gathered, will they all be going by the same names?

On 7 October, Biohub announced $1.8 billion alongside the Department of Energy, the National Institutes of Health, Google DeepMind and Meta.

What held my attention in that announcement was not the sum. It was a verb.

The verb written against Biohub's name is not generate. It is standardize. In the release that launched the same initiative six months earlier, neither 'AI-ready' nor 'standardize' appears once.

Language models had it easier. Decades of human writing were already piled up on the internet, and the people building the models could simply scrape the pile. The library came first, and the readers arrived later.

Biology is what I would call a field without a library. There is no pile to scrape, so the books have to be written first, and written in a shape that lets dozens of institutions with different instruments shelve them together. Rives put it this way: "We need to capture the language of biology, we need to capture the language of the cell. And that doesn't exist today."

To say there is no language is also to say there is no grammar. Without an agreement about what gets called what, measurements can accumulate forever and still never meet inside a single model.

The slow part of diagnosing enterprise data at Pebblous has always been in the same place. Deciding how to connect the pipeline usually goes quickly. Establishing that two departments have been calling the same thing by different names, and getting them to settle on one, takes far longer.

"Money can make data. Can it also make that data mesh well enough to train on?"

Biohub has taken the side that says it can. And it seems to know technology alone will not get there, because it wrote a condition in alongside: data paid for by companies opens to the public only after those funders have had their turn with it first. A price was put on standardization.

The first dataset arrives in about a year. That is roughly when the design starts to answer for itself.
