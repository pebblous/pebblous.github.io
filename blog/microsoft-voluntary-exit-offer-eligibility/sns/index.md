## LinkedIn (EN)

Microsoft asked about 60,000 of its employees on October 26 to decide for themselves whether to leave. The people who build and run its data centers did not get the email.

The company calls it a Voluntary Exit Offer. Nobody is being told to go; the person who receives it decides. It went to staff at level 70 and below in the United States, Canada, India and the United Kingdom, about 27% of the workforce, and answers are due in early December.

The rank condition sorts almost nobody. Level 70 sits in the Distinguished Engineer band near the top of Microsoft's technical ladder, and very few positions remain above it. What actually drew the line was the organization an employee belongs to. Copilot and CoreAI, the teams building AI products, are eligible. Data centers, which run the AI, and sales, which is where the revenue comes in, and security, which holds the defensive line, are not.

Microsoft says this is not about needing fewer people but about one of several ways to meet shifting business needs. It has not said why any organization was left out. Capital spending in the fiscal year that just closed ran to roughly $145 billion on the guidance issued at its start.

## Facebook (EN)

There are mornings when a single email tells you which column your job has been placed in.

About 60,000 people at Microsoft got that email on October 26.

Nobody was told to go. The offer leaves the decision with the person who received it, and answers are due in early December.

It was not the 60,000 that stopped me. It was the eligibility the company published alongside it. Country, rank ceiling, and the organizations the offer skipped. Three axes written as conditions rather than as sentences.

Look closely at the rank condition and something odd happens.

Level 70, the ceiling, sits near the very top of Microsoft's technical ladder. Almost no positions remain above it, which means the condition screens out close to nobody.

A criterion that is written down and passes nearly everyone. I have come to call that a 'nominal criterion'.

So what did the sorting?

The organization people work in.

Copilot and CoreAI, the teams building AI products, are eligible. The data centers that run the AI are not. Neither are sales and security.

"If AI is replacing people, why are the people building it the ones being asked?"

Microsoft has not said. Line the excluded organizations up and they share something, which is that pulling anyone out of them costs money immediately, but that is a guess made from outside.
