## LinkedIn (KO)

물리학자 42명이 자기가 실제로 했던 연구를 시험 문제로 바꿔 냈고, 그 시험에서 가장 잘한 AI 에이전트가 168번 가운데 46번을 통과했다.

11월 4일 arXiv에 올라온 FrontierPhysics 이야기다. 제출된 과제 가운데 56건이 채택됐고, 이론과 계산과 실험을 아우르는 아홉 개 분야에 걸쳐 있다. 과제 하나는 문헌 조사에서 시작해 연구 계획과 실험을 거쳐 논문 초고 제출까지를 열 시간 안에 요구한다.

채점이 두 갈래다. 한쪽은 시험 스크립트가 에이전트의 수치를 참고값과 대조하는 결정론적 검증이고, 다른 한쪽은 출제자가 직접 쓴 채점 기준표다. 기준표 항목은 하나만 어겨도 통과가 막히는 필수 항목과, 통과 여부에 관여하지 않고 품질 점수만 매기는 나머지 항목으로 갈린다. 두 관문을 모두 넘어야 한 건의 통과로 기록된다.

성적표의 맨 윗자리는 Claude Opus 5.5의 통과율 27.4%다. 어떤 구성도 자기 몫의 시행에서 그보다 많이 통과하지 못했다. 연구진은 점수에서 멈추지 않고 실패한 기록을 사람 손으로 읽어 분류했는데, 가장 자주 걸린 자리는 추론의 엄밀함과 물리적 직관이었다. 물리 개념을 잘못 알았거나 코드가 돌지 않아 막혔다는 항목은 상위에 없다.

이 분석의 범위는 좁혀 읽어야 한다. 기록을 읽은 대상은 모든 모델이 아니라 GPT-6 Astra 한 모델의 시행 168건이고, 분모는 그 가운데 실패로 기록된 143건이다. 같은 읽기에서 아홉 건은 에이전트가 과제를 제대로 해내고도 실패로 기록된 경우였다.

기준이 너무 가혹한 것 아니냐는 반박도 저자들이 먼저 막아 뒀다. 이미 발표한 자기 연구 자료를 같은 기준표에 올려 채점하자 평균 96.2%가 나왔다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 결함을 정량 진단한다. 이 벤치마크에서 빌려 올 만한 자리는 항목을 가르는 방식이다. 통과를 막는 결함과 점수만 깎는 흠을 한 평균에 섞어 두면, 치명적인 한 건이 사소한 여럿 뒤로 사라진다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Forty-two physicists turned research they had actually carried out into exam tasks, and the best AI agent to sit the exam passed 46 of its 168 runs.

That is FrontierPhysics, posted to arXiv on November 4. Of 182 submitted tasks, 56 were accepted, spanning nine domains across theoretical, computational, and experimental physics. A single task asks for the literature review, the research plan, the experiments, and a submitted manuscript, all inside a ten-hour run.

Grading runs on two tracks. A test script compares the numbers an agent produced against reference values, and alongside it sits a rubric whose per-task half is written by the physicist who set the task. Rubric items divide into blockers, where breaking one shuts off a pass, and remaining items, which never decide a pass and only produce a quality score. A run counts as a pass only when it clears both gates.

At the top of the table is Claude Opus 5.5 with a 27.4 percent pass rate. No configuration passed more of its own runs than that. The team did not stop at the score: they read the failure logs by hand and sorted them, and what caught the agents most often was rigor of argument and physical intuition. Misunderstood physics and code that would not run are nowhere near the top.

The scope of that reading is narrow. It covers one model, GPT-6 Astra, over its 168 runs, and the denominator is the 143 of those recorded as failures. The same pass also found nine runs where the agent had done the work and was marked down anyway.

The authors closed off the obvious objection that the bar is simply too high. They put their own published research through the same rubric and it averaged 96.2 percent.

## Facebook (EN)

Some researchers went to working physicists and asked them to turn one project they had actually carried out over the past few years into an exam task.

Of the 182 submissions that came back, 56 were accepted. Behind one task sits a project of roughly 270 days on average, more than 8,000 hours of research once they are added up. For some of the accepted tasks the underlying work has already appeared in Nature.

The exam built that way was then handed to AI agents. Each task starts at finding the literature and has to end with a submitted manuscript, inside ten hours. The best of them, Claude Opus 5.5, passed 46 of its 168 runs, and no configuration went past that line.

The part of this paper I read twice was not the scoreboard. The authors sat their own exam first. They put their own published research through the same rubric, and it averaged 96.2 percent, which told them in advance that the bar was not set beyond what a person can clear. Even so, three of those 36 gradings missed a blocker item on work the authors had published themselves.

'An exam its own writers sat before anyone else did'.

Pebblous judges the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Rubrics get written often, and this is the step that slides to later every time. What the most carefully built dataset in the building would earn under that rubric is a ceiling almost nobody measures, and almost nobody writes down.

"What would our rubric give the best data we have?"

When a model scores low, whether that is a story about the model or a story about the rubric stays undecided until somebody measures the ceiling.
