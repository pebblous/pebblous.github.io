## LinkedIn (KO)

9월 말부터 열흘 가까이 오픈AI의 매출로 돌아다닌 700억 달러는 오픈AI가 낸 수가 아니었다.

10월 8일 파이낸셜타임스는 투자자에게 돌린 자료를 근거로 이 회사의 1년치 환산 매출이 500억 달러에 가깝다고 전했다. 앞서 쓰이던 값과 200억 달러가 벌어진다. 같은 날 나스닥은 8월 중순 이후 가장 나쁜 하루를 보냈고, 코어위브는 8% 빠졌다. 오픈AI의 매출을 미래 수요의 대리 지표로 삼아 설비와 칩에 값을 매겨 온 종목들이다.

수요가 꺾여서 생긴 간격이 아니다. 오픈AI는 같은 기간 3분기 런레이트가 77% 늘었다고 밝혔다. 700억 달러는 8월에 알려진 약 400억 달러 기준선에 그 성장률을 얹어 투자자들이 직접 만든 값이다. 파이낸셜타임스는 그 출처를 앤트로픽의 연환산 매출과 직접 비교할 수 있게 만들려는 오픈AI 투자자들 자신의 시도라고 적었다. 곱셈은 맞았다. 틀린 것은 곱한 두 수의 단위였다. 앤트로픽은 고객이 AWS나 구글 클라우드를 거쳐 낸 금액을 전부 자기 매출로 적고, 오픈AI는 그중 자기에게 돌아오는 몫만 적는다.

정정이 나온 뒤에도 두 회사를 맞대는 일은 성립하지 않는다. 오픈AI의 500억 달러는 자기 몫만 센 값이고, 앤트로픽이 7월 말에 적은 650억 달러는 파트너를 거친 판매를 전액으로 센 값이다. 애초에 이 지표는 회계 기준이 정의한 것이 아니다. 그래서 상장사들은 보고서에 정의와 한계를 함께 적는다. 업랜드 소프트웨어는 2025 회계연도 10-K에 자사 ARR의 계산식을 밝혔다. 그러고는 이렇게 덧붙였다. "표준화된 뜻이 없고, 다른 회사가 제시하는 같은 이름의 지표와 비교할 수 없을 수 있다." 표준이 없다는 사실 자체가 공시의 대상이 되어 있는 셈이다.

이번 건에서 빠진 칸도 중복된 행도 없었다. 500억 달러도 650억 달러도 각자의 정의 위에서는 정확한 값이다. 틀린 자리는 값이 아니라 그 한 층 위, 값이 무엇을 센 것인지 적어 두는 자리였다. 페블러스가 ISO/IEC 5259를 기준으로 데이터 품질 인증 체계를 운영하면서 점수보다 용어 정의를 앞에 두는 것도 이 층을 겨눈다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The $70 billion that stood in for OpenAI's revenue for most of ten days did not come from OpenAI.

On October 8 the Financial Times put the company's annualized revenue near $50 billion, citing material circulated to investors. That is $20 billion below the figure in use until then. The Nasdaq had its worst session since mid-August that day, and CoreWeave gave up 8%. These are names priced on equipment and chips with OpenAI's revenue standing in for future demand.

Demand is not what opened the gap. OpenAI had disclosed third-quarter run rate growth of 77% over the same period. The $70 billion was built by investors themselves, who took the roughly $40 billion baseline known in August and extended it by that growth rate, and the Financial Times traced it to attempts by OpenAI's own investors to produce a direct comparison with Anthropic's annualised revenues. The multiplication was sound. The units were not. Anthropic records the full amount a customer pays through AWS or Google Cloud, while OpenAI records only the share that reaches it.

The comparison still does not hold after the correction. OpenAI's $50 billion counts its own share; the $65 billion Anthropic was reported to have at the end of July counts partner sales in full. Annualized revenue is not a metric the accounting standards define, which is why listed companies write the definition and its limits into their filings. Upland Software gave the formula for its own ARR in its fiscal 2025 Form 10-K and then added that the measure "does not have any standardized meaning and may not be comparable to similarly titled measures presented by other companies." The absence of a standard has itself become something to disclose.

Nothing here was missing and nothing was duplicated. The $50 billion and the $65 billion are each exact on their own definitions. What failed sits one layer above the values, where what a value counts is written down. Pebblous runs its data quality certification on the ISO/IEC 5259 series, which spends its opening part on terminology rather than on scoring, and that is the layer it is aimed at.

## Facebook (EN)

When two numbers sit in one table, I mostly read them without checking whether a single ruler produced both.

Last week the market did the same.

Add 70% to $40 billion and you reach $68 billion. For most of ten days at the end of September, that was the figure the market held as OpenAI's annualized revenue.

The arithmetic was right.

On October 8 the Financial Times put the number near $50 billion, and $20 billion appeared to vanish overnight. Nvidia, Oracle and CoreWeave all fell that day.

What was wrong was not the calculation but the units of the two things multiplied.

Anthropic records the whole dollar a customer pays through AWS or Google Cloud. OpenAI records only the share that comes back to it. The $40 billion was counted the second way, and investors applied the first way as they extended it by the growth rate, because they wanted the two companies in one row.

It is measuring a length in centimetres and then adding the increase in inches.

The $50 billion, and the $65 billion Anthropic was reported to hold at the end of July, are each exact on their own definitions. Nothing was missing and no row was duplicated.

And still, the moment one was restated onto the other's method so the two could be compared, a $20 billion misreading appeared.

What failed was not the values but the layer above them. The layer where what a value counts is written down.

That layer is invisible. A total that fails to add up announces itself; a definition mismatch travels all the way to the conclusion with the numbers looking fine.

Inside a company's own dashboard the same thing happens more quietly. Whether an active user is anyone who logged in once or someone who used a core feature, whether the denominator of a conversion rate is visitors or sign-ups. Teams settle these differently, put them on one screen, and whoever is looking takes them for values measured the same way. The screen is built that way.

"Outside the where clause in your query, is that metric's definition written down in a document anywhere?"

Pebblous runs its data quality certification on the ISO/IEC 5259 series. What the standard puts first is not a score but the vocabulary. It fixes what gets called what, and starts from there.

Sharing a name does not mean counting the same thing.

And a shared name is usually why two numbers end up side by side at all.
