## LinkedIn (KO)

구글이 궤도에 올린 AI 칩이 지상에서 올려보낸 질문에 답을 내놨다. 돌아온 것은 시 세 줄이었다.

10월 1일 스페이스X 트랜스포터-18 편으로 저궤도에 오른 선캐처 시제 위성 이야기다. 몇 주에 걸친 점검을 마친 뒤 지상국 통과 때 프롬프트가 올라갔고, 위성에 실어 둔 Gemma 4가 우주를 소재로 한 하이쿠를 지었으며, 노르웨이 스발바르 상공을 지날 때 그 세 줄이 내려왔다. 구글은 10월 30일 이를 데이터센터급 TPU가 궤도에서 낸 첫 AI 응답이라고 적었다.

표현은 조심해서 고른 쪽이다. 소형 위성에서 가벼운 모델을 돌린 사례는 이미 있고, 새로운 쪽은 지상 데이터센터에 꽂히는 것과 같은 계열의 칩이 궤도에서 추론을 냈다는 지점이다. 증명된 것도 성능이 아니라 사슬이다. 올려보낸 글자가 TPU 트레이까지 가서 답이 되고 그 답이 다음 통과 때 내려오는 고리 가운데 하나만 어긋나도 아무것도 돌아오지 않는다.

공개되지 않은 쪽이 더 많다. 추론에 걸린 시간도, 칩이 쓴 전력도, 그때 올라간 온도도 나오지 않았고 지상에서 같은 모델을 돌렸을 때와 견줄 기준값도 없다. 구글이 적은 다음 할 일의 첫 줄 자체가 열과 방사선 아래에서 TPU가 어떻게 동작하는지를 평가하는 일이다. 바깥의 지적은 또 다른 곳을 짚는다. 카네기멜런대 브랜던 루시아 교수는 기술자가 걸어 들어가 칩을 고칠 수 없는 자리에서는 비용과 복잡성이 10배에서 100배까지 커진다고 말했다.

다음 관문은 2027년 초다. 지구관측 위성 회사 플래닛 랩스와 함께 위성 두 기를 올려, 실험실에서만 되던 빛의 링크가 초속 몇 킬로미터로 나는 두 물체 사이에서도 되는지를 본다.

위성이라는 자리를 걷어 내면 남는 것은 오래된 질문이다. 데이터를 처리하는 곳까지 옮길 것인가, 생긴 자리에서 처리하고 결과만 옮길 것인가. 궤도에서 거른 원본은 지상에 아예 도착하지 않고, 어떤 모델이 어떤 임계값으로 걸렀는지가 결과에 붙어 내려오지 않으면 받은 쪽은 그 데이터의 출처를 설명할 수 없다. 페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 숫자로 진단한다. 계산이 센서 쪽으로 걸어가면 그 진단을 붙일 자리도 함께 옮겨 간다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

An AI chip Google put in orbit answered a question sent up from the ground. What came back was three lines of verse.

The craft is the Project Suncatcher prototype that rode SpaceX's Transporter-18 into low Earth orbit on October 1. After weeks of checkout, a prompt went up during a ground station pass, Gemma 4 aboard the satellite wrote a haiku about being in space, and the three lines came down on a pass over Svalbard in Norway. Google published the result on October 30, calling it the first AI response from a data center class TPU in orbit.

The wording was chosen with care. Small satellites have run lightweight models before; what is new is that a chip of the kind that fills a ground data center produced an inference in orbit. And what the demonstration proves is not performance but an unbroken chain. Characters sent up reach the host computer, the TPU tray powers on, the model composes an answer, and the answer comes down on the next pass. One link out of place and nothing returns.

More was withheld than released. How long the inference took, how much power the chip drew, how hot it ran: none of it was published, and there is no ground baseline to set beside it. The first line of Google's own list of next steps is evaluating how the TPUs behave under heat and radiation. Outside voices point elsewhere. Brandon Lucia of Carnegie Mellon noted that where no technician can walk in to fix a chip, cost and complexity get amplified by a factor of 10, maybe 100.

The next gate comes in early 2027. Two satellites go up with Planet Labs, the Earth observation company, to see whether an optical link that works on a bench also works between two objects moving at kilometers per second.

Strip away the orbit and an old question is left standing. Do you move the data to where it gets processed, or process it where it was made and move only the result? A frame filtered out in orbit never reaches the ground, and unless the model, version and threshold that dropped it travel down attached to the result, the receiving side cannot account for what it holds. ## Facebook (EN)

In a Google office, people watching one screen broke into applause.

What was on the screen were three lines in English.

"Blue marble below / Data streams in silent grace / Starlight feeds my core."

They were not written by a model on the ground. They were written by a chip inside a refrigerator-sized satellite that went into low Earth orbit on October 1.

A question went up during a ground station pass. The satellite composed the answer inside itself. The answer came down over Svalbard in Norway. On October 30 Google published it as the first response from a data center class chip in orbit.

I have no wish to read too much into one poem.

What I came away with was not the verse but the "unbroken chain."

Characters sent up reached a chip in orbit, and what that chip produced made it all the way home. Along that path the ground did nothing but hand over a question and take back an answer.

Observation satellites have always worked the other way around. They store what they capture, pour it down during a pass over a ground station, and the usable frames get picked out later, on the ground. Passes are short and bandwidth is fixed, so there is always imagery that was captured and then overwritten before it could come down.

Filter in orbit and send only what changed, and the same bandwidth carries far more usable observation. But a frame dropped up there never reaches the ground at all.

"Of the data your team works from, how much has already been filtered once, somewhere no human hand could reach?"
