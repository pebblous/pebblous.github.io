## LinkedIn (EN)

Law firm and bank customers have already handed 30 to 50 percent of their communications work to an AI twin.

The figure comes from Twin1 AI, which left stealth on August 20 alongside a $20 million seed co-led by Bessemer, Tribeca and Aramco Ventures. Linklaters and Orrick are among the firms that have run the product for over a year. The twin is not a separate app; it sits inside Slack and Outlook like a colleague.

Handing over that much work means a person's judgment and way of speaking are already inside the system. Which is why the differentiator the company leads with is not model performance but permission design, the rules that decide who may see how much of that context.

Open that design up and the unit of control becomes visible. What a twin knows inherits the access rights the employer issues and revokes, and the moment all the data is erased is written as the end of the contract between the customer and Twin1, not the day an employee leaves. What a person can claim about that context afterward appears nowhere in the published documents. Andrew Bratt, an employment partner at Gowling WLG, put it plainly: the law has not yet answered these questions.

Once a person's work traces become a learning asset, the first question is no longer which value is wrong. It is what you count the owner of this data by. Count by account, by person or by contract, and the same data ends up following different rules.

동료들의 이름이 줄지어 있는 슬랙 사이드바에, 이름이 하나 더 있습니다.

'누구의 트윈'.

트윈1 AI라는 회사가 며칠 전 스텔스에서 나오며 공개한 제품 화면입니다. 지식노동자 한 사람마다 AI 트윈을 하나씩 붙여 주고, 그 트윈은 따로 있는 앱이 아니라 이미 일하고 있는 도구 안에 동료처럼 들어앉습니다.

저를 오래 붙잡은 것은 투자 금액이 아니라, 이 트윈이 무엇을 먹고 자라는가였습니다.

이메일과 회의와 문서. 그러니까 결재 문서로는 남지 않은 판단의 경위들. 상대가 누구냐에 따라 말투를 바꾸는 그 사람의 습관까지 배운다고 회사 FAQ는 적어 두었습니다.

로펌과 은행 고객은 커뮤니케이션 업무의 30~50%를 이미 트윈에 넘겼다고 보고했습니다. 그만큼을 넘기려면, 그 사람의 판단과 관계가 이미 시스템 안에 들어가 있어야 합니다.

## Facebook (EN)

In a Slack sidebar of familiar names, there is one more name.

Someone's Twin.

That is a product screen from Twin1 AI, which came out of stealth a few days ago. Every knowledge worker gets a twin of their own, and the twin is not a separate app. It settles in among the tools people already use, like a colleague.

What held me was not the size of the round. It was what the twin feeds on.

Email, meetings, documents. The reasoning behind a judgment, the part that never made it into a filed document. The company FAQ says the twin also learns how a person shifts tone depending on who is on the other end.

Law firm and bank customers report that 30 to 50 percent of their communications work already runs through a twin. To hand over that much, a person's judgment and relationships have to be inside the system first.

So the differentiator this company leads with is not performance. It is permission design.

The governance page opens by declaring that your data is yours. A few lines down, the subject quietly changes. What the twin knows is bounded by access rights the employer issues and revokes, and all the data is erased when the contract between the customer and the vendor ends. There was no clause for the day a person leaves the organization.

Over the same body of knowledge, three routes are open at once. Buy the company outright, spend $500 million building your own, or rent it one person at a time. The starting points differ. The place where the data settles overlaps.

"What do you count the owner of this data by? By account, by person, or by contract?"

In data quality work, the question Pebblous meets most often is which value is wrong. Once a person's work traces become a learning asset, one more question arrives. Change the unit you count by, and the same data ends up following different rules.

The law has not answered this yet. Until it does, the only place that rule can be written may be a contract rather than a product.
