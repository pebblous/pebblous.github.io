## LinkedIn (KO)

의견이 많이 붙은 조항일수록 더 많이 고쳐졌을 것이라는 기대는, 의견 7만 건을 의무 단위로 다시 세어 보니 거의 남지 않았다.

미국 환경보호청 규칙제정 36건을 대상으로, 규칙 전체가 아니라 규칙이 담은 개별 의무 하나하나에 접수 의견을 붙여 제안 문안과 최종 문안을 대조한 연구가 arXiv에 공개됐다. 같은 도켓 안에서 의견이 많이 붙은 의무가 고쳐질 확률이 높긴 했지만 그 차이는 작았고, 지지와 반대 어느 쪽으로 쏠렸는지는 최종 문안의 향방을 가르지 못했다. 조직이 다수를 이룬 참여는 의무의 실질을 바꾸는 결과보다 표현을 정돈하는 결과 쪽에 몰려 있었다.

저자들이 형평성 문제의 자리를 옮기는 지점이 여기다. 기관이 어떤 의견에 답하느냐가 아니라, 애초에 어떤 의무가 걸려 있는지 찾아내고 해석하고 다툴 수 있느냐에서 갈린다는 것이다.

한계도 분명하다. 세 결과는 인과가 아니라 함께 나타난 정도의 서술이고, 제출자가 개인인지 조직인지는 원자료에 깔끔하게 남아 있지 않아 저자들이 느슨하게 재구성한 값이다. 사람이 눈을 가린 채 다시 라벨을 붙인 뒤에도 결론은 유지됐지만, 같은 검증에서 텍스트 유사도만으로는 자구 수정과 실질적 규제 변경을 구분할 수 없다는 사실도 함께 나왔다.

한국 AI 기본법 시행령은 두 차례 의견수렴을 거쳐 지난 7월 21일 시행됐다. 접수 일정은 공고에 남아 있지만, 어떤 의견이 어느 조항을 어떻게 바꿨는지 조항 단위로 이어 볼 자료는 공개된 것이 확인되지 않는다. 페블러스가 데이터 계보에서 반복해 온 구분이 규제 문안에서도 그대로 성립한다. 데이터셋 단위 출처 목록은 기록이고, 컬럼과 값 단위로 내려가야 감사다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Counting public comments turns out to be a poor way to tell whether public comment worked.

Two researchers matched 70,075 comments filed on 36 EPA rulemakings to the individual duties written into those rules, then compared each duty in the proposed text against the final text. Within a docket, duties that drew more comments were somewhat more likely to be revised, but the gap was small. Whether comments ran in support or in opposition did not separate the outcomes at all. Participation dominated by organizations clustered in wording-level revisions rather than substantive change to the duty itself.

The paper moves the equity question upstream. It is not primarily about which comments an agency answers. It is about who can locate the specific legal obligation at stake, read it, and argue with it in the first place.

The caveats travel with the findings. These are associations, not causal estimates, and the raw record does not cleanly mark whether a filer is an individual or an organization, so the authors reconstructed that split loosely. A blind human audit held the third result up. That same audit also showed that text-similarity methods cannot tell a copy edit apart from a substantive regulatory change.

Korea's AI Framework Act decree went into force on 21 July after two comment rounds. The filing dates are on the record. What is not on the public record is which provision each comment addressed and what happened to it. This is the distinction Pebblous keeps drawing about data lineage: a dataset-level source list is a record, and only column-level and value-level detail makes an audit.

## Facebook (EN)

If you have ever filed a comment on a proposed rule, you probably wondered what happened next.

A receipt arrives. After that, silence.

Months later the final text is published, and you end up setting it beside the draft and working out on your own what moved.

I kept returning to that scene while reading an EPA study posted on arXiv. Out of 786,197 comments filed with the agency between 2010 and 2022, two researchers took 70,075 of them and matched each one not to a rule but to the individual duty written inside that rule, then compared the proposed text against the final text.

The findings sit at an angle to what most of us assume. Duties that drew more comments were revised somewhat more often, but only somewhat. Whether the comments ran in support or in opposition told you nothing about where the duty ended up. Participation dominated by organizations landed on wording fixes more than on the substance of the duty.

The sentence that relocates the equity question comes at the very end of the paper. The divide is not mainly in which comments an agency answers. It is in who can find the obligation at stake, read it, and argue with it.

Korea's AI Framework Act decree took effect on 21 July after two rounds of public comment. The filing dates are on the record. Bring this study's question to it and there is nothing to answer with.

"Which provision did this comment actually reach?"

What survives is the record of receipt. The record of uptake does not. It has the same shape as something Pebblous keeps saying about data lineage: a dataset-level source list is an inventory, and the audit only begins at the column and the value.

The unit you choose to record in decides what anyone can ask later.
