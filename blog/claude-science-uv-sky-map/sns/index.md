앤트로픽이 10월 8일 공개한 전천 자외선 지도는 하늘의 3분의 1을 관측이 아니라 추정으로 채웠고, 어느 칸이 추정인지를 픽셀마다 적어 두었습니다.

과학 에이전트 클로드 사이언스가 미 항공우주국의 갈렉스와 스위프트, 한국 과학기술위성 1호에 실렸던 FIMS/SPEAR, 유럽의 TD-1이 남긴 관측을 하나의 좌표계로 합친 결과입니다. 자외선은 오존층에 막혀 우주에서만 관측되고, 가장 넓게 훑은 갈렉스도 하늘의 3분의 2까지였습니다. 남은 3분의 1에는 자외선 기록이 아예 없습니다.

빈 자리는 인페인팅으로 채웠습니다. 관측된 3분의 2에서 자외선 밝기가 가시광·적외선·전파의 밝기와 어떻게 맞물리는지를 배운 뒤 그 관계를 빈 곳에 적용했고, 이미 데이터가 있는 자리를 일부러 가려 맞혀 본 시험에서 추정값은 실제 측정값과 약 10% 안쪽으로 들어왔습니다.

가린 자리는 한 번은 관측됐던 곳, 다시 말해 갈렉스가 찍을 수 있었던 비교적 단순한 하늘입니다. 정작 비어 있는 쪽에는 먼지와 가스와 어린 별이 뒤엉킨 은하면이 상당 부분 들어 있습니다. 기술 매체 런타임와이어는 이 시험이 골라낸 기존 관측 영역에서의 성능을 보여 줄 뿐이라고 짚었습니다.

이 지도가 흔하지 않은 이유는 추정을 해서가 아니라 추정한 자리를 데이터에 남겼기 때문입니다. 픽셀마다 측정인지 예측인지가 붙고, 예측으로 분류된 픽셀에는 불확실도가 따라붙습니다. 라벨이 없는 표에 남는 선택지는 전부 믿거나 전부 버리는 두 갈래뿐입니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Anthropic published the first all-sky ultraviolet map on October 8. A third of it is not observation. It is estimate, and the map says so pixel by pixel.

Claude Science, Anthropic's science agent, pulled ultraviolet data from NASA's GALEX and Swift, the FIMS/SPEAR spectrograph aboard Korea's STSAT-1, and Europe's TD-1 into a single coordinate frame. The ozone layer blocks ultraviolet light, so it can only be collected from space, and GALEX, the widest sweep ever made in the band, reached about two-thirds of the sky. The remainder carries no ultraviolet record at all.

The blanks were filled by inpainting. The model learned from the observed two-thirds how ultraviolet brightness tracks brightness in visible, infrared and radio light, then applied that relation where no ultraviolet data existed. Asked to reconstruct patches hidden on purpose, it landed within about 10% of the real measurements.

Those patches had been observed at least once, which means sky GALEX was able to image: no very bright stars, relatively simple structure. The genuinely empty third holds much of the galactic plane, where dust and gas and young stars are wound together. Runtimewire noted that the test shows performance in selected known regions and does not establish accuracy where nothing has been observed.

What makes this map unusual is not that it estimated but that it recorded where it estimated. Every pixel is marked measured or predicted, and predicted pixels carry an uncertainty. Strip those labels and a downstream researcher has two options left: trust all of it, or throw all of it out.

## Facebook (EN)

"This cell was measured. That one was predicted."

I cannot remember the last time a published map said that, cell by cell.

The all-sky ultraviolet map Anthropic released on the 8th does.

Ultraviolet light never reaches the ground; the ozone layer takes it, so the telescope has to be in orbit. GALEX, the widest sweep ever made in the band, got about two-thirds of the sky. Nobody has ever seen the remaining third in ultraviolet. Claude Science estimated that part from the relation it learned on the observed side.

And it marked every place it filled.

Call it cell-level provenance.

What held my attention was not how accurate the estimates were. It was the marking.

"In the table I just received, which cells are observations and which ones did a model supply?"

A column whose gaps were filled with the mean, or a label a model handed over, is the same situation. A relation learned on the observed part, applied to the part nobody observed, and once applied it sits in the table as a number no different from the rest. That distinction falls off while files are merged, gets wiped while column names are tidied, and folds into a single average on the way into a report.

With the map nearly done, Brice Ménard, an astrophysicist at Johns Hopkins, noticed faint circles scattered through the dark regions. Not objects in the sky, but the footprint each exposure left behind. The flaw had already cleared two rounds of agent review. In numerical terms it was too small to trip any statistic, and the data would have satisfied every check on format and range. Recognizing it took knowing both what the GALEX field of view looks like and what shape atmospheric glow leaves.

This is the wall Pebblous tends to hit when it measures data quality. Whether a value is correct is comparatively easy to test. When the table no longer says whether that value was observed or filled in, the ground for any quality claim runs out right there.

Estimating is something science has always done. But the fact of having estimated does not survive unless it is written down at the moment the blank is filled. It is not a record anyone can reconstruct later.

Whether the table I handed off last week still carried that column, I honestly could not say.
