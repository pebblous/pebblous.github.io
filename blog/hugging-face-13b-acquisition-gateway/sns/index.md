## LinkedIn (KO)

130억 달러가 매겨진 것은 허깅페이스가 가진 자산이 아닙니다.

비즈니스 인사이더가 주말 사이 허깅페이스에 130억 달러 이상 밸류에이션의 매각 제안이 들어왔다고 보도했고, 테크크런치가 이를 정리했습니다. 인수 상대는 확인되지 않았고 합의된 딜도 없습니다. 3년 전 마지막 라운드가 매긴 값은 45억 달러였습니다. 클렘 들랑그 대표는 최근 팟캐스트에서 회사가 흑자에 가깝다고 말했을 뿐입니다. 매출로 설명되는 숫자가 아닙니다.

값이 붙은 곳은 손익계산서가 아니라 위치입니다. 트랜스포머스에서 모델을 부르는 가장 흔한 방법은 저장소 이름 하나를 from_pretrained에 넘기는 것이고, 그 한 줄이 기본적으로 허브를 향합니다. 같은 달 스트라이프가 라우팅 계층인 오픈라우터를 70억 달러 이상에 사기로 한 것도 같은 종류의 위치입니다.

그런데 그 위치 위에 쌓인 200만 개 넘는 공개 모델은 회사 것이 아닙니다. 이용약관의 Your Content 조항은 소유권을 게시자에게 남겨 두고, 공개 저장소에는 이미 모든 이용자에게 영구적이고 철회 불가능한 라이선스가 걸려 있습니다. 다만 그 라이선스는 문언상 "우리의 서비스와 기능을 통해" 부여됩니다. 인수로 바뀔 수 있는 것은 소유권이 아니라 신규 게시 정책, 검색 노출 순서, 추론 엔드포인트 가격 같은 경로의 조건입니다.

모델 조달을 허브 한 곳에 맡겨 온 조직이라면, 협상 결과를 기다리기 전에 런타임에 허브를 부르는 지점부터 목록으로 만들 수 있습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A $13 billion price has been put on Hugging Face. Almost nothing that sits on it belongs to Hugging Face.

Business Insider reported over the weekend that the company was approached at a valuation of $13 billion or more, and TechCrunch wrote it up this week. No buyer has been named and no deal agreed. The last round, three years ago, valued the company at $4.5 billion. CEO Clem Delangue has said only that Hugging Face is close to profitability. Revenue does not explain the gap.

What carries the price is a position. In the Transformers library the ordinary way to load a model is to hand a repository name to from_pretrained, and that one line points at the hub by default. Stripe agreed in the same month to buy OpenRouter, a routing layer, for more than $7 billion. Neither deal is aimed at a model. Both are aimed at the road models travel on.

The more than two million public models on that hub were never the company's to sell. The terms of service say "You own the Content you create!", and public repositories already carry a perpetual, irrevocable licence granted to every user. The catch sits in the wording: that licence runs "through our Services and functionalities." An acquisition does not move title. It moves posting policy, ranking in search, inference pricing and access terms for particular providers.

If your team sources models through a single hub, the list of places where production calls that hub at runtime can be written today, whatever the talks produce.

## Facebook (EN)

from_pretrained, and a repository name in the brackets.

Almost every team has written that line. You name the repo, the weights come down. Where the line actually points is not something most of us stop to ask. It is the default.

Over the weekend a price appeared on that default. Thirteen billion dollars or more. No buyer named, no deal agreed.

My first thought was about what the money would actually be buying. There are more than two million public models on the hub, and none of them belong to the company. The terms of service put it in a single line: "You own the Content you create!"

So the thirteen billion is not on the warehouse. It is on the door into the warehouse.

Call it a gateway asset. A place that makes nothing itself, yet everything made passes through it. Stripe agreed to buy OpenRouter in the same month, and that is the same kind of place. OpenRouter had spent a year describing itself as the single access point that keeps you out of lock-in. The position that saves you from lock-in turned out to be a position too.

Reading the terms again, one clause catches. The perpetual licence on public repositories is granted "through our Services and functionalities."

The right stays. Nothing in the document promises that the road to the right stays.

This is the distinction Pebblous keeps meeting in data sourcing work. What an organisation manages is often not the data but the route the data arrives by. Everyone knows which models they use. Far fewer have written down where those models come from and on what terms.

"What is our pipeline passing through right now?"

That question does not need the outcome of any negotiation. It is a map you can draw today, and it holds whichever way the reporting turns out.
