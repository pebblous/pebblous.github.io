## LinkedIn (KO)

벌금 조항을 가진 주법은 보름을 주고, 아무것도 강제하지 못하는 편지는 이틀을 준다.

미 하원 세 상임위의 민주당 간사가 10월 27일 앤트로픽·구글·메타·오픈AI·SpaceXSI 앞으로 서한을 보냈다. 에너지·상업, 과학·우주·기술, 법사 위원회의 소수당 간사 셋이라 소환장이 아니고, 따르지 않아도 제재가 없다.

요구는 둘이다. 지금까지 확인한 AI 오정렬 사고와 안전 사고를 11월 11일까지 전부 낼 것. 그리고 뒤에 발견되는 사고는 48시간 안에, 핵심 기반시설이 걸리면 24시간 안에 알릴 것.

읽을 만한 대목은 시한이 아니라 거기 붙은 서식이다. 사고 하나마다 a부터 n까지 열네 가지를 적으라고 했고, 그 안에 사고가 일어난 날짜와 사고를 발견한 날짜가 따로 들어 있다. 두 칸이 갈라져 있으면 그 사이의 길이가 드러난다. 다만 48시간 시계는 앞 칸이 아니라 뒤 칸에서 출발한다.

비교 대상을 옆에 놓으면 순서가 뒤집혀 있다. 캘리포니아 SB 53은 신고서에 네 가지를 요구하고 기한은 15일이며, 어기면 건당 최대 100만 달러가 붙는다.

EU AI법 73조도 인지 뒤 15일이 기본이다. 제재를 쥔 쪽이 느슨하고, 쥐지 못한 쪽이 촘촘하다.

다섯 장이 같은 서식으로 올라와도 포개지지는 않는다. 서한 2번 항목이 각 회사에 사고 식별 절차를 따로 묻는데, 그 절차가 1번 표에 몇 줄이 올라올지를 정한다. 표가 짧은 회사는 사고가 적었을 수도 있고 적게 찾아냈을 수도 있는데, 두 경우가 같은 모양으로 제출된다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A state law with a million-dollar penalty allows fifteen days. A letter that can compel nothing allows two.

On October 27 the ranking Democrats on the House Energy and Commerce, Science, and Judiciary committees wrote to Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceXSI. They want every AI misalignment and safety incident identified so far filed by November 11, anything discovered afterwards reported within 48 hours, and within 24 hours where critical infrastructure looks to be involved. All three write as minority ranking members, so this is a request rather than a subpoena, and nothing happens to a company that ignores it.

The clock is not the interesting part. The form is. Each incident has to come back with fourteen items lettered a through n, among them the date it happened and the date it was found, held in separate fields. Split that way, the interval between the two shows on the page. The 48-hour clock, though, starts from the later field.

Set the comparable rules beside it and the ordering inverts. California's SB 53 asks for four items, allows fifteen days, and carries civil penalties reaching a million dollars per violation. Article 73 of the EU AI Act also runs on fifteen days. The instruments that can punish are the loose ones; the instrument that cannot punish is the exacting one.

Five submissions on one form still will not stack. Request 2 asks each company separately what process it uses to identify these incidents, which is an admission that the process decides how many lines appear in the Request 1 table. A short table may mean few incidents or few incidents found, and both arrive looking identical.

Pebblous measures the state of AI training data with DataClinic. Pulling records from several organizations into one sheet, the column to read first is not any value but the gap between those two dates. That gap sizes up how quickly an organization notices its own events, not how serious the events were.

Once November 11 passes, the thing to check is not whether five companies answered but whether fields b and c came back holding different dates.

## Facebook (EN)

What got me started was a reporting form with its date field split in two.

One date for when the thing happened. One for when somebody noticed.

The form is attached to a letter the ranking Democrats on three House committees sent to Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceXSI on October 27. Fourteen items per incident, and two of them are those two dates.

Nothing happens to a company that sets the letter aside. Meanwhile the California statute that does carry a penalty asks for four items on its report and gives fifteen days. This letter says two.

Splitting the date field is what stayed in mind. A form with only one date has nowhere to record that the detection came late. With two, the length of that stretch shows itself.

There is a layer below that. Whatever happened in a stretch where monitoring was not running does not come back as an empty field. The line never gets created.

'Places where no row is ever written'.

One sentence in the letter sketches exactly that. It describes a company running monitoring across most of its internal use, and not running it on the evaluation work where the incident occurred.

Pebblous grades the condition of AI training data with DataClinic. Gathering records from several teams into one place, the last thing to surface is when each team began logging this sort of event at all.

"In our own records, which field is late detection written into?"

The team with the most entries may not be the sloppiest team, only the most diligent one. Move on the totals and you move exactly backwards.
