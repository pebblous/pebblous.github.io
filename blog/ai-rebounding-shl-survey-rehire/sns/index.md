## LinkedIn (KO)

AI에 자리를 내준 미국 직장인 다섯 중 한 명이 같은 회사에서 돌아와 달라는 연락을 받았다.

인재평가 기업 SHL의 조사 결과다. 설문은 프로펠러 인사이츠가 맡아 미국과 영국의 현직 직장인 2,036명에게 돌렸다. 미국 응답자의 19.1%가 그런 요청을 받은 적이 있다고 답했고, 18~24세에서는 24%까지 올라간다. SHL은 이 현상에 'AI 리바운딩'이라는 이름을 붙였다.

SHL에서 제품·디자인을 총괄하는 루시 보몬트는 원인을 기술이 아니라 순서에서 찾았다. 회사가 어떤 직무 안에 들어 있는 사람의 역량을 제대로 평가하기 전에 그 직무부터 자동화했다는 것이다. 같은 설문에서 회사가 AI 도구를 들이고 있다는 응답은 절반에 가까웠는데, 그 도구에 맞춰 직무를 다시 짜고 있다는 응답은 24%에 그쳤다. 가트너의 캐시 로스는 인원을 줄이는 근거가 "입증된 결과가 아니라 기대되는 AI 가치"였다고 말했다.

돌아가는 값도 올랐다. 조건으로 큰 폭의 임금 인상을 꼽은 응답이 51%, 고용 보장을 서면으로 받겠다는 응답이 42%, AI를 내 직무의 어디에 쓰는지 미리 알려 달라는 응답이 40%였다. 회사에 가장 까다로운 것은 셋째다. 설명하려면 먼저 적어 두어야 하는데, 직무를 다시 설계하고 있다는 곳은 넷 중 하나다.

다만 이 숫자들은 직장인이 스스로 적어 낸 경험이다. 고용 기록을 맞춰 본 집계가 아니라서 실제로 몇 명이 대체됐고 몇 명이 돌아갔는지는 알 수 없고, 복귀 조건의 분모도 매체마다 달리 적혀 있다.

자동화할 업무의 내용이 숫자로 남아 있지 않으면, 그 결정이 옳았는지는 사람이 빠져나간 뒤에 비용으로만 드러난다. 페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터가 어디서 모자란지를 진단한다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Nearly one in five American workers who lost a job to AI have been asked to come back.

The talent assessment firm SHL commissioned Propeller Insights to survey 2,036 workers in the U.S. and the U.K. over June and July. Among U.S. respondents, 19.1% said the employer that replaced them later offered to take them back.

The youngest workers report it most. Among respondents aged 18 to 24, the share was 24%. SHL named the pattern AI rebounding.

Lucy Beaumont, SHL's global SVP for product and design, locates the cause in the order of the decision rather than in the technology. Rebounding, by her definition, is "what happens when a company automates a role before it has genuinely assessed the human capability inside it." The same survey found close to half of workers saying their employer is bringing in AI tools, against 24% saying the work itself is being redrawn to fit them. Kathy Ross, a VP analyst at Gartner, describes headcount decisions made on "expected AI value rather than demonstrated results."

The returning workers are charging for the trip. A substantial pay increase was the condition for 51%, job security in writing for 42%, and being told in advance where AI will sit in the job for 40%. The third is the hardest to supply. Explaining where AI is used requires having written it down first, and only a quarter say that redesign work is underway.

The limits are worth stating. Every figure here is something a worker typed about their own year, not a line matched to a payroll record, so the survey cannot say how many were replaced or how many went back. Fortune and CNBC also put the conditions on different denominators.

Two surveys of employers point the same way. Orgvue in March and Robert Half in July both found 32% of those that had cut staff for AI hiring again, most often for organizational knowledge the tools had not carried away.

## Facebook (EN)

Someone is told that software will be doing their job from now on.

Months later the same company calls to ask whether they would consider coming back.

SHL asked 2,036 working adults in the U.S. and the U.K. about it. Roughly one American in five said some version of this had come their way. The firm settled on a name for it: AI rebounding.

The part I keep returning to is not that the tools disappointed. It is the order in which things were done.

Lucy Beaumont of SHL puts the automation ahead of the assessment: the role was switched over before anyone had taken a proper reading of the person doing it. In the same report, 42% of workers said their manager fully understands what they can do beyond their job title.

The capability was in the building. It was not visible from the room where the decision was made.

'The rehire did the measuring.'

Robert Half asked employers why they brought people back, and the answer given most often was the organizational knowledge and context the tools had not carried away. Its presence was confirmed by its absence.

Beaumont also repeated the question people turn over while they decide.

"How do I know that this isn't going to happen again?"

The work being handed to AI sits in the same position. If the volume and the exceptions were not kept anywhere, what that seat was holding up becomes legible on an invoice, after the person has gone.
