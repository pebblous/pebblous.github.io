10월 26일 공시와 같은 날 나온 아폴로 보도자료에 따르면 아폴로 글로벌 매니지먼트가 운용하는 법인이 엔비디아 베라 루빈 칩과 서버 장비를 사들이고, 스페이스X는 그것을 빌려 쓴다. 분기마다 내는 임대료에서 원금 몫은 그 물량 조달원가의 5%다. 스무 번을 내면 조달원가 전액이 된다.

값을 다 치르는 쪽과 장비를 가진 쪽이 갈린다. 그런데 리스가 끝난 뒤 장비가 어디로 가는지, 남은 가치가 누구 몫인지는 공시에도 아폴로 보도자료에도 적혀 있지 않다. 지금까지 공개된 조건은 금액이든 기간이든 금리 산식이든 모두 나가는 돈을 적은 값이다.

같은 날 앤트로픽은 스페이스X와 맺은 컴퓨팅 계약을 상장 신청서에 적었다. 최대 1,205억 달러짜리인데, 초기 구속 기간이 끝나면 양쪽 다 90일 통지로 끊을 수 있다.

5년 넘게 묶이는 의무 위에 석 달이면 풀리는 매출이 올라가 있는 셈이다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

SpaceX has agreed to pay the full $40 billion price of its AI chips, and Apollo will own them.

Under the October 26 filing and the Apollo press release that followed it the same day, an entity managed by Apollo Global Management acquires Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips and server hardware, and SpaceX leases that equipment. The principal portion of each quarterly rent payment is 5% of what the batch cost to acquire. Twenty payments cover the acquisition cost in full.

The party paying the whole price and the party holding the equipment are not the same. Where the equipment goes once the lease ends, and who keeps whatever value remains in it, appears in neither the filing nor Apollo's press release. Every condition disclosed so far, the amount and the term and the formula for the rate alike, records money going out.

The same day, Anthropic set out its compute agreement with SpaceX in its registration statement. The ceiling is $120.5 billion, and once the initial committed period expires either side can end it on 90 days' notice.

An obligation that runs more than five years is carrying revenue that unwinds in ninety days.

Pebblous works on the quality of AI training data. The checkpoints and failed-run logs that pile up while a model trains are usually treated as belonging with the hardware, and that assumption breaks the moment the hardware belongs to someone else.

## Facebook (EN)

When I read about AI investment, I always looked for the list first.

So many GPUs, so many data centers. Counting what a company had bought told me roughly how far it meant to go.

This contract has no such list.

It is the filing SpaceX made on October 26. The party acquiring Nvidia's next-generation chips and server hardware is an entity managed by Apollo Global Management, and SpaceX leases that equipment for a little over five years. The principal portion of each quarterly rent payment is 5% of what the batch cost, and twenty payments cover the cost in full.

The whole price gets paid, and the equipment belongs to another company the entire time.

Laying out the disclosed terms line by line left one side empty. The amount is there, the term is there, the method for setting the rate is there. The line saying where the equipment goes when the term runs out is not. So I began calling that missing line "the closing position."

"Once the price has been paid in full, who owns the equipment in five years?"

Neither the filing, nor Apollo's press release, nor any of the reporting that carried them has an answer.

The blank matters more on the data side, for a reason. What stays on disk while a model trains on that hardware is not only weights. Intermediate checkpoints, logs from failed runs, records of which batch produced which result all accumulate alongside. If the server is ours, we have simply assumed the records inside it are ours too. When the owner sits outside, that assumption breaks once.

Pebblous puts a number on the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Before any score is given, the first thing to establish is where that data sits and who owns it.

"Whose books do those accumulated records go on?"

I have yet to see a contract with a column set aside for that question.
