에이전트가 대신 써 준 신청서가 이미 행정 창구를 메우고 있습니다. 11개 관할에서 84건, 앞으로를 경고하는 글이 아니라 접수된 서류를 세어 본 기록입니다.

GovAI와 헤르티 스쿨 연구진은 열두 나라의 정부 서비스를 훑어, AI가 이용 비용을 낮춘 경로와 수요 변화의 증거와 정부의 명시적 귀속이 모두 확인된 사례만 남겼습니다. 연구진은 이 상태를 에이전틱 플러딩이라 부릅니다. 사법·법률 서비스가 목록의 앞자리를 차지했고, 독일의 한 사회법원에는 4,000쪽이 넘는 서한이 접수됐습니다.

먼저 뚫린 창구에는 공통점이 있었습니다. 신청이 성공하면 돈이 되고, 그동안 절차의 복잡성이 수요를 눌러 온 곳입니다. 아무도 복잡함을 방어벽으로 설계하지 않았지만 결과는 그랬습니다.

오래 남는 대목은 대응 쪽입니다. 실제로 요청을 줄인 조치 열네 건은 모두 마찰을 더하는 방식이었습니다. 수수료 재도입과 IP 차단은 결제 시스템이 이미 깔려 있으면 오늘 시행할 수 있지만, 걸러지는 쪽은 돈이 없고 디지털 문해력이 낮은 이용자입니다. 반면 처리 역량을 늘리는 길은 예산과 입법을 거쳐야 해서, 급증이 시작된 뒤에는 선택지로 남지 않습니다.

한국은 민사 전자소송의 지급명령 신청으로 표본에 올라 있습니다. 유입 비용이 갑자기 0에 가까워지면, 그 창구를 지켜 온 것이 정책이었는지 그냥 번거로움이었는지가 드러납니다. 페블러스가 데이터 파이프라인에서 반복해 보는 장면도 같습니다. 유입량이 늘어난 뒤에 세운 필터는 대개 나쁜 데이터가 아니라 형식이 낯선 데이터를 걸러 냅니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Applications written by AI agents are already crowding government desks. Researchers have counted 84 such cases across 11 jurisdictions, and not one of them is a forecast.

GovAI and the Hertie School screened government services in twelve countries and kept only the cases where a plausible cost-lowering pathway, a documented shift in demand, and an explicit attribution to AI all held at once. The paper names the condition agentic flooding. Courts and legal services topped the list, and one German social court took in a letter running past 4,000 pages.

The desks that gave way first had something in common. A successful application pays out, and procedural complexity had been holding demand down. Nobody designed that complexity as a defense, but it worked as one for years.

The response side is what lingers. Every measure that actually reduced incoming requests worked by adding friction, and there were fourteen of them. A fee can be reinstated today wherever a payment system already exists, and it screens out users without money and without digital literacy first. Expanding capacity runs through budgets and legislation, so it stops being an option once the surge has begun.

When the cost of entering a channel falls to nearly zero, you find out whether the channel was protected by policy or only by inconvenience. The same pattern shows up in data pipelines at Pebblous, where a filter added after the volume arrives tends to catch unfamiliar formats rather than bad data.

## Facebook (EN)

Anyone who has given up on an application because of a single form knows how welcome a tool that fills the form in for you can be.

I was on that side of it.

Reading the 84 cases that researchers at GovAI and the Hertie School pulled out of government services in twelve countries, I saw for the first time where that welcome comes back around.

Applications written by agents are piling up in small claims courts, freedom of information offices, and welfare desks. One German social court received a letter running past 4,000 pages. The researchers gave the condition a name: "agentic flooding."

What took longer to sit with was the response.

The fourteen measures that actually reduced incoming requests all worked by adding friction. A fee reinstated. An IP range blocked. Where a payment system is already in place, a fee can go up today.

And a fee screens out, first, the people without money and without digital literacy. The same people whose wall the form-filling tool was supposed to lower.

"Was this desk protected by policy, or only by inconvenience?"

That is the question I was left with. Nobody designed complexity as a defense, and yet it served as one for a long time. What goes in its place, now that the inconvenience is gone, has not been decided.

We keep meeting a version of this in data pipelines at Pebblous. Put the filter in after the volume arrives, and what it catches is usually not bad data but data in an unfamiliar shape.

Thresholds tend to arrive late, and a late threshold usually stops the wrong thing.
