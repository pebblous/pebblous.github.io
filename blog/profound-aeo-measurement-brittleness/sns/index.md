## LinkedIn (EN)

A company that measures which brands an AI names when it composes an answer, and sells that measurement, raised $180M on September 15.

Profound, based in New York, was valued at $1.8B in a round co-led by Sequoia Capital and Kleiner Perkins. Its February Series C put the figure at $1B, and enterprise customers have since passed 1,000.

An audit posted to arXiv in May tested the metric the industry sells. Put the same commercial question in slightly different words and more than half of the recommended brands change: a Jaccard overlap of 0.288, below what a rerun of the untouched sentence produces.

The response from the company named in that paper is the part worth reading. In July its own research blog listed phrasing sensitivity as one of three forces that move the numbers, and stated that phrasing drives what the model retrieves. That is the chain the audit points at, written by the firm the audit criticised.

AI share of voice is not an observed fact. It is a designed number: how many prompts, how many runs, which models, and what counts as a mention. When that design is not written down, only the number survives. That is the question we start from in data quality work at Pebblous.

The $1.8B was placed on a category whose ruler has not settled yet.

## Facebook (EN)

There is an arrow in the weekly report. It says our brand came up more often in AI answers than it did last week.

I sat with that arrow for a while, wondering how much it is actually allowed to say.

There is a company whose whole product is that measurement: which brands an AI names when it composes an answer.

Profound, based in New York, raised $180M on September 15. The price put on it is $1.8B, 1.8 times what the round seven months earlier settled on.

An audit posted to arXiv in May reports that asking the same commercial question in different words turns over more than half of the recommended brands. Running the untouched sentence again moves the list less than that does.

The company named in the paper has not denied the chain. In July its own research blog wrote that a changed answer after a changed phrasing is not a malfunction, but the model answering a genuinely different question.

The same post says that phrasing drives what the model retrieves.

Which makes AI share of voice less an observed fact than a designed number. How many prompts were chosen, how many times they were run, which models received them, what was allowed to count as a mention. Someone decided all of it before the arrow appeared.

"How many questions did that number in our report come from?"

What we ask first in data quality work at Pebblous is close to this. Not what the value is, but where it came from and what it passed through on the way.

Prices arriving before the ruler settles is not a new pattern. What is new is that the reader of the ruler is shifting from a person to an agent, and the error will pass by more quietly there.
