## LinkedIn (KO)

그리스가 계정을 끊는 결정을 알고리즘 손에서 거둬들이는 법안을 내놨다. 노동사회보장부가 11월 5일 공개 의견수렴에 올렸고, 접수는 11월 19일 밤 11시에 끝난다.

한 법안에 성격이 다른 두 묶음이 들어 있다. 앞쪽은 일터에서 AI를 쓰는 규칙이다. 계정을 막거나 끊는 일, 보수를 주지 않기로 하는 일, 계약상 지위를 가르는 일은 사람이 내려야 한다. 효력이 생기는 날까지 사유를 서면으로 주고, 일하는 쪽은 설명을 요구하고 재심사를 청할 수 있다. 보도가 전한 금지 목록은 다섯이다. 노조 가입 성향 예측, 감정 데이터 수집, 업무 시간 밖 감시가 거기 들어간다.

뒤쪽은 지위이고, 방향이 뒤집히는 쪽이 여기다. 플랫폼이 일을 지휘하고 통제하면 고용으로 보고, 아니라는 쪽을 플랫폼이 증명한다. 현행 노동법 4808/2021은 거꾸로 자영업을 추정으로 깔아 두었다. 유럽 안에서도 드문 설계였고, 2024년 EU 지침 2024/2831이 모든 회원국에 반대 방향을 요구했다.

일정을 쥐고 있는 것은 유럽연합 쪽 시계다. 지침 전환 시한인 12월 2일은 27개 회원국에 똑같이 걸려 있고, 의견 접수가 끝나고 2주 뒤다.

감독에 거는 조건은 선언보다 길다. 지침 제7조는 감독을 맡은 사람에게 자동 결정을 뒤집을 권한과 그만한 훈련을 요구하고, 그 일을 했다는 이유로 해고나 징계를 당하지 않게 막는다. 최소 2년에 한 번은 근로자 대표와 함께 시스템의 영향을 다시 평가한다. 그리스 법안은 전문 교육을 받은 인력과 도입 전 근로자 대표 협의, 데이터보호영향평가를 여기에 덧댄다고 전해진다.

한계도 같이 적어 둔다. 법안 원문이 올라간 opengov.gr 페이지가 외부에서 열리지 않아, 조 번호까지 확인되는 것은 EU 지침과 현행 그리스법뿐이다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A Greek bill takes account suspensions and terminations out of the decisions an algorithm may make alone. The labour ministry opened it for comment on November 5, and the window shuts at 11 p.m. on November 19.

Two different things travel in one text. The first is a rulebook for AI at work. Cutting off or closing an account, withholding pay, settling where someone stands under contract: a person has to take those calls, written grounds are owed by the day the decision takes effect, and the worker can ask for an explanation and a review. Reporting puts the list of outright prohibitions at five, among them predicting who might join a union, harvesting emotion data, and watching what people do off the clock.

The second bundle is status, and the reversal sits here. Where a platform directs and controls the work, employment is presumed, and it falls to the platform to show otherwise. Greece's current statute, Law 4808/2021, starts from the opposite end and treats a platform worker as self-employed. It was an unusual design even in Europe; Directive 2024/2831 asked every member state in 2024 to start from the other side.

The calendar belongs to Brussels. December 2, the transposition deadline, falls on all 27 member states alike, two weeks after comments close.

The oversight clauses run longer than a slogan. Article 7 of the directive requires the person doing the monitoring to hold the authority to reverse an automated decision, with the training that implies, and bars dismissal or discipline for exercising it. At least every two years the system's effect on working conditions is reassessed with worker representatives present. Reporting indicates the bill adds specifically trained staff, consultation with worker representatives such as a union before a system is brought in, and a data protection impact assessment.

One limit is worth stating. The page carrying the text cannot be reached from outside Greece, so article numbers can be cited only for the directive and the statute in force.

## Facebook (EN)

Somewhere inside a platform there has to be a desk whose job is to look again at what the system decided.

Suppose the person at that desk writes "this one is wrong" and reverses the decision.

For having done so, that person cannot be dismissed or disciplined. The European directive that a Greek bill would carry into national law says as much.

The bill went out for public comment on November 5 from the Ministry of Labour and Social Security, covering AI at work and platform work together. The window shuts at 11 p.m. on November 19.

Restricting, suspending or terminating an account, or refusing to pay, is not something the algorithm settles by itself. A person decides, and a person writes down why.

Which leaves a quieter condition. The person looking again needs something to look at.

Between the directive and what reporting describes of the bill, four records are called for, and each is written at a different moment. The register of platforms before operations begin. The data protection impact assessment before a system is brought in. The written grounds by the day the decision takes effect. The impact review at least every two years.

The third one held me the longest. The deadline for writing the grounds is not the day somebody challenges the decision. It is the day the decision takes effect.

It is not a document assembled after the complaint arrives.

Does that reason get written on the day the decision goes out?
