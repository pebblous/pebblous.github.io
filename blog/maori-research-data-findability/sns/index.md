## LinkedIn (EN)

New Zealand's universities cannot say how much Māori research data sits inside their own systems. With no internal mechanism to track it, neither the institution nor the community the data came from can account for it. A paper posted to arXiv this month calls that state an information vacuum.

The Māori research data sovereignty the authors set out rests on three things holding each other up: findability, governance, and community engagement. Where findability is missing, governance defaults to generic institutional norms.

FAIR never asked for data to be open. It asked for conditions of access to be clearly defined, and that distinction collapses in practice. In most catalogs the ownership field is a steward's contact and the access field is a public-or-private flag, so where a dataset came from and on whose terms it can be used ends up in a contract outside the catalog.

It is the same ground on which Pebblous has argued that data lineage belongs at column and value level rather than dataset level. Authority you never recorded in the catalog is authority you cannot ask about later.

## Facebook (EN)

"Where did this dataset actually come from?"

When that question lands in a meeting, most of us go looking for a person's name first.

Open the catalog and what you find is an owning team, a contact, and a flag that sorts the record into public or private. Those fields tell you who keeps the data. They do not tell you who it came from.

Five researchers in New Zealand published a paper on arXiv this month that gives the empty column a name: an information vacuum. Their universities have no mechanism for tracking how much Māori research data sits inside their own systems. Neither the institution nor the community the data came from can account for it.

One line from the paper stayed with me. "Communities cannot exercise authority over data they cannot locate or recognise."

If authority is written into a policy but has nothing in the system to attach itself to, where does the declaration actually live?

FAIR never told anyone to open their data. It asked for the conditions of access to be written down clearly. Where that distinction gives way, what is left is data that is easy to find and impossible to place: nobody can say how far it may be used, or on whose word.

This is close to why Pebblous has kept arguing that lineage belongs at column and value level rather than dataset level. To be able to ask later, someone has to write it down now.

Does your catalog have a field for the relationship?
