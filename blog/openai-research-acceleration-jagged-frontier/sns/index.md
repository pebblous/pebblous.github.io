## LinkedIn (KO)

오픈AI가 11월 18일 두 번째 연구 가속 보고서를 냈다.

사람이 2~4시간 걸릴 연구 작업을 코딩 에이전트가 중간에 사람 손을 타지 않고 끝낸 비율이 3월 23%에서 10월 46%로 올랐다.

그런데 그 그래프를 설명하는 자리에 보고서가 한 문장을 덧붙였다. 에이전트가 유능해지면서 연구자들이 각 시간대 안에서 맡기는 작업의 종류를 바꿨을 수 있다는 것이다.

성공률은 성공한 작업 수를 맡긴 작업 수로 나눈 값이다. 앞이 커져도 오르고, 뒤에 들어가는 일이 쉬워져도 오른다. 보고서가 인정한 쪽은 뒤다. 같은 "2~4시간 작업" 이름표를 달고 있어도 3월에 그 칸에 들어간 작업과 10월에 들어간 작업이 같은 종류라는 보장이 없다.

작업 종류별로 끊으면 격차가 보인다. 학습 실행을 지켜보는 일은 79%, 추론·서빙 코드를 쓰는 일은 25%다. 종류에 따라 세 배 넘게 갈리는 지표라면 구성이 조금 기우는 것만으로 평균이 움직인다.

숫자 자체에 붙는 제한도 있다. 전부 오픈AI가 사내 기록으로 추정한 값이고 외부 감사를 거치지 않았으며, 통제된 실험이 아니라 연구자들이 매일 편한 대로 일을 나눠 준 기록이다.

그래도 남는 것은 발표자가 자기 숫자로 할 수 없는 말을 먼저 적어 뒀다는 점이다. 분기 보고 자료에 올라가는 'AI 도입 효과'는 대개 그 칸이 비어 있다. 도구가 쓸 만해질수록 사람들이 넘기는 일의 종류가 바뀌는데, 이번 달의 '자동 처리 건'은 지난달과 같은 모집단이 아니다. 이름표만 같다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

OpenAI released its second research acceleration report on November 18.

The share of research tasks that would take a person two to four hours, and that a coding agent finished without anyone stepping in, rose from 23 percent in March to 46 percent in October.

Then, in the text explaining that chart, the report added a line of its own. As agents became more capable, it said, researchers may also have changed the kinds of tasks they delegate within each duration bucket.

A success rate is completed tasks over delegated tasks. It climbs when the top grows and it climbs when the bottom gets easier. The report conceded the second. Two buckets carrying the same "two-to-four-hour task" label seven months apart are not guaranteed to hold the same kind of work.

Cut by task type and the spread shows. Monitoring a training run clears 79 percent. Writing inference and serving code clears 25. When a metric splits more than three to one across kinds of work, shifting the mix alone moves the average.

One limit comes attached to the figures themselves. They are OpenAI's own estimates from internal logs, unaudited outside the company, and drawn from researchers dividing up work as they saw fit rather than from a controlled trial.

What survives is that the company wrote down what its number cannot say. The "AI impact" figures in quarterly decks usually leave that line blank. As a tool becomes usable the kinds of work people pass to it change, and this month's automatically handled cases are not the population last month's were. Only the label holds.

## Facebook (EN)

Think of the line on a quarterly deck that reports the share of cases handled automatically.

The number is higher than last quarter, and nobody who wrote it made anything up.

What almost never gets checked is whether the two quarters counted the same work.

The report OpenAI published on November 18 opened that exact seam itself.

It recorded that the share of two-to-four-hour research tasks an agent carried to the end on its own had gone from 23 percent in March to 46 percent in October, and then added, beside the chart, that the kinds of work going into that bucket may have changed along the way.

A bucket that keeps its label and changes its contents.

The denominator of a success rate gets chosen again every month, by people. The more they learn about what an agent is good at, the more the list of what they hand over shifts, and when the list shifts the contents behind the label have already moved. Monitoring a training run clears 79 percent while writing serving code clears 25, so a small tilt in the mix carries the average with it.

"Did that number go up because we got better, or because we handed over more of the easy work?"

Pebblous examines the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. On a month when the score rises, the first thing we pull is the data that left the list.

A list quietly getting shorter while the number climbs is not something the number will ever show you.
