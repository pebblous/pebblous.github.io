## LinkedIn (KO)

업무협약 한 장에 이름이 두 개 붙었다. 현대차그룹은 '차세대 지능형 충전 인프라'를 제목 앞에 놓았고, 한국도로공사는 '피지컬 AI 기반 로봇 기술'을 앞에 놓았다.

9월 30일 양재사옥에서 서명하고 이튿날 양측이 각자 공개한 문서다. 과제는 셋이다. 휴게소 충전 인프라 연구가 하나. 제로원이 투자한 스타트업의 피지컬 AI를 도로 안전·유지관리에 적용하는 연구가 둘. 아직 내용이 비어 있는 미래 도로 공동 연구가 셋이다.

둘째 과제가 여는 공간은 한국도로공사가 관리하는 고속도로 4,483km다. 교량 하부나 터널처럼 사람이 들어가기 어려운 구간에 로봇이 먼저 들어가 점검 데이터를 모으고, 판단은 사람이 한다는 구상이다.

이 자리가 왜 값나가는지는 데이터 쪽 사정을 보면 안다. 로봇 학습 데이터를 모으겠다고 21개 기관이 손을 모은 Open X-Embodiment도 과제 16만여 건 규모에 그쳤다. 밤낮과 날씨, 공사 구간, 요일별 교통량처럼 실험실에서 만들어 낼 수 없는 조건은 기계를 실제로 그 자리에서 돌려야만 기록이 된다.

도로공사 쪽 사정도 숫자로 나와 있다. 협약이 공개된 날 국회에 제출된 자료를 보면 관리 구조물 8,469개소 가운데 17.0%가 준공 30년을 넘겼다.

2027년부터 2035년까지 쌓일 유지보수 수요는 확보 전망 예산보다 2조 8,808억 원 많다는 것이 같은 자료의 추산이다. 점검 대상은 늘고, 사람을 보내기 어려운 자리가 그중에서도 먼저 늙는다.

다만 보도자료와 후속 기사 어디에도 그 기록을 누가 보유하고 누가 쓸 수 있는지 적은 대목은 없다. 업무협약 단계에서 드문 일은 아니다. 공공 자산 위에서 모으는 데이터다. 1차 수혜자가 특정 투자 포트폴리오 안으로 좁아지는 구조라면, 그 조건은 언젠가 공개된 자리에서 다뤄져야 한다.

오간 것은 기술이 아니라 현장이다. 피지컬 AI 판에서 더 구하기 어려운 쪽은 후자다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

One memorandum, two titles. Hyundai Motor Group led with next-generation intelligent charging infrastructure. Korea Expressway Corporation led with robots.

The two signed on September 30 at Hyundai's Yangjae headquarters and each published its own version the next day. Three tasks sit inside the document: charging infrastructure at rest areas, physical AI from ZER01NE-backed startups applied to road safety and maintenance, and a third line on future road technology that is still empty.

The second task is the one that opens ground. Robots are to enter the undersides of bridges and the interiors of tunnels, places hazardous for a person, gather inspection records there, and leave the judgment to people who read them. The site is the 4,483 kilometers of expressway the corporation runs.

Why that site is scarce shows up on the data side. Open X-Embodiment, the largest pooled effort to assemble robot training data, brought 21 institutions together and reached roughly 160,000 tasks. Night and day, weather, work zones that appear and vanish, traffic that differs by weekday: none of it can be manufactured in a lab. A machine has to run in that exact place before any of it becomes a record.

The road operator has its own numbers. Data submitted to the National Assembly on the day the agreement went public counts 8,469 structures under management, 17.0% of them past thirty years of service.

The same material puts maintenance demand between 2027 and 2035 at 2.88 trillion won above the budget expected to be secured. The inspection load grows, and the spots hardest to send a person into age first.

Neither the press release nor the follow-up coverage says who holds the resulting records or who may use them. That is unremarkable at the memorandum stage. It still deserves an answer eventually, since the records are gathered on a public asset while the first users would sit inside one investment portfolio.

What changed hands here is not technology but a site. In physical AI, the site is the harder of the two to come by.

## Facebook (EN)

A watering robot. A security robot. A delivery robot carrying boxes.

The three of them went through their usual work at the Yangjae headquarters last week, once the signatures were done.

Watering, patrolling, hauling. All three already run inside buildings today.

Nothing on display was new.

What the demonstration was really asking, I think, was for everyone present to picture those same machines out on a highway.

That is where the second task in the agreement between Hyundai Motor Group and Korea Expressway Corporation sits. Robots from ZER01NE-backed startups are to go under bridges and into tunnels, the places that are hard for a person to enter, and gather inspection records there.

The stage is 4,483 kilometers of expressway.

Indoors and outdoors are further apart than they sound.

A factory floor keeps the lighting and the walking paths people set for it. A highway does not. Night turns into day, rain turns into snow, a work zone appears and then is gone.

Almost every threshold physical AI has failed to cross lives inside that variation. And none of it can be produced in a lab. A machine has to run in that exact place before anything becomes a record.

I have started calling this the outdoor record. It is hard to buy, and a day that passes unlogged does not come back.

That is what actually changed hands. Hyundai Motor Group brought charging technology and an investment portfolio. Korea Expressway Corporation brought the ground.

"Whose hands do those records accumulate in, and who gets to use them?"

The press release does not say. Neither does the coverage that followed. Nothing unusual about that at the memorandum stage. But records gathered on a public asset will have to meet that question somewhere public eventually.

Turn the question inward and it gets closer to home.

Pebblous works on data quality. The shape we meet most often is not wrong data but absent data: a site where the same work happens in the same place every day, nobody ever decided to store it, and so nothing remains.

A site that goes unrecorded never becomes an asset.

Korea Expressway Corporation held its 4,483 kilometers all along. What changed is that it found a counterpart willing to turn the activity on top of them into records.

Of everything that passed through your own sites today, how much can you look at again tomorrow?
