프랑스 A31 고속도로에서 시도한 차선변경 27번 가운데 6번이, 유엔 규정 79호가 정한 최소 안전거리를 밑돈 상태로 차선을 넘었습니다.

유럽연합 집행위원회 공동연구센터가 지난 3월 벌인 시험입니다. 시험장을 빌리지도, 제조사에 협조를 구하지도 않았습니다. 이미 형식승인을 받고 팔리는 승용차 세 대로 대열을 짜 디종에서 낭시 방향으로 달렸고, 지붕에 얹은 라이다로 규정이 판정 시점으로 못 박은 그 순간의 차간 거리를 직접 쟀습니다. 시도된 기동 중 열여덟 번이 완료됐고 아홉 번은 시스템이 스스로 취소했습니다.

왜 공도로 나갔는가에 답이 있습니다. 차선변경 보조는 대개 특정 고속도로 구간에서만 작동하도록 지오펜싱돼 있어서, 시험장에서 기능을 켜려면 제조사의 협조가 필요합니다. 승인을 내주는 자리에서는 문제가 되지 않습니다. 이미 팔린 차가 규정대로 동작하는지 확인하려는 자리에서는, 검사 대상이 검사 조건의 열쇠를 쥐게 됩니다.

라이다에도 오차가 있습니다. 위치 표준편차 0.83m를 판정에 얹으면 여섯 건 가운데 절반만 99% 신뢰구간에 남습니다. 논문은 둘 중 하나를 고르는 대신 여섯과 셋을 나란히 적고 나머지를 2시그마로 표시했으며, 미달로 분류된 기동 중 어느 것도 시험 담당자에게 위험하게 인지되지 않았다는 단서와 차량 한 대를 시험했다는 단서를 함께 답니다.

형식승인 성적서는 시험 결과가 사실이라고 말합니다. 그 결과를 만든 조건을 누가 통제했는지까지 말해 주지는 않습니다. 페블러스가 자율주행과 로봇 분야의 AI-Ready 데이터 인프라를 다루는 자리에서도 같은 질문이 먼저 옵니다. 이 수치는 누가, 어떤 경로로 만들었습니까.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Six of 27 lane changes on a French motorway crossed the lane marking with the approaching car closer than UN Regulation No. 79 allows.

Researchers at the European Commission's Joint Research Centre ran the campaign on the A31 in March. They booked no proving ground and asked no manufacturer for access. Three production cars, all already type-approved and on sale, drove north from Dijon toward Nancy with a LiDAR on the roof recording the gap between them at the exact instant the regulation uses for its verdict. Eighteen manoeuvres completed and the system suppressed nine.

The reason for going to the public road sits in the function itself. Lane change assist is usually geofenced to specific motorway stretches, so switching it on inside a proving ground takes the manufacturer's cooperation. That is unremarkable when a manufacturer arrives to be approved. It is a different arrangement once the question is whether a car already on sale still behaves as the regulation requires, because the party under inspection then holds the key to the inspection.

LiDAR carries error of its own. Put a position standard deviation of 0.83 m into the verdict and half of the six survive at 99% confidence. The paper picks neither number over the other: it reports both, marks the remaining cases at 2-sigma, and states that none of the manoeuvres were perceived as dangerous by the testing personnel and that a single vehicle was tested.

A type-approval certificate attests that a test result is real. It does not attest to who controlled the conditions that produced it. The same question opens our own work on AI-ready data for automated driving and robotics: who made this number, and by what route.

## Facebook (EN)

There is a moment on a motorway when the car in the next lane signals and slides in ahead of you.

If that decision came from a function rather than a person, who was measuring whether the gap it took was inside the rules?

A team at the European Commission's Joint Research Centre went out and measured it, on the A31 in France, in March. No proving ground was booked and no manufacturer was asked for access. Three cars already on sale drove north from Dijon toward Nancy, a LiDAR on the roof recording the distance between them. Of 27 lane changes attempted, six crossed the marking with less than the distance the regulation requires.

What stays with me is not the six. It is the route the six travelled to become a number.

Lane change assist is usually locked to specific motorway stretches. Switching it on inside a proving ground takes the cooperation of the company that built it. When that company has come to be approved, this is unremarkable. When the question is whether a car already on the road still behaves as required, it is not.

"If the party being inspected supplies the data that proves compliance, what is the certificate attesting to?"

There is a second careful thing in this study. LiDAR has error too, and once the position error is carried into the verdict, three of the six remain at 99% confidence. The paper does not write only six, and does not write only three. It sets six and three side by side and leaves the three in between marked at 2-sigma. It also records, in the conclusions, that none of the manoeuvres were perceived as dangerous by the testing personnel.

Writing down how certain a value is, next to the value itself. Our own work on data for automated driving and robotics keeps arriving back at that spot. Leave the strength of a finding off the page and the next person to cite it has no way of knowing how far the claim can travel.

The regulation was already there. What was missing was a "second record", one taken outside the proving ground. Put that second record next to the certificate and the certificate starts saying something slightly different.
