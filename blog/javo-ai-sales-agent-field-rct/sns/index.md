조건을 함께 읽어야 합니다. 이 시험을 설계하고 보고한 사람은 Javo를 운영하는 AISO Boost의 창업자 겸 대표 한 명입니다. 독립적인 통계 검수도, 사전 등록도, 외부 윤리 승인도 받지 않았다고 논문이 스스로 적습니다. 참여한 사이트 상당수가 저자의 고객·에이전시 네트워크를 통해 들어왔다는 것도 한계 절에 있습니다. 사람과의 비교 역시 이겼다는 판정이 아니라 비열등 판정이고, 우월성은 통계적으로 유의하지 않았습니다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

An AI agent sitting in a website chat window converted at the same rate as the staff who normally answer it. Across the 20 sites that keep live chat reps, conversion among engaged visitors came in at 11.26% for Javo and 11.20% for the humans.

The trial ran for five months across 100 live websites, randomizing 14.8 million visitors three ways: a window with no chat agent, a window with Javo, and a window wired identically but running a frontier model in place of Javo's.

The paper credits the material rather than the model size. Javo is built on Qwen3-32B, an open-weights model, fine-tuned on 4.6 million commercial conversations. Against the arm where only the model changed, engaged conversion moved by 21.6%.

The conditions travel with the result. One person designed and reported the trial, and he is the founder and CEO of AISO Boost, which operates Javo. The paper states that it claims no independent statistical review, no preregistration and no external ethics approval. Its limitations section adds that many participating sites came in through the author's own client and agency network. The comparison with human reps is a non-inferiority finding, not a win; superiority was not statistically significant.

Whether the numbers are true is not the question. The question is how far they have been checked. Confidence intervals and a prespecified margin are in the text, and 21 sites agreed to be named, but nobody outside the company has measured the same result.

## Facebook (EN)

4.6 million. For a while the figure sat in my head as a measure of scale.

Then came the line describing what those conversations were: people asking whether something was in stock, hesitating at checkout. The same figure read differently after that.

An Israeli company called AISO Boost trained a sales agent on them. The agent is Javo. Its base is an open-weights model anyone can download, so size is not the pitch.

From May through September, 100 live websites across 14 industries split 14.8 million visitors three ways. A window with no chat agent. A window with Javo. And a window wired identically, same tools, with a frontier model swapped in for Javo's.

On the 20 sites that keep live chat reps, a human window ran alongside. Among visitors who sent a message, conversion came in at 11.26% for Javo and 11.20% for the reps. Handling one conversation cost $3.40 on the human side and $0.06 on Javo's.

Stop there and this is a story about cost.

The place I came back to while writing was not the cost table but the material behind the score.

With the interface and the tools held identical and only the model swapped, engaged conversion moved by 21.6%. What made that difference is not the layout of the window. It is the sentences the model read. Those sentences were somebody's customer inquiry.

On where they came from, the paper offers a single line: a collection of commercial conversations held under a management framework, gathered between February 2024 and March 2026. It does not say which company, which websites, or whose visitors.

De-identification comes with numbers. An audit of 2,500 conversations found personal information still present in 0.16% of them.

Consent does not. There is a sentence saying the conversations were collected with user consent. There is none about the screen it was taken on or the words printed there.

"Did the person who left that conversation know their question would become teaching material for a sales model?"

It is only fair to note who wrote the report card. The paper has one author, and he is the founder and CEO of the company that runs Javo. It states that there was no independent statistical review, no preregistration, no external ethics approval. That was written down rather than hidden, so a reader can carry the condition along.
