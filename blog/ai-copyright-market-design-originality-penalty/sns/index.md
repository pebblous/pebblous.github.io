## LinkedIn (EN)

Strengthen copyright to protect creators, and the most original creators are the first to be pushed out. That is the counterintuitive result an MIT economics team draws from a game-theory model.

The reason sits on the other side of the market. When an AI company stands as effectively the only large buyer of training data, pricing power shifts to the buyer, not the seller. Irreplaceable original content, the expensive kind, is bargained down the hardest, and in the model's equilibrium original contributors put in only about half the socially desirable level of effort. The authors call it the originality penalty.

If neither free scraping nor strong copyright works, the way out is to redesign the market. The paper proposes a data intermediary that represents creators and bargains collectively with AI companies.

It is a theoretical model, but the question cuts to the core of practice. Before you compute how to split the pie, can you keep the pie being baked at all?

## Facebook (EN)

Strengthen copyright, and you protect creators. It's a belief most of us have held without much question.

Lately the other side of that belief keeps catching my eye.

In a game-theory model built by an MIT economics team, the people who cut their effort first under stronger copyright turned out to be the most original creators. The authors call it the 'originality penalty.'

Follow the reasoning and it leaves a chill. When an AI company becomes effectively the only large buyer of training data, the power to set the price passes from the seller to the buyer. And it is the irreplaceable, expensive, original work that gets bargained down first. A rule meant to shelter creators pushes out the very people it was built to protect.

One question keeps circling. Before we carefully work out how to divide the pie, shouldn't we first ask whether anyone is left to keep baking it?

That is the same place Pebblous keeps starting from, in settlement ledgers and per-song contribution accounting. Not only how to split the data that already exists, but whether good data can keep being made at all.
