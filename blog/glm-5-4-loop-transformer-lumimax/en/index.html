Executive Summary

China's Zhipu AI (智谱) has released GLM-5.4, a loop transformer that sends tokens through 28 layers of a GLM-5-based model twice with the same weights, adding computational depth without adding parameters. Haima Cloud (海马云) has launched LumiMax, a knowledge base that compiles company documents into a network of sourced facts for agents to query, and says it took first place on eight public benchmarks. GLM-5.4 targets reasoning inside the model; LumiMax targets the memory kept outside it.

Both scorecards come with conditions. Zhipu has published no benchmark table for the 750B flagship, only an ablation that adds and removes the loop on a 30B model of the same design. Its claim of "intelligence levels comparable to 2T-3T parameter models" carries no numbers. Counting LumiMax's published per-question result files, its 92.84% on FRAMES is 765 correct answers out of 824 under what the company calls its "official" grading; 466 answers (56.6%) match the reference answer exactly.

Sections 1 to 4 report what we confirmed in the two companies' announcements, their public files and the original benchmark papers. Section 5 is Pebblous's interpretation: because agents multiply errors across steps, a compiled knowledge network needs the same diagnosis as training data.

Zhipu's GLM-5.4 Is a Loop Transformer That Runs 28 GLM-5 Layers Twice

Z.ai, Zhipu's international brand, introduced GLM-5.4 on its official blog on November 23 as "our first flagship model built on the Loop Transformer architecture." In an ordinary transformer, the input passes through each layer once. A loop transformer runs a block of weight-sharing layers several times, refining in each pass the internal representation built by the previous one. GLM-5.4 sends tokens through 28 layers of a GLM-5-based model twice. The stored weights do not change, so the parameter count stays the same, while the computation each token goes through gets deeper.

Z.ai acknowledged in the post that the idea is not new. The Universal Transformer proposed weight-shared depth in 2018, and later studies showed gains at small scale. But models trained as loops from scratch fell behind ordinary models given the same pretraining compute, Z.ai says. So Zhipu took a base model that had already finished pretraining and converted it to the loop design through continued training.

The design is summarized below. Z.ai shows where the 28 layers sit within the full stack only in a figure and does not give layer numbers.

Zhipu made three changes to make this work at 750B scale. First, each pass has its own per-token gate that decides how much of the newly computed value overwrites the old one. Running existing layers twice without it, Z.ai explains, makes activations grow with every pass until they exceed the range the later layers can handle. After training, a division of labor emerged: the first pass writes little and the second writes a lot, and the first pass writes more on harder tokens. Second, the second pass reuses the KV cache selected by the first pass and keeps only a short sliding window of its own, so one more loop costs one window rather than the full context. Third, the sparse-attention index is also taken from the first pass. Z.ai says the results were nearly the same as recomputing it each time.

According to Z.ai's developer documentation, the context length is 1 million tokens and the maximum output is 128,000 tokens. The blog says only that the deployment cost increase over GLM-5.3 at 1M context stays "well under control," with no price or multiple given. Output speed is up to 200 tokens per second off-peak and 70 to 80 tokens per second at peak, without saying under what request conditions this was measured.

For now the model is available only on Zhipu's own services. It is open to all GLM Coding Plan subscribers and on ZCode and Z.ai Chat, and weekly-quota plans get 1.5 times the GLM-5.3 quota. The developer documentation says the API will open after a security assessment, and the blog says the weights will be open-sourced "after completing necessary safety hardening." Neither has a date.

GLM-5.4's Evidence Is a 30B Ablation; No Table for the 750B Flagship

The performance figures in the Z.ai blog come from a single table image. According to the post, Zhipu validated the loop design on 1B, 4B, 30B and 750B base models in turn, and ran a parameter-matched ablation on a 30B model with the same architecture as the 750B base. The looped version used about 10% more total training compute. A note under the table reads: "Evaluated on the 30B model after 200K long-context SFT, with thinking mode enabled."

Here are the five rows of that table. The "What it tests" column is a Pebblous addition describing how each benchmark is commonly used.

GPQA-Diamond Graduate-level science, multiple choice 47.58 56.26 +8.7 −2,473 tokens (−11.7%)
AA-LCR Reasoning over long documents 40.33 47.67 +7.3 −539 tokens (−9.7%)
MMLU-Pro Multi-domain knowledge and reasoning, multiple choice 71.65 75.05 +3.4 −863 tokens (−14.9%)

▲ Transcribed from the table image in the "Scaling Correctness & Controlled Iso-Parameter Ablations" section of the Z.ai blog. 30B model, after 200K long-context SFT, thinking mode.

Z.ai drew two conclusions from the table. One is that the gains are largest on tasks with long reasoning chains, such as math, scientific reasoning and long-context work. The other is that "across most benchmarks like GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro," average output length fell by 10 to 14%. Z.ai reads this as part of the reasoning moving from visible tokens into the loop's internal depth.

Read row by row, the table shows which benchmark falls outside "most." AIME25, which gained the most, shortened its output by only 2%. Working backward from the tokens saved and the percentages, AIME25's average output is around 30,000 tokens, GPQA-Diamond's around 21,000, and the other three in the 5,000 to 6,000 range (approximate, since the percentages are rounded). The large cuts in output length came on benchmarks whose outputs were short to begin with.

2.1No Table Behind the "2T–3T Class" Claim

The opening of the blog and the developer documentation both say GLM-5.4 reaches "intelligence levels comparable to 2T-3T parameter models." Neither names the 2T–3T models or the benchmarks used for the comparison. There is a sentence saying the loop was also validated on the 750B base model, but those numbers were not published. With both weights and API closed, there is currently no way for anyone outside to measure the claim again.

The company also names a limitation itself. Z.ai writes that the model "tends to get caught in overly long 'marathon thinking' loops," and adds that this prolonged reflection and self-correction "does consistently improve accuracy beyond its parameter count." If AIME25's average output really is around 30,000 tokens, as the calculation above suggests, this tendency shows up as response time and cost on tasks like math.

From here on is interpretation. The 30B ablation matches parameter count, not compute. The looped model used about 10% more training compute, and at inference each token passes through the 28 layers one extra time. The gains in the table therefore mix the effect of the architecture with the effect of spending more compute. The company says shorter outputs partly offset this, but total inference cost, combining per-token compute and output length, has not been published. Nor does any public material yet show that the gains seen at 30B carry over to 750B at the same rate.

Haima Cloud's LumiMax Compiles Company Documents Into a Sourced Knowledge Network

Haima Cloud is a Chinese company founded in 2014. Its legal name is Anhui Haima Cloud Technology Co., Ltd. (安徽海马云科技股份有限公司), and its website describes it as one of the first companies in China to offer GPU cloud services at large scale. It runs cloud gaming and cloud PC services and RunningHub, an AI content creation platform. LumiMax appears in its product list, described as in-depth analysis of documents running to tens of millions of characters.

The LumiMax website describes the product, in Chinese, as an external knowledge base system that lets large models handle complex documents, and says every conclusion carries a link to its source. Instead of the model searching and reading documents after a question arrives, LumiMax reads documents when they are uploaded, organizes them into entities and relationships in advance, and lets agents query the result. For on-premises deployment, the site says it installs DeepSeek V4.1 Flash together with a retrieval model.

Chinese tech outlets, drawing on company materials, describe the following components. The company calls the system a Deep Evidence Network.

  • Entity and relation extraction: pulls companies, people, events and their relationships from documents, links them to evidence in the source, and stores them as entity–event–relation–evidence records. It merges different references to the same company, such as its full name, an abbreviation or "that supplier."
  • Page positions preserved: parses documents while keeping chapter, page and table positions tied to the source, so answers cite document and page.
  • Conflict flags: in one example where a report gives earnings per share as −0.16 and another as −0.19, the system flags the mismatch and suggests possible causes, but leaves it marked as uncertain if the evidence is insufficient.
  • Incremental updates and MCP access: when documents are added, the network is updated in place, and external agents connect through MCP.

Several Chinese outlets presented LumiMax as a product version of the "LLM wiki" idea that Andrej Karpathy posted in a GitHub gist in April 2026. Karpathy's idea is that instead of searching documents every time a question comes in, an LLM reads the documents ahead of time, organizes them into an interlinked wiki and keeps revising it. One outlet noted that LumiMax did not take Karpathy's code or method and that the two only share the concept. Pebblous covered the idea in its April report, "LLMs That Compile Knowledge," and named the open question as who checks the quality of a knowledge base built that way.

LumiMax's 92.84% on FRAMES Is 466 of 824 Questions by Exact Match

The headline on the LumiMax website claims first place on all eight international benchmarks. The company measured LumiMax's scores itself and took the comparison scores from other companies' technical reports, model cards and the original papers' results tables. The site's scripts attach a source link and conditions to each comparison score, and per-question result files for each benchmark can be downloaded. The company says it publishes test conditions and versions together with its scores. The counts below come from those files.

According to Chinese press reports, the eight benchmarks are FRAMES and NoLiMa-32K plus MultiHop-RAG, OpenDocVQA, WildGraphBench, ViDoSeek, RAMDocs and GraphRAG-Bench. We opened the files for FRAMES and NoLiMa-32K, the two whose conditions can be checked against baselines in the original papers. The website lists scores and comparisons for the other six as well, but we did not check them question by question.

4.1What FRAMES and NoLiMa Measure

FRAMES is an 824-question benchmark built by Google DeepMind researchers. Each question requires combining facts from 2 to 15 Wikipedia articles, and it tests factuality, retrieval and reasoning together. In the original paper, Gemini-Pro-1.5-0514 scored 0.40 without retrieval and 0.66 with multi-step retrieval. In the "oracle prompt" condition, where all the reference articles used to write the question are placed in context, it scored 0.729, which the paper treats as the ceiling under perfect retrieval. Answers are free-form, so an LLM grader judges whether they mean the same as the reference answer.

NoLiMa is a long-context benchmark from Adobe Research and collaborators, presented at ICML 2025. It modifies the "needle in a haystack" test, in which a single clue sentence is hidden in a long stretch of unrelated text, by minimizing the words shared between the question and the clue. The model has to find the clue by inferring a hidden association, not by matching words. The paper evaluated 13 LLMs that claim to support at least 128,000 tokens; at 32,000 tokens, 11 fell below half their short-context performance. Even GPT-4o dropped from 99.3% to 69.7%.

4.2LumiMax's FRAMES File: 824 Questions, 824 Knowledge Bases

The FRAMES result file is an Excel sheet whose Chinese title translates as FRAMES Benchmark Test Report. Each row records one question: the question, the reference answer, LumiMax's raw answer, the answer used for grading, whether it is a strict match, the "official" verdict (官方口径), response time, a session ID and a knowledge base ID. Of the 824 questions, 765 (92.84%) are marked correct under the "official" standard, and 466 (56.6%) have a graded answer that matches the reference exactly. The 299 in between were accepted as the same answer in different words. The FRAMES file does not say which grading model made the "official" calls.

All 824 knowledge base IDs are different. It appears that a separate knowledge base was built for each question and the answer was found inside it. The public file does not say what went into each knowledge base. Response time per question had a median of 84 seconds and a mean of 111 seconds; the slowest question took 17 minutes.

The systems the website lines up for comparison were each measured under different conditions. Here are the conditions the site's scripts attach to each score.

4.3LumiMax's 90.52% on NoLiMa-32K Is Compared With Models That Read the Whole Text

The NoLiMa-32K file contains 1,740 questions. LumiMax answered 1,575 correctly for 90.52%, and 1,528 answers (87.8%) match exactly. Here too all 1,740 knowledge base IDs are different, and the median response time is 157 seconds. The question template LumiMax used includes the instruction "Use the exact same wording as the source text."

The comparison scores for GPT-4.1 (79.8%), GPT-4o (69.7%) and Gemini 2.5 Flash (48.4%) come from the 32,000-token column of the official NoLiMa results table. Those models received the 32,000-token text in their context in one go and answered. LumiMax built a knowledge base from the same text and then searched it to answer.

From here on is interpretation. LumiMax's NoLiMa score is hard to read as a test of how well a model attends to a long text read in one pass. It measures whether a retrieval system spending more than two minutes per question found the needle, which is a different thing from what the original paper's models were measured on. On FRAMES, the key condition is that each question had its own knowledge base. If each held only that question's Wikipedia articles, the setup is close to the paper's oracle condition, and it is a different test from the one Tongyi DeepResearch took while searching the open web up to 128 times. Since the contents are not published, there is no way to tell which. Gemini's 2024 oracle score of 72.9 comes from an older model, so beating it is not by itself suspicious. Add the split between 92.84 and 56.6 depending on the grading standard, and what this score confirms is that LumiMax produced it under its own conditions. No third party has yet re-measured it under the same conditions.

Why Pebblous Watches the Quality of LumiMax-Style Knowledge Networks

A knowledge network built by a product like LumiMax is data: entities and relationships extracted from documents, plus their evidence. Company documents often disagree with each other, as in the company's own example, reported by Chinese outlets, of earnings per share given as −0.16 in one report and −0.19 in another. Pebblous sees four kinds of defects arising in a compiled network like this.

  • Conflicts between documents: the same figure or fact stated differently in different documents. If a conflict is flagged but not resolved, the agent picks one of the two.
  • Unsupported links: links created by relation extraction that the source does not actually support. Even with a page citation attached, whether that page states that relationship has to be checked separately.
  • Stale facts: content that changed after upload, such as revised rules or updated results. Incremental updates add new documents; removing old facts is a separate job.
  • Empty regions: topics with no documents and so nothing in the network. The agent does not know the gap exists and pulls a similar-looking answer from somewhere else.

5.1At 95% Per Step, a 20-Step Task Succeeds 36% of the Time

An agent does not query a knowledge network once and stop. It plans, queries, calls tools, reads the results and queries again. If each step is right with probability 0.95 and the steps are treated as independent, the chance of getting all 20 steps right is 0.95 to the 20th power, about 0.36. Raising per-step accuracy to 0.99 gives about 0.82 over the same 20 steps. Cutting the per-step error rate from 5% to 1% more than doubles the success rate of the whole task (Pebblous calculation).

The two announcements touch different terms of this product. GLM-5.4's loop tries to raise per-step accuracy by deepening the reasoning within a step. LumiMax deals with the quality of the memory consulted at each step. If the facts in the network are stale or the links are wrong, no amount of deeper reasoning helps; the model is reasoning on top of wrong facts.

Lay out both announcements' test conditions in four columns (task, time, tools and grading) and you can see where skill and measurement effects get mixed. On task, both used public benchmarks, but GLM-5.4 was tested on a 30B model rather than the flagship, and LumiMax answered each question from its own separately built knowledge base. On time, GLM-5.4 reported only output token counts, not latency or total inference cost. LumiMax took a median of 84 seconds per question on FRAMES and 157 seconds on NoLiMa-32K, while the NoLiMa comparison models read the text in one go and answered. On tools, the GLM-5.4 table note mentions only thinking mode; LumiMax used its own retrieval, while Tongyi DeepResearch, its FRAMES comparison, searched the open web up to 128 times. On grading, the GLM-5.4 table note gives no method, and LumiMax's score, as shown above, depends on which standard is used. Until these four columns are matched, there is no way to say how much of a score gap is the skill of the model or product and how much is the effect of the test conditions.

The 92.84% and 56.6% in LumiMax's FRAMES file raise the same question in practice. Unless you decide what counts as a correct answer, the same answer sheet leads to different conclusions. An organization adopting a knowledge network should settle what a correct answer means for its own work, and how it will be graded, before looking at vendor scores.

As knowledge networks grow, a reuse loop forms. Answers an agent pulls from the network become reports, those reports go back into the network, and the accumulated knowledge becomes data for training the next model. Pebblous believes this loop needs a quality gate: diagnose the network to find conflicts, unsupported links, stale facts and empty regions; fill empty regions with synthetic data; remove conflicting, duplicate or stale entries; and diagnose the result again. Pebblous runs DataClinic, a service that diagnoses the quality of AI training data, and in its April report on Karpathy's wiki it argued that compiled knowledge bases need a data-quality layer.

The scope of what we checked: GLM-5.4's figures and statements were confirmed in the original Z.ai blog post, its table image and the developer documentation. LumiMax's scores and comparison conditions were confirmed from the company website's scripts and by counting the published per-question result files ourselves. LumiMax's product components (the Deep Evidence Network, conflict flags and so on) rely on Chinese tech press reports. We could not find in public materials what went into each question's knowledge base, which model did the "official" grading, or which base LLM LumiMax ran on for the tests. Neither company's scores have yet been reproduced by a third party.
