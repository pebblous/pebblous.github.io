## LinkedIn (KO)

거래소가 털렸는데 개인키는 새지 않았다. 한국시간 10월 30일 새벽 바이낸스에서 열 개 블록체인의 자산이 한꺼번에 공격자 주소로 넘어갔고, TRM Labs 추산 피해액은 63억 6천만 달러다. 가상자산 역사상 가장 큰 탈취다.

돈 대부분이 빠져나가는 데는 17분이 걸렸다. 바이낸스의 1차 조사 발표로는 데브옵스 엔지니어 한 명의 노트북이 탈취됐고, 공격자는 그 경로로 서비스 컨테이너에 닿아 승인되지 않은 출금을 제출했다. 공격자가 서명을 만든 것이 아니라, 유효한 서명이 붙어 나가는 길목을 쥔 쪽에 가깝다. 회사는 하루 뒤 단독범의 소행이 아닌 복합적 공격이라고 설명을 정정했다.

범인을 좁히는 데는 하루가 걸리지 않았다. 체이널리시스는 탈취 자금이 한 달 전 비트겟 해킹 자금과 같은 지갑 체계에서 실시간으로 섞이는 흐름을 포착했고, 비트겟 건은 이미 북한 조직 트레이더트레이터의 소행으로 분류돼 있었다. 제보도 압수한 서버도 아니고 공개 장부에 남은 거래 기록이 근거였다. 다만 분석업체들이 쓴 표현은 확신 수준이 높다거나 가능성이 높다는 쪽이지 확정은 아니다.

돈을 실제로 멈춘 쪽은 거래소가 아니었다. 테더는 자금이 옮겨진 지 37분 만에 첫 공격자 지갑을 동결했다. 발행사가 장부를 쥔 스테이블코인에는 그런 장치가 있고 비트코인과 이더에는 없다. 지난해 업비트 사고에서 솔라나로 바뀐 자산이 5%만 묶였던 것과 이번에 절반 가까이 묶인 것의 차이가 거기서 갈렸다.

한 사건이 데이터에 대해 반대 방향의 두 장면을 보여 준다. 기록이 공개되고 지워지지 않고 시간순으로 이어져 있어서 추적이 됐고, 어느 계정이 어느 시스템의 어디까지 닿는지가 좁혀져 있지 않아서 사고가 시작됐다. 페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단한다. 그 일에서도 지표값만큼 자주 보게 되는 것이 그 값을 사후에 되짚을 수 있는 기록이다. 어느 레코드가 언제 어떤 손을 거쳤는지가 남아 있지 않으면 진단 결과는 그 자리에서만 유효하다. 추적 가능성은 사고가 난 다음에야 값이 매겨지는 품질 항목이다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The exchange was robbed, and no private key leaked. On the evening of October 29 UTC, assets sitting on ten blockchains moved out of Binance to attacker-controlled addresses at once, and TRM Labs puts the loss at $6.36 billion. It is the largest crypto theft on record.

Most of the money took 17 minutes to leave. Binance's first investigation said a DevOps engineer's laptop was compromised, and that the attacker used that route to reach a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawals. The attacker never had to produce a signature; what they held was the path along which valid signatures were attached and sent. A day later the company revised its account, calling the breach a compound attack rather than the work of a lone actor.

Naming the culprit took less than a day. Chainalysis watched the stolen funds mixing in real time with money from the Bitget hack a month earlier, and Bitget had already been classified as the work of the North Korean group TraderTraitor. The grounds were neither an informant nor a seized server. They were the transaction records left on a public ledger. The firms wrote in terms of high confidence and high likelihood, which is not the same as settled fact.

Stopping the money fell to someone other than the exchange. Tether froze the first attacker wallet 37 minutes after the funds moved. Stablecoins whose issuers run the ledger carry that lever; Bitcoin and Ether do not. In last year's Upbit breach the stolen assets were traced to Solana in full and only 5% was locked down, against close to half this time.

A single incident shows two scenes pointing in opposite directions. Tracing worked because the records were public, unerasable and ordered in time. The breach began because no one had narrowed down which account could reach how far into which system. Pebblous writes the quality of AI training data down as a number, with DataClinic. In that work the record behind a metric gets looked at about as often as the metric itself. If nothing says which record passed through whose hands and when, a diagnosis holds only for the moment it was run. Traceability is the quality dimension that gets priced after the incident, not before.

## Facebook (EN)

At 7:46 on the morning of October 30, a notice went up in a Korean exchange app.

Take particular care when withdrawing to Binance, it said.

What had happened on the other side of the world at five that morning, Korean time, arrived a couple of hours later as a single push notification.

Assets on ten blockchains had left Binance almost simultaneously. Most of it in 17 minutes, and about $6.36 billion by TRM Labs' count.

The number that stayed with me after closing the article was not the 17 minutes.

37 minutes.

That is how long it took Tether to lock the first attacker wallet after the money moved.

Narrowing the culprit to a North Korean group took less than a day as well. No informant, no seized server. The conclusion came from reading the transaction records left on the blockchain.

What produced that speed was not the analytics but the bones of the record.

Public. Unerasable. Ordered in time. A ledger with those three properties can be walked back after the fact.

'The public ledger' has long been discussed as a design that favors the attacker. This time the same design became a tool for the side doing the chasing.

The place Binance first pointed to as the start of the breach was neither the cryptography nor the consensus algorithm.

It was one DevOps engineer's laptop. No private key leaked, and the attacker took up position on the path where valid signatures were attached and sent. A day later the company revised its account, calling it a compound attack rather than the work of a lone actor.

"Is our own record in a shape that can be walked back after an incident?"
