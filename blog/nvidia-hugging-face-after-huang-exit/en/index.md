# Will Hugging Face Stay Open Once Nvidia Owns It?

_Interim CEO Colette Kress said on Nvidia_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Nvidia's agreement to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion survives the departure of Jensen Huang. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer who has taken over as interim CEO, said so on the November 17 earnings call. Huang had resigned with immediate effect five days earlier.

> Hugging Face is the shared warehouse where 18 million developers and 200,000 companies upload and download models and datasets. Nvidia has promised to keep that platform open, and the promise sits in more than a press release: it is written into the Form 8-K the company filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on September 2, down to a line about supporting other silicon vendors. What the filing never states is how much support that means, and the yardstick it sets, "Hugging Face's existing practices," has no public definition anywhere. The US Department of Justice is already examining the deal.

> Sections 1 through 4 are what the announcements, the filings and the reporting say. Section 5 is this article's reading.

### Key Figures

Four numbers give the shape of the deal: the price, the size of what is being bought, how far that price has moved in three years, and when the transaction actually closes.

## What the Interim CEO Confirmed, and What Went Unasked

Nvidia reported third-quarter results for fiscal 2027 on November 17. It was the first call held after Huang's departure, and the person leading it was Colette Kress, the chief financial officer who now doubles as interim chief executive.

Kress addressed the Hugging Face acquisition in prepared remarks. "At Nvidia, we are committed to fostering a vibrant open model ecosystem, so every country, company, and industry can build its own intelligence," Kress said, adding that "together, we will scale its platform and make intelligence more open, more capable, and more accessible." Harvey Jones, who has taken the newly created role of board chair, said the same day that there is no plan to bring Huang back as chief executive, and that the board has full confidence in Kress's leadership and capabilities. The search for a permanent CEO is being run by the executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles.

Then there is the part that was not confirmed. The acquisition never came up in the analyst question-and-answer session that day. Kress's sentence was a line written in advance, not an answer given under questioning. That is no reason to think Nvidia's stance has weakened, but it does mean the commitment has yet to face public scrutiny with Huang gone.

The terms themselves were documented back in September. Nvidia's board approved the definitive agreement on September 2 and announced it the following day. Of the $12.9 billion total, roughly $11.9 billion goes to Hugging Face shareholders in cash, and up to $1 billion is equity compensation meant to retain employees who move over to Nvidia. This is the second-largest acquisition in Nvidia's history, targeted to close in the first half of 2027, subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory clearances in multiple jurisdictions.

How quickly the two companies closed the distance stands out as well. Nvidia joined a $235 million funding round in 2023 alongside Google and Salesforce, when Hugging Face carried a $4.5 billion valuation. In 2025, according to the Financial Times, Hugging Face turned down a $500 million investment at a $7 billion valuation. The following September it signed an agreement to sell the whole company for $12.9 billion.
