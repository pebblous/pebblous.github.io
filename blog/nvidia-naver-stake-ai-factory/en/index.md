# Naver Builds the AI Factory, and Nvidia Owns a Piece of It

_Nvidia pays $1 billion for 4.54% of Naver, which had to secure $9 billion of compute first._

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Nvidia is buying Naver shares and will hold more of the company than all but two other owners. Under the schedule Naver filed on October 30, the deal hands over a 4.54% stake in return for $1 billion through a third-party allocation of new shares. The money arrives on November 6, and the newly issued shares list on November 27. That seat sits directly behind the National Pension Service at 9.49% and BlackRock at 7.31%, and it pushes Lee Hae-jin, the board chair who had occupied it, from 4.03% down to 3.84%.

> The condition draws the eye more than the amount does. The share subscription agreement signed on July 24 carried a condition precedent: separately from this investment, Naver first had to secure at least $9 billion worth of computing infrastructure. Naver cleared that condition on October 27 by signing with Brookfield, and only then was the payment date fixed. The company selling the chips is becoming a shareholder in the company buying them, and the agreement told the buyer to go raise the money for chips first.

> Sections 1 through 3 follow what can be confirmed in Naver's filings, the two companies' announcements, and the reporting that carried them. The passage reading why Nvidia wants equity rather than chip sales alone cites a brokerage analysis, with the source attached in place. The power, the land, and the operator are domestic, while the capital, the chips, and the design come from outside. Section 4 asks what the phrase sovereign AI actually secures in that structure, and that section is this article's reading.

## Nvidia Becomes Naver's Third-Largest Shareholder

The filing Naver made on October 30 fixed the schedule. The issue price was set at 204,500 won a share, and the filing puts the new shares going to Nvidia at 7,241,564. That share count is still provisional. The payment date is November 6, and the new shares are scheduled to list on November 27. The dates originally written in were October 30 for payment and November 20 for listing, and each slipped by a week. Those earlier dates were outside deadlines written into the contract to allow for the time it would take to satisfy the condition precedent.

Where the amount gets written in won, the reporting splits. Some articles put it at 1.34 trillion won, others at 1.48 trillion. Neither is wrong. Only the dollar figure of $1 billion is fixed, and each article converted at a different moment. The final share count and the won proceeds will be set using the won-dollar market average exchange rate on November 3.

Once payment clears, the top of the register changes. The National Pension Service stays the largest holder at 9.49%, BlackRock is second at 7.31%, and Nvidia stands behind them at 4.54%. Founder and board chair Lee Hae-jin sees his holding thinned by the new issuance, from 4.03% to 3.84%. The shares Nvidia receives are registered electronically with the Korea Securities Depository under a mandatory holding condition, and for one year they can be neither withdrawn nor sold. The filing states that the entire sum raised goes to new businesses related to the AI factory.

Seen from Nvidia's side, the amount itself is not large. Cho Jae-yoon, an analyst at Daishin Securities, wrote in an [analysis](https://www.mt.co.kr/stock/2026/07/27/2026072708311112686) published right after the July announcement that $1 billion comes to 1% of Nvidia's first-quarter revenue of $81.6 billion, leaving the financial impact limited. The meaning of the deal, he argued, lies in where the capital went rather than how much of it went. Naver is Korea's largest internet platform and an AI infrastructure customer running HyperCLOVA X, its own large language model. Cho read the move to hold equity directly, instead of stopping at chip sales, as a way of tying Korean and Asian sovereign-AI demand into Nvidia's ecosystem. The same analysis noted that 92% revenue concentration in data centers and dependence on hyperscalers have long been counted among Nvidia's weak points.

On Naver's side the dilution reads as a price paid knowingly. Lee said at the November 2025 earnings briefing that the business comes first and that a drop in his ownership is the lesser question. After this filing Naver said it does not expect to run short of capital for five to six years or more of running the business on the funds raised now, and added that if more money is needed after that, it will look for ways to raise it in line with the business situation at the time.

## The Condition That Came Before the Money

When the share subscription agreement was signed on July 24, Nvidia attached one condition ahead of payment. Naver had to secure at least $9 billion worth of computing infrastructure, separately from this $1 billion. Naver met it on October 27 by signing with Brookfield. In effect the investor told the company receiving the money to place the order first for what that money would buy, and the gap between the two figures deserves a look of its own. The equity money coming in is $1 billion. The condition standing in front of it is nine times that.

The contract that satisfied the condition is built this way. Brookfield funds in full a special purpose vehicle called Assetco, and that entity spends up to $9 billion buying GPUs and a data center. Naver Cloud takes computing resources from Assetco as a service and sells them to customers. Naver and Naver Cloud lead the business, and Nvidia handles the technology with GPU supply and platforms such as DSX. The side that put up the money, the side that holds the assets, and the side that runs the business are all different.

Accounting counts it differently. Brookfield holds all of Assetco's equity, but under Korean IFRS, Naver is recognized as holding control, so the entity enters Naver's consolidated accounts as a subsidiary. Legal ownership and accounting control come apart. The GPU assets, and the depreciation that writes those assets down year by year, therefore land on Naver's consolidated financial statements. Korea Investors Service expects Naver's annual depreciation to rise by about 2 trillion won from after 2028, when the GPU investment finishes being deployed. Naver's consolidated depreciation last year was 680 billion won.

The other obligations Naver has taken on are in the filings as numbers too. Naver has guaranteed 18.74 trillion won of Naver Cloud's usage-fee payment obligations. The amount equals 64.73% of its equity, and the term runs from October 27, 2026 to December 31, 2034. The figure is the nominal total of compute service payments due through the end of 2034, assuming Brookfield's $9 billion investment ceiling is drawn in full. The actual amount will vary with how much gets deployed, and Brookfield's invested principal, its return, and the GPUs' residual value fall outside the guarantee. Naver filed on the same day a related transaction under which it will receive a guarantee fee of 670.725 billion won from Naver Cloud.

> [!callout]
> What Naver has put into this structure is not cash but credit. Brookfield provides the money to buy the GPUs and an entity Brookfield established holds the assets, and yet if Naver Cloud cannot pay for the compute that entity supplies, Naver pays instead. The shape where a financial firm holds the assets while the user pays long-term fees is one we have seen before in [Apollo's GPU lease to SpaceX](/blog/spacex-apollo-gpu-lease-ownership/en/).

## The Factory in Sejong Opens at 55 Megawatts

The money is headed for Gak Sejong, Naver's data center in the city of Sejong. The plan is written in four steps. Operation starts at 55 megawatts in the first half of 2027, reaches a cumulative 100 megawatts by the end of that year and a cumulative 200 megawatts in 2028, and takes 1 gigawatt as its long-term target. Short summaries often drop the 100-megawatt step in the middle, and it takes all four to see capacity very nearly double inside the single year of 2027.

Of the four steps, only the first three carry a year. The last box holds a capacity and no date.
