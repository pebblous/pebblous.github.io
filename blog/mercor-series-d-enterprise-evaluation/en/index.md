# Mercor Grades AI on Your Company

_Mercor raised $750 million at a $20 billion valuation on November 10, with Nvidia, Salesforce and Zoom on the list, to grade agents on each company_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Mercor, which produces the data used to train and evaluate AI, has raised $750 million at a valuation of $20 billion. The Series D was announced on November 10, and Nvidia, Salesforce and Zoom are on the investor list. The new names on it are companies that sell chips and workplace software, not labs that build models.

> In the same announcement the company rewrote who it is selling to. Deeptune, which it acquired in July, had spent two years rebuilding hundreds of enterprise applications as environments where an agent can practice the job. Bolt that onto a network of more than five million registered experts and the plan becomes legible: grade one company's own operations the way the firm used to grade a frontier lab's models. Co-founder Brendan Foody's framing is that every company will need a way to train and evaluate agents on its own work.

> Sections 1 through 4 stay with what the company announced and what was reported. Section 5 is how the news looks from the side that builds evaluation data, and from there on it is this article's own reading.

## Salesforce and Zoom Sell the Software an Agent Will Work In

Mercor said on November 10 that it had raised $750 million in a Series D, and the valuation attached to it is $20 billion. The Series C in October 2025 was $350 million at $10 billion, so the price has doubled in a little over a year. Existing investors came back in, Felicis among them, which led that earlier round, and no lead was named this time. The announcement states that the round included an employee tender alongside the new money. How much of the total that accounts for was not disclosed.

The names that stand out are the new ones. Nvidia, Salesforce and Zoom appear on the investor list, next to funds managed by affiliates of Apollo, Sapphire Ventures, StepStone Group, G Squared, Glade Brook Capital, XTX Ventures and Fundomo. Benchmark and General Catalyst, both returning, are on the list as well. Salesforce and Zoom do not build models; they sell the software companies work inside. They are also the owners of the screens where an agent will be expected to do the work.

What the company puts forward as its asset is people. The announcement puts the network at more than five million experts. In the same post the company notes that the United States alone holds more than 800 distinct occupations, each carrying workflows that no model has been evaluated against yet. Mercor started in 2023 by connecting AI labs with specialists in fields such as law, finance and medicine. In July, Foody said annualized revenue had passed $2 billion as of June.

## Twice This Year, Mercor Bought a Maker of Practice Environments

The parts for this shift came in four months ago. Deeptune was a New York company led by Tim Lupo, and what it built were reinforcement learning environments that copy the software knowledge workers use every day, which the company called training gyms. An agent practices real tasks inside mock spreadsheets and Salesforce queues. Two years went into rebuilding hundreds of enterprise applications that way.

The sequence does not look like coincidence. Deeptune raised a $43 million Series A in March 2026 led by Andreessen Horowitz, and Foody came into that round as an angel with his own money. Four months later, on July 9, Mercor acquired the company. The price was not disclosed. Asked by Fortune whether an acquisition was on his mind when he wrote the angel check, Foody answered that in a lot of ways it was the main motivation.

Deeptune was not the first purchase of the year either. Five months before it, on February 6, 2026, Mercor acquired Sepal AI. That price went undisclosed as well, and the record of the deal is an announcement from Orrick, the law firm that advised it. Mercor described Sepal as a team working at the intersection of human data, reinforcement learning environments and specialized research projects. February and July, twice in a single year, and in both cases the target was a company building the places where agents practice.
