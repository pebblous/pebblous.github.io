## LinkedIn (KO)

AI 모델을 다시 가르치는 자리에 변호사와 애널리스트를 앉히겠다는 회사가, 창업 1년 만에 기업가치 3억 달러를 인정받았다.

Trajectory는 11월 2일 세쿼이아가 이끈 4,500만 달러 시리즈 A를 발표하고 같은 날 지속 학습 플랫폼을 일반에 열었다. 엔비디아와 베서머 벤처 파트너스, 베인캐피털 벤처스가 함께 들어왔다. 직원은 18명이다.

파는 것은 한 바퀴다. 고객사가 오픈소스 모델을 자사 앱에 연결하면 플랫폼이 실제 업무에서 나온 피드백을 모아 모델을 다시 학습시킨다. Harvey에서는 특정 유형의 계약서 검토를, Rogo에서는 정해진 방법론에 따른 기업 가치 평가를 모델에 익힌다. 공개된 고객사 명단에는 이번 라운드의 투자자인 엔비디아도 있다.

회사가 내세우는 자리는 그 바퀴의 마지막 칸이다. 어느 답이 더 낫다는 판정은 지금까지 연구팀이 설계한 지침을 받은 라벨러의 몫이었다. 이 구조에서 그 판정은 계약서를 검토하던 변호사가 자기 자리에 앉은 채 남긴다. 업무 기록과 학습 데이터 사이의 경계가 사라진다.

같은 진단에서 반대쪽으로 간 Mercor는 외부 전문가를 시급으로 모아 AI 랩에 후학습 데이터를 공급하고, 2025년 10월 보도 기준 기업가치가 100억 달러였다. 그 Mercor가 Trajectory의 고객사 명단에 있다.

그림자는 비용 쪽에 있다. 변호사가 모델 초안을 고치는 데 쓰는 10분은 다른 사건에 쓰지 못한 10분인데, 어느 평가 항목에도 모델을 가르쳤다는 줄은 없다. 데이터 소유권도 기사에 나오지 않는다. 그 판정이 그 고객사만의 모델에 남는지, 계약이 끝날 때 가지고 나올 수 있는지는 계약서가 정할 문제다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단한다. 라벨러 둘이 같은 사례를 다르게 판정하면 모델이 흔들린다. 현업 전문가 스무 명이 각자의 기준으로 신호를 보내는 구조에서는 그 흔들림을 잡아 줄 지침이 어디에도 없을 수 있다. 품질 관리의 대상이 학습 전의 자료에서 모델이 돌아가는 동안으로 넓어진다.

좋은 모델을 고르는 문제는 모델 회사가 푼다. 우리 업무에서 무엇이 좋은 답인지는 누구도 대신 적어 주지 않는다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

A company that wants lawyers and analysts, rather than researchers, to do the teaching has been valued at $300 million about a year after it was founded.

Trajectory announced a $45 million Series A led by Sequoia on November 2 and opened its continual learning platform to the public the same day. NVIDIA, Bessemer Venture Partners and Bain Capital Ventures joined the round. The company has 18 employees.

What it sells is a loop. A customer attaches an open-source model to its own application, the platform gathers feedback from real work, and the model is trained again on what comes back. At Harvey that means reviewing a particular kind of contract; at Rogo, valuing a company by a set methodology. NVIDIA sits on the published customer list as well as in the round.

The claim rests on the last step of that loop. Deciding which answer is better has until now belonged to labelers working from guidelines a research team wrote. Here the lawyer makes the call from the same desk, on the same contract, as part of the job. The line between a work record and a training set stops existing.

Mercor read the same diagnosis and went the other way. It hires outside experts by the hour and supplies post-training data to frontier labs, and reporting from October 2025 put it at a $10 billion valuation. Mercor is also one of Trajectory's customers.

The cost shows up where nobody counts it. The ten minutes a lawyer spends correcting a draft are ten minutes not spent on another matter, and no performance review has a line for teaching a model. Ownership goes unaddressed too. Whether those judgments stay inside one customer's model, and whether they can leave when the contract does, is a question for the contract.

Pebblous quantifies the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Two labelers who grade one case differently will unsettle a model. Twenty working specialists, each applying a private standard, may have no written guideline to be unsettled against. Quality control moves from the material gathered before training to the judgments produced while the system runs.

Which model to buy is a question model companies answer. What counts as a good answer in your own work is not a question anyone answers for you.

## Facebook (EN)

An AI drafts the contract review first. The lawyer corrects it and sends it out.

Nothing marks the place where the correction happened. The work is finished, so it is finished.

But if that correction becomes the teaching material for the next model, what kind of ten minutes was that?

On November 2 a company built on exactly those ten minutes raised $45 million from Sequoia. Trajectory, founded about a year ago by three Stanford classmates, eighteen people.

A customer attaches an open-source model to its own application, and the platform collects the feedback that real work produces and trains the model again on it. The way a lawyer at Harvey reads a contract, the way an analyst at Rogo values a company, moves across to the model.

Writing down "this answer is better than that one" has until now been the labeler's task, carried out from a research team's guidelines. Here it comes up onto the desk of the person handling their own caseload.

'Work that doubles as curriculum'.

It is a comfortable arrangement and an unnerving one. You no longer need a team whose job is to define quality, which also means the model changes without anyone writing the standard down.

Pebblous quantifies the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Two labelers who grade one case differently will unsettle a model, and twenty working specialists, each with a private standard, may have no written guideline to be unsettled against.

"Where in our work is the standard for a good answer actually written? Whose job is it to keep writing it?"

One question the article leaves open. Who owns those judgments once they accumulate, and can they leave with you when the contract ends? The model having started out open-source does not settle that.

Keeping a person inside the training loop is the safer design, the co-founder said. Accepting that means also accepting that the person inside the loop has one more thing to do today.
