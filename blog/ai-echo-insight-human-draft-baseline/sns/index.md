AI가 쓴 심장 초음파 판독 초안의 절반을 전문의가 크게 고쳤다. 같은 시험에서 사람이 쓴 초안도 절반이었다.

카이저 퍼머넌테 북캘리포니아가 11월 9일 시카고에서 열린 미국심장협회 학술대회에서 무작위 시험 AI ECHO INSIGHT의 결과를 내놓았다. 성인 경흉부 심장 초음파 1,200건마다 예비 판독을 세 벌씩 받았다. AI와 초음파기사와 심장전문의가 한 벌씩 썼다. 세 벌은 각각 다른 전문의에게 흩어졌고, 받아 든 쪽은 출처를 듣지 못했다. 크게 손본 비율은 AI 쪽 50.6%, 사람 쪽 49.7%로 나왔다.

양쪽 모두 절반 가까이 올라온 것은 걸러내는 체의 눈이 아주 성글기 때문이다. 이 시험은 판독이 정답에 닿았는지를 묻지 않는다. 박출률 10%p, 등급 한 칸, 압력 추정치의 30mmHg 선. 미리 적어 둔 항목 가운데 하나만 초안과 확정본 사이에서 움직여도 그 검사는 실질 변경으로 넘어간다.

그래서 숫자의 뜻을 정하는 쪽은 대조군이다. 50.6% 하나만 돌아다녔다면 기사 제목은 AI의 실패였을 것이다. 49.7%가 옆에 적히는 순간 같은 값이 확정 심사라는 공정의 기준선으로 읽힌다. 판독을 확정하는 데 걸린 시간은 8.5분에서 4.6분으로 떨어졌다.

그늘은 이름에 있다. 시험 등록 정보는 1차 평가지표를 정확도라고 부르는데, 그 아래 달린 정의가 재는 것은 두 판독이 서로 얼마나 가까웠는가다. AI 보조 쪽이 환자의 지난 기록과 더 자주 맞았다는 부차 결과도 같은 모호함 위에 놓인다. 기준을 흔들림 없이 적용한 쪽과 지난 판독문의 문장을 따라 쓴 쪽은 바깥에서 보면 구분되지 않는다. 동료 심사 전 초록이고, 통합 의료 시스템 한 곳에서 나온 값이다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Cardiologists rewrote half the echocardiogram drafts an AI had produced. They rewrote half the drafts that people had produced too.

Kaiser Permanente Northern California reported the AI ECHO INSIGHT trial on November 9 at the American Heart Association's Scientific Sessions in Chicago. Each of 1,200 adult echocardiograms was drafted three times over: once by the AI, once by a sonographer, once by a cardiologist. Every draft then went to a different cardiologist for signing, with no indication of where it had come from. Substantial changes landed on 50.6% of the AI drafts and 49.7% of the human ones.

Both rates sit near half because the sieve was built with very coarse mesh. The trial never asked whether a read was correct. It asked whether any item on a list fixed in advance moved between the draft and the signed report, and one item moving was enough to count.

Which is why the control arm carries the result. On its own, 50.6% files as an AI failure. Printed beside 49.7%, the same value describes the signing step, which has apparently always rewritten about half of what reaches it. Turnaround on a finalized report dropped from 8.5 minutes to 4.6.

The shadow is in the naming. The registration calls the primary endpoint an accuracy rate, while the definition sitting under that label measures how closely two reads agreed. A secondary finding, that AI-assisted reports matched each patient's older records more often, rests on the same ambiguity. Criteria applied without wavering and wording copied forward from last year look identical from outside the room. This is a late-breaking abstract from a single health system, not yet peer reviewed.

## Facebook (EN)

A cardiologist opens a draft report, and nowhere on it is there a name.

It might have come from the AI. It might have come from a sonographer, or from the colleague two doors down.

That is how Kaiser Permanente Northern California built AI ECHO INSIGHT, the trial it carried to the American Heart Association's Scientific Sessions on November 9. The same 1,200 echocardiograms were drafted three times over, and whoever signed the final report was never told which version was in front of them.

Substantial changes landed on 50.6% of the AI drafts. More than half.

Read that figure on its own and there is only one place to go. Half of what the machine writes has to be fixed by a person.

Then you notice 49.7% printed next to it, the same ruler held up to drafts that people wrote.

Two values side by side, and the sentence changes. Signing off an echo report rewrites about half of whatever arrives, and it was doing that long before any model came near the workflow.

What this trial newly exposed may have less to do with the AI's score than with the everyday scale of reading hearts.

"The half we also measured."

"Beside that number on the scorecard, is anyone writing down what a person scores on the same task?"
