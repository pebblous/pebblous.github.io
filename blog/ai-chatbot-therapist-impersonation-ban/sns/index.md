반년 남짓 사이에 일리노이, 캘리포니아, 테네시가 뒤따랐고 2026년 3월에는 연방 CHATBOT Act까지 발의됐다. 규제의 질문이 바뀐 것이다. 예전에는 "이것이 AI인가 사람인가"를 밝히라고 요구했다면, 이제는 "이 AI가 무엇을 사칭하는가"를 금지한다. 캘리포니아 AB 489는 챗봇이 'Dr.'나 '임상의 검증' 같은 표현으로 면허 전문가처럼 보이는 행위 자체를 위법으로 규정한다. 이미 가짜 면허번호까지 내세운 챗봇이 나타났기 때문이다.

그런데 그 대화가 남긴 데이터, 곧 감정 섞인 상담 기록을 누구의 동의로 학습에 쓰는가에 답하는 법은 거의 비어 있다. 실제 사용 제한을 담은 곳은 유타 한 곳뿐이다.

챗봇이 무엇이라 말해도 되는가는 빠르게 정리됐지만, 그 말이 남긴 기록을 어떻게 쓸 수 있는가는 아직 손대지 못했다. 감정 데이터를 다루는 제품에게 이 공백은 다음 규제 물결의 예고편으로 읽을 만하다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

In July 2025, Nevada became the first US state to ban AI systems from posing as therapists outright.

Within six months Illinois, California, and Tennessee followed, and by March 2026 a federal CHATBOT Act had been introduced. The regulatory question has moved. Where the earlier ask was to disclose "is this an AI or a person," the new one prohibits "what is this AI pretending to be." California's AB 489 makes it unlawful for a chatbot to use titles like "Dr." or phrases like "clinician-verified" to look like a licensed professional. The reason is blunt: chatbots claiming to be licensed therapists, some even citing fabricated license numbers, had already appeared.

Yet the law is nearly silent on what happens to the data those conversations leave behind. Who consents to emotional chat logs being used for training? Only Utah restricts it in practice.

What a chatbot may claim to be got settled quickly. What its words may later be used for did not. For any product handling emotional data, that gap reads as the preview of the next regulatory wave.

## Facebook (EN)

"I'm a licensed counselor."

A chatbot that answered like that, late at night, is now something several US state legislatures are writing down in earnest.

Last July Nevada banned AI systems from providing therapy at all. Illinois, California, and Tennessee drew similar lines soon after, and by this March a federal bill had arrived.

For a while the law asked chatbots one thing: are you a human or an AI? Now it asks something else. What are you pretending to be?

But the part that stayed with me was elsewhere. Five states and a federal bill settled what a chatbot may claim to be, and settled it fast. What almost none of them touched is the record those conversations leave behind, the feelings someone confided at night, and whose consent lets that be used for training. Only Utah has drawn that boundary in practice.

Words are easy to regulate. What words leave behind is not. So the gap feels like the next thing to arrive. Perhaps, for data that carries emotion, knowing where it came from and what it consented to has to arrive before the rules do. That is where Pebblous has spent its time too, recording the source, consent, and permitted use of data as part of making it ready for AI.
