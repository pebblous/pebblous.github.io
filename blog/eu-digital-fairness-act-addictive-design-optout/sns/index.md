## LinkedIn (KO)

유럽연합이 자동재생과 끝없는 피드, 사람 관계를 흉내 내는 챗봇을 법 조문에 적어 넣었다. 적어 넣은 자리는 금지 목록이 아니라 설정 화면이다.

집행위원회가 11월 11일 내놓은 디지털공정법 초안 제17조는 성인 이용자가 이 기능들을 하나씩, 또는 한꺼번에 끌 수 있게 하라고 요구한다. 걸리는 상대는 강박적이거나 과도한 사용을 부추기도록 제품을 설계했거나 그렇게 되리라고 충분히 예상되는 사업자다. 같은 법 안에서 가짜 품절 문구 같은 다크패턴은 쓰는 것 자체가 위반인데, 중독성 설계는 끌 방법만 주면 된다.

차이는 기본값에서 난다. 끄는 단추가 생겨도 설정을 찾아 들어가지 않은 사람의 화면은 오늘과 같다. 녹색당·유럽자유동맹 보고관 킴 반 스파런타크는 집행위가 물러섰고 중독적 설계가 켜져 있다는 것을 법에 적어 넣고 있다고 비판했다. 무엇을 먼저 보여 줄지 고르는 추천 알고리즘은 끄기 목록에 아예 없다. 미성년자에게는 같은 기능이 별도 법안에서 금지된다.

가장 많이 인용된 전 세계 연매출 6%는 디지털공정법이 아니라 같은 날 함께 내놓은 소비자보호협력 규정 개정안에 적혀 있다. 한쪽은 무엇이 금지되는지를, 다른 쪽은 누가 어떻게 집행하는지를 정한다. 둘 다 아직 제안이고 유럽의회와 이사회 심의가 남았다.

페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단한다. 세션 길이와 대화 턴 수는 제품이 쓸 만하다는 신호로 모으기 시작한 숫자인데, 붙잡는 데 성공했다는 신호이기도 하다. 로그 한 줄만 놓고 보면 두 경우가 똑같이 생겼다. 끄는 단추가 달리면 끈 사람과 끄지 않은 사람이 갈린다. 그 갈림이 이 물음에 처음으로 비교할 짝을 준다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The European Union has written autoplay, endless feeds and chatbots that imitate a relationship into a bill. It put them in the settings menu rather than on the banned list.

Article 17 of the Digital Fairness Act, proposed by the European Commission on November 11, requires that adult users be able to switch those features off, one at a time or all together. It reaches only traders whose products are built to encourage compulsive or excessive use, or can reasonably be foreseen to do so. Within the same bill, dark patterns such as a fake low-stock warning are prohibited whatever they lead to. Addictive design is not. Supplying the switch is enough.

The default is where that distinction bites. A switch changes nothing for anyone who never opens the settings, and the Greens/EFA negotiator Kim van Sparrentak said the Commission "has caved" by writing into law that addictive design starts out on. Recommender systems, which decide what appears first, are not on the list at all. For minors the same features are banned, under a separate bill.

The 6% of worldwide turnover quoted in most coverage belongs to the Consumer Protection Cooperation regulation revised the same day, not to the Digital Fairness Act itself. One text says what is forbidden, the other says who enforces it. Both are proposals, with Parliament and Council still to come.

Pebblous diagnoses the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Session length and turns per conversation begin as evidence that a product is worth using, and they double as evidence that it held on to someone. A single line of a log cannot say which. An off switch at least separates the people who used it from the people who did not, and that is the first comparison this question has ever had.

## Facebook (EN)

A video ends, and the next one begins on its own.

I did not decide anything in that moment.

The Digital Fairness Act, which the European Commission put forward on November 11, asks services to let adult users switch that off. Autoplay and feeds without a floor. Notifications that have nothing to do with what you were doing. Rewards that break if you skip a day. And chatbots that keep asking for one more exchange.

It asks for a switch, not for the feature to go away. Anyone who never opens the settings sees the same screen tomorrow.

For minors the same features are banned outright, under a different bill. Only adults get the choice.

Critics say the Commission backed down, and about the design I think they are right.

On the record side, though, something exists that did not exist before.

Once the switch is there, the people who use it part from the people who leave it alone, and that parting lands in the log.

Until now there was no material for asking whether a longer visit was worth something or was simply held in place. In a log the two look identical.

How clean that comparison turns out to be is another matter. The cookie consent banner went the same way once. When a screen that asks for a choice becomes a screen that leans on one, the group that switched the feature off is already tilted.

"The half hour someone spent here: did they want to stay, or did the next video just start?"

Pebblous diagnoses the quality of AI training data with DataClinic. Good data, I think, is not clean data. It is data whose meaning somebody knows.

Time on screen, collected without anyone knowing what produced it, is a number that cannot yet be read.
