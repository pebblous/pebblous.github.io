## LinkedIn (EN)

Fifteen of 123 corporate privacy policies promise one thing in one section and document a conflicting practice in another, according to a paper posted to arXiv this week. Roughly one in eight, judged by three LLMs from different providers voting by majority.

Notion's policy states that it does not disclose user information to advertise third-party products. Elsewhere in the same document, it notes that it discloses online identifiers through advertising services in a way that may constitute a sale or sharing under CCPA. Seven months later the author replaced every extraction and judge model and re-ran the corpus, and the rate came back at 13.0%.

The caveats are the author's own. Requiring unanimity instead of a majority cuts the confirmed companies from 15 to eight, and no stage of the pipeline has ever been checked against human labels, which is why the paper calls the figure a property of the pipeline rather than a measure of how common contradictions are.

The finding travels well beyond privacy policies. Any organization that documents its data governance across security policies, handling guidelines, model cards and customer commitments rests on the same untested assumption, that those documents agree with one another. Pebblous spends most of its diagnostic work on the gap between what data is documented to be and what it is.

## Facebook (KO)

개인정보 처리방침을 끝까지 읽어 본 게 언제였는지 기억나지 않습니다.

어떤 연구자는 123곳의 처리방침을 끝까지 읽었습니다. 정확히는 끝까지 읽는 일을 기계에 맡겼고, 우리가 보통 묻지 않는 것을 물었습니다.

읽기 쉬운가도 아니고, 법이 요구하는 항목을 다 담았는가도 아니었습니다.

"이 문서는 자기 자신과 일관적인가?"

여덟 곳 중 한 곳에서 그렇지 않다는 답이 나왔습니다. 앞쪽 어느 절에 적어 둔 약속이 뒤쪽 어느 절에 적어 둔 관행과 어긋났습니다.

저자는 이것을 거짓말이라 부르지 않습니다. 대신 '조각난 문서'라 부를 만한 구조를 지목합니다. 한 파일에 수십 개 사업부가 들어가고, 절마다 다른 시기에 다른 팀이 손을 대고, 새 규제가 생길 때마다 문구가 한 줄씩 덧붙습니다.

숨기려는 사람이 없어도 문서는 어긋납니다.

오래 남은 대목은 따로 있었습니다. 아마존과 넷플릭스, 에어비앤비에서는 확정된 모순이 하나도 나오지 않았습니다. 그런데 에어비앤비 정책 전체에서 뽑힌 약속 문장은 셋뿐이었습니다. 약속을 적게 적은 문서는 어긋날 자리도 적습니다.

정합성만 재는 검사기는 약속을 지운 문서에 가장 좋은 점수를 줍니다.

페블러스가 데이터 품질을 진단할 때 자주 만나는 상황은 문서와 실제가 따로 노는 경우입니다. 이 연구가 재는 것은 그 이전 단계, 문서가 자기 자신과 따로 노는 경우입니다.

그래서 읽고 나서 남은 물음은 처리방침에 관한 것이 아니었습니다.

"우리가 대외로 내보낸 데이터 관련 약속이 어느 문서 몇 곳에 흩어져 있는지, 목록으로 갖고 있는가?"

목록이 없으면 대조는 시작되지도 않습니다.

## Facebook (EN)

I cannot remember the last time I read a privacy policy all the way through.

Someone did. A researcher read 123 of them, or rather handed the reading to machines, and asked a question I had not thought to ask.

Not whether the document is readable. Not whether it covers what the law requires.

"Does this document agree with itself?"

In one out of eight, it did not. A commitment written in one section clashed with a practice written in another. Notion's policy says it does not disclose user information to advertise third-party products, and elsewhere notes that it discloses online identifiers through advertising services in a way that may constitute a sale or sharing under California law.

The paper declines to call this lying. What it points to instead is a shape I would call the assembled document. Dozens of business units live in one file, sections are revised by different teams in different years, and a clause is bolted on each time a new regulation arrives.

No one has to be hiding anything for a document to contradict itself.

The line that stayed with me came later. Amazon, Netflix and Airbnb had no confirmed contradictions at all. But the pipeline extracted only three commitment sentences from Airbnb's entire policy. A document that promises less has less to contradict.

A checker that measures only consistency gives its best score to the document that deleted its promises.

Pebblous spends most of its diagnostic work on the gap between what data is documented to be and what it actually is. This paper measures the step before that, the gap inside the document itself.

So the question I am left holding is not about privacy policies.

"Do we have a list of every promise we have made about data, and where each one lives?"

Without the list, there is nothing to compare.
