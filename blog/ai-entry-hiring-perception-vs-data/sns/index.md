## LinkedIn (KO)

AI가 신입 채용을 줄이고 있다고 믿는 노동자는 62%였다. 그런데 자기 분야에서 그 변화를 실제로 겪었다는 사람은 31%, 정확히 그 절반이었다.

인디드 플렉스가 미국·영국 노동자 약 2,000명에게 물은 결과다. 두려움은 통계보다 앞서 달렸다. 그렇다고 위축이 허구인 것도 아니다. 스탠퍼드가 미국 최대 급여 처리 업체의 데이터를 분석하니, AI 노출도가 큰 직무에서 22~25세 초년차 고용이 13~16% 줄어 있었다.

설문은 사람들이 느끼는 바를 재고, 급여 대장은 실제로 몇 명이 뽑혔는지를 잰다. 두 계측기가 다른 값을 낼 때, 정책과 채용을 먼저 움직이는 쪽은 대개 더 크고 자극적인 느낌이다.

같은 AI가 어떤 자리에선 신입을 줄이고 어떤 자리에선 늘리는 이유는 하나다. 실행 업무를 대체하는 데 쓰였는가, 신입의 판단을 보조하는 데 쓰였는가. 감정 데이터와 실측 데이터를 갈라 읽는 일에서 그 답이 시작된다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

62% of workers believe AI is shrinking entry-level hiring. Only 31% have actually seen it happen in their own field, exactly half.

That gap comes from an Indeed Flex survey of roughly 2,000 workers across the US and UK. Fear ran ahead of the numbers. Yet the squeeze isn't a fiction: Stanford's analysis of the largest US payroll processor found that employment for 22-to-25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs fell 13-16%.

Surveys measure what people feel. Payroll measures how many were actually hired. When the two instruments disagree, policy and hiring tend to follow the louder, more alarming feeling.

Why does the same AI cut junior roles in one firm and add them in another? It comes down to one split: is AI replacing the execution work, or assisting the junior's judgment? Reading perception data and measured data separately is where the answer starts.

## Facebook (EN)

Picture someone in a graduation gown, sending résumés into a market that keeps repeating one line: AI is erasing your seat.

A survey stopped me the other day. Six in ten workers believe AI is shrinking entry-level hiring, yet only three in ten have actually felt that change in their own field.

Belief runs at twice the rate of experience. I sat with that gap for a moment.

And yet the fear isn't groundless. When Stanford opened up real payroll records, the seats for young workers in the most AI-exposed jobs had quietly thinned.

So a question forms. "Is the squeeze we feel the same size as the squeeze that actually happened?"

Surveys measure the mind; payroll measures the hire. When the two instruments disagree, the louder one moves us first. Yet policy and hiring have to stand on what actually happened, not on what we fear. Asking what the data truly says, rather than what it seems to say, is the habit Pebblous keeps returning to.

The same AI becomes a reason to let juniors go in one company and a reason to hire more in another. The split lies in whether AI is used instead of people or beside them. Only after separating feeling from measurement does that split come into view.
