## LinkedIn (KO)

앤트로픽이 상장 서류를 고치면서 한 문장의 시제를 바꿨다. 자사 AI의 가속을 앞으로 못 알아볼 수 있다던 대목이, 과거에도 못 알아본 적이 있다는 문장이 됐다.

11월 2일 미국 증권거래위원회에 낸 수정 상장 서류의 위험 요인 대목이다. 일주일 전 원본에 없던 문장도 그 뒤에 새로 붙었다. 미래 모델에 관한 내부 추정과 예상이, 책임 있는 스케일링 정책이 정의한 'AI에 기인한 극적으로 가속된 진전' 문턱을 임박해 충족할 수 있음을 가리킨다는 것이다. 같은 문단에 현재 모델 가운데 그 문턱을 넘었다고 지정한 것은 없다는 단서도 함께 적혔다.

보도는 대개 여기까지를 AI가 AI를 만든다는 소식으로 전한다. 공시에서 더 눈에 띄는 쪽은 그 옆 문장이다. 가속의 증거는 대체로 뒤늦게 잡히는 지표에서 나오고, 속도를 예측하거나 측정하는 능력은 제한적이라고 회사가 직접 적었다.

문턱이 느슨해서 그런 것이 아니다. 정책은 AI 전체 능력의 진전 속도가 기준선의 2배가 되는 지점에 선을 긋는데, 각주가 든 예시로는 기준선이 한 해 9배 확장일 때 그 2배가 81배를 뜻한다. 다만 그 선을 넘었는지 가리려면 AI가 없었다면 어땠을지를 함께 추정해야 하고, 일어나지 않은 쪽은 관측되지 않는다. 정책 변경 이력에 앤트로픽이 직접 적은 표현으로, 이 문턱은 운용 가능한 형태로 만들기가 어려운 것으로 드러났다.

판정이 나오는 자리도 좁다. 문턱 충족 여부는 3개월에서 6개월마다 내는 위험 보고서에 담기고, 최종 판정은 최고경영자와 책임 있는 스케일링 담당자 두 사람이 내린다. 이사회와 장기 이익 신탁은 판정에 참여하지 않고 승인 뒤에 통보받는다. 외부 검토가 의무가 되는 조건은 정해져 있지만 아직 발동된 적이 없다.

지금까지 공개된 판정은 두 번이다. 가장 최근 것은 기준 시점이 7월 15일이고, 전체 진전 속도가 아직 정책이 그은 선 아래에 있다고 결론지었다.

같은 문장이 단서를 달았다. 자료를 더 동원했는데도 이 결론은 불확실하고 시차를 타며, 아주 최근의 가속은 측정하기 어려울 것이라는 말이다. 11월 2일 공시는 그 기준 시점에서 석 달 반 뒤에 나왔고, 그 사이 구간을 다룬 공개 판정은 없다.

회사 안에서 자동화가 어디까지 왔는지는 같은 서류가 숫자로 보여 준다. 2026년 9월 자사 코드베이스 기여의 90% 이상을 클로드가 썼다. 다만 정책이 재겠다고 한 것은 코드량이 아니라 진전 속도이고, 각주는 투입이 는 것과 속도가 빨라진 것을 구별하라고 따로 짚는다.

페블러스가 이 공시를 데이터 쪽 문제로 읽는 이유가 여기 있다. 무엇이 달라졌는지는 그 변화가 지나가는 동안 무엇을 재어 두었느냐로만 뒤에 확인된다. 기준선을 언제 떴는지, 측정을 얼마나 자주 돌렸는지, 그 값을 누가 언제 승인했는지가 남아 있지 않으면 변화를 설명할 방법이 없다.

세계에서 가장 계측에 밝은 축에 드는 회사가 투자자용 문서에 자기 능력이 얼마나 빨리 오르는지 재는 능력은 제한적이라고 적었다. 그보다 계기판이 얕은 조직에서는 같은 일이 더 조용히 지나간다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Amending its IPO filing, Anthropic changed the tense of one sentence. Where it had said it may fail to notice an acceleration in its own AI, it now says it has already failed to notice one.

The sentence sits among the risk factors of the amended registration statement filed with the SEC on November 2. A second sentence, absent from the original a week earlier, now follows it. Internal estimates and expectations about future models, not current ones, indicate the company may imminently meet the threshold its Responsible Scaling Policy calls dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI. The same paragraph notes that no current model has been designated as having crossed it.

Most coverage stops there and files it under AI building AI. The line worth reading is the one beside it. Evidence about the pace of acceleration comes largely from lagging indicators, the company wrote, and its ability to forecast or measure that pace is limited.

This is not for want of a strict threshold. The policy draws its line where the rate of progress in overall AI capability reaches twice a baseline, and the footnote offers an example: if the baseline is a 9x year of effective scaling, twice that rate means 81x. Deciding whether the line has been crossed, though, requires an estimate of what would have happened without AI, and the thing that did not happen cannot be observed. In Anthropic's own words in the policy change log, the threshold has proven difficult to operationalize.

The place where the call gets made is narrow too. Whether the threshold has been met is settled in a Risk Report published every three to six months, and the final determination belongs to two people: the chief executive and the Responsible Scaling Officer. The board and the Long-Term Benefit Trust do not take part in it; they are informed once it is approved. The conditions that would make outside review mandatory are written down, and they have not yet been triggered.

Two determinations have been published. The most recent covers the company's position as of July 15 and concluded that the overall rate of progress still sits below the line.

The same sentence attaches a caveat. Even with additional sources, the conclusion is uncertain and lagging, and a very recent acceleration would be hard to measure. The November 2 filing arrived three and a half months after that coverage date, and nothing covering the interval has been published.

How far automation has come inside the company is in the same document. Claude wrote more than 90% of the contributions to Anthropic's own codebase in September 2026. What the policy set out to measure, though, is the rate of progress rather than lines of code, and the footnote makes a point of keeping added input and an increased rate apart.

This is why Pebblous reads the filing as a data problem. What changed can only be established afterward from what was recorded while the change was passing through. Without a record of when the baseline was taken, how often the measurement was run, and who signed off on it, there is nothing to account for the change with.

One of the most measurement-literate companies in the world told its investors that its ability to measure how fast its own capabilities are rising is limited. In organizations with a shallower apparatus, the same thing passes more quietly.

"When exactly did this model get good at that?"

Most people who run models have sat through that question. Everyone agrees something changed. Nobody can give the date.

On November 2, Anthropic amended its IPO filing and wrote down a version of the same admission.

A week earlier, the original read that the company may not identify a significant acceleration in its rate of development until after it has occurred. The amendment replaced that with a sentence saying it has, in the past, failed to identify one.

A hypothetical had become a memory.

The new line beside it is the one that made the news. Internal estimates about future models indicate the company may imminently meet its own policy's threshold for dramatically accelerated progress.

The sentence beside it is the heavier one. Evidence about the pace of acceleration comes largely from lagging indicators, Anthropic wrote, and its ability to forecast or measure that pace is limited.

"A dashboard that can only be read afterward."

The threshold itself is not loose. The line is drawn where the rate of progress reaches twice a baseline, and a footnote spells out that if the baseline is a ninefold year, twice that rate means eighty-one. But deciding whether the line has been crossed requires estimating what would have happened without AI, and the thing that did not happen cannot be observed. The policy says of itself that the threshold has proven difficult to operationalize.

The determination appears as rarely as twice a year, and the final call belongs to two people. The most recent one covers the position as of July 15, and it attached its own caveat: a very recent acceleration would be hard to measure. This filing arrived inside the stretch that caveat was pointing at.

What Pebblous means by AI-Ready data sits in exactly this gap. You can only say afterward what changed if something was measured before it changed. Without the date a baseline was taken, the interval the measurement ran on, and the person who approved the value, a change is not explained. It is guessed at.

"Of the models your team runs, how many could you give a date for?"

One of the most measurement-literate companies in the world told its investors that its ability to measure how fast its own capabilities are rising is limited. Where the instruments are thinner, the same thing will pass more quietly.
