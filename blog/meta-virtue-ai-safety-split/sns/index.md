## LinkedIn (KO)

메타가 영입한 Virtue AI 창업자 세 명이 넉 달 만에 떠난다. 10월 2일 세마포가 단독으로 전한 소식이다.

메타 대변인 앤디 스톤의 설명은 짧다. 이 방식이 계획대로 되지 않았다는 한 문장, 그리고 업무 방식이 서로 부딪혔다는 한마디가 전부다. 당사자들의 공개 발언은 아직 없다.

이 소식이 인사 뉴스에서 끝나지 않는 이유는 6월에 메타가 산 것이 무엇이었는지에 있다. 법인도, 지적재산도, 고객 계약도 가져가지 않고 사람만 데려갔다. 회사에 남아 있던 검사 체계는 8월 17일 보안 기업 포티넷이 통째로 사 갔다. 50개가 넘는 격리 시험 환경, 모델을 고칠 때마다 같은 검사를 되풀이하는 구조, 그리고 그 검사가 마지막에 내놓는 증거 묶음이 함께 넘어갔다.

레드티밍의 값어치는 그날의 점수가 아니라 누적된 기록에서 나온다. 어떤 판본에 어떤 공격을 걸었고, 고친 뒤에 같은 공격이 다시 통했는지가 줄줄이 남아야 다음 판본을 평가할 수 있다. 넉 달은 그 판정 기준을 사내 언어로 옮겨 적기에 짧은 시간이고, 세 사람은 한 팀으로 묶이지도 않았다. 보 리와 던 송은 냇 프리드먼에게, 산미 코예조는 FAIR를 이끄는 롭 퍼거스에게 보고했다.

페블러스가 AI-Ready Data를 이야기할 때 되풀이하는 문장이 여기에도 닿는다. 출처와 이력이 기록되지 않은 데이터 위에서는 검증도 검증이 아니다.

한 회사가 두 갈래로 갈라졌고, 안전을 사람으로 산 쪽이 먼저 흩어졌다. 기술과 기록으로 산 쪽은 지금 보안 제품 안에서 돌아가고 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Meta is parting ways with the three Virtue AI founders it brought in during June, four months after they arrived. Semafor broke the story on October 2.

Andy Stone, a Meta spokesperson, kept it to one line: the arrangement didn't work out as planned. He gave clashing work styles as the reason. None of the three has spoken publicly.

What keeps this from being a staffing item is what Meta actually bought in June. No legal entity, no intellectual property, no customer contracts. Only people moved. The testing system left behind went to Fortinet on August 17, along with more than 50 sandboxed environments for attacking autonomous agents, a battery that reruns at every model update, and audit-ready evidence rather than a score at the end of it.

Red-teaming earns its value in the accumulated record, not in a single day's result. Which build was attacked, with what, and whether the same attack still worked after the fix. Four months is short for rewriting those criteria in a new employer's language, and the three were never grouped into one team. Li and Song reported to Nat Friedman inside Superintelligence Labs; Koyejo reported to Rob Fergus at FAIR.

The line Pebblous keeps returning to in its work on AI-Ready Data applies here as well. Where provenance has not been recorded, verification is not verification.

One company split in two directions. The side bought as people scattered first. The side bought as technology and records is running inside a product.

## Facebook (EN)

On September 17, Bo Li's own university posted the news that she was going to Meta.

It ran on the Siebel School's news page at Illinois, a short item, closer to congratulation than to announcement.

The report that she and her co-founders were leaving came on October 2. Two weeks.

I read it first as a story about people changing jobs. Then I looked again at the shape of the June deal, and the reading shifted.

Meta did not buy the company. It did not take the intellectual property, or the contracts with customers. Only people moved.

What stayed behind was sold to the security vendor Fortinet in August: the sandboxed environments where a model is attacked on purpose to find its weak points, and the records that testing leaves behind.

So one company went in two directions.

Safety bought as people, and safety bought as records.

Four months later, it is the people side that scattered.

Testing for weak points is not something you finish once. Which build was attacked, with what, and whether the same attack still worked after the fix. Without that trail, there is no way to say the next build is better.

The sense of what counts as a pass and what counts as a failure settles in people before it settles in documents. Moving it into an organization's procedures and data takes time.

Four months is short for that move.

"What was tested inside Meta during those four months, and what did the tests catch?"

From outside, at least, there is no way to find out.

The line Pebblous keeps returning to in its work on AI-Ready Data sits in the same place: where provenance has not been recorded, verification is not verification.

Safety evaluation records, attack histories and pass criteria belong closer to managed data than to documents. That is how I have come to see it.

How many of the model evaluations your team ran last quarter left results you can set beside the next build?

Counting them may make these four months read less like somebody else's problem.
