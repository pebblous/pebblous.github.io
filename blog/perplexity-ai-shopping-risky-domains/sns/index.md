## LinkedIn (EN)

A risky web address turned up in 15% of Perplexity's shopping answers, against 8% for ChatGPT and 4% for Gemini, according to a study by the brand-protection firm Podqi.

Working with Gauge, an AI search analytics firm, Podqi wrote five shopping questions, put them to logged-out versions of the three chatbots across 151 consumer brands, and collected 6,795 answers. Reviewers opened every site that came up, classified 4,437 domains, and flagged 718 as risky.

The split inside that 718 is the more interesting number. Stores impersonating a brand or selling counterfeits accounted for 351. The larger group, 367, sold nothing at all: addresses that had already closed. Lapsed registrations go back on sale, so an address a chatbot keeps naming is worth buying. The report's own phrasing is that today's dead domains are tomorrow's fakes.

Three things qualify the figures. Podqi counted an address as exposure even when the answer warned against it. The five questions were written for the study rather than drawn from user logs. And the firm sells the service that finds impersonating sites. Against that, the report also published a trend that does its business no favours: risk exposure fell from 12% to 7% between the two September rounds.

Of the times a risky address appeared, 57% were recommendations and 9% carried a warning. Pebblous assigns numbers to AI training data with DataClinic. Whenever a figure goes into a diagnosis, the line that has to sit beside it is what the count treated as one item.

## Facebook (EN)

There is a kind of link that opens to nothing. You assume you mistyped something and move on.

Podqi, a brand-protection firm, went through 6,795 answers collected from three chatbots asked where to buy particular brands.

Of the 718 addresses it flagged as risky, 367 were links of exactly that kind. The stores impersonating a brand or selling counterfeits came to 351, so the ones selling nothing at all were the larger group.

The money disappears at the fake store. The 367 are the harder ones to think about.

Those addresses were once perfectly alive. A regional storefront, an old official shop, written into the articles and links of that period. The model learned those records. The one thing never written down anywhere was the day the brand took the site down.

A domain goes back into circulation once its registration lapses. The string stays the same. The person standing at the end of it does not.

The report puts it plainly: today's dead domains are tomorrow's fakes.

DataClinic is how Pebblous arrives at a number for AI training data. What the material holds can be opened and checked. Whether the outside addresses written into it still belong to the same owner is not held there at all.

"The address we shut down a few years back: who is answering at it now?"

A name belongs to you while you are using it. After you stop, the thing still calling that name is no longer you.
