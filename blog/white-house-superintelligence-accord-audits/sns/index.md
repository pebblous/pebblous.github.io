AI 안전을 감독하겠다는 약속이 네 겹으로 쌓였는데, 그중 회사 바깥에 있어야 할 한 겹을 회사가 직접 고른다.

2026년 9월 29일 백악관에서 구글·앤트로픽·메타·오픈AI·xAI·엔비디아 최고경영진이 '초지능에 관한 백악관 협약'에 서명했다. 사내 통제를 두고, 그 통제가 작동하는지 볼 내부 팀을 두고, 바깥에서 독립 감사인을 부르고, 이사회 독립위원회가 그 보고서를 검토하는 순서다. 안쪽에서 바깥쪽으로 원이 하나씩 커지는 이 모양은 상장사의 회계 감사에서 빌려 왔다.

빌려 오지 않은 것은 공시 규범이다. 감사인을 공표할 의무가 없고, 감사 결과를 공개할 의무도 없고, 어겼을 때 따르는 제재나 이행 기한도 문서에 적혀 있지 않다. 내부 검토부터 이사회 승인까지 전 과정이 회사 밖으로 한 줄도 나오지 않은 채 끝날 수 있다.

가장 크게 비어 있는 칸은 따로 있다. 감사인이 모델 가중치를 직접 받아 보는지가 협약에 없다. 배포 환경에서 모델을 다뤄 보는지, 아니면 회사가 작성한 요약 보고서만 받는지도 마찬가지다. 세 경우는 이름만 같은 감사이지 확인할 수 있는 범위가 전혀 다르다.

감사의 성패를 가르는 쪽은 감독의 층수가 아니라 감사인이 무엇을 열람하느냐다. 그리고 그 열람 범위는 감사 시점이 아니라, 학습 데이터의 출처와 평가 로그를 남기기로 한 시점에 이미 정해진다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Six of the largest AI companies have promised four layers of safety oversight. One of those layers is an outside auditor the company itself gets to hire.

Chief executives from Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI and Nvidia signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence on September 29. Internal controls, an internal team checking that those controls work, an independent external auditor, and an independent board committee reading the reports. The shape is borrowed from the way a listed company handles its books.

What was not borrowed is the disclosure regime. Nothing in the accord requires a company to name its auditor, publish the findings, meet a deadline, or absorb any penalty for walking away. The whole sequence, from internal review to board sign-off, can close without a single line leaving the building.

The largest blank is elsewhere. The text never settles whether the auditor receives model weights, probes the system under deployment conditions, or reads a summary the company wrote for the occasion. Those are three different jobs wearing the same word.

What decides an audit is not how many layers sit above it, but what the auditor is allowed to open. And that gets settled long before anyone is hired, at the point where training data provenance and evaluation logs are either recorded or not.

## Facebook (EN)

An auditor walks in and sits down. The folder waiting on the desk was put together by the company that called them in.

The same company chose the auditor.

On September 29, 2026, chief executives of six companies signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence. The promise was to watch their models through four layers.

Controls inside the company. A team checking that those controls run. An independent auditor called in from outside. A committee of the board reading the reports that come back.

The third layer, the one that comes from outside, is chosen by the company it will be examining.

No penalty, no duty to name the auditor, no date by which any of it has to exist. The whole sequence, from internal review to board sign-off, can finish without a line of it leaving the building.

The blank that weighed on me sits somewhere else.

Nothing in the accord settles what the auditor gets to open.

There is an audit where the weights arrive and the auditor runs the model in their own environment. There is an audit that reaches the deployed service and probes it under real conditions. And there is an audit that reads a summary the company assembled for the occasion. In the last one, what gets verified is not the model. It is the company's paperwork.

The accord fixed who does the auditing. What it left unfixed is the material to be audited.

Widening the auditor's rights does not settle it either. Grant unrestricted access and the audit still fails if nothing was kept. Training data whose provenance was never written down at collection does not come back six months later, and neither do the evaluation logs nobody saved at the time.

The enforcement decree of Korea's AI Framework Act puts a number on this. A provider of high-impact AI has to keep documentary evidence of the measures it took for five years. That clause is not about how many layers of watchers to stack. It is about what to keep, and for how long.

Pebblous works on data quality. What separates data you can diagnose from data you cannot is rarely the values themselves. It is whether the line recording where those values came from is still there.

"Suppose the question came from outside the building: where did the training data behind the model we run today come from, and how fast could we show it?"

What the auditor sees when they walk in was settled long before anyone opened the door.
