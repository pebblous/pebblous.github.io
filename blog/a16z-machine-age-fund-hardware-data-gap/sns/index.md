## LinkedIn (KO)

소프트웨어가 세상을 먹어 치운다고 쓴 회사가, 이번에는 하드웨어만 담는 펀드를 따로 만들었다.

a16z가 8월 28일 공개한 머신 에이지 펀드다. 규모는 11억 달러이고, 칩과 메모리와 네트워킹에서 시작해 데이터센터와 로봇, 가정용 AI 기기까지가 투자 대상이다. 회사가 스스로 붙인 표현은 하드웨어를 공식 영역으로 올린다는 것이다.

근거로 든 숫자는 전력이다. 랙 한 대가 지금 100~250킬로와트를 쓰고 있고, 3년 안에 1메가와트에 이른다는 것이 회사의 전망이다. 반면 하드웨어 공급은 잘해야 연 20~30% 성장에 맞춰진 산업이다. 수요는 세 자릿수 성장을 요구하는데 공급은 그 속도로 늘지 않는다는 진단이다.

그런데 발표문 본문에서 데이터라는 단어는 네 번 나오고, 네 번 다 데이터센터를 가리킨다. 로봇과 엣지 기기는 AI가 바깥 세계에 닿게 해 주는 장치로만 서술되고, 그 기기들이 움직이는 내내 쌓을 기록에 대해서는 한 문장도 없다. 자산으로서의 데이터센터는 있고, 기록으로서의 데이터는 없다.

물리 계층에 들어간 돈은 로보틱스만 세어도 올해 6월 하순까지 188억 달러다. 그 계층의 행동을 담은 공개 데이터셋은 아직 수백 시간으로 센다. 기록이 학습에 쓸 수 있는 형태인지, 어디까지가 자사 소유인지, 실패한 시도가 지워지지 않고 남는지. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data 작업에서 되풀이해 마주치는 질문이 그 세 가지다. 하드웨어에는 값이 매겨지고 있고, 그 하드웨어가 내놓을 기록에는 아직 값이 매겨지지 않았다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

The firm that declared software was eating the world has raised a fund that buys hardware and nothing else.

Andreessen Horowitz closed the $1.1 billion Machine Age Fund on August 28. It covers compute infrastructure such as chips, memory, and networking, and it extends to the finished systems that run AI: data centers, robotics, and home appliances. The firm's own phrase is that hardware is now an official area of investment.

The number it leads with is power. A rack now draws 100 to 250 kilowatts, and a16z expects it to reach a megawatt within three years. Hardware supply, meanwhile, is an industry built to grow 20% to 30% a year at best, while demand asks for triple digits.

Yet the word data appears four times in the announcement, and all four times it means data center. Robots and edge devices are described only as what AI needs in order to reach the world outside. Not one sentence covers the record those machines will pile up while they work. The data center is an asset in this document. The data is not.

Robotics startups alone took in $18.8 billion through late June this year. The public datasets that capture how those machines actually move are still counted in hundreds of hours. Whether a record is in a shape you can train on, how much of it you own, and whether failed attempts survive instead of being wiped are the three questions Pebblous keeps running into in its AI-Ready Data work. Hardware is being priced. The record it will produce is not.

## Facebook (EN)

I read the announcement and ended up counting a word.

It was a16z saying it had raised $1.1 billion to spend on hardware and nothing else. Chips and memory, then data centers, then robots and home AI appliances.

The word "data" showed up four times.

All four times it meant data center. Buildings, power, real estate.

I am not calling that a flaw. The fund said it buys chips and power and machines, not datasets, so the post simply drew its own boundary accurately. But something sits outside that boundary. Leave one robot on a site for a day and joint angles, gripper pressure, camera frames, and every failed attempt just accumulate. Nobody offered to buy that record, and it appears anyway. I have started thinking of it as the data that never became the subject of the sentence.

"Who owns that record, and in what shape does it survive?"

The only material we have for guessing at an answer is the size of the public datasets. One widely used robot manipulation set represents 350 hours, gathered by 50 collectors across three continents over twelve months. Set that next to language models raised on an internet-scale corpus, and the ground the robots stand on is still thin.

Whether a record is in a shape you can train on, how much of it the customer owns, whether failed attempts survive instead of being wiped. Those three are exactly what Pebblous keeps running into when it talks about AI-Ready Data.

Capital is counted in billions. The record its machines will leave is still counted in hours.

That the announcement has not opened the question yet does not mean the answer is settled. It means the space where the answer goes is still empty.
