## LinkedIn (EN)

Scraping has quietly given way to a settlement dashboard. When Microsoft launched its Publisher Content Marketplace this February, the infrastructure to count how much AI uses your content and pay for it stopped being theory.

But that infrastructure has paid only a handful. News Corp reportedly signed with OpenAI for $250M over five years, and names like Reddit and Wiley collect disclosed sums. The smallest disclosed deal still sits around $10M, and below that the long tail converges to zero, left as free training data.

What sets the price is not quality but leverage. An archive like the WSJ's or the AP's cannot be reconstructed elsewhere, so it can name a number; content that exists in a million near-identical copies cannot.

As AI summaries replace search, large publishers lost about 22% of their traffic while small ones lost about 60%. The market excludes most decisively the very publishers it has already hurt most.

The value of AI-ready data comes not from quality but from irreplaceability. This market makes that point once more.

## Facebook (EN)

I keep coming back to a simple gap while watching the content licensing market this year: writing something good and getting paid for it turn out to be two different things.

This February, Microsoft opened its Publisher Content Marketplace.

The idea that AI counts how much of your work it uses, and pays for it, is now a visible thing on a dashboard.

And yet only a handful of names actually made it onto that ledger. News Corp reportedly signed with OpenAI for $250M over five years, and the smallest disclosed deal still hovers near $10M. Everything below that keeps flowing into training for free, cited in answers but never settled.

"So what decides the price?" I sat with that for a while, and the answer was never quality. It was whether the work can be replaced, and therefore whether its owner has any leverage to name a number. As AI summaries took over search, large publishers lost about 22% of their traffic while small ones lost 60%, and the market pushes out most firmly the very people it hurt most.

The pipe is laid. But getting your content onto that pipe turned out to be a completely different story from the pipe existing at all.

A place where the value of data is filtered by the leverage of whoever holds it, not by its quality. It's the same landscape Pebblous has long faced in its work on AI-Ready Data.
