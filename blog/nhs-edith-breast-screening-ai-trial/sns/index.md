70만 명을 상대로 'AI가 전문의 한 명을 대신해도 되는가'를 묻는 시험이 시작됐다. 그런데 이 시험에는 AI가 혼자 사진을 끝까지 읽는 갈래가 하나도 없다.

영국 NHS의 유방암 검진 임상시험 EDITH다. 11월 23일 케임브리지셔에서 첫 참가자가 등록했고, 50~71세 약 70만 명을 전국 30개 검진 기관에서 모은다.

연구비는 국립보건연구원이 대는 1천만 파운드다. NHS 잉글랜드는 여기에 더해 약 6백만 파운드로 검진 영상 연구 플랫폼 AIR-SP를 짓는다.

지금 영국에서는 사진 한 장이 전문의 두 명에게 따로 가고, 두 사람은 서로의 판단을 모른 채 읽는다. EDITH는 참가자를 세 갈래로 무작위 배정해 그 구조를 흔들어 본다. 지금과 같은 2인 판독, 전문의가 먼저 읽고 AI가 뒤에서 보는 갈래, AI가 먼저 가려낸 뒤 전문의가 읽는 갈래. 뒤의 둘은 'AI 보조'라는 같은 이름으로 묶이지만 사람이 보는 순서가 달라 같은 조건이 아니다. 도구도 하나로 고정하지 않았다. 사전 검증을 거친 네 개 시스템이 들어가고 배정은 기관 단위로 무작위다.

앞선 근거가 없지는 않다. 스웨덴 MASAI 임상시험은 10만여 명 기준으로 AI 보조 판독 쪽 민감도가 더 높았다고 보고했다. 다만 올해 6월 란셋 서신은 그것이 중간 지표이며 과잉진단과 사망률 자료가 빠져 있다고 지적했고, 영국 국가검진위원회는 지금도 AI를 검진 프로그램에 권고하지 않는다. EDITH가 주 결과지표를 암 발견율과 재검률 두 개로 세우고 검진 뒤 4년을 더 추적하기로 한 것은 그 빈칸을 겨냥한 값으로 읽힌다.

가져올 것은 모델이 아니라 기록이다. 회사가 "AI를 도입했다"고 적을 때 그 한 줄은 사람이 먼저 보고 기계가 확인한 배치와, 기계가 먼저 거르고 사람이 확인한 배치를 똑같이 덮는다. 페블러스는 DataClinic으로 AI 학습 데이터의 품질을 정량 진단한다. 어느 건을 어느 쪽이 먼저 봤는지는 판정이 필요해진 시점에 소급해 복원되지 않는다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Britain has started recruiting for a trial that asks whether AI can take one of the two radiologist seats in national breast screening. No arm of the trial lets a machine finish reading an image on its own.

The trial is EDITH, and the first participants signed up in Cambridgeshire on 23 November. It wants about 700,000 women aged 50 to 71, drawn from 30 screening centres.

Funding is £10 million from the NIHR. NHS England is spending roughly £6 million more on AIR-SP, a platform that collects screening images and reading outcomes for research.

English screening currently sends one mammogram to two radiologists, each reading without knowing what the other decided. EDITH randomises participants across three arms: that standard double read, a radiologist reading first with the machine checking behind them, and the machine sorting first with the radiologist reading the flagged image. The last two share one label, AI-supported, yet the human looks at a different moment in each, so they are not the same condition. The tools are not fixed either. Four pre-validated systems take part, and which centre gets which one is drawn at random.

Earlier evidence exists and it stops short. Sweden's MASAI trial reported higher sensitivity for AI-supported reading across more than a hundred thousand women, but a letter in The Lancet this June called those measures intermediate, with overdiagnosis and mortality figures missing. The UK National Screening Committee still declines to recommend AI. EDITH answers that by naming two primary outcomes rather than one, cancer detection rate alongside recall rate, and by tracking participants for four years after screening via cancer registry linkage.

What transfers here is the bookkeeping, not the model. When a company records that it adopted AI, that line covers both arrangements equally: the human looking first, and the machine looking first. ## Facebook (EN)

Miss one mammogram and, for the woman it belongs to, three years open up before the next invitation arrives.

That is why England sends a single image to two radiologists. Each one reads without knowing what the other concluded, and when they disagree the case goes to arbitration.

A trial asking whether AI can take one of those two seats enrolled its first participants in Cambridgeshire on 23 November.

It is called EDITH, and it wants about 700,000 women aged 50 to 71.

The scale was not the part that held me.

Participants are split three ways, and two of those three ways carry the same label: AI-supported.

In one, the radiologist reads first and the machine looks at the same image afterwards.

In the other, the machine sorts the image first and the radiologist reads it with the marks already on it.

Put the same model and the same person into both and there is no promise the two arms score alike. What separates them is not accuracy. It is the order. England decided to pull those two orders apart and measure each on its own.

Whichever arm a woman lands in, her image passes under a human eye at least once. The trial asks whether replacement would be safe without ever carrying one out while it runs.

When a company writes down that it adopted AI, that one line covers both arrangements without distinguishing them.

"Last month, on that case, which side looked first?"

Working on data diagnosis, I stop at this question often. Volume and turnaround get counted automatically. Whether a person or a model touched the case first is usually written nowhere at all. That is the spot Pebblous means when it talks about AI-Ready Data.

Few organisations can run a trial of 700,000 people. But the column where the order goes is only open on the day the decision is made.
