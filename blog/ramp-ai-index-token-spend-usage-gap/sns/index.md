## LinkedIn (KO)

Ramp AI Index의 토큰 집계에서 10월 23일로 끝나는 주의 기업 AI 토큰 사용량은 네 주 전 같은 주보다 37.1% 늘었고, 같은 비교에서 토큰 지출은 8.9% 늘었다.

Ramp는 법인카드와 지출관리 소프트웨어를 파는 회사이고, 이 지수는 자사 플랫폼에 올라온 미국 기업 8만 5,000곳 이상의 결제에서 AI 결제만 추려 매달 낸다. 테크크런치는 11월 6일 이 집계를 앤트로픽의 기업공개 준비와 나란히 놓고 보도했고, AI 지출이 멈춰 섰다는 제목이 거기서 나왔다. 근거가 된 대목은 7월 한 달 42% 늘던 토큰 지출이 8월과 9월에는 두 달을 합쳐 1.5% 느는 데 그친 구간이다.

두 증가율의 간격은 토큰 한 개당 값이 네 주 만에 약 21% 내렸다는 뜻이기도 하다. 오픈AI는 9월에 두 모델의 API 가격을 절반으로 내렸고, 앤트로픽도 가격을 깎았다. Ramp의 수석 이코노미스트 아라 카라지안은 AI를 오래 써 본 회사일수록 긴 작업에 비싼 모델이 붙지 않도록 기본값을 걸고 라우터로 작업 일부를 더 싼 경량 모델에 넘긴다고 설명했다.

인용하기 전에 걸러야 할 조건은 셋이다. 1.5%는 두 달 합계이고 42%는 한 달 치라 나란히 둘 수 없다. 사용량과 단가가 들어간 수치는 Ramp 고객 전부가 아니라 자사 토큰 지출관리 상품을 쓰는 기업만 센다고 회사가 밝혀 두었다.

셋째는 쏠림이다. 오픈AI와 앤트로픽에 나가는 돈의 80%는 기업 1%에서 나와, 수백 곳이 지갑을 닫으면 지표 전체가 함께 멈춘다.

상위 1%를 보면 둔화이고 중앙값 기업을 보면 확산이다. 어느 쪽을 말할지는 데이터가 아니라 분모가 정한다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

In the token tally inside the Ramp AI Index, corporate AI token usage in the week ending October 23 ran 37.1% above the same week four weeks earlier, while token spend over the same comparison ran 8.9% above it.

Ramp sells corporate cards and spend management software, and the index pulls the AI-related payments out of transaction data at more than 85,000 American firms on its platform. TechCrunch placed the tally next to Anthropic's preparations for an initial public offering on November 6, and the stalling headlines followed. The passage behind them: token spend grew 42% during July alone, then 1.5% across August and September together.

Divide the two October growth rates and the average price of a token comes out about 21% lower than four weeks earlier. OpenAI halved API prices on two models in September and Anthropic cut its own. Ara Kharazian, Ramp's head economist, described what buyers learned to do: set defaults that keep expensive models off long-running tasks, cap employee budgets, and send part of the load to cheaper light models through routers. "You can use more AI, but use it for less," he said. "That's great for the customers…but it's not necessarily great for the model companies."

Three conditions are worth clearing before quoting any of it. The 1.5% covers two months and the 42% covers one, so they cannot sit side by side. The usage and price figures sample only firms running Ramp's Token Spend Management product, not its whole customer base.

The third is concentration. Of everything that reaches OpenAI and Anthropic, 80% comes from 1% of firms, so a few hundred companies can move the entire index.

Look at the top 1% and this is a slowdown. Look at the median firm paying for AI, whose spend per employee rose from $11.38 in May to $15.70 in September, and it is a spread. Which one you get to say is settled by the denominator rather than by the data.

The figure 1.5% turns up twice in one report, and in the two places it means opposite things.

In the first, corporate AI token spend grew 1.5% across August and September together, two months of almost nothing after July alone grew 42%. In the second, spending in early October stood 1.5% above the previous August peak, a fresh record. One 1.5% says growth almost stopped. The other says it has never been higher.

Lift the number out of its sentence and either article can be written.

Ramp hid nothing. Each of the two sits inside a sentence that carries its own condition. The Ramp AI Index is a monthly tally drawn from the card payments of more than 85,000 American companies, and what falls away usually falls away at the point of quoting.

What took longer to put down was the pair of figures beside it. In the same report's token tally, usage in the week ending October 23 ran 37.1% above four weeks earlier, and on the same comparison token spend ran 8.9% above it. The average price of a token fell about 21% over those four weeks. Those token figures cover only the firms running Ramp's Token Spend Management product, not its whole customer base.

A bill that shrank because prices fell and a bill that shrank because the work stopped look the same in the books. What tells them apart sits in usage, and usage leaves no mark on a corporate card.
