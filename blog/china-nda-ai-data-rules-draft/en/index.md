# China

_The 12-measure draft lets AI firms train on lawfully obtained and value-adding processed data, and urges them to pay for it_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> China's government has put in writing which data AI models may be trained on and how companies should pay for it. The General Affairs Department of China's National Data Administration (NDA, 国家数据局综合司) published a public-comment draft of its "Opinions on Improving Data Rules to Empower AI Innovation and Development" (《关于完善数据规则 赋能人工智能创新发展的若干意见》) and is taking comments until December 6. The draft has 12 measures in three parts.

> The draft allows AI training and inference on lawfully obtained data and on derived data (衍生数据): data that has been processed with expert knowledge so that its content, form or structure changes substantively and its value rises clearly. It encourages AI companies to pay for high-quality data through purchases, data-for-data swaps and model-for-data exchanges. It also recommends keeping records of where data came from and what permissions cover it, calls for a data-quality testing and certification system, and targets data poisoning, tampering and fabrication.

> The detailed rules for deciding what counts as derived data are still to come; the draft lists them as a topic for further study. For companies that train on outside data, a record of what data they received, from whom and under what permission becomes the basis for deciding liability after the fact.

## China's National Data Administration Publishes a 12-Measure Draft on AI Data

The NDA's General Affairs Department released the draft in a notice dated November 26. Comments go by email to zhidujianshechu@nda.gov.cn until December 6. The full notice ran on November 27 on a government account hosted by The Paper (澎湃新闻), a Chinese news outlet. The 21st Century Business Herald (21世纪经济报道) and the legal news service MLex also reported on the draft.

The 12 measures fall into three parts. Part 1 sets which data may be used for training, Part 2 covers the rights and payment between the parties that exchange data, and Part 3 covers liability and security after a transaction.

| Part | Measures | Main content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Support AI training and inference on lawfully obtained data | 1–4 | Applying the data property rights system; using data collected in the course of business; using derived data; using data obtained by contract |
| Protect the rights of all parties equally under the law | 5–7 | Limits on what entrusted processors may do; paying for high-quality data; responsibility for managing risks in AI output |
| Establish liability in data circulation and improve security management | 8–12 | Quality testing and certification; liability rules based on registration credentials; dispute resolution and source records; using AI to fix data management problems; data security |

Source: summarized and translated by Pebblous from the NDA General Affairs Department notice (reprinted by The Paper). Part names follow the original subheadings.

This is a draft for public comment, not final policy. The notice gives no date for a final version.

## The NDA Draft Accepts Derived Data That Changes Substantively and Gains Value

Part 1 widens what companies can do with data they already hold. Measure 2 says a company has the right to obtain, copy or transfer data generated on smart devices or cloud services and collected by the provider with authorization. The company can use that data itself or hand it to other companies for AI training and inference.

Measure 3 covers derived data. The draft defines it as "data formed by substantively changing the content, form, structure, etc. of data and clearly raising its value, through processing using expert knowledge, modeling analysis, extraction of key information, etc." Plain copies, or repackaging that only changes the format, fall outside this definition. Derived data that meets the conditions may be used directly or provided to others for AI training and inference.

The draft sets no threshold for what counts as a "substantive change" or how much value must rise to be "clear." Measure 3 says only that the authorities will "study and refine guidelines on rules for recognizing derived data" (研究细化衍生数据认定规则指引). Who does the recognizing is not decided either.

Measure 4 covers data obtained by contract. It supports buying registered data for AI use and promotes standard data circulation contracts. If a contract does not specify how the data may be used, the company may use it directly or provide it to others for training and inference, as long as it breaks no law and infringes no one else's rights.

## Purchases, Data Swaps and Model-for-Data Deals: How the NDA Wants Data Paid For

Measure 6 supports AI companies in setting up data departments and raising their spending on data. As ways to pay, it lists "lawful collection, negotiated purchase, data-for-data swaps (以数换数), model-for-data exchanges (模型换数据), etc." A data-for-data swap trades one dataset for another. A model-for-data exchange provides a model in return for data.

The same measure turns to creators. It encourages AI companies to strengthen mechanisms that return benefits to creators and to explore medium- and long-term benefit-sharing arrangements. It does not say how much should be shared or how.

Measure 5 narrows what outsourced data processors may do. Unless a law or contract says otherwise, an entrusted processor holds no right to possess, use or commercially exploit the data it handles. It needs explicit authorization from the client before using that data for AI training or inference, or passing it on. A cloud or data-processing vendor that wants to train its own models on customer data would need the customer's explicit permission.

## Data Property Registration Credentials as Evidence of a Buyer's Care

Measure 9 deals with how to split liability when a problem surfaces after data has changed hands. The draft says it will explore treating a data property registration credential (数据产权登记凭证) as a factor in judging whether the buyer fulfilled a duty of reasonable care and whether it was subjectively at fault. A company that bought registered data could use the credential to reduce or avoid liability if the data later turns out to be flawed.

The same measure has a sentence about models already built. If a company builds a model on data it obtained and a compliance problem with that data is found later, the company may keep its rights to the model's results "where it bears the corresponding liability in accordance with the law and does not violate legal provisions." A defect in training data does not automatically cost the company its rights to the model. That condition is about bearing liability, and it is written separately from the reasonable-care test in the sentence before it.

Measure 10 calls for mediation, arbitration and notarization systems for disputes, and encourages data handlers to "record and keep data sources, permission credentials, processing steps, etc., so that sources can be verified and use can be traced." The liability test in Measure 9 depends on such records existing. A company that kept no record of sources and permissions has little to show that it took reasonable care when a dispute arises.

## Data Quality Certification and Poisoning Prevention in the NDA Draft

Measure 8 covers data quality. The draft says it will "build data-quality testing and certification systems by industry and by scenario, improve data-quality testing standards for AI applications, and support qualified institutions in testing data quality." It does not specify what will be measured or who will certify.

Measure 12 strengthens AI data security under the Data Security Law and the Personal Information Protection Law, and targets malicious acts such as data poisoning (数据投毒), data tampering, data fabrication, illegal caching and illegal cross-border transfer. Data poisoning is an attack that slips deliberately wrong samples into training data so the model misbehaves.

Measure 7 requires AI service providers to take reasonable steps against harmful output and rights infringement, and to set up channels for complaints. A provider that fails to take reasonable and necessary steps and harms the public interest or others' rights is liable under the law. The same measure says that steps taken after meeting legal requirements can count as factual grounds for reducing or avoiding liability. Measure 11 encourages using AI to fix data management problems such as inconsistent standards and scattered data.

## Why Pebblous Is Watching This Draft

In the United States, the limits on training data are being fought out in court, as in The New York Times' December 2023 copyright suit against OpenAI and Microsoft. China's draft writes down two things first in a government document: lawfully obtained data may be used for training, and data should be paid for. If the draft is finalized, the terms on which Chinese companies use training data will be set by administrative rules, contracts and registration credentials before any court rules.

In Korea, the Personal Information Protection Commission gave advance notice on November 23 of a draft enforcement decree for the AI exception in the Personal Information Protection Act. It would let companies train on original personal data if they appoint a responsible officer, keep an internal management plan, control access and destroy the data afterward (see "[Korea's PIPA AI Exception Lets You Train on Raw Data If You Can Prove It](/blog/korea-pipa-ai-exception-enforcement-decree/en/)"). The two documents cover different data, but both ask companies that train on data to keep a record of what data they used and under what permission. In October, China also set up the National Data Group, a state-owned company that supplies training data (see "[China's National Data Group Supplies the Data That AI Learns From](/report/china-national-data-group-ai-training-data-2026-10/en/)"). The National Data Group supplies data; the National Data Administration is the government agency that writes the rules.

Pebblous measures and diagnoses the quality of AI training data. The draft's industry-level quality testing and certification system in Measure 8 and its standard for recognizing derived data in Measure 3 both come down to measuring data and making a judgment. A claim that processing "clearly raised the value" of data can only be proven with measurements of quality before and after processing.
