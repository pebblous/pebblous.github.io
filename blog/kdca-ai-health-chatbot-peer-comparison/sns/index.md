## LinkedIn (KO)

질병관리청과 과기정통부가 10월 28일 'AI 대국민 건강도우미 챗봇'을 전 국민에게 열었다. 키와 몸무게, 생활습관 설문을 넣으면 성별과 연령이 비슷한 또래와 비교한 내 건강 위치가 신호등으로 돌아온다. 밑에 깔린 것은 20년간 쌓인 500만 건 이상의 국가 건강조사 자료다.

신호등 색의 뜻은 비교 대상이 누구냐에 달려 있다. 그런데 발표 어디에도 그 또래가 어느 조사의, 어느 해의, 몇 살 간격 집단인지 적혀 있지 않다.

챗봇이 올라앉은 지역사회건강조사는 전국 평균을 내려고 만든 조사가 아니다. 지역보건의료계획에 쓸 동네 단위 숫자를 내려고 시·군·구마다 약 900명씩 뽑는다. 인구 70만 도시와 인구 3만 군에서 표본 크기가 같다. 여기서 전국 또래 분포를 만들려면 지역별 몫을 인구 비례로 다시 가중하는 단계가 한 번 들어가야 하는데, 그 가중 방법은 공개되지 않았다.

문항도 해마다 같지 않다. 2010년부터는 문항마다 1년·2년·4년 주기로 나눠 묻고, 지역선택문항은 코로나 기간 네 해 동안 비어 있었다. 조사 방식도 바뀌는 중이다. 18년 동안 조사원이 집을 찾아가 1:1로 물어 쌓은 자료인데, 챗봇은 이용자가 화면에서 혼자 채운 답을 그 분포와 맞댄다. 묻는 방식이 응답 분포를 바꾸는 모드효과는 질병관리청이 지금 시범사업으로 재고 있는 질문이다.

신호등 옆에 네 줄만 더 있으면 된다. 어느 해 자료인지, 또래를 몇 살 간격으로 묶었는지, 바뀐 문항을 어떻게 이어 붙였는지, 화면에 적은 답을 대면조사 분포와 어떻게 맞댔는지. 넷 다 모델을 바꿔도 답이 달라지지 않는 항목이고, 서비스를 만든 쪽은 이미 답을 쥐고 있다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Korea's disease control agency and its science ministry opened an AI health chatbot to the entire public on October 28. Enter your height, weight and a short questionnaire on daily habits, and the service returns where you stand against peers of similar sex and age, drawn as a traffic light. Underneath it sit more than 5 million records from two decades of national health surveys.

What the color means depends on who you are being compared with. Nowhere in the announcement is it written which survey those peers come from, which years, or how many years wide the age band is.

The Korea Community Health Survey the chatbot sits on was never built to produce a national average. It samples about 900 people in each city, county and district to produce neighborhood-level figures for local health plans, which means a city of 700,000 and a county of 30,000 contribute the same number of respondents. Turning that into a national peer distribution takes a reweighting step, and the weights have not been published.

The questions are not fixed either. Since 2010 items have been asked on one-year, two-year and four-year cycles, and the regionally selected items sat empty for four years through the pandemic. The method is changing too. For 18 years an interviewer went to the door and asked in person; the chatbot now sets answers typed in alone on a screen against that distribution. Whether the mode of asking shifts the answers is precisely what KDCA is measuring in a pilot right now.

Four more lines beside the traffic light would be enough. Which years the distribution came from, how wide the age band is, how items that changed were spliced, and how screen-typed answers were matched to face-to-face responses. None of the four would change under a better model, and the people who built the service already hold the answers.

## Facebook (EN)

You type in your height and your weight, and a traffic light comes back.

It means that among people of your sex and roughly your age, this is about where you stand.

The screen belongs to the AI health chatbot that Korea's disease control agency and science ministry opened to the whole public on October 28. Behind that color sit 5 million records from twenty years of national health surveys.

Twenty years that existed only as statistical tables and microdata now come back wearing the face of "people like me." As a picture of what it looks like when public data becomes usable by AI, I find it hard to beat.

Still, once the color arrives, a question stays behind.

"Which years are these peers from?"

The announcement says only that they are of similar sex and age. Which survey, which years, and how many years wide the band is are not in it.

Fold twenty years into one pile and the forty-somethings of 2008 sit at equal weight with the forty-somethings of 2025. They are not the same people. They eat differently and they sit for different numbers of hours. A review in Population Studies this year, pooling 51 papers from the British birth cohort studies, found almost no evidence that later-born generations arrived at the same ages in better health.

An average folded that way belongs to neither year. It is a 'hypothetical peer.'

The survey underneath was never one even surface either. Since 2010 its items have turned on one-year, two-year and four-year cycles, and the regionally selected ones sat empty for four pandemic years. For 18 years an interviewer asked these questions at the door; now a user fills them in alone on a screen.

A good deal of what we do at Pebblous lives right here. Putting a number on data quality means, before any model is chosen, joining tables that differ year by year, mapping items that changed wording, and deciding what to do with the years that are missing.

The announcement passes over all of that with one line about applying AI technology. The hardest work got the shortest write-up.

I would like to see four more lines beside the traffic light. Which years, how wide the band, how changed items were spliced, how a screen-typed answer was set against a doorstep interview.

None of the four would move under a better model, and the people who built it already have the answers.

If you have tried the chatbot, I am curious whether anything beside your traffic light told you who you were being compared with.
