콜로라도가 AI 규제 시행규칙 76쪽을 채택했다. 그런데 그 규칙을 집행할 수 있는 단 한 사람은 조사조차 시작하지 못한다.

콜로라도 법무부가 11월 19일 자동화 의사결정법(SB 26-189)과 챗봇안전법의 시행규칙을 채택했다. 불리한 결정을 알리고, 추가 설명 요청에 답하고, 사람이 다시 들여다본 기록을 남기는 기한이 조문마다 날짜로 적혀 있다. 두 법의 시행일은 2027년 1월 1일이다.

집행은 그보다 먼저 멈췄다. xAI가 수정헌법 제1조 위반을 주장하며 주 법무장관을 상대로 소송을 냈고, 4월 27일 연방 치안판사가 "조사의 개시가 포함되되 거기에 그치지 않는" 집행 착수를 금지했다. 이 법에는 개인이 직접 소를 낼 길이 없어 집행 권한이 법무장관에게 전속한다. 그 자리의 착수를 막으면 집행에 남는 통로가 없다. 보도가 정지를 원고 한 곳이 아니라 적용 대상 기업 전체에 미치는 것으로 읽는 이유다.

금지가 풀리는 기준은 xAI의 가처분 신청에 법원이 판단을 내린 날로부터 14일이다. 그 신청은 11월 16일까지 접수되지 않았다. 멈춤의 끝을 정할 수 있는 쪽이 아직 아무것도 내지 않았다.

그래서 지키지 않아도 조사받지 않는 구간이 기업 앞에 열려 있다. 다만 규칙이 요구하는 것 가운데 응답 기한과 재검토 기록 보관은 그때 가서 만들 수 없는 종류다. 어떤 데이터가 어떤 결정에 쓰였고 누가 다시 들여다봤는지는 사후에 복원되지 않는다. 집행이 돌아왔을 때 그 구간을 설명할 수 있는 회사와 그렇지 못한 회사를 가르는 것은 멈춰 있던 동안 남긴 기록이다. 페블러스가 AI-Ready Data라고 할 때 붙드는 조건도 그 기록이다.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Colorado has finished writing the rules for its AI law. The one official who can enforce it is barred from opening an investigation.

The Colorado Department of Law adopted 76 pages of rules on November 19 implementing the state's automated decision-making act (SB 26-189) and its chatbot safety act. The text puts dates on when a company must disclose an adverse decision, answer a request for further explanation, and retain the record of a human review. Both statutes take effect on January 1, 2027.

Enforcement stopped long before that. xAI sued the state attorney general in April on First Amendment grounds, and on April 27 a federal magistrate judge barred him from starting enforcement, "including but not limited to the initiation of an investigation." The act carries no private right of action, so the power to enforce it sits in that single office. Block that office and the statute has no remaining path. Reporting therefore reads the freeze as covering every company the law applies to, not only the plaintiff.

The bar lifts 14 days after the court rules on xAI's preliminary injunction motion. As of November 16 that motion had not been filed. The side that could set the end date has filed nothing.

So a window is open in which the obligations exist and nothing follows from ignoring them. What that reading misses is that much of the rulebook cannot be satisfied after the fact. A response deadline cannot be met by designing the process once the request lands, and a retention duty only holds if the record existed when the review happened. Which data fed which decision, and who looked at it again, does not come back later. That is what Pebblous checks before it checks a number.

## Facebook (EN)

A rulebook that is finished, and a state that cannot use it. I had to read that twice before it settled.

On November 19 Colorado's Department of Law adopted 76 pages of rules for its AI law.

They set out what a company owes a person after an automated system decides against them. How soon the notice goes out. What has to be in it. How long the record of a human review is kept.

And since April the official who would enforce any of it has been barred from opening an investigation.

The order came out of xAI's lawsuit against the state attorney general. It lifts fourteen days after the court rules on a preliminary injunction motion that xAI has not filed.

The side that could set the end date has filed nothing.

In my own notes this stretch has a name now: the gap in the record.

"When enforcement comes back, what do you show for the months it was gone?"

Most of what the rules ask for cannot be built later. A deadline for answering cannot be met by designing the process after the request lands. A retention duty only holds if the record was there when the review happened.

Which data fed which decision, and who looked at it again, does not come back.

Korea has the same question standing in the opposite place. The AI Framework Act took effect in January, and as of mid-October the two notices that would say how high-impact operators actually comply had not been issued. Colorado wrote the how first and lost the enforcement behind it. Korea stood the law up first and left the how empty.

Before Pebblous looks at a number, it looks for where a decision came from. What went in, who checked it, when. That line only gets written at the moment the decision is made.

The companies in trouble when enforcement returns may not be the ones that broke a rule. They may be the ones that cannot say what they did while nobody was watching.
