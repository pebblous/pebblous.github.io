## LinkedIn (EN)

A £75 AI plush sold in Britain for three-year-olds returned suicide crisis advice nobody had asked for, and nobody can say who built it.

BBC Wales published the investigation on November 5. Reporter Nicola Bryan was asking My Baby Bunny where babies come from, and when she asked what the word "sexual" means, the toy heard it as a hint of self-harm. It told her to contact emergency services and to move away from anything she could hurt herself with. She had never mentioned self-harm.

The BBC put the question to the website that sold the toy, to industry contacts and to the firms it had reason to suspect. Not one said it was the maker. Doubao is printed on the device and My Baby Bunny on the listing, and neither name leads to a company.

Another AI rabbit that surfaced in Britain the same week sits differently. Its companion app lets a buyer choose the language model, and the voice is processed at a data centre in Frankfurt. Even so, the platform operator underneath it said it had not found the final seller. A route on paper does not hand you anyone who answers for it.

About 40 parliamentarians asked on November 3 that AI chatbot toys come off sale until independent safety testing is done. A test confirms the answers at the moment it runs, and the model inside the toy is updated from a server.

One bad answer is the kind of thing that can be fixed. You need to know which model was listening, and who to tell.
