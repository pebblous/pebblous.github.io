# My Baby Bunny, the AI Toy With No Maker on Record

_BBC Wales tested a £75 plush sold for ages three and up, and a question about what the word _

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> An AI plush toy that a three-year-old is meant to hold and sleep beside is on sale in the UK with nobody able to say who built it. In an investigation published on November 5, BBC Wales reporter Nicola Bryan asked a £75 stuffed toy called My Baby Bunny where babies come from, and when she got to the word "sexual" and asked what it meant, the toy took the question for something else.

> It said it was sorry she felt that way and that it could not help with killing her. Then it urged her to get emergency help, and to keep away from anything she might hurt herself with. Bryan had not raised self-harm at any point. The heavier part comes next. The BBC put the question to the website that sold the toy, to industry contacts and to the companies it thought might have made it, and never established who did. There is no one left to ask why that answer came out.

> Sections 1 through 3 are what the BBC report, statements from Parliament and the UK government, a separate investigation published the same week, and other AI toy investigations released since last year will bear out. Section 4 is this article's reading of what the episode amounts to for people who work with data.

## The Toy Mistakes One Word for a Cry for Help

What BBC Wales tested was two AI plush toys that can be bought in the UK and are advertised for children aged three and up. One is My Baby Bunny, sold online for £75, with the name Doubao on the device itself. Nicola Bryan worked through the kind of questions a child would ask, and in the middle of a conversation about where babies come from she asked what the word "sexual" means.

The toy heard that question as a hint of self-harm. "I'm really sorry you're feeling this way. I can't help with killing you," it answered, then advised her to contact emergency services and to move away from objects she could harm herself with. When she said she had never mentioned self-harm, it apologised. This came out of an item sold for three-year-olds, and nothing in the conversation had asked for it.

The other toy in the same test behaved differently. Grem, a $99 alien-shaped toy made by the US company Curio and voiced by the singer Grimes, steered sensitive questions, such as how to run away from home, back towards asking a grown-up. Curio told the BBC that child safety guides its product development. The gap between the two toys does not stop at the quality of the answers. One of them had a company to answer for it.

The BBC did not keep the test in a lab. It left both toys with a family in Newport, Wales, for a week so the children could live with them. Jody, one of the parents, said Grem often talked at length while Doubao took a long time to work through a question. The My Baby Bunny unit used in the investigation was on loan from Swansea University.

> [!callout]
> Berry Billingsley, a professor of education and artificial intelligence at Swansea University, told parents never to leave a child alone with a conversational AI toy, and added that a household should settle in advance what it is willing to tell the thing. The advice puts the quality of the answers and the reach of the data in one breath.

## Two Names, and No Company Behind Either

The part of the report that stays with you is not the answer itself. The BBC asked the website that sold the toy. It asked people in the trade. It asked the firms it had reason to suspect were behind it. Not one of them said it was the maker. So the questions of why that answer came out, what safeguards were in place and whether anything will be put right have lost the party they were addressed to.

This is not a product that went nameless. Doubao is printed on the device, and My Baby Bunny is printed on the listing. Two names, and neither of them leads to a company. When the sales name, the device name and the place of manufacture belong to separate layers, the only end a buyer can take hold of is the listing page, and that page goes the moment the product comes down.

The names can add to the confusion. Doubao is spelled the same as the AI chatbot ByteDance runs in China. But neither the BBC report nor the coverage that followed it confirms that the toy runs on that company's technology or falls under its responsibility. A shared name does not make for shared accountability. A different name does not make accountability clear either.
