# Korea

_One wrong answer in 160 questions, and the exam body found no anomaly_

## Executive Summary

> [!callout]
> Korea's top scorer on the 63rd certified tax accountant exam has admitted to photographing the question paper with a tiny camera, sending the images to a generative AI, and reading the answers off a smartphone. The admission came on November 15, on the YouTube channel of Han Won-seok's lawyer, and a written confession went to the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency the same day. A day earlier the denial still stood, in a phone interview with a newspaper.

> Two people were in the room. The Human Resources Development Service of Korea, which administers the exam, said two proctors were assigned to that room and that all four proctors across the first and second rounds reported nothing unusual. The check that runs after grading did not catch the paper either. On the first round in April, one wrong answer out of 160 questions produced 397.5 points out of 400, and that score passed the post-exam review unflagged. The criteria HRD Korea published for picking out suspect cases, when it opened a full review after the case, are a score far above the three-year average, the gap in points to the next-ranked candidate, the spread across subjects within one candidate's scores, and year-over-year movement in the pass rate. All four are values that can be computed once grading is finished.

> Sections 1 through 4 are the facts as they stand in the reporting and in the announcements from HRD Korea and the government. Section 5 is our reading.

## The top scorer sat at the front of the room both times

The candidate who went to the police is Han Won-seok, 34. The handle Han used online became public along with the rest, and the bill now being prepared in the National Assembly carries that nickname. On the first round of the 63rd certified tax accountant exam in April, 159 of 160 questions came back correct, and the second round in July produced a high enough score to place first overall. The seat was the middle of the second row from the front in April, and the middle of the first row in July. Both were close to the proctors.

The method was one piece of equipment and one channel. A tiny camera photographed the question paper, the image went to a generative AI, and the answer came back on a smartphone. Han has also admitted to using the same method on the first round of the certified public accountant exam. That one ended in a fail, as the Financial Supervisory Service confirmed.

The account HRD Korea gave after the allegations surfaced ran along two tracks. One is people. Two proctors were in that exam room, and all four proctors across the two rounds answered that nothing was out of the ordinary. The room had no CCTV. The other is data. The post-exam review, which covers the first-round grading data among other things, turned up no anomaly either, HRD Korea said.

The account from Han changed within a day. In a phone call of about 34 minutes with the Korea Economic Daily on November 14, Han dismissed the allegation of AI-assisted cheating as nonsense. The next day he appeared on his lawyer's YouTube channel and reversed himself, giving financial trouble, a failed business and the anxiety of the middle thirties as the reasons.

## The anomaly was a number on the answer sheet

397.5 is not merely a high score. The first round of the tax accountant exam converts 160 questions into 400 points, which puts each question at 2.5 points. A score of 397.5 means exactly one question wrong out of 160, and anyone holding the grading results can work that back.

After the case HRD Korea opened a full review of AI-assisted cheating across the 2026 national qualification exams. It covers 491 national technical qualifications and 37 national professional qualifications, 528 in all, taken by about 4.5 million people a year. HRD Korea named four criteria for pulling out the cases worth suspecting.

- A score far above the three-year average
- The gap in points to the next-ranked candidate
- The spread across subjects within one candidate's scores
- Year-over-year movement in the pass rate

Tips from online communities and submissions to a newly opened cheating report center feed in alongside those. Cases that come through go in three stages. Data pulls out the suspect cases, several grading specialists re-examine the first- and second-round answer sheets, and a review meeting of specialists settles the case before it goes to the police. HRD Korea said further cases had been caught beyond those already made public, and that nine of the confirmed cases have been referred for investigation.

The phrase full review is narrower than it sounds, though. It does not mean re-grading every answer sheet across 528 qualifications. It means widening to all qualifications the pool from which those four indicators pull the cases worth a closer look. The tax accountant exam is being handled separately as well, with the scores of everyone who passed in the last three to five years under re-examination. The indicators still run after the exam is over, and that is where the criticism about what they are worth comes from.

Two of the four criteria meet this answer sheet head-on. A score far above the three-year average, and the gap in points to the next-ranked candidate, overlap with a top paper that missed one question in 160 closely enough to look as though they were written for it. These criteria were published after the case broke, and the review that ran before it is on record as having found no anomaly.

## The detector looks for something this method never used

The Korea Communications Agency has put out a tender to develop a radio detector for AI smart glasses. The budget is 50 million won, on condition of a prototype within 70 days of the contract. Proposal evaluation is set for November 20 and the completion target is February 2027, so the agency has confirmed that the device cannot be used at the College Scholastic Ability Test on November 19. Reports differ on the tender date, October 26 in some and November 2 in others. Either way it comes before the confession of November 15.

The money to build the device and the money to put it in exam rooms differ by orders of magnitude. The 50 million won in the tender buys a prototype, and actual deployment takes a separate budget. A budget increase prepared by the office of Representative Kim Wi-sang of the People Power Party, ahead of review by the National Assembly's Climate, Energy, Environment and Labor Committee, would add 6.573 billion won to HRD Korea's qualification testing program. Of that, 3.6 billion won covers deploying 7,200 radio detectors. Another 2.973 billion won covers 23,559 staff dedicated to watching for cheating with smart glasses and similar devices. The figures were estimated by HRD Korea and reported to the representative's office, and the increase still has to clear the budget subcommittee and the full committee, so it is not a confirmed budget. Nothing was allocated to this line this year.

The device works in two stages. Bluetooth Low Energy signals first establish the presence of smart glasses and the manufacturer, and Wi-Fi traffic volume and transmission patterns are then analyzed for movement that looks like photographing questions and sending them out. It measures 14cm by 9.5cm, about the size of a power bank, and a proctor carries it, with a suspect signal setting off the screen, an LED and a vibration. The hard part of the development is telling smartphones, smartwatches and wireless earbuds apart from smart glasses, to keep false alarms down.

That hard part is where the device and this case come apart. The equipment Han used was a tiny camera rather than smart glasses, and the answers arrived on a smartphone. What stage one is designed to find was not in Han's hands, and the device that was sits on the side stage two has to filter out to avoid false alarms. Jang Dong-hyuk, who leads the People Power Party, also said this is not a situation that catching smart glasses alone can stop.
