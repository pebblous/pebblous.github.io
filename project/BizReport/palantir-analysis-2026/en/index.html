Executive Summary

Palantir Technologies now earns more from enterprise customers than from the U.S. government. In the third quarter of 2026, U.S. commercial revenue reached $984 million, up 148% year over year, edging past U.S. government revenue of $953 million by $31 million. For a company that started in 2003 selling to intelligence agencies, that order had never flipped before. Total quarterly revenue came in at $2.313 billion, the first quarter above $2 billion, growing 96%.

Three forces produced the crossover. AIP Bootcamps and AgentCamps compressed the distance from pilot to signed contract from quarters into weeks. The Ontology had already wired enterprise data into a shape a model can act on. And sovereign AI demand, meaning the insistence on keeping your data, your model and your execution inside your own jurisdiction, arrived at the same time across defense, manufacturing and healthcare. Government revenue also grew 96% in the quarter, so nothing cooled off on that side. Commercial simply accelerated harder.

The same release carries a counter-signal. Total contract value closed in the quarter was $2.918 billion, up only 6%. The stock slid after the print, and the valuation argument reopened, with Michael Burry's short position at its loudest edge. Pebblous reads the gap in between. An Ontology only works if the data loaded into it has already been straightened out. The Changshin partnership announced on November 6 shows what that bottleneck looks like on a Korean factory floor. This piece belongs to the series curated at the Neuro-Symbolic × Ontology hub, as the largest live proof that ontology converts into revenue.

Three figures capture the character of the quarter. A Rule of 40 score of 158%, the sum of growth and adjusted operating margin, says this growth was not bought by giving up profit.

1

The Quarter It Crossed

Palantir reports revenue in four buckets: U.S. government, U.S. commercial, international government and international commercial. Since the direct listing, the largest bucket had always been U.S. government. In the third quarter of 2026 that changed. U.S. commercial came in at $984 million against $953 million for U.S. government. The $31 million gap is barely 1.3% of quarterly revenue, but the direction is unambiguous. One quarter earlier the same two lines read $764 million and $809 million. U.S. commercial grew 29% sequentially and took the lead.

Reading the crossover as government slowing down gets the quarter wrong. U.S. government revenue also grew 96%. A software company's public-sector business doubled in a year, and the bucket next to it merely grew two and a half times. Two engines are running, and one of them accelerated harder. That is the precise sentence for this quarter.

1.1Eighty-four percent of the business sits in one country

The $1.937 billion earned in the United States is 84% of quarterly revenue. U.S. commercial customers now number 686, up 29% year over year with 33 added in the quarter. International commercial customers number 218, up 2.8%. International commercial revenue stayed below $200 million on the quarterly slides, growing around 20%. Growth today happens almost entirely inside the United States, and that concentration is both the strength and the exposure. Section 4 returns to it through the British case.

1.2Full-year guidance moved up twice in nine months

The first 2026 revenue guide, issued in February, was $7.182–7.198 billion. August raised it to roughly $8.15 billion. The third-quarter release raised it again, to $8.530–8.534 billion, which is 19% above the opening guide and implies 91% annual growth. U.S. commercial alone is now guided above $3.559 billion, implying at least 143% growth. Fourth-quarter revenue guidance is $2.649–2.653 billion.

💡 Chapter Takeaway

U.S. commercial revenue of $984 million passed U.S. government revenue of $953 million for the first time. Because government also grew 96%, the crossover is a difference in speed rather than a decline on either side. But 84% of the growth still happens inside one country.

2

Why Commercial Ran Faster

Enterprise software sales cycles are long. Gathering requirements, running a proof of concept and clearing security review before anyone signs typically takes six months to a year. The AIP Bootcamp, which Palantir has pushed since 2023, inverts the sequence. Instead of a deck, the team walks in with the customer's own data and stands up a working workflow in days. In 2026 the AgentCamp was added on top, where customers assemble agents themselves. The first engine behind the Q3 crossover is that compression.

2.1How long it took to get from pilot to contract

The expansion cases disclosed on the November 2 call put numbers on that compression. An industrial materials company started with a $3 million pilot in the second quarter and moved to $29 million in annual contract value in the third. A global consumer electronics firm went from $1 million to $3 million to $15 million. A sports apparel company went from $1.6 million in the first quarter to roughly $21 million by the third, and another apparel firm from $2.5 million to $11.5 million. On November 5, insurance distributor AmeriLife announced that Palantir-powered capabilities had gone into production at Crump Life Insurance Services following a pilot.

Alex Karp described the conversion on the call as a "first date into bed kind of thing." The blunt metaphor matters less than the line that followed it: "when it gets real, you need us." His claim is that options narrow the moment a deployment moves into real operations. He also argued that approaches amounting to reselling tokens at a markup are being rejected by the market.

2.2What the Ontology actually does

The Ontology is the layer where a company's objects and their relationships are written down in a form a model can read. Orders, machines, workers and suppliers are defined, along with how they relate and what actions can be taken on each one. If a database schema settles where things are stored, the Ontology settles what they are and what can be done with them. A language model cannot produce a useful answer over enterprise data without that layer underneath it.

Chief Technology Officer Shyam Sankar put it on the call as "the atomic unit of the enterprise is the workflow." The unit of account is the process, not the model. Seen that way, what Palantir sells is not intelligence but the wiring intelligence plugs into. Net dollar retention of 165% and average trailing-twelve-month revenue of $146 million across the top 20 customers, up 76% year over year, are what that wiring produces once it is in place.

2.3The demand that goes by the name sovereign AI

The third engine is sovereignty, the requirement that data, model and execution all stay within an organization's own control. Karp wrote in the release that "the Sovereign AI moment is purpose-built for Palantir." The sovereign AI customer list the company published on October 21 includes L3Harris, McCarthy Building Companies, NVIDIA, Ondas, Regna AI and Tampa General Hospital. On October 1, a partnership with Armada validated running AIP, the Ontology, Foundry and Apollo on modular data centers built in the United States and allied countries.

Sovereignty turns into money at the cost line. L3Harris reported a 95% cost reduction by fine-tuning open-source models on its own data. McCarthy post-trained NVIDIA's Nemotron and cut 30,000 hours a year from contract drafting. Tampa General reported a 7% performance gain and a 22-fold reduction in latency through the same route. On the defense side, a 30-billion-parameter post-trained model outperformed a three-trillion-parameter general model during a U.S. Indo-Pacific Command exercise under power constraints. Maven now has more than 100,000 users across the services.

💡 Chapter Takeaway

Bootcamps and AgentCamps shortened the path from pilot to contract, the Ontology laid the wiring a model works through, and sovereign AI demand raised what that wiring is worth. The three do not operate separately; they arrive as one package.

The Other Numbers in the Same Release

The stock fell on the day of the print. Revenue, profit and guidance all beat, and it fell anyway. The reason sits in the same press release. Total contract value closed during the quarter was $2.918 billion, up 6% from a year earlier. In a quarter when revenue grew 96%, new signings grew 6%.

3.1Four metrics that should not be read the same way

Deal counts rose, customer counts rose, and spending by existing customers rose. Total signed value stayed flat. When three indicators point one way and a fourth points the other, look at the denominator. Closed TCV was $4.262 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 138%, and $3.370 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 49%. Within that second quarter, U.S. commercial TCV of $2.132 billion was an all-time record, up 153%. The Q3 figure landed against a comparison base that had just been raised sharply.

Another angle: dividing closed TCV by quarterly revenue gives 1.26 times in the third quarter against 1.74 times in the second. The cushion of new contracts covering the revenue being consumed got thinner. Remaining deal value of $13.4 billion is still thick, and the U.S. commercial portion of $6.517 billion grew 79%, so revenue will not turn in the near term. But if that ratio stays low for another two or three quarters, the 2027 growth picture looks different from today's. TechCrunch flagged exactly this number on November 3 as the "foreboding signal" hiding inside a skyrocketing quarter.

3.2What a 113x price tag assumes

The November 16 close was $160.39, for a market capitalization of $386.5 billion. That is about 25% below the all-time high of $212.94 reached on October 12. The trailing price-to-earnings ratio is 113 and the forward ratio on this year's earnings is 70. Dividing market cap by full-year revenue guidance of $8.53 billion gives roughly 45 times forward revenue. The average target price among 33 analysts is about $223, and Barclays raised its target from $265 to $273 on November 4.

On the other side stands Michael Burry. He has maintained a short position and disclosed buying puts struck at $100 expiring in March 2027, arguing that deferred revenue at roughly 32% of revenue makes the business resemble a consulting firm more than a subscription software company. Karp answered that the campaign is market manipulation. The sell side is split as well: Jefferies called the valuation excessive, RBC noted deepening concentration in the growth model, and Deutsche Bank said the current price is hard to make sense of. The 2.09% decline on November 13 came out of a broad technology selloff rather than anything company-specific.

3.3Forward deployed engineers as acquisition cost, not cost of sales

Read Palantir's income statement with a conventional software lens and the people line looks too heavy. Forward deployed engineers embed with the customer, connect the data and build the workflows. Reclassify that spending as customer acquisition cost rather than cost of service and the picture changes. Once an engineer has laid down an Ontology, subsequent expansion happens without adding headcount. Net dollar retention of 165% and a 76% rise in average revenue across the top 20 customers are the numbers that arrive afterward.

The balance sheet is what made nearly two decades of that patience possible. The quarter ended with $10.4 billion in cash and treasuries and no debt. A 49% net margin, a 62% adjusted operating margin and a 53% adjusted free cash flow margin say the model no longer burns anything to operate. Set against the criticism that greeted the 2020 losses, the thing that needed patience was not the company so much as the framework being used to score it.

💡 Chapter Takeaway

Revenue up 96% and closed contract value up 6% are figures from the same quarter. A thick backlog keeps growth intact for now, but the drop in new signings from 1.74 times quarterly revenue to 1.26 is the first thing anyone underwriting 2027 should check.

The Moat and Its Shadow

Commercial pulling ahead does not demote the government business. At $953 million it is still 41% of the quarter, and 96% growth would be fast for any enterprise software line outside of Palantir's own commercial side. What matters more is that this segment underwrites the other one. A track record inside classified environments does the security argument for a private customer before the first meeting.

4.1When trust moves faster than procurement

The memo signed on August 4, 2026 by Deputy Secretary of Defense Stephen Feinberg directed up to $244 million in spending with Palantir through March 2027 and asked for additional funding to be identified for April 2027 through December 2028. As DefenseScoop and Defense News reported, the document made no mention of competitive procurement, and procurement watchdogs objected on exactly that point. In the September 1 TITAN production decision, Palantir's share was $127 million against Anduril's $65 million. The $48.1 million ammunition management award on September 24 was decided after a three-stage evaluation that ran six weeks.

Speed favors the incumbent and strains the institution. Once one vendor holds the data integration layer, the benchmark for the next award is effectively that same vendor. Viewed through revenue it is a moat; viewed through procurement it is a concentration problem. The risk in this segment only becomes visible when both readings are held at once.

4.2Britain showed where the ceiling is

On November 16 the UK Department of Health and Social Care said it would extend the NHS Federated Data Platform contract by two years to 2029 and then end it, declining to exercise the break clause available next spring. Health minister James Frith told MPs that procurement for a replacement would begin in the second half of 2027 and was expected to take 18 months, and he called the extension "a bridge, not the destination." His stated reasons were rebuilding public trust, ending over-dependence on a single supplier and giving UK companies a path into critical digital infrastructure. The original 2023 contract was worth £330 million, and as of May 2026 more than 120 NHS trusts had signed up, covering 84% of hospital trusts.

The same week in Britain produced news running the other way. Bedfordshire, Cambridgeshire and Hertfordshire police agreed to spend £10.1 million on Foundry. The award was made on October 9, the notice published November 6, and the term runs to October 2031. The Register reported it on November 13 as Palantir's largest UK policing contract. London Mayor Sadiq Khan had previously blocked a £50 million Metropolitan Police award, and the suit Palantir filed over it settled in October. In the same days, reporting from the Bureau of Investigative Journalism revealed that former Met commissioner Dame Cressida Dick has been a paid Palantir adviser since September 2025. Dick said she only advised on how such products might serve the public sector and had never lobbied or made introductions.

What Britain illustrates is a political ceiling rather than a technical contest. A data integration layer is hard to replace once installed, and that difficulty is itself what draws parliamentary and public resistance. With 84% of revenue coming from the United States, the resistance bites hardest outside it.

💡 Chapter Takeaway

Government work is 41% of revenue and the security collateral for the commercial side. Procurement is getting faster in the United States while Britain has set an end date of 2029. The switching cost that builds the moat is the same thing that provokes the backlash.

5

Coexistence, Not Combat

Treating the data platform market as winner-take-all misplaces Palantir. The landscape in late 2026 looks less like several companies fighting over one layer and more like layers being stacked. Storage and compute settle below; meaning and execution settle above.

Anthropic Enterprise models Supply Selectable model inside AIP
Open-source models Build-it-yourself demand Absorb Pulled in as post-training material and turned into a cost advantage
NVIDIA Sovereign AI infrastructure Partner Collaboration widened to defense, manufacturing and healthcare on November 2

5.1Sit on top instead of copying the data

The point of the March 2025 Palantir and Databricks partnership is that data does not move. Customer data stays in Databricks and the Ontology and AIP run over it. The same pattern was applied with SAP. Replacing a warehouse is a multi-year decision for a large company; adding a semantic layer above one can be decided in a quarter. That is also what lets a Bootcamp produce results inside a week. Microsoft Fabric, Snowflake Cortex and Databricks Unity Catalog each pull governance and AI execution toward themselves, so coexistence and competition hold true with different counterparties at the same time.

5.2How the 7% drop of April 2026 ended

In April 2026 the stock fell nearly 7% in a day on the view that Anthropic could collide with Palantir in the enterprise market. Seven months on, the facts read differently. The October product notes show Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5 available inside AIP through AWS Bedrock in region-restricted UK environments, with Fireworks-hosted models added for region-restricted commercial environments in the United States. AIP groups model choices into fast, balanced and smart categories that an organization can set as defaults. The model became an interchangeable part; what cannot be swapped out is the Ontology and the execution layer. The presumed rival arrived as a supplier.

5.3Open source as raw material rather than threat

Do-it-yourself was always the sharpest threat here. The better open models get, the more customers want to build for themselves. Palantir's response was to pull that impulse inside rather than block it: post-train an open model on the customer's data so it performs better on the customer's own cases, and run the whole process within AIP. L3Harris cutting costs 95%, McCarthy saving 30,000 hours a year, and a 30-billion-parameter model beating a three-trillion-parameter one at an Indo-Pacific Command exercise all came out of that route. Sankar also described a customer that built a production model beating frontier models on its own use cases in 48 hours at a hackathon. The wider the band where a small model wins at a specific job, the more the value shifts from the model to the data and the wiring around it.

💡 Chapter Takeaway

Palantir chose to sit above the storage layer rather than fight for it. The company called a rival in April became a model supplier inside AIP, and open source became post-training material. Split the market into layers and the list of real threats changes.

6

Where Pebblous Stands

Everything above describes what happens after an Ontology is in place. Pebblous looks at what comes before it. The Ontology records objects and relationships precisely, and that precision stops working if the data being recorded is inconsistent. When one machine goes by three names across three systems, when defect images are missing labels, when sensor timestamps do not line up, no semantic layer will produce a useful answer.

6.1The bottleneck upstream of the Ontology

A large share of the early hours a forward deployed engineer spends on site goes into that cleanup. The fact that the work is still bound to human hands explains part of Palantir's cost structure, and it also indicates where value accrues if the same work can be automated. Pebblous DataClinic diagnoses the quality of unstructured and domain-specific data and determines whether it is in a state AI can use. If Palantir is the execution layer, DataClinic is the preparation layer ahead of it, and the two connect in sequence rather than compete.

6.2What the Changshin deal says about Korean manufacturing

On November 6, 2026 Palantir announced a partnership with Changshin, headquartered in Busan. Founded in 1981, Changshin produces roughly 69 million pairs of Nike shoes a year and employs more than 80,000 people across Vietnam, China and Indonesia. Its 2025 revenue was 2.28 trillion won with operating profit of 118.5 billion won. The partnership connects development through production using Foundry and AIP. The way it was closed is the notable part: rather than starting from a contract, the two sides ran workflows on live operating data at an AgentCamp and signed afterward. Comments from Palantir Chief Revenue Officer Ryan Taylor and Changshin CEO Nam Chung-il appeared in The Korea Times on November 6.

For that approach to work on a Korean factory floor, one condition has to hold: the operating data used in the demonstration has to be readable on the spot. In a plant where process data, quality inspection images and equipment logs accumulate in separate systems, a camp measured in days gets consumed by cleanup on day one. That is precisely the demand Pebblous sees in manufacturing data quality diagnostics.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean that U.S. commercial revenue passed government revenue?

In Q3 2026 Palantir earned $984 million from U.S. enterprise customers against $953 million from the U.S. government, a $31 million gap and the first such quarter since the listing. Government revenue grew 96% in the same period, so this is commercial accelerating rather than government stalling.

How is AgentCamp different from an AIP Bootcamp?

Both are on-site sessions that build something working from the customer's own data within days. A Bootcamp shows an organization new to AIP what it can do; an AgentCamp leans toward building agents that carry out the work. The Changshin partnership was signed after workflows ran on live operating data at an AgentCamp.

How does the Ontology differ from a database schema?

A schema decides where data lives and in what format. The Ontology sits above that and records the objects, the relationships between them, and the actions that can be taken on them. That is why a model can read that an order is built on a given line and that a delay triggers a specific response.

If bookings rose only 6%, how did revenue rise 96%?

Revenue is recognized as previously signed contracts burn down, while closed contract value is what was newly signed in the quarter. Q3 total contract value of $2.918 billion was only 6% above last year. Remaining deal value of $13.4 billion keeps near-term revenue intact, but a sustained slowdown in signings shows up in growth a few quarters later.

Why did the stock fall on a strong quarter?

Shares slid from the day of the print, and most coverage pointed at the deceleration in closed contract value. The November 16 close of $160.39 is about 25% below the all-time high of $212.94 set on October 12. A trailing P/E of 113 also prices in continued acceleration.

What is Michael Burry's case against the stock?

Burry has kept a short position and disclosed buying March 2027 puts struck at $100. His core argument is that deferred revenue at roughly 32% of revenue makes the model look closer to a consulting firm than a subscription software business. Alex Karp has called the campaign market manipulation.

Where does the government moat actually come from?

From procurement history and clearances more than from technology. In July 2025 the U.S. Army consolidated 75 contracts into a single enterprise agreement with a $10 billion ceiling, and an August 2026 deputy secretary memo directed up to $244 million in spending through March 2027. Watchdogs criticized that memo for making no mention of competitive procurement.

Is Anthropic a competitor to Palantir?

It read that way in April 2026, but as of the October product notes, Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5 are offered inside AIP through AWS Bedrock in region-restricted UK environments. The model became a component AIP selects; what Palantir defends is the Ontology and the execution layer.

How does Pebblous DataClinic relate to Palantir?

Palantir builds an Ontology on the assumption that the data is already in order. DataClinic works upstream of that, diagnosing the quality of unstructured and domain-specific data so it is ready for AI to use. The two are sequential steps, not competing products.
