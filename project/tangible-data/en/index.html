Data says more when you can pick it up, turn it around, and press on it than when you read it in a table.

Tangible Data is a series in which Pebblous opens the explorers we build during our own research to readers, as they are. Instead of an article telling you the conclusion, you pick a point, switch the lens, step through the layers, and check the same data yourself. Photos, coordinates, and numbers on screen are real measurements; synthetic examples built to explain a principle are labeled as such.

The first explorer is built on 300 photos of common-bean leaves taken in Ugandan fields. You can see how four AI models arrange the same photos differently, and how the numbers change as an image passes through the layers of a neural network. We add an explorer whenever an experiment leaves data worth handling.

The name comes from 'Tangible Data: From Data Nature to Data Culture', the interactive media artwork the Pebblous Data Art Lab (DAL) showed at Hyundai Motor Group's ZER01NE Day 2024. Visitors there handled a universe of data with their hands; this series carries that feeling over to research data.

How to Read an Explorer

Measured and synthetic are kept apart

Photos, coordinates, neighbors, and layer values are measured. Examples built with mathematics to show a principle are marked as synthetic on screen. Every explorer uses the same rule so the two are never read as one.

We do not stop at "these look close"

Points clustering together does not mean the model knows why. Each explorer keeps the representation, the distance, and the selection rule in view, with sources and the computation path, so you can trace how a result was made.

The data is downloadable

Every explorer ships with the data files it draws and a list of sources. Data is tangible only if a reader can ask a different question of the same material.

The explorers in Tangible Data come out of Pebblous research on diagnosing and selecting AI training data. See also DataClinic, where data quality diagnosis, curation, and certification meet, and the DataClinic Diagnosis Stories.
