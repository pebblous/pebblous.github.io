Tangible Data is Pebblous's effort to make data something you can handle: to pick up, turn around, and press on what you used to read in tables and charts.

The effort has two hands. One is Tangible Data Story, data-journalism articles in which readers explore research data themselves. The other is Tangible Data Art, works by the Pebblous Data Art Lab (DAL) that turn data into sensory experience. Stories handle data through accuracy and sources; artworks handle it through experience and questions.

The two strands follow different reading rules, so this hub keeps them under one roof without mixing them. Stories separate measured from synthetic and list sources and limits. Artworks are records of exhibitions and interaction.

How to Read a Story

Measured and synthetic are kept apart

Photos, coordinates, neighbors, and layer values are measured. Examples built with mathematics to show a principle are marked as synthetic on screen. Every story uses the same rule so the two are never read as one.

We do not stop at "these look close"

Points clustering together does not mean the model knows why. Each story keeps the representation, the distance, and the selection rule in view, with sources and the computation path, so you can trace how a result was made.

The data is downloadable

Every story ships with the data files it draws and a list of sources. Data is tangible only if a reader can ask a different question of the same material.
